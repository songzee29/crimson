# Chapter Seventy: Beneath the Fallen Mountain

For one terrible heartbeat...

No one moved.

Dust rolled through the gorge, swallowing everything beyond the collapsed mountainside.

Then Lyra screamed.

"Aidan!"

She lunged toward the wall of broken stone.

Cassian caught her before loose rock buried them both.

"It's still falling!"

"Let me go!"

She fought against him with every ounce of strength she had left.

"He'll be trapped!"

"I know!"

His voice cracked.

"I know."

...

Rowan reached the collapse seconds later.

His eyes swept across the shattered cliff.

No opening.

No path through.

Only thousands of tons of broken mountain.

"Dig."

The order came without hesitation.

Nobody questioned whether it was possible.

Malik drove his shovel into the rubble.

Students who had escaped the gorge returned without being asked.

Parents handed frightened children to strangers and joined the rescue.

The people Aidan had saved refused to leave him behind.

...

Far beneath the collapse...

Aidan slowly opened one eye.

Darkness.

Again.

His first thought wasn't fear.

It was disappointment.

*Not again.*

He tried to breathe.

Something heavy pressed across his legs.

His left shoulder screamed.

His ribs felt as though they had finally shattered completely.

He closed his eyes.

The second heartbeat continued beneath his own.

Steady.

Patient.

Waiting.

He listened.

Nothing.

Not yet.

Then...

Very faintly...

Stone.

Moving.

They were digging.

Of course they were.

He leaned his head against cold rock.

"They never quit..."

The words barely escaped his lips.

...

Hours passed.

He drifted in and out of consciousness.

Sometimes he heard Lyra shouting.

Sometimes Cassian.

Sometimes only the mountain settling around him.

More than once he reached for his staff.

His fingers no longer had the strength to close around it.

Blood had soaked through every remaining bandage.

His breathing became shallower.

Not because he wanted to stop.

Because his body had less and less left to give.

...

Above...

Helena grabbed Rowan's arm.

"He can't survive another full collapse."

Rowan never stopped clearing stone.

"Then we reach him first."

"We've been digging for six hours."

"Then we dig seven."

Cassian said nothing.

He simply lifted another slab with his good arm until the muscles trembled violently.

Malik's damaged leg gave out beneath him.

He stood again.

Lyra's hands bled openly.

She never noticed.

No one left.

Not one.

...

Late into the night...

A faint knock echoed from somewhere beneath the mountain.

Everyone froze.

Cassian dropped to one knee.

"Aidan!"

Silence.

Then...

Another weak knock.

Further away than before.

The mountain had shifted.

"He moved," Elias whispered.

"No."

Rowan looked toward the unstable rock overhead.

"The mountain moved."

Hope flickered.

Only for an instant.

The ceiling groaned.

A fresh shower of stone crashed into the rescue tunnel.

Everyone was forced backward.

Another path sealed itself.

Another hour was lost.

Cassian slammed his fist into the rubble.

For the first time in years...

He looked utterly helpless.

# Chapter Seventy-One: The Road That Ends

Dawn broke over East Hollow.

The rescued civilians gathered in silence near the ruined gorge.

No one celebrated surviving.

Too many people had watched the Crimson Witch disappear beneath the mountain to mistake survival for victory.

A little boy quietly walked toward Rowan.

It was the same child Aidan had nodded toward before releasing the Law.

The boy held something tightly in both hands.

"I found this."

Rowan looked down.

Aidan's crimson cloak clasp.

Bent.

Scratched.

Covered in dust.

He accepted it carefully.

Without a word.

...

By midday...

The rescuers finally broke through into a narrow pocket beneath the collapse.

Cassian crawled through first.

"Aidan!"

No answer.

Only slow breathing.

He found him sitting against a broken pillar of stone.

Half-buried.

Eyes closed.

Still conscious.

Barely.

Cassian knelt immediately.

"We've got you."

Aidan opened his eyes.

For a moment...

He didn't seem to recognize where he was.

Then his gaze settled on Cassian.

"The people?"

"They made it."

Aidan nodded once.

That was enough.

He tried to stand.

His legs failed immediately.

Cassian caught him before he fell.

"You don't have to prove anything."

Aidan looked toward the narrow shaft of daylight above them.

"I wasn't."

His voice had become quieter than ever.

"I was trying to walk."

Together, they slowly freed him from the surrounding stone.

Every movement drew another sharp breath from Aidan.

His body shook uncontrollably.

Not from fear.

From exhaustion.

When they finally reached the surface...

The rescued villagers stood waiting.

No cheers greeted him.

Only lowered heads.

A path quietly opened through the crowd.

People understood.

The man walking between them had paid for their lives with pieces of his own.

A little girl stepped forward.

She couldn't have been older than eight.

She held out a small wildflower.

Aidan looked at it for several seconds before accepting it.

"Thank you," she whispered.

He wanted to tell her she didn't owe him thanks.

He wanted to tell her to grow up somewhere peaceful.

He wanted to tell her the world could still become kinder.

Instead...

He simply gave the smallest nod.

There were no words left that felt large enough.

As the column began the slow journey back toward Blackwood Academy...

Aidan walked at its center.

Not because he was strongest.

Because he was the one everyone instinctively gathered around.

Each step cost him.

Each breath hurt.

His body had begun to fail him in ways even Helena could no longer hide.

Yet he kept walking.

Not toward victory.

Not toward hope.

Only toward the next place someone might still need him.

And somewhere, beyond the mountains and beyond his sight...

The road carrying Aidan Darkheart toward his final day as a human grew shorter still.
