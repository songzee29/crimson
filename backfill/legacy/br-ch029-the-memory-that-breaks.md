Aidan didn’t accept it.

Not immediately.

But hesitation has a cost of its own.

The corridor behind them tightened like a closing throat. The silhouettes of the Veil hunters grew clearer now—not rushing, not panicking, just arriving as if the space itself had agreed to deliver them.

Lyra’s grip on his arm tightened.

“Aidan, don’t—”

But the System interrupted her.

Not with words this time.

With presence.

---

> SYSTEM NOTICE  
> DECISION WINDOW CLOSING  
> MEMORY ANCHOR STABILITY: FAILING

---

Aidan felt it then.

Something inside his mind… slipping.

Not pain.

Not injury.

**Loss without movement.**

Like a rope inside his chest being slowly unknotted from something important.

His vision flickered.

For half a second, he saw something else layered over the tunnel.

A different place.

A warmer place.

A voice laughing somewhere just out of reach.

Then it vanished.

Aidan stumbled.

“What’s happening to me?” he whispered.

Lyra looked terrified now—not at the hunters.

At the System.

“They’re not taking your life,” she said. “They’re taking the thing that tells you you’re alive.”

A step echoed behind them.

Closer.

---

> SYSTEM NOTICE  
> MEMORY ANCHOR REQUIRED: EXECUTION INITIATED

---

Aidan clenched his jaw.

“I don’t even know what it is,” he said.

“You don’t have to know,” Lyra replied sharply. “That’s how it works. The System protects you by giving things meaning. If it takes one, you lose part of yourself.”

Another flicker hit him.

Stronger this time.

A memory tried to rise.

A child’s hand.

A woman’s voice.

The word *“hide—”*

It shattered before it fully formed.

Aidan grabbed his head.

“No… no, wait—”

The System didn’t wait.

---

> MEMORY ANCHOR SELECTED  
> EXECUTING EXTRACTION

---

Something tore loose.

Not physically.

Mentally.

Aidan gasped like he had been hit.

And suddenly—

he couldn’t remember why the feeling in his chest mattered.

Not fully.

It was still there.

The emotion remained.

But the *reason* behind it was gone.

Lyra saw the change immediately.

“Aidan… what did you lose?”

He blinked at her.

Confused.

“I… don’t know.”

That answer alone made Lyra go silent.

Because she understood what that meant.

The System didn’t just take memories.

It took **context.**

Behind them, the corridor shifted again.

The Veil hunters paused.

One of them tilted his head.

“Partial destabilization achieved,” he said. “He is becoming readable.”

Lyra pulled Aidan harder.

“We’re leaving. Now.”
