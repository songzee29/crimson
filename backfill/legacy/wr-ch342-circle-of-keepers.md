The heartbeat beneath the mountain sounded again.

Closer.

The vibration rolled through the Foundation Hall, sending ripples across every golden thread connecting the expedition.

The crystal’s light dimmed.

Not dying.

Entrusting.

Its work was finished.

Now…

The Keepers had to do theirs.

A deep fracture split the floor beyond the Hall of Laws.

Not violently.

Deliberately.

The stone slowly separated, revealing an immense circular chamber hidden below.

Unlike the Hall above…

This place bore scars.

The walls were blackened.

Ancient Witch runes had been burned into glass.

The ceiling disappeared into darkness, supported by twelve immense pillars wrapped in petrified roots.

At the center…

A single circle had been carved directly into the mountain.

Half of it still glowed with warm gold.

The other half…

Had turned black.

Professor Helena stared.

“The Seal Circle…”

Master Rowan nodded once.

“This is where Grey Hollow made its final stand.”

⸻

They descended together.

No one spoke.

Foundation Threads streamed toward the circle from every direction.

Hundreds.

Thousands.

Every remaining thread of Grey Hollow ended here.

Aidan knelt.

His Foundation Awareness reached farther than ever before.

The circle wasn’t broken.

The black half wasn’t consuming the gold.

It was…

Pushing.

Like two tides meeting without ever crossing.

The Witch Curse wasn’t inside.

It was pressing from beneath.

Trying to force the Foundation to yield.

“It’s holding…” Aidan whispered.

“…but it’s exhausted.”

Professor Helena joined him.

“Can it be repaired?”

He closed his eyes.

“No.”

Everyone fell silent.

“The Foundation isn’t asking to be repaired.”

He opened them again.

“It’s asking…”

“…to be relieved.”

⸻

The ancient voice returned.

Weak.

Fragmented.

Yet unmistakably peaceful.

The burden…

Must be shared.

Master Rowan looked toward Aidan.

“What did the Legacy just give you?”

Aidan understood immediately.

“Circle Weaving.”

“Then trust it.”

⸻

He stood in the center of the ancient Seal Circle.

His breathing slowed.

No spell came to mind.

No incantation.

This wasn’t about commanding magic.

It was about trusting people.

He looked at each of them.

“Take a place.”

No one questioned him.

Cassian stepped to the northern point.

Lyra to the east.

Malik to the west.

Elias to the south.

Darius and Kael naturally filled the diagonal positions.

Professor Helena completed the final point of the circle.

Rowan and Bennett remained beyond its edge.

Watching.

Guarding.

Teaching.

The Foundation Threads immediately shifted.

Golden lines stretched from each person to Aidan.

Not taking their power.

Acknowledging their willingness.

The Crimson Legacy appeared only to him.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

CIRCLE WEAVING

Ready.

Trust cannot be borrowed.

Confirm synchronization?

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Aidan took one steady breath.

“Confirm.”

The mountain answered.

Golden light surged through the ancient circle.

Lyra felt Aidan’s determination.

Cassian felt Malik’s resolve.

Elias suddenly understood Helena’s calm.

Each of them remained themselves…

Yet none stood alone.

The Foundation breathed.

For the first time in generations…

Its burden rested upon many shoulders instead of one.

The black half of the Seal Circle stopped advancing.

Not retreating.

Stopping.

The chamber shuddered.

A long crack running through one of the twelve pillars slowly sealed itself.

Professor Helena smiled.

“It worked.”

Rowan quietly shook his head.

“No.”

“They worked.”

The Crimson Legacy shimmered.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

CIRCLE WEAVING

First Successful Synchronization

Judgment:

Leadership accepted responsibility.

Companions accepted burden.

Foundation Stability Increased.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The message faded.

No reward followed.

The Foundation itself was the reward.

⸻

Then…

Everything became still.

Too still.

The golden threads connecting the expedition abruptly tightened.

Not painfully.

Urgently.

Aidan’s Foundation Awareness erupted.

Something had changed.

Below them.

The pressure vanished.

His eyes widened.

“It moved.”

The heartbeat sounded again.

Not beneath the chamber.

Behind them.
