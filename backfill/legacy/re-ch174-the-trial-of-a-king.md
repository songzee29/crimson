The Judge moved.

Nobody saw it.

One moment he stood across the cathedral.

The next...

His sword crashed into the floor where Aidan had been.

The impact split the stone for hundreds of feet.

"HEX STEP!"

Aidan vanished.

Appearing behind the Judge.

Crimson Shackles erupted.

The chains wrapped around the giant warrior—

And shattered instantly.

The Judge didn't even look back.

"Power..."

He swung his sword again.

"...without purpose..."

A wave of crimson force exploded through the cathedral.

Everyone was thrown backward.

"...is merely destruction."

Lyra slammed into a pillar.

Malik barely caught Elias before he hit the ground.

Sienna disappeared into the shadows to flank the Judge.

The Guardian stood still.

Watching.

He wasn't allowed to interfere.

This was the Heir's trial.

Aidan wiped blood from his lip.

Then...

He smiled.

Not because he was winning.

Because...

He finally understood.

This wasn't about defeating the Judge.

It was about understanding what kind of king the Legacy would accept.

The Witch Authority stirred quietly within him.

Not with anger.

With resolve.

For the first time since inheriting the Crimson Legacy...

Aidan stepped forward without fear.

Not because he knew he would win.

But because there were people behind him worth protecting.

The Judge noticed.

For the first time...

The ancient warrior smiled beneath his helmet.

Very slightly.

Almost imperceptibly.

As if, after centuries of waiting...

He had finally seen the answer he had been looking for.

Deep within the cathedral, hidden behind walls no one had yet discovered, something ancient awakened.

Not the Second Seal.

Not the First Fang.

Something older.

A heartbeat.

Slow.

Heavy.

Patient.

**...Thump.**

The entire cathedral trembled.

The Guardian's eyes widened.

He whispered to himself:

> "...Impossible."

Because that heartbeat...

Did not belong to the cathedral.

It belonged to something sleeping beneath it.

Something even Zee had chosen not to awaken.

And far above, beneath the flowing waters of the Mississippi, the river itself rippled as if something deep below had just opened its eyes.
