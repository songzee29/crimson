# Chapter Thirty-Four: The Things He Could Not Save

Blackwood Academy buried three instructors at sunrise.

No speeches echoed across the courtyard.

No grand ceremony attempted to give meaning to lives that had ended too soon.

Only silence.

Students stood shoulder to shoulder while Rowan lowered each staff into the earth.

Professor Helena spoke no words.

She simply placed her hand upon each grave before turning away.

Aidan remained at the back.

He had saved the academy.

He had not saved them.

The difference mattered.

...

Sleep never came that night.

Nor the night before.

Nor the one before that.

When Aidan closed his eyes...

He heard collapsing bridges.

Burning villages.

Children screaming.

The hunter's whisper.

*You have your traitor to thank for this.*

He rose before dawn.

Again.

The old oak waited exactly where it always had.

Unchanging.

Unlike him.

His reflection in a rain-filled puddle startled him.

His face had grown thinner.

The bruises beneath his eyes had darkened.

The easy warmth that once lived there had quietly disappeared.

He could not remember the last time he had laughed.

...

"Aidan."

Lyra approached slowly.

"You left before breakfast."

"I wasn't hungry."

"You weren't hungry yesterday either."

He looked away.

"I forgot."

"No."

Her voice remained gentle.

"You didn't."

She held out a small piece of warm bread.

He forced himself to take it.

Forced himself to eat.

Each bite felt like work.

Halfway through...

He stopped.

He simply couldn't continue.

"I'm trying."

"I know."

She smiled sadly.

"I just miss seeing you enjoy things."

The words lingered after she walked away.

He looked down at the untouched bread still resting in his hand.

For the first time...

He couldn't remember what hunger used to feel like.

...

The next burden arrived before midday.

Grey Hollow.

Again.

Not attacked.

Flooded.

Heavy rains had burst the northern riverbanks.

Half the village now stood beneath rising water.

Malik stared at the messenger.

"We just rebuilt it."

The messenger lowered his head.

"I know."

No hunters.

No claws.

No traps.

Just another disaster.

Another choice.

Another demand.

Rowan looked toward Aidan.

Neither spoke.

Both already knew they were going.

...

The river had become a monster.

Homes drifted downstream.

Families clung desperately to rooftops.

Children cried from shattered wagons trapped against broken trees.

Aidan stood at the riverbank.

His ribs still hadn't healed.

His side reopened the moment he raised both hands.

"Hold."

The river fought him.

Not with malice.

With weight.

Thousands upon thousands of gallons pressed against invisible crimson barriers.

His muscles trembled.

Blood seeped beneath his bandages.

Malik carried children across newly stabilized debris.

Cassian rescued an elderly couple trapped beneath collapsed beams.

Lyra guided families toward higher ground.

Elias reinforced every barrier Aidan created.

Hour after hour.

The flood slowly retreated.

The village survived.

Again.

Barely.

Aidan fell to one knee the instant the final family reached safety.

The river no longer needed holding.

His body simply refused another second.

...

Helena met them before they returned to Blackwood.

She looked at the blood soaking through Aidan's clothes.

Then at the exhausted expressions surrounding him.

"You fought?"

"No."

Malik answered quietly.

"We saved people."

Helena closed her eyes.

"I don't think your body knows the difference anymore."

# Chapter Thirty-Five: The Weight That Never Lifted

That night Blackwood Academy remained awake.

Not because another attack had come.

Because everyone expected one.

The waiting itself had become exhausting.

Students startled at every shifting shadow.

Faculty slept in armor.

Lanterns remained lit until dawn.

Fear had become routine.

...

Aidan sat alone inside the strategy hall.

Maps covered every table.

Villages.

Supply routes.

Collapsed tunnels.

Missing patrols.

Destroyed bridges.

Every solved problem had created another.

He quietly moved another marker across the map.

Grey Hollow.

Safe.

For now.

A knock interrupted his thoughts.

Cassian entered.

"You've been in here for four hours."

"I lost track."

"I know."

Cassian sat across from him.

Neither man spoke for several minutes.

Finally Cassian asked,

"Do you remember the first day we met?"

Aidan smiled faintly.

"You challenged me."

"You looked terrified."

"I was."

"You hid it badly."

Silence.

Cassian's expression softened.

"I don't remember the last time I saw that version of you."

The words struck harder than any hunter's claw.

"I don't either."

It was the first completely honest answer Aidan had given in days.

...

Long after Cassian left...

Aidan remained alone.

His hearing stretched across the academy.

Every patrol.

Every conversation.

Every heartbeat.

Then...

One heartbeat faltered.

A young apprentice collapsed from exhaustion during the midnight watch.

Aidan was already standing before anyone called for help.

He reached the courtyard moments before Helena.

The apprentice looked up weakly.

"I'm sorry..."

"You don't have to apologize," Aidan said quietly.

"You were trying."

The apprentice smiled.

"So are you."

The simple words left Aidan unexpectedly speechless.

...

Later...

Standing once more beneath the old oak...

He rested his forehead against its ancient trunk.

He didn't ask for strength.

He didn't ask for victory.

He asked for one thing.

"Just..."

His voice barely carried above the wind.

"...let them survive."

No answer came.

Only the familiar second heartbeat beneath his own.

Steady.

Patient.

Waiting.

Far beyond Blackwood...

The Commander stood atop a ridge overlooking the sleeping academy.

The Observer carved another symbol into black stone.

Not a victory.

Not a defeat.

A burden.

The Commander watched the exhausted Crimson Witch standing alone beneath the old oak.

It had learned something new.

The fastest way to weaken Aidan was no longer through battle.

It was through responsibility.

If every problem became his to solve...

If every life became his to carry...

Then one day...

When the impossible choice finally came...

He would already be too exhausted to survive it.

And somewhere deep inside Aidan...

Without anyone realizing it...

Another quiet piece of the boy he used to be slipped away.
