# Chapter Fifty-Six: The Price of Standing

Aidan woke to darkness.

Not underground.

The infirmary.

Rain tapped softly against the windows.

The familiar scent of herbs lingered in the air.

Every part of his body hurt.

For one brief moment...

He almost welcomed it.

Pain meant he was still alive.

The thought no longer comforted him.

...

"You should still be unconscious."

Helena's voice broke the silence.

She stood beside his bed, exhaustion etched into every line of her face.

"You've been asleep for almost two days."

Aidan tried to sit.

The room spun violently.

Pain tore through his ribs.

His side burned beneath layers of fresh bandages.

His shoulder refused to lift higher than his chest.

Helena gently pushed him back.

"No."

"I have patrol."

"No."

"The rescue reports."

"No."

"The northern villages—"

"No."

Every answer came firmer than the last.

"You almost died."

Silence.

Finally Aidan asked the question that truly mattered.

"The apprentices?"

"They're alive."

His shoulders relaxed.

Only then.

Helena noticed.

He had asked about everyone else before himself.

Again.

...

Later that afternoon...

Rowan entered alone.

Neither spoke immediately.

The old master quietly placed another weathered map beside the bed.

Three fresh marks.

Three separate villages.

"These came while you were unconscious."

Aidan closed his eyes.

"How bad?"

"One caravan disappeared."

He moved one marker.

"A bridge collapsed."

Another.

"And an entire watch patrol never returned."

The last marker remained untouched.

"We haven't found them."

Aidan slowly sat upright despite Helena's orders.

Every movement hurt.

He ignored it.

"I'll go."

"No."

Rowan's answer came without hesitation.

"You can barely breathe."

"They still need someone."

"They need you alive."

Aidan looked toward the rain outside.

"If I stay here..."

He swallowed.

"...someone else won't."

The room fell silent.

Neither man won.

Neither man was wrong.

...

By evening...

Aidan had left the infirmary anyway.

His pace was slower.

His breathing shallower.

The academy noticed immediately.

Students stopped pretending not to stare.

Some smiled with relief.

Others looked frightened.

Not because he was injured.

Because they had begun believing he couldn't be.

He walked past them quietly.

One first-year rushed forward.

"Master Aidan..."

He still wasn't used to hearing that title.

"We repaired the eastern barrier."

The young witch smiled proudly.

"We did it the way you taught us."

For the first time in days...

Aidan almost smiled.

"Good."

Only one word.

Yet the student beamed as though he'd received the greatest praise imaginable.

Aidan continued walking.

The moment passed.

His smile disappeared before anyone else noticed it.

# Chapter Fifty-Seven: What the Rain Revealed

The rain finally stopped.

Fog replaced it.

Heavy enough that Blackwood's outer walls vanished into white.

Perfect weather for another disappearance.

Everyone knew it.

Patrols doubled.

Again.

Messengers traveled in groups of four instead of two.

Again.

The academy adapted.

Again.

Yet the uneasiness remained.

...

Aidan insisted on joining the northern patrol.

Helena argued until her voice grew hoarse.

Rowan remained silent.

When Aidan finally stood before the gates fully dressed for travel...

The old master simply handed him a new walking staff.

"Lean on this."

It wasn't permission.

It wasn't refusal.

It was acceptance that Aidan would go regardless.

...

The forest felt different.

Not hostile.

Empty.

Even Malik noticed.

"I hate this."

Cassian nodded.

"I'd rather hear something."

The silence pressed against them.

Every snapped twig sounded too loud.

Every drifting branch became another question.

Halfway through the march...

Aidan stopped so suddenly Lyra nearly walked into him.

"What?"

He didn't answer.

His hearing stretched through the fog.

Water.

Wind.

Then...

Footsteps.

Running.

Not toward them.

Away.

Fast.

"Someone's moving."

"Where?"

He pointed.

Everyone ran.

The chase lasted only minutes.

It ended beside a narrow ravine.

Fresh footprints stopped at the edge.

No sign of climbing down.

No sign of jumping across.

Simply...

Gone.

Cassian studied the mud.

"Impossible."

"No."

Aidan knelt despite the pain pulling through his side.

His fingers brushed one of the prints.

Still warm.

Whoever had been here had vanished only moments before.

A faint sound drifted upward from the ravine.

Not a voice.

Not an animal.

Rock.

Breaking.

Very deep below.

His instincts screamed.

"Back."

No one questioned him.

The patrol retreated.

Three heartbeats later...

The entire edge of the ravine collapsed into the darkness.

Earth thundered downward.

Trees disappeared.

The place where they had stood seconds earlier simply ceased to exist.

Malik stared into the widening chasm.

"If we'd stayed..."

"We'd be buried."

Aidan finished quietly.

His knees buckled.

The sudden movement reopened the wound along his side.

Blood soaked through the fresh bandages almost immediately.

Lyra caught him before he hit the ground.

"You shouldn't even be here."

He leaned heavily against the new staff Rowan had given him.

"I know."

His voice sounded distant.

Almost detached.

"I just..."

He looked toward the shattered ravine.

"...couldn't stay behind."

No one answered.

Because every one of them understood.

As they turned back toward Blackwood...

Aidan glanced once more into the fog.

For the briefest instant...

He thought he saw a lone figure standing across the ravine.

Still.

Watching.

The fog shifted.

Nothing remained.

He said nothing.

Not because he doubted what he had seen.

Because he no longer trusted the difference between what the forest showed him...

And what it wanted him to see.

Behind them, unnoticed by the patrol, a fresh rockslide sealed the ravine completely.

Whatever answers might have existed below...

Were buried before anyone had the chance to ask the questions.
