# Chapter Sixty-Four: The Ones He Couldn't Reach

The messenger never reached the strategy hall.

He collapsed inside Blackwood's gates.

"North..."

His voice cracked.

"...please..."

Then nothing.

Helena rushed forward.

She found a pulse.

Weak.

Barely.

Aidan was already moving.

No discussion.

No planning.

Only action.

...

The northern valley lay under a curtain of smoke.

Not fresh.

Hours old.

Too late.

The realization settled over the rescue party long before they reached the village.

They found survivors.

Not many.

A little girl sat beside a burned doorway holding the hand of a woman who would never wake again.

She looked up as Aidan approached.

"You came."

Her voice held no anger.

Only exhaustion.

He knelt despite the pain tearing through his ribs.

"I'm sorry."

She nodded once.

"I know."

She had heard those words before.

Perhaps from someone else.

Perhaps only in stories.

Now they belonged to him too.

...

The search continued.

Every house held another answer they wished they hadn't found.

A father beneath collapsed beams.

A healer beside an empty medicine chest.

A patrol of six.

Only two still breathed.

Cassian helped lift broken timber.

Malik carried the wounded toward the wagons despite the agony in his damaged leg.

Lyra wrapped frightened children in blankets.

Elias quietly wrote names.

Someone had to remember them.

Aidan moved from one ruin to the next.

His shoulder burned with every lift.

His side bled through fresh bandages.

His breathing remained uneven.

He stopped beside one destroyed home.

Too late.

Again.

He closed his eyes.

One slow breath.

Then another.

"Move."

Only one word.

The rescue continued.

...

They left the valley carrying fewer people than they had hoped.

More than they had feared.

It did not feel like victory.

No one called it one.

# Chapter Sixty-Five: The Law That Failed

The wagons had barely crossed Blackwood's gates when the western alarm bell began screaming.

Not ringing.

Screaming.

Three guards stood atop the walls waving crimson signal flags.

The western bridge.

Again.

Aidan looked toward Rowan.

The old master saw the exhaustion written across every face.

"We don't have time."

No one did.

...

The bridge had already begun collapsing before they arrived.

Nearly forty civilians remained trapped on the far side.

Rain had swollen the river below into a violent torrent.

The old supports groaned beneath impossible strain.

Aidan stepped onto the bridge.

"Stay behind me."

His voice was quiet.

Steady.

Cassian moved beside him.

"No."

Aidan didn't look back.

"I'll hold them."

He raised both hands.

"Hold."

Crimson light spread across the fractured bridge.

The timbers stopped falling.

For one heartbeat.

Then another.

People ran.

Children first.

Then the elderly.

Then the wounded.

The bridge shook violently.

Aidan's breathing became ragged.

Blood ran freely from his nose.

His injured shoulder trembled uncontrollably.

The Law flickered.

"Aidan!" Lyra shouted.

He didn't answer.

The bridge lurched again.

His vision blurred.

He forced more will into the Law.

The crimson light brightened.

Then—

It shattered.

The Law broke.

The bridge collapsed.

Cassian leaped forward, catching one child before the river claimed her.

Malik threw himself toward an elderly man, dragging him onto solid ground.

Lyra caught another survivor by the wrist.

Not everyone made it.

The river swallowed several figures before anyone could reach them.

Aidan fell to one knee.

His chest heaved.

He stared at the water rushing beneath the broken bridge.

For the first time since becoming the Crimson Witch...

His Law had failed because his body could no longer sustain it.

No lack of knowledge.

No lack of will.

Only exhaustion.

Helena reached him moments later.

He didn't look at her.

He kept watching the river.

"They were..."

His voice nearly disappeared.

"...too slow."

Helena followed his gaze.

"No."

Her answer came softly.

"You were asked to hold up the impossible."

He never replied.

The survivors gathered on the riverbank.

Some embraced.

Some wept.

Some searched desperately downstream for people who would never return.

Behind them...

The broken bridge drifted away piece by piece.

Aidan remained kneeling.

Water roared below.

His breathing wouldn't steady.

His hands wouldn't stop shaking.

He had saved many.

He had not saved all.

This time...

He had watched the moment hope slipped through his own fingers.

And when Rowan quietly placed a hand on his shoulder, Aidan did not speak.

He simply stood.

Turned toward the next road.

And began walking.

Because somewhere else...

Another bell would ring.

And someone would still be waiting.
