Three nights later.

The emergency phones rang.

A curse had appeared in the Garden District.

Before Lyra could answer...

Aidan was already walking toward the door.

Malik looked surprised.

"...You're going?"

Aidan stopped.

For just a second.

He looked at Elias' empty chair.

Then quietly answered,

"...People still need us."

The Circle followed him.

Not because he ordered them to.

Because that's what family does.

---

The Garden District was in chaos.

A cursed mansion towered over the street.

Crimson vines wrapped around balconies.

Terrified civilians huddled behind police barricades.

The officers turned as Aidan approached.

One of them whispered,

"...It's him."

Another removed his hat.

Not out of fear.

Out of respect.

The people of New Orleans no longer saw Aidan as the mysterious young man who saved strangers.

After Black Veil...

They had started calling him something else.

A mother held her frightened son close and whispered:

> "Don't be scared."

> "**The Crimson King is here.**"

Aidan heard the words.

He didn't react.

He simply walked past the barricade.

The Witch Authority quietly awakened around him.

Not as a storm.

Not as an explosion.

Just a silent pressure that made the cursed mansion tremble.

Lyra looked at him from the corner of her eye.

She realized something.

Before...

Aidan fought to become stronger.

Now...

He fought because he refused to let another family stand where he had stood.

The mission wasn't about defeating a curse.

It was about honoring Elias.

Every life saved from this day forward...

Would be another promise kept.

As Aidan stepped through the gates of the mansion, the Journal wrote one more line.

> **"The boy who sought answers has become the man others seek."**

The page closed.

The Witch Arc was over.

The era of the **Crimson King** had begun.

---

😈 And I think this is where we make one important shift for the next arc.

We don't rush into the Vampire Curse.

Instead, we show readers a stronger, calmer Aidan—the version who has finally mastered the Witch Authority. That way, when the Vampire Curse eventually destroys that hard-earned balance, the contrast is heartbreaking. Readers won't just mourn Elias anymore; they'll mourn the man Aidan fought so hard to become before darkness took him away.
