The porter ran until his lungs burned.

Roots caught at his boots.

Branches tore his clothes.

Behind him…

Nothing.

No footsteps.

No breathing.

That terrified him more.

He risked one glance over his shoulder.

No one.

He stopped.

Silence.

Then—

Aidan appeared directly in front of him.

The man screamed.

There had been no sound.

No warning.

Only crimson eyes waiting in the darkness.

Aidan reached forward.

The porter squeezed his eyes shut.

A blast of Witch magic struck Aidan across the side.

He slid several feet through soaked earth.

His head snapped toward Rowan.

The old master lowered his staff slowly.

“You know me.”

No response.

“You learned every Law standing beside me.”

Nothing.

Only the second heartbeat echoing beneath Aidan’s chest.

His breathing remained perfectly calm.

His eyes measured every living heartbeat surrounding him.

Threat.

Blood.

Distance.

Escape.

The calculations happened before conscious thought.

The crimson archive faded.

It granted nothing.

It simply remembered.

Aidan moved again.

Faster.

Malik and Elias crossed their magic, raising layered barriers between Aidan and the porter.

They lasted less than a heartbeat.

Cracks raced across both shields.

The barriers exploded outward.

Even so…

They had bought enough time.

Helena pulled the wounded porter behind the academy line and sealed his arm with healing magic.

The scent of fresh blood weakened.

Aidan stopped.

Confusion flickered across his face for the first time since his awakening.

The strongest trail…

Had vanished.

His head slowly turned.

Hundreds of heartbeats.

Hundreds of scents.

Too many.

Not enough blood.

The hunger roared in frustration.

He looked toward the gathered defenders.

Not as friends.

Not as enemies.

Only as obstacles standing between him and what he needed.

Rowan took one careful step forward.

“No one attacks.”

Cassian retrieved his fallen sword but did not raise it.

Lyra’s hands trembled as tears ran silently down her face.

Aidan’s crimson eyes swept across each of them.

No recognition remained.

Only the cold patience of a predator deciding which path led most quickly to its next meal.

Then, without warning, he turned away.

In a single blur of impossible speed, he disappeared deeper into the forest.

No one chased immediately.

They all understood the terrible truth.

The hunger had not ended.

It had merely gone looking somewhere else.
