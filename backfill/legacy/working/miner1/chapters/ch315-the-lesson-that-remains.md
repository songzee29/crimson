The academy had just remembered another name.

Silence lingered inside the western archive.

Dust drifted lazily through the shafts of afternoon light as Aidan continued looking at the portrait.

The crimson in the painted eyes had already faded back into ordinary pigment.

The words beneath the frame had disappeared.

It looked like nothing more than an old piece of forgotten history.

Master Rowan walked slowly to stand beside him.

He did not look at Aidan.

He looked at the portrait.

“I wondered when it would happen.”

Aidan finally spoke.

“It changed.”

“Yes.”

“You saw it.”

“I did.”

Aidan waited.

The old traveler smiled faintly.

“You want me to explain.”

“I do.”

Rowan gently shook his head.

“I can’t.”

Aidan looked at him.

“Or you won’t?”

The old man considered the question.

“Both.”

He rested one weathered hand against the old wooden frame.

“There are some truths…”

“…that become smaller the moment another person tells them to you.”

Aidan remained quiet.

“The Founder believed discovery should belong to the one who earns it.”

He looked toward Aidan.

“So tell me…”

“What did you feel?”

Aidan didn’t answer immediately.

He searched for the right words.

“Not chosen.”

“Good.”

“Not important.”

“Good.”

He looked back toward the portrait.

“…Remembered.”

Master Rowan closed his eyes.

When he opened them again…

There was unmistakable pride within them.

“That…”

“…is the right answer.”

The two left the archive together.

Neither hurried.

The library had begun filling again as students searched for books before the evening study period.

Life continued around them.

Ordinary.

Comforting.

At the library doors…

Master Rowan stopped.

“Aidan.”

“Yes?”

“When people remember you…”

He smiled warmly.

“…make sure they remember how you treated them.”

“Not how powerful you became.”

The old traveler quietly walked away.

Leaving the words behind.

⸻

Outside…

Cassian stood in the courtyard balancing an apple on Elias’s shoulder.

“This is a terrible idea.”

Elias didn’t move.

“I agree.”

“So why are you standing there?”

“Because you said if I flinched…”

Cassian grinned.

“…I’d owe you lunch.”

Malik looked toward Aidan the moment he stepped outside.

“Perfect timing.”

Lyra folded her arms.

“Please tell him this is stupid.”

Aidan looked from the apple…

To Cassian…

To Elias.

“It is.”

Cassian sighed dramatically.

“I expected more support.”

“You have terrible ideas.”

“I have ambitious ideas.”

“You nearly broke three practice dummies this week.”

“They were emotionally fragile.”

Malik burst into laughter.

Even Elias smiled.

For a moment…

Nothing existed except friends teasing one another beneath the afternoon sun.

⸻

Cassian suddenly tossed the apple toward Aidan.

Without thinking…

Aidan caught it.

Cleanly.

Cassian blinked.

“Show-off.”

“You threw it.”

“I wasn’t expecting you to catch it.”

“You should’ve.”

The words escaped before Aidan realized it.

Everyone looked at him.

Then…

Cassian smiled.

“You’re right.”

“I should have.”

It wasn’t about the apple.

It wasn’t about reflexes.

It was trust.

Cassian had thrown it without thinking.

Aidan had caught it without doubting.

Small moments.

Quiet moments.

The kind Rowan insisted built civilizations.

⸻

The five eventually wandered toward the western gardens.

The old oak welcomed them with familiar shade.

No one sat in the same place intentionally.

Yet somehow…

They always ended up in the same places.

Cassian lay on the grass looking toward the clouds.

“If we all graduate…”

He stopped.

“When we graduate…”

Malik corrected.

Cassian smiled.

“When we graduate…”

“We should travel.”

Elias looked over.

“Where?”

“Everywhere.”

Lyra laughed.

“That’s not a destination.”

“It’ll become one.”

Cassian turned toward Aidan.

“What about you?”

Aidan looked across the academy.

“I’ve never really thought about it.”

“You should.”

Cassian shrugged.

“Life can’t only be responsibilities.”

For a brief moment…

Aidan remembered the Founder’s observation.

Remember this day.

Not because it is extraordinary…

But because one day…

You will wish for one more ordinary afternoon.

He quietly smiled.

“Then let’s make sure we get there together.”

Cassian extended his fist without another word.

One by one…

Malik.

Elias.

Lyra.

Finally…

Aidan.

Five fists met in the center.

No promises spoken aloud.

None were needed.

⸻

That evening…

The Crimson Legacy quietly appeared.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

FOUNDER OBSERVATION

The greatest oaths…

Are often never spoken.

Character Progress Recorded.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The message faded.

Aidan never mentioned it.

He simply looked at the four people beside him.

And silently agreed.

The four people beside him.

And silently agreed.

The sun slipped behind the western mountains.

Long shadows stretched across the academy grounds.

Students slowly returned to their dormitories while lanterns flickered awake one by one.

The five friends remained beneath the old oak.

None of them seemed eager to leave.

Finally…

Lyra stood.

“I should probably study.”

Cassian looked horrified.

“Voluntarily?”

“I enjoy passing.”

“I enjoy surviving.”

“You call that surviving?”

“It has worked so far.”

Elias rose beside her.

“I’ll walk with you.”

The two headed toward the academic halls, still debating tomorrow’s assignments.

Malik stretched.

“I promised Coach Bennett I’d help move equipment.”

Cassian looked at Aidan.

“Guess it’s just us.”

⸻

The two walked without destination.

Past the training grounds.

Past the fountain.

Past the eastern arch.

Neither spoke for several minutes.

The silence never felt uncomfortable.

Cassian finally broke it.

“You know…”

“I never thanked you.”

Aidan frowned.

“For what?”

“The evaluations.”

Aidan looked at him.

“What about them?”

Cassian kicked a small pebble along the path.

“When I read mine…”

“I almost believed it.”

He laughed once.

Without humor.

“Not because I trusted the paper.”

“Because…”

He searched for the words.

“…part of me wondered if maybe I’d been holding everyone back.”

Aidan stopped walking.

Cassian did too.

“You’ve never held us back.”

“You don’t know that.”

“I do.”

Cassian looked away.

“I make jokes all the time.”

“You make people laugh.”

“I break things.”

“You also fix things.”

“When?”

“When nobody’s looking.”

Cassian frowned.

Aidan smiled.

“You carried Elias’s books yesterday.”

“You made sure Malik actually ate after training.”

“You repaired the first-years’ practice rack after you accidentally broke it.”

“I didn’t tell anybody.”

“I know.”

Silence settled.

Cassian looked almost embarrassed.

“I just…”

“…didn’t want anyone dealing with extra problems.”

Aidan nodded.

“I know.”

For the first time since they had met…

Cassian didn’t hide behind another joke.

He simply smiled.

A real one.

Then…

Without warning…

He wrapped one arm around Aidan’s shoulders.

“You’re getting annoyingly good at noticing people.”

Aidan laughed.

“I had good teachers.”

“No.”

Cassian shook his head.

“You’ve become one.”

Those words struck deeper than he expected.

⸻

They reached the old stone bridge overlooking the academy stream.

Water rushed quietly beneath them.

Cassian leaned against the railing.

“If something ever goes wrong…”

He spoke casually.

“So casually it almost sounded meaningless.”

“You’ll tell me.”

It wasn’t a question.

Aidan looked at him.

“I will.”

“You promise?”

“I promise.”

Cassian nodded once.

“Good.”

Then…

As naturally as breathing…

They started walking again.

⸻

Far behind them…

Master Rowan watched from the library balcony.

He had seen the entire conversation.

Headmaster Aldren quietly joined him.

“They’ve become brothers.”

Rowan’s smile carried both warmth and sorrow.

“Not by blood.”

“The strongest kind rarely are.”

Aldren looked toward the two young men disappearing into the evening light.

“That bond will matter.”

“It already does.”

The old traveler lowered his eyes.

“And someday…”

“…it will cost them both.”

⸻

Night settled over Blackwood Academy.

Aidan stood alone outside his dormitory before going inside.

He looked toward the old oak.

Toward the eastern arch.

Toward the mountain beyond the academy walls.

Everything seemed peaceful.

The Crimson Legacy appeared one final time before the night ended.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

FOUNDER OBSERVATION

When hardship comes…

You will not remember every lesson.

You will remember…

The people who taught you how to stand again.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The words faded into the quiet night.

Aidan remained still for a long time.

Then…

He whispered into the darkness.

“I won’t forget.”

Far beneath Blackwood Academy…

The ancient heartbeat answered.

Boom.

This time…

It was joined immediately by the second heartbeat.

No longer distant.

No longer approaching.

It had arrived.

And somewhere within the stone beneath the academy…

Something ancient…

Began walking.”
