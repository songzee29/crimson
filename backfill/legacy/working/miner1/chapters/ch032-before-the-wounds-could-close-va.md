The stitches across Aidan’s side lasted less than twenty-four hours.

Not because they failed.

Because another alarm split the morning before sunrise.

Three sharp pulses.

North.

Then one pulse.

West.

A pause.

Then the eastern signal rune flared crimson.

Not another attack.

Three attacks.

At once.

The strategy hall erupted into motion.

Messengers crossed the room carrying reports faster than anyone could read them.

“The northern patrol lost contact.”

“Grey Hollow’s warning rune activated again.”

“The western supply road has collapsed.”

Rowan spread the reports across the table.

“They’re forcing decisions.”

Helena immediately answered.

“We don’t have enough people.”

“No.”

Aidan’s voice remained quiet.

“They know that.”

The room fell silent.

The hunters weren’t becoming stronger.

They were becoming patient.

…

“We split.”

“No.”

Every eye turned toward Aidan.

“They expect that.”

Cassian folded his arms.

“Then what?”

Aidan stared at the map.

His hearing drifted beyond the room.

Students moving supplies.

Guards changing positions.

The wind against the western towers.

Then…

A heartbeat.

Not inside Blackwood.

Outside the eastern wall.

Waiting.

Watching.

“They’re counting.”

Rowan noticed Aidan’s focus.

“What did you hear?”

“They’re waiting to see who leaves.”

No one questioned him.

Not anymore.

…

Blackwood changed its response.

Instead of sending one large rescue force…

Small teams departed in different directions.

Some were real.

Others carried empty wagons.

Some left through the gates.

Others through hidden tunnels.

The academy adapted again.

…

The hunters adapted too.

Aidan realized it first.

“They ignored the decoys.”

Cassian looked toward him.

“How do you know?”

“They never moved.”

A minute later…

A messenger arrived.

“The false convoy reached Grey Hollow untouched.”

Another followed.

“The real supply wagon was attacked in the southern valley.”

The room became silent.

“They watched long enough to recognize the difference,” Elias whispered.

“They’re learning faster.”

“No.”

Aidan looked toward the southern valley.

“They’re watching longer.”

…

By midday…

The rescue force reached the southern road.

The wagons still stood.

The horses did not.

The drivers had survived.

Barely.

No hunters remained.

Only another message.

This one carved into the wagon itself.

One black line.

Then another.

Then another.

Thirty-two.

Cassian frowned.

“They’re counting the hunts.”

Aidan shook his head.

“They’re counting me.”

The realization lingered between them.

The Observer wasn’t recording battles anymore.

It was recording Aidan.

…

As they searched the surrounding forest…

Aidan stopped.

Not because he saw something.

Because he smelled it.

Fresh blood.

Very little.

Hidden.

His feet carried him toward the scent before he consciously chose a direction.

He froze halfway there.

No.

He forced himself to stop.

Cassian watched quietly.

“You found something.”

Aidan nodded.

“Over there.”

Cassian discovered an injured merchant concealed beneath fallen branches.

Alive.

Bleeding.

Breathing.

He looked back at Aidan.

“How?”

“I…”

The answer stayed trapped inside him.

“I don’t know.”

But he was beginning to.

His body knew before his mind accepted it.

…

They returned to Blackwood after sunset.

No sooner had they crossed the gate than another messenger arrived.

“The northern watchtower has gone silent!”

Malik let out a tired breath.

“We just got back.”

“We’re going,” Rowan answered immediately.

No one protested.

No one had the energy.

Still…

They went.

Because someone always needed them.
