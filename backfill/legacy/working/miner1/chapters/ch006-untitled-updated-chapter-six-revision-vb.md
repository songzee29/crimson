Rain continued falling long after night settled over the mountains.

The abandoned ranger station creaked with every gust of wind.

Aidan remained awake.

Not still.

Never still.

His senses stretched outward in every direction.

Counting.

Tracking.

Measuring.

The slow rhythm of Rez’s heartbeat behind him.

The frantic pulse of a rabbit nearly a mile away.

The distant metallic scent of blood carried on the storm.

Every drop of rain striking wood, glass, earth—each impact cataloged, mapped, understood.

Watching.

Listening.

Hunting.

“You’ve checked every entrance six times.”

Rez’s voice was calm.

“I checked them seven.”

Aidan didn’t turn.

Rez studied him for a moment.

“You’re not checking for threats anymore.”

Aidan’s eyes remained fixed on the window.

“I’m checking everything.”

“No,” Rez said quietly. “You’re avoiding thinking about the people you left behind.”

Silence stretched.

Aidan’s jaw tightened—barely.

“They’re safer without me.”

“That’s not what you believe.”

“It’s what’s true.”

Rez didn’t argue immediately.

“That’s what you need to believe.”

Aidan didn’t respond.

But the rabbit’s heartbeat in the distance suddenly stopped.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The rain intensified.

Water streamed down the cracked windows.

Aidan’s crimson-gold eyes followed a single droplet as it slid across the glass.

Halfway down…

It stopped.

Not frozen.

Waiting.

Then…

It slowly drifted sideways.

Against gravity.

Toward him.

Aidan’s pupils narrowed.

“Again.”

Rez had noticed.

“This is the fourth time.”

“It isn’t coincidence.”

“No.”

The droplet pressed against the inside of the glass.

It didn’t break through.

But it shouldn’t have been able to touch it at all.

It lingered.

Then slid downward again.

Normal.

As if nothing had happened.

Silence.

“What do you think?” Aidan asked.

Rez didn’t answer immediately.

“I think you’re paying attention to the wrong thing.”

Aidan’s gaze didn’t shift.

“Explain.”

“You’re watching the water.”

“I’m watching everything.”

“No,” Rez said again, firmer this time. “You’re letting it distract you.”

Aidan finally turned.

“And what should I be focusing on?”

Rez met his eyes.

“The fact that something out there recognizes you.”

Aidan’s expression didn’t change.

But the air in the room grew colder.

“Water doesn’t recognize.”

“No,” Rez agreed.

“But something inside it might.”

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Before Aidan could respond—

The Crimson System appeared.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

CRIMSON SYSTEM

Archive Update

Journey Recorded

Soul Link Stable

Unknown Water Resonance

Observation Continues

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The notification vanished.

No objective.

No reward.

Only another observation.

“You’ve noticed,” Rez murmured.

“The System isn’t trying to explain it either.”

“It usually doesn’t.”

“It waits.”

“Exactly.”

Aidan’s gaze returned to the storm.

Waiting.

Like something else was.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

By morning…

The storm had passed.

Aidan continued north.

Every creek.

Every pond.

Every river crossing…

The water reacted.

At first, it was subtle.

Ripples bending toward him.

Currents slowing as he approached.

Then it grew stranger.

Fish began swimming alongside him—matching his pace for miles before vanishing without a trace.

Rain fell around him…

But never touched him.

Droplets curved midair, sliding past his skin as if avoiding contact.

At one crossing, his reflection in the water didn’t move when he did.

It watched him.

Smiling faintly.

Before snapping back into place.

At another, the river went perfectly still.

No current.

No sound.

No movement.

As if the entire body of water had stopped breathing.

Each encounter left the same quiet feeling.

Not danger.

Recognition.

And something else.

Expectation.

Chapter Seven

Three hundred miles away…

Lyra unfolded another map across the table.

Red markings covered nearly every state bordering Louisiana.

Each one represented a confirmed sighting.

Each one had ended the same way.

Too late.

Elias leaned over the map.

“He isn’t staying anywhere.”

“No,” Malik answered.

“He’s moving with purpose.”

Draco studied the newest reports in silence.

Finally he spoke.

“Not randomly.”

The room turned toward him.

“He isn’t hiding.”

“He isn’t wandering.”

“He’s following something.”

Lyra crossed her arms.

“What makes you think that?”

Draco placed three reports side by side.

“The timing.”

“The direction.”

“The distance.”

“He changes course…”

“…only after crossing major rivers.”

Silence settled over the room.

Malik frowned.

“You think the waterways matter?”

“I don’t know.”

Draco shook his head.

“But I don’t believe in this many coincidences.”

Elias looked toward Lyra.

“Then we stop chasing footprints.”

“We start following rivers.”

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Hours later…

They arrived at their next lead.

A small town near the Mississippi.

Too quiet.

Too empty.

The sheriff refused to speak at first.

Until Malik showed him the badge.

Then the man’s hands started shaking.

“He was here,” the sheriff whispered.

Lyra stepped forward.

“When?”

“Last night.”

Too late.

Again.

“What happened?” Elias asked.

The sheriff swallowed hard.

“You don’t want to know.”

“We do.”

Silence.

Then—

“He didn’t attack anyone.”

Relief flickered.

Then died.

“But something did.”

They followed the sheriff to the edge of town.

Three bodies lay beneath tarps.

Lyra forced herself to look.

The wounds were precise.

Clean.

Efficient.

Predatory.

But not chaotic.

Not uncontrolled.

Aidan had been here.

But this…

This wasn’t him.

Malik crouched beside one of the bodies.

“No signs of struggle.”

Draco’s eyes narrowed.

“They didn’t even try to run.”

Lyra’s voice was quiet.

“He wasn’t the only thing hunting.”

The sheriff spoke again.

“The river went silent before it happened.”

All of them froze.

“Silent?” Elias asked.

“No current. No sound. Nothing.”

Draco looked back toward the map in his mind.

“Rivers,” he murmured.

Lyra clenched her fists.

“We’re getting closer.”

“Or walking into something worse,” Malik said.

Neither disagreed.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Far away…

Aidan reached another river crossing.

He stepped onto the old wooden bridge.

The current below slowed.

Then stopped.

Completely.

The surface became perfectly smooth.

A mirror.

Too perfect.

Too still.

Aidan stepped forward.

The water beneath him shifted.

Not flowing.

Moving.

Forming.

Lines spread across the surface.

Curving.

Connecting.

A symbol.

Ancient.

Unfamiliar.

Watching.

Rez’s voice dropped to a whisper.

“Aidan…”

The symbol pulsed once.

Then—

Something beneath the surface opened.

One enormous eye.

It stared directly up at him.

Unblinking.

Waiting.
