Blackwood Academy welcomed them home with another emergency.

Not an attack.

A collapse.

One of the oldest tunnels beneath the eastern library had given way.

No students were trapped.

But the collapse had exposed another forgotten chamber beneath the academy.

Helena looked at the fractured stone.

“It wasn’t on any map.”

Rowan answered quietly.

“There are becoming fewer places I’m willing to say that.”

…

The chamber was small.

Dust covered everything.

Broken shelves leaned against cracked walls.

At its center stood a stone pedestal.

Empty.

Whatever had once rested there was gone.

Long gone.

Aidan slowly circled the room.

His fingertips brushed the wall.

Tiny scratches.

Straight.

Deliberate.

Too weathered to be fresh.

Too orderly to be random.

Cassian crouched beside him.

“What is it?”

“I don’t know.”

That answer came more often now.

He disliked how natural it had become.

…

As they prepared to leave…

Aidan stopped again.

His hearing caught something beneath the rubble.

Not movement.

Breathing.

Very slow.

Very faint.

He held up one hand.

“Wait.”

Everyone became still.

He knelt beside the collapsed wall.

Carefully removed one stone.

Then another.

Then another.

At last…

A frightened apprentice looked back at him through the gap.

Covered in dust.

Shaking.

Alive.

The chamber had collapsed around a hidden maintenance passage.

The apprentice had been trapped for hours.

Helena immediately pulled the young witch free.

“You found him.”

Aidan looked at the opening.

“I heard him.”

The apprentice frowned weakly.

“I wasn’t calling.”

“You weren’t?”

“I… I fell asleep.”

Silence.

Aidan slowly looked back at the narrow opening.

If the apprentice hadn’t been awake…

What had he heard?

He didn’t ask.

He wasn’t sure he wanted the answer.

…

That night the academy slept in uneasy shifts again.

Aidan remained beneath the old oak.

The wind carried damp earth and distant rain.

He listened.

No strange voices.

No footsteps.

Only the academy.

Then…

Something else.

A heartbeat.

Not approaching.

Retreating.

Far beyond the walls.

He followed the sound until it faded into the forest.

It never came back.

For reasons he couldn’t explain…

That frightened him more than if it had.

Because whoever—or whatever—had been close enough for him to hear…

Had left without being seen.

And somewhere beyond Blackwood’s walls…

The next move had already begun.
