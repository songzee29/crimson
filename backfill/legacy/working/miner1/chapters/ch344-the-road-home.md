The climb from the Second Foundation felt longer than the descent.

Not because the stairs had changed.

Because every heartbeat carried weight.

No one spoke as they emerged beneath the Keeper’s tree.

The afternoon sky over Grey Hollow had darkened.

Clouds churned overhead in slow crimson-gray spirals.

There was no thunder.

No rain.

Only pressure.

Professor Helena looked toward the sky.

“It wasn’t like this when we arrived.”

Mr. Bennett quietly knelt beside the old tree.

Its leaves, once dull and brittle, now carried faint streaks of living green.

“The Foundation survived.”

He rested one weathered hand against the bark.

“It will heal.”

Master Rowan turned toward Aidan.

“And now…”

“The real trial begins.”

They left Grey Hollow behind.

For the first hour, the forest remained silent.

Then…

The silence became wrong.

Aidan stopped walking.

Foundation Awareness had faded with distance.

Something else had taken its place.

Not the steady pulse of civilization.

A sharp ache behind his eyes.

Like invisible fingers brushing against his thoughts.

Lyra immediately noticed.

“Aidan?”

“I’m fine.”

He wasn’t.

The sensation disappeared almost as quickly as it came.

Rowan watched him without speaking.

⸻

The group continued.

Another mile.

The ache returned.

Longer this time.

Along with it came a whisper.

Not through his ears.

Inside his mind.

Heir…

Aidan’s steps faltered.

Cassian caught his shoulder before he stumbled.

“You heard it too?”

Cassian frowned.

“I didn’t hear anything.”

Aidan looked around.

Everyone else continued walking normally.

Only Rowan had stopped.

The old traveler sighed.

“It has begun.”

⸻

“The Curse?” Professor Helena asked.

Rowan nodded.

“It no longer needs the Foundations.”

“It has found what it was searching for.”

Aidan clenched his jaw.

“It can speak?”

“Not yet.”

Rowan’s expression hardened.

“For now…”

“It only wants you to know it is there.”

⸻

The whisper returned.

Closer.

How long…

…can you…

The words dissolved before they finished.

Aidan instinctively pressed two fingers against his temple.

“Hold.”

The Law settled across his own mind.

The pressure immediately stabilized.

Not gone.

Contained.

The whisper retreated.

Master Rowan smiled.

“You’ve stopped trying to overpower your Laws.”

Aidan slowly lowered his hand.

“I’m beginning to understand them.”

“No.”

Rowan answered.

“They’re beginning to understand you.”

One more tool.

One more step.

One step closer.

By sunset…

The towers of Blackwood Academy appeared beyond the trees.

No one felt relief.

The air surrounding the school had changed.

Students crowded the courtyards.

Faculty hurried between buildings.

Protective wards shimmered visibly around the academy grounds.

Professor Helena’s expression tightened.

“They activated every barrier.”

Headmaster Aldren stood waiting at the front gate.

He didn’t smile.

“You felt it.”

Rowan answered simply.

“Yes.”

Aldren looked directly at Aidan.

“It arrived an hour before you did.”
