The convergence had begun.

The crimson lines vanished as quickly as they had appeared.

For a heartbeat…

The entire academy remained perfectly still.

Then the moment passed.

Students slowly resumed walking.

Conversations returned.

Yet they carried a different tone now.

Quieter.

More thoughtful.

No one laughed quite as loudly.

No one admitted why.

⸻

High within the Headmaster’s Tower…

The emergency meeting resumed without being called one.

No summons had been necessary.

Every senior instructor had arrived on instinct.

Headmaster Aldren stood before a map of the academy marked with charcoal circles and crimson ink.

Three new marks had appeared since dawn.

Professor Helena carefully studied them.

“They’re following the oldest foundation.”

Mr. Bennett rested both hands upon the table.

“They’re not spreading randomly.”

“They’re tracing something.”

Master Rowan nodded.

“They’re remembering.”

Aldren looked toward him.

“The same conclusion?”

“The same.”

“What changed?”

The old traveler answered quietly.

“It no longer feels like an awakening.”

The room fell silent.

“It feels…”

He paused.

“…like a return.”

⸻

No one argued.

No one wanted to.

⸻

“The eastern passages remain closed.”

Helena made another mark upon the map.

“I’ve reassigned evening study rooms.”

“So students naturally avoid that side of the academy.”

Mr. Bennett continued.

“I’ve doubled patrols beneath the lower halls.”

“No one goes alone.”

Aldren nodded.

“Good.”

Then…

He looked toward Rowan.

“And you?”

The old traveler smiled faintly.

“I’ll keep teaching.”

Helena couldn’t help asking,

“Is that enough?”

“No.”

He answered honestly.

“But frightened students who stop learning become easier to break than frightened walls.”

No one spoke after that.

⸻

The academy adjusted quietly.

No announcements.

No panic.

The eastern corridor remained “under maintenance.”

Several staircases were politely redirected.

The library’s oldest archive closed for “preservation work.”

Evening curfew arrived half an hour earlier than usual.

Most students accepted the changes without complaint.

Life continued.

Only differently.

⸻

That afternoon…

Aidan and the others carried supplies from the workshops to the northern practice fields.

No instructor had asked all five to volunteer.

They simply had.

Cassian balanced two crates upon one shoulder.

“I still think Elias packed rocks instead of equipment.”

“They’re books.”

“They weigh the same.”

“They absolutely do not.”

“They do if you don’t like reading.”

Malik laughed.

“I hate that your logic almost makes sense.”

“It always makes sense.”

“It absolutely doesn’t.”

Lyra looked toward Aidan.

“You’ve stopped correcting him.”

“I’m learning.”

“Learning what?”

“Some battles aren’t worth winning.”

Cassian grinned.

“I taught him that.”

“No.”

Elias answered.

“You demonstrated it repeatedly.”

⸻

They reached the practice grounds together.

Without discussion…

Everyone naturally fell into a rhythm.

Cassian unloaded the heavier equipment.

Malik assembled the targets.

Elias checked every fastening twice.

Lyra organized the practice materials.

Aidan quietly moved wherever another pair of hands became necessary.

No one directed him.

No one thanked him.

They simply expected him to be there.

Just as he expected them.

Professor Bennett watched from across the field.

Master Rowan quietly joined him.

“They don’t compete for importance anymore.”

“No.”

Rowan answered.

“They compete…”

His smile deepened.

“…to make one another’s burdens lighter.”

⸻

As the work finished…

A sudden gust swept across the practice grounds.

Every target swayed.

The lantern hanging beside the storage shed flickered crimson for a heartbeat.

Then returned to normal.

Students froze.

The wind stopped.

Not gradually.

Immediately.

Cassian slowly looked around.

“…That wasn’t normal.”

“No.”

Aidan answered.

“It wasn’t.”

Several first-years exchanged nervous glances.

Before fear could spread…

Cassian clapped his hands loudly.

“Good.”

Everyone looked toward him.

“If the mountain wanted me…”

He dramatically pointed toward himself.

“…it would’ve picked somebody less handsome.”

Groans echoed across the field.

Several students laughed despite themselves.

Even Helena, watching from the hill above, hid the smallest smile.

The tension broke.

Like ice beneath warm sunlight.

Aidan quietly looked toward Cassian.

He understood.

The joke hadn’t been for himself.

It had been for everyone else.

⸻

Later…

As they walked back together…

Aidan caught up beside him.

“You did that on purpose.”

Cassian shrugged.

“What?”

“The joke.”

“I don’t know what you’re talking about.”

“You saw they were scared.”

Cassian remained silent for several steps.

Finally…

“My dad used to do that.”

Aidan looked over.

“When things got bad…”

“…he’d make the worst joke imaginable.”

Cassian smiled faintly.

“Everyone would complain.”

“But…”

He shrugged again.

“They’d stop looking afraid.”

Silence settled comfortably between them.

Aidan nodded once.

“It worked.”

Cassian bumped his shoulder.

“Don’t tell anyone.”

⸻

That evening…

The Crimson Legacy appeared.

FOUNDER OBSERVATION

Some protect others…

With swords.

Some protect others…

By giving them one reason to smile.

Both stand upon the same wall.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Aidan looked toward Cassian ahead of him.

For the first time…

He understood that courage could wear many faces.
