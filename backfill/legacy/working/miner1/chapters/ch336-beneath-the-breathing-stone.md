No one moved.

The laughter had vanished as quickly as it had come.

The silence that followed felt heavier than before.

Aidan continued staring into the endless darkness beneath the well.

Foundation Awareness hadn’t disappeared.

It had intensified.

The countless streams of ancient magic flowing beneath Grey Hollow no longer appeared scattered.

They spiraled.

All of them.

Around something deep below.

Like rivers circling a hidden mountain.

Professor Helena stepped beside him.

“What do you feel?”

Aidan didn’t answer immediately.

“It isn’t calling for help.”

“No?”

“It’s…”

He frowned.

“…holding on.”

Master Rowan slowly nodded.

“Exactly.”

“The Foundation is spending what little strength remains preserving memories.”

“Not itself.”

Lyra looked toward the abandoned homes surrounding them.

“So it chose the people…”

“…before its own survival.”

Mr. Bennett smiled sadly.

“Every true Foundation does.”

Cassian knelt beside the stone rim.

His hand brushed one of the weathered runes.

“They’re worn smooth.”

“They weren’t destroyed.”

“They were touched.”

Professor Helena joined him.

“Generations.”

She traced another faded carving.

“Families laid their hands here before leaving home.”

“Children probably played around this well.”

“Elders blessed them before journeys.”

She looked around the empty village.

“The Foundation wasn’t hidden from its people.”

“It belonged to them.”

⸻

Aidan quietly closed his eyes.

He stretched Foundation Awareness farther.

Beyond the well.

Beyond the nearest cottages.

Beyond the broken streets.

Every faded protection rune…

Every carved doorway…

Every old boundary stone…

They all remained connected.

Barely.

Like a spiderweb surviving after countless strands had snapped.

One thread suddenly trembled.

Aidan opened his eyes.

“West.”

Everyone looked toward him.

“The strongest remaining thread.”

Rowan gestured for him to lead.

“This time…”

“You choose the path.”

⸻

They followed the invisible current through the abandoned village.

The deeper they walked, the stranger Grey Hollow became.

Doors stood open.

Tables remained set.

Children’s wooden toys rested exactly where they had been dropped.

Nothing had decayed naturally.

It had simply…

Stopped.

Time itself seemed reluctant to disturb the village.

Elias quietly picked up a carved wooden wolf from the ground.

“No dust.”

Malik frowned.

“That’s impossible.”

Darius gently took the toy.

“The dust is everywhere else.”

“Just…”

“…not here.”

Kael studied the tiny carving.

“The Foundation is preserving places that mattered.”

Mr. Bennett smiled.

“You’re learning.”

⸻

They stopped before a modest stone house at the edge of the village.

Unlike every other home…

Its protective runes still glowed.

Faintly.

Aidan stepped closer.

Foundation Awareness pulsed.

Here.

Professor Helena whispered,

“This was a Keeper’s house.”

Master Rowan slowly nodded.

“The last one.”

⸻

The wooden door refused to open.

Not because it was locked.

Because something held it closed.

Aidan placed one hand against the weathered wood.

The sensation surprised him.

The magic wasn’t resisting.

It was shaking.

Like exhausted hands struggling to keep something together.

Without thinking…

He spoke.

“Hold.”

The crimson Law flowed into the ancient door.

Not forcing it open.

Steadying it.

The trembling stopped.

A soft click echoed from inside.

The door slowly opened by itself.

⸻

The house was untouched.

Books rested neatly upon shelves.

A kettle still hung above an old stone hearth.

A half-finished blanket remained folded beside a rocking chair.

Lyra quietly covered her mouth.

“It feels…”

“…like they’re coming back.”

No one answered.

Because everyone felt it.

⸻

At the center of the room rested a circular table.

Upon it lay an enormous parchment map of Grey Hollow.

Every Foundation marker had been carefully drawn.

Three had been crossed out.

Only one remained circled.

The symbol beneath the well.

Professor Helena leaned closer.

“The Core.”

Aidan nodded.

“But…”

He pointed toward a series of handwritten notes surrounding the circle.

“It wasn’t meant to be reached from the well.”

Rowan smiled.

“What does the Keeper’s map say?”

Aidan studied the faded ink.

Then looked toward the floor.

“There’s another entrance.”
