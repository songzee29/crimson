The rain finally stopped.

Fog replaced it.

Heavy enough that Blackwood’s outer walls vanished into white.

Perfect weather for another disappearance.

Everyone knew it.

Patrols doubled.

Again.

Messengers traveled in groups of four instead of two.

Again.

The academy adapted.

Again.

Yet the uneasiness remained.

…

Aidan insisted on joining the northern patrol.

Helena argued until her voice grew hoarse.

Rowan remained silent.

When Aidan finally stood before the gates fully dressed for travel…

The old master simply handed him a new walking staff.

“Lean on this.”

It wasn’t permission.

It wasn’t refusal.

It was acceptance that Aidan would go regardless.

…

The forest felt different.

Not hostile.

Empty.

Even Malik noticed.

“I hate this.”

Cassian nodded.

“I’d rather hear something.”

The silence pressed against them.

Every snapped twig sounded too loud.

Every drifting branch became another question.

Halfway through the march…

Aidan stopped so suddenly Lyra nearly walked into him.

“What?”

He didn’t answer.

His hearing stretched through the fog.

Water.

Wind.

Then…

Footsteps.

Running.

Not toward them.

Away.

Fast.

“Someone’s moving.”

“Where?”

He pointed.

Everyone ran.

The chase lasted only minutes.

It ended beside a narrow ravine.

Fresh footprints stopped at the edge.

No sign of climbing down.

No sign of jumping across.

Simply…

Gone.

Cassian studied the mud.

“Impossible.”

“No.”

Aidan knelt despite the pain pulling through his side.

His fingers brushed one of the prints.

Still warm.

Whoever had been here had vanished only moments before.

A faint sound drifted upward from the ravine.

Not a voice.

Not an animal.

Rock.

Breaking.

Very deep below.

His instincts screamed.

“Back.”

No one questioned him.

The patrol retreated.

Three heartbeats later…

The entire edge of the ravine collapsed into the darkness.

Earth thundered downward.

Trees disappeared.

The place where they had stood seconds earlier simply ceased to exist.

Malik stared into the widening chasm.

“If we’d stayed…”

“We’d be buried.”

Aidan finished quietly.

His knees buckled.

The sudden movement reopened the wound along his side.

Blood soaked through the fresh bandages almost immediately.

Lyra caught him before he hit the ground.

“You shouldn’t even be here.”

He leaned heavily against the new staff Rowan had given him.

“I know.”

His voice sounded distant.

Almost detached.

“I just…”

He looked toward the shattered ravine.

“…couldn’t stay behind.”

No one answered.

Because every one of them understood.

As they turned back toward Blackwood…

Aidan glanced once more into the fog.

For the briefest instant…

He thought he saw a lone figure standing across the ravine.

Still.

Watching.

The fog shifted.

Nothing remained.

He said nothing.

Not because he doubted what he had seen.

Because he no longer trusted the difference between what the forest showed him…

And what it wanted him to see.

Behind them, unnoticed by the patrol, a fresh rockslide sealed the ravine completely.

Whatever answers might have existed below…

Were buried before anyone had the chance to ask the questions.
