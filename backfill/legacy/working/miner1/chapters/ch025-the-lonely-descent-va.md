“Aidan!”

Lyra’s voice thundered through collapsing stone.

Then…

Nothing.

Silence swallowed everything.

Dust rolled through the narrow cavern while shattered rock sealed the passage above.

Aidan forced himself upright.

Pain exploded through his ribs.

His injured shoulder struck the wall as he caught himself.

For several long breaths…

He couldn’t hear anyone.

Not Lyra.

Not Cassian.

Not Rowan.

Only dripping water.

Then…

His hearing reached farther.

The frantic pounding of fists against collapsed stone echoed somewhere above.

They were trying to reach him.

His hand touched the cave wall.

“No…”

His voice remained calm despite the fear tightening inside him.

“They’re too far.”

He knew it instinctively.

The collapse hadn’t merely buried him.

It had redirected the tunnels.

The hunters had prepared the mountain.

This had never been an accident.

Slow footsteps echoed ahead.

One.

Then another.

Then several.

Not charging.

Waiting.

Watching.

The pale hunter from before remained standing in the center of the tunnel.

It still didn’t attack.

Behind it…

More shapes emerged.

Not one.

Not two.

Five.

Each different.

One broad-shouldered and scarred.

One unnaturally lean.

One carrying long hooked claws worn smooth from stone.

One whose pale skin bore rows of carved black marks.

The last remained farther back than all the others.

It never stepped fully into the light.

It simply watched.

The Commander.

Aidan felt it immediately.

Not because of strength.

Because every other hunter subtly adjusted around it.

None moved without its silent approval.

“So…”

Aidan slowly raised his hands.

“You finally stopped hiding.”

No answer came.

The scarred hunter attacked first.

Fast.

Direct.

Aidan twisted aside before conscious thought caught up.

Stone exploded where he’d been standing.

Another hunter immediately struck his injured shoulder.

Not random.

Deliberate.

They remembered.

His arm nearly went numb.

A third attack swept low.

Toward his ribs.

Again.

His old injuries.

They weren’t guessing anymore.

They knew.

“They’ve been watching…”

Aidan whispered.

The hunters answered with action.

No two attacked together.

One forced him left.

Another drove him backward.

A third collapsed part of the tunnel behind him.

Every movement limited his choices.

They weren’t trying to overpower him.

They were herding him.

The broad hunter slammed him into a stone pillar.

His fractured ribs screamed.

The pale observer remained in the rear.

Its black-marked arm calmly carved another line into the cavern wall.

Recording.

Learning.

Aidan’s breathing became ragged.

His body reacted again before his thoughts.

He ducked.

Turned.

Caught one claw with a Crimson Law.

Spun beneath another strike.

His instincts kept him alive.

His injuries prevented him from winning.

The Executioner stepped forward.

Larger than the rest.

Silent.

Patient.

It had waited until Aidan was exhausted.

Only then did it attack.

One blow shattered his barrier.

The second sent him crashing across broken stone.

His head struck the cavern floor.

Stars filled his vision.

Blood ran freely from his forehead.

Warm.

The scent reached him immediately.

His breathing faltered.

No…

Not now.

He clenched his jaw hard enough to taste iron.

The hunger pushed back.

The Executioner didn’t rush.

It walked.

Every step deliberate.

It knew he couldn’t stand.

Still…

Aidan forced himself upright.

One shaking hand touched the ground.

Another pressed against his broken ribs.

“I…”

Blood escaped between his teeth.

“…am not…”

He rose.

“…finished.”

The Commander finally moved.

Only one step.

Every other hunter immediately stopped attacking.

The cavern fell silent.

The Commander studied Aidan.

Not with hatred.

With calculation.

As though measuring something.

Then it nodded once.

The Executioner struck.

Not to kill.

To break.

Its claw tore across Aidan’s chest.

Another ripped through his side.

A final blow drove him against the cavern wall so hard the stone cracked behind him.

His legs failed.

He collapsed to one knee.

His vision blurred.

The hunters surrounded him.

None finished him.

Why?

The Commander turned away first.

The others followed.

One remained.

The lean hunter.

It crouched beside Aidan.

Close enough that he could hear its slow breathing.

Close enough that Aidan smelled cold stone…

And old blood.

The hunter leaned toward his ear.

Its voice was rough.

Almost human.

Quiet enough that only Aidan could hear.

“You have your traitor to thank for this.”

The words settled into the silence.

Before Aidan could speak…

The hunter stepped back.

Every pale figure disappeared into the darkness.

Not one looked back.

They left him alive.

…

Aidan remained kneeling.

His heartbeat pounded weakly.

The second heartbeat answered.

Steady.

Patient.

His thoughts drifted.

Traitor.

Impossible.

Blackwood?

No…

Rowan?

Never.

Lyra?

Cassian?

Malik?

Elias?

No.

No.

No…

Then…

How?

How had they known every patrol?

Every weakness?

Every injury?

Every choice?

How had they always arrived prepared?

His breathing grew shallower.

Darkness pressed inward.

Far away…

He heard explosions above.

The academy.

They were attacking without him.

His fingers dug weakly into the stone.

He tried to stand.

His body refused.

The last thing he heard before consciousness slipped away…

Was the distant sound of Rowan’s voice somewhere beyond the collapsed mountain.

Still searching.

Still refusing to abandon him.

And beneath that…

A single question quietly rooted itself in Aidan’s heart.

Not belief.

Not accusation.

Only doubt.

What if the hunter hadn’t lied?
