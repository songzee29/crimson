The western wall was abandoned.

Not because Blackwood Academy had surrendered it.

Because Rowan refused to waste lives rebuilding a position the enemy had already learned.

New defensive circles formed deeper inside the academy.

Every retreat shortened the distance between the hunters…

And the students they protected.

No one complained.

They understood.

Holding ground meant nothing if no one survived to stand on it.

…

Three days passed.

Three days without an attack.

No alarms.

No tunnels collapsing.

No creatures appearing beneath the walls.

The silence became unbearable.

“They’re making us wait,” Helena said quietly.

“They’re making us tired,” Cassian corrected.

Sleep came in short moments.

Meals were interrupted by patrol rotations.

Every unexpected sound sent students reaching for weapons.

Blackwood was surviving.

Barely.

…

Aidan stood alone atop the central tower.

His ribs remained tightly wrapped beneath fresh bandages.

Every deep breath still hurt.

The wound across his shoulder had finally begun knitting together, though the scar would remain.

He rested both hands against the cold stone.

Below…

The academy breathed.

He heard everything.

Children whispering inside the shelters.

Teachers changing patrols.

Water running beneath the mountain.

Wind crossing broken towers.

Then…

A heartbeat.

Different.

Not beneath him.

Beyond the walls.

His head lifted.

One.

Two.

Three…

Nine.

His eyes narrowed.

Nine heartbeats.

Not seven.

“They’ve grown.”

He didn’t realize he had spoken aloud.

Rowan stepped onto the tower behind him.

“You heard something.”

“There are nine now.”

The old master watched him carefully.

“I’ll trust your ears.”

No hesitation.

No questioning.

Only trust.

That somehow frightened Aidan more.

…

An hour later every senior member of Blackwood gathered inside the command hall.

Nine markers rested upon the academy map.

Not because anyone else had confirmed them.

Because Aidan had.

“If he’s wrong…” Malik began.

“He isn’t,” Rowan interrupted.

Cassian nodded once.

“He hasn’t been wrong yet.”

Aidan wished they would question him.

Instead…

They believed him.

The responsibility settled heavily across his shoulders.

“They’re spreading farther apart,” he said quietly.

“They’re surrounding us.”

Helena studied the map.

“Preparing for a siege?”

“No.”

Aidan looked toward the eastern wall.

“They’re removing every escape.”

Silence followed.

Nobody liked the answer.

…

That evening…

The attack finally came.

Not against the academy.

Against the supply convoy bringing medicine from the southern villages.

The warning arrived from a wounded messenger who barely reached the gates before collapsing.

“They’re trapped…”

He struggled to breathe.

“…the bridge…”

His eyes rolled back.

Helena immediately took over treating him.

Rowan looked toward Aidan.

“You can’t.”

The words came before Aidan spoke.

“Your ribs haven’t healed.”

Aidan met his gaze.

“If those supplies don’t reach Blackwood…”

He looked toward the crowded infirmary.

“…people die anyway.”

Rowan closed his eyes.

Only briefly.

Then nodded.

“Bring them home.”

…

Rain poured across the southern valley.

The bridge crossed a deep gorge where the river below crashed violently against black stone.

Half the convoy remained stranded on the opposite side.

The bridge had partially collapsed.

Waiting on the near side…

Were three pale hunters.

Waiting on the far side…

Were six more.

Nine.

Exactly as Aidan had heard.

“They divided themselves,” Lyra whispered.

“They’re forcing us to split.”

“No.”

Aidan’s eyes studied the broken bridge.

“They’re forcing us to choose.”

The convoy.

Or each other.

The hunters didn’t attack.

They watched.

Learning.

Again.

The first hunter stepped onto the bridge.

Not toward Aidan.

Toward the trapped civilians.

Cassian cursed.

“They’re making us protect people instead of fighting.”

“That’s the point,” Rowan answered.

The hunters had evolved.

They no longer cared about winning battles.

They cared about exhausting heroes.

…

The first cable snapped.

The bridge lurched violently.

Screams echoed across the gorge.

Aidan moved.

Not toward the hunters.

Toward the bridge.

“Hold!”

Crimson magic surged beneath ancient timber.

The collapsing bridge stopped.

Only barely.

Hundreds of strained beams shuddered beneath impossible pressure.

Every broken rib in Aidan’s chest felt as though it were breaking again.

Blood escaped between his lips.

Still…

He held.

“Move!”

Malik and Cassian rushed civilians across the suspended bridge.

Lyra shielded the children.

Elias reinforced weakened supports with trembling runes.

The hunters attacked then.

Not Aidan.

The civilians.

They had learned.

If Aidan held the bridge…

He couldn’t defend everyone.

One hunter slipped past Cassian.

Straight toward a frightened little boy frozen halfway across.

The creature raised one claw.

Aidan saw it.

His body reacted before thought.

He released one hand from the Law.

The bridge groaned violently.

His free hand shot outward.

“Still!”

The creature froze for one precious heartbeat.

Just long enough.

Cassian reached the boy first.

His blade drove through the hunter’s chest.

The creature collapsed into the raging river below.

The bridge immediately dropped another foot.

“Aidan!” Lyra screamed.

“I know!”

He didn’t.

His body did.

Every muscle trembled violently.

His vision darkened around the edges.

Pain no longer felt sharp.

It had become distant.

Muted.

His body simply refused to stop.

The final civilian reached safety.

“Now!” Rowan shouted.

Aidan released the Law.

The bridge collapsed into the river with a deafening roar.

The remaining hunters vanished into the storm without another attack.

Again…

They had learned enough.

…

Aidan dropped to one knee.

Then both.

Blood stained the rain-soaked earth beneath him.

He couldn’t hear the river anymore.

Only heartbeats.

Dozens of them.

Strong.

Fast.

Alive.

The scent of blood mixed with rain.

His own.

Cassian’s.

One injured merchant.

One frightened child who had scraped both hands crossing the bridge.

His breathing became uneven.

His eyes found every wound without meaning to.

Warm.

Living.

The hunger surged.

Stronger than ever before.

His fingers dug into the mud.

“No…”

He whispered.

Not here.

Not in front of them.

Lyra knelt beside him.

She didn’t speak.

She simply took his blood-covered hand in both of hers.

The warmth of another person grounded him.

The hunger retreated.

Only slightly.

Enough.

Enough to stand.

Far above the gorge…

Hidden among rain-soaked cliffs…

One pale hunter remained watching long after the others had gone.

It did not celebrate.

It did not mourn.

With one claw…

It carved another mark into the stone.

Not for the bridge.

Not for the battle.

For the moment the Crimson Witch had chosen strangers over his own failing body.

Another lesson learned.

Another weakness confirmed.

The hunt was becoming personal.
