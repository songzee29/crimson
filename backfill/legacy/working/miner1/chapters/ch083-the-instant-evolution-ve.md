Aidan’s heart slammed once.

Then stopped.

Lyra caught him before he hit the ground.

“AIDAN!”

His heartbeat returned with enough force to send a shockwave through his body.

Aidan screamed.

Not from magic.

From bone.

Something inside his skeleton moved.

His back arched violently.

Lyra dropped beside him, holding his shoulders as his entire body convulsed.

Rowan spun around.

“What happened?”

“I DON’T KNOW!”

Aidan’s fingers dug into the stone.

His nails cracked.

Regrew.

Cracked again.

Blood poured from beneath them.

His spine shifted beneath his skin.

One vertebra.

Then another.

Then another.

Aidan roared through clenched teeth.

“Something’s—”

A crack tore through his shoulders.

He screamed again.

Lyra’s face went pale.

“Aidan!”

His blood ignited.

Literally.

Crimson fire raced beneath his veins.

His Vampire healing tried to repair the damage while something new continued creating it.

Flesh split along his shoulder blades.

Closed.

Split again.

The smell of blood filled the air.

And then—

Bone pushed outward.

Aidan’s scream ripped across the mountain.

Two structures forced themselves from his back.

Not magic.

Not light.

Bone.

Muscle.

Blood vessels.

Living supernatural anatomy building itself in seconds.

Lyra grabbed his hand.

“I’m here.”

Aidan nearly crushed her fingers.

“Don’t let go.”

“I won’t.”

The structures expanded.

Joints formed.

Membranes followed.

Crimson-black scales spread outward across them as two enormous wings unfolded behind Aidan for the first time.

Dragon wings.

Real.

Bleeding.

Incomplete for only seconds before his regeneration sealed the torn flesh connecting them to his back.

Aidan collapsed forward, panting.

Then his skull began to change.

“No—”

Two points of pressure formed above his temples.

He grabbed his head.

The skin split.

Dark crimson horns forced themselves upward.

Curved.

Sharp.

Beautiful in a way the pain refused to be.

Scales followed.

Across his neck.

His shoulders.

His ribs.

His forearms.

Deep crimson edged with near-black shadows.

Not covering him completely.

Claiming sections of his body.

His bones became heavier.

Denser.

His muscles tightened around them.

His lungs expanded.

His heart strengthened.

His skin hardened beneath the scales.

Dragon instinct entered his senses like fire entering dry wood.

Possession.

Territory.

Protection.

Power.

Aidan gasped.

And then the next inheritance struck.

Water.

There was no ocean anywhere near him.

Yet suddenly Aidan heard one.

Miles upon miles of impossible depth rushed through his ears.

Pressure crushed against his body from every direction.

He choked.

Lyra grabbed his face.

“Aidan, breathe!”

“I can’t—”

His throat changed.

For one horrific second, his lungs forgot how to breathe air.

Thin supernatural structures opened along the sides of his ribs.

Aidan clawed at his chest.

Then they sealed again.

His body corrected itself.

Adapted.

His hearing changed next.

The heartbeats around him became musical.

Not metaphorically.

Each living person carried a rhythm.

Lyra’s was strongest.

Cassian’s broken rhythm crawled farther behind them.

Rowan.

Malik.

Elias.

Draco.

Aidan heard emotion inside their voices before they even spoke.

Fear had a frequency.

Grief had weight.

Blood itself seemed to sing.

Aidan opened his mouth to breathe—

And a note escaped.

One note.

Rowan dropped to one knee.

Malik froze.

Elias’s eyes widened.

Draco’s head snapped toward Aidan as though compelled by an instinct older than thought.

Lyra staggered but remained standing.

Aidan immediately closed his mouth.

“What the hell was that?”

No one answered.

Because crimson fire erupted around him.

Not ordinary flame.

It did not spread across the ground.

It spread across Aidan.

His entire body disappeared inside a storm of crimson-gold fire.

Lyra screamed his name.

She tried to reach him.

The fire bent around her hand.

It did not burn her.

Inside the flames, Aidan screamed again.

This pain was different.

The Dragon had changed his body.

The Siren had changed how his body answered the world.

The third inheritance changed the blood itself.

Every drop inside him combusted.

His Vampire nature healed him.

The fire burned him again.

He regenerated.

Burned.

Regenerated.

Burned.

Again.

Again.

Again.

Until destruction and restoration became one continuous process.

Aidan could smell his own flesh burning.

Then healing.

Then becoming harder to burn.

Crimson feathers appeared inside the fire.

Not wings.

His Dragon wings remained spread behind him.

The feathers formed momentarily across his shoulders and arms before dissolving back into crimson flame.

Aidan threw his head back.

Fire poured from his mouth.

The mountain sky turned red.

Then every flame collapsed into him.

Silence.

Aidan remained on his knees.

Smoke rolled from his body.

Dragon horns curved from his head.

Crimson-black scales covered sections of his skin.

Two enormous Dragon wings rested behind him.

His eyes burned brighter than before.

But he was still Aidan.

Humanoid.

No Dragon form followed.

No beast replaced him.

Lyra slowly approached.

“Aidan?”

His eyes found hers.

“I’m here.”

Then the System returned.

Barely.

“Does that hurt?”

“No.”

Her eyes moved upward.

To the horns.

Then behind him.

To the wings.

“Aidan…”

“I know.”

“You have wings.”

“I noticed.”

Despite everything…

Lyra almost smiled.

Then Aidan’s expression changed.

His fangs descended.

Every vein in his body darkened.

His stomach twisted so violently he doubled over.

The sound that came from him made Lyra step backward.

Not because it was loud.

Because she recognized it.

Hunger.

Aidan lifted his head.

The Dragon smelled blood.

The Siren heard it.

The Wolf located it.

The Vampire demanded it.

The Witch felt life moving through every vessel around him.

The Crimson Phoenix had burned through more energy than his body had ever spent at once.

And True Crimson Blood wanted fuel.

Aidan looked at Lyra.

Her heartbeat became deafening.

He immediately turned away.

“Run.”

Lyra froze.

“Aidan—”

“RUN.”

His claws tore grooves into the stone.

“I can hear your blood.”

Lyra’s expression hardened.

“I’m not leaving you.”

Aidan’s wings snapped open.

The force threw dust across the mountain.

“I don’t know how long that’s going to matter.”

Then another scent reached him.

Farther away.

Many heartbeats.

Many bodies.

Supernatural blood.

Familiar.

Cassian slowly lifted his head from where he had been crawling.

Aidan turned toward him.

Cassian saw the hunger.

And understood before anyone said the words.

His face went white.

“My coven.”

Aidan disappeared into the sky.

His Dragon wings beat once.

The mountain cracked beneath the force.

And the Crimson Heir flew for the first time.

Straight toward blood.
