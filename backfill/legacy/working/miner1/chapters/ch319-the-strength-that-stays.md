Because…

It had reached its destination.

Far beneath Blackwood Academy…

Silence settled.

Not the silence of emptiness.

The silence of something that had finally arrived exactly where it intended to be.

The second heartbeat no longer wandered.

It rested.

Then…

Without warning…

The ancient wards woven through the mountain answered.

Not with light.

With sound.

A deep, almost imperceptible hum rolled through the stone before vanishing as though it had never existed.

⸻

Aidan stopped walking.

So did Lyra.

Cassian turned.

“What?”

“You felt that?”

Malik asked quietly.

Aidan nodded.

“It was under our feet.”

Elias looked toward the mountain beyond the academy walls.

“I thought it was thunder.”

“There aren’t any clouds.”

Cassian answered.

The evening sky remained perfectly clear.

No one spoke again.

Yet none of them continued walking immediately.

Something inside each of them insisted…

The academy itself had just taken a breath.

⸻

That night…

The animals noticed first.

The academy’s messenger ravens refused to roost upon the eastern tower.

Instead they crowded together along the western walls, restless and unusually quiet.

The horses in the lower stables repeatedly lifted their heads toward the mountain, ears pinned forward as though listening for footsteps no human could hear.

Even the old sheepdog belonging to the groundskeeper abandoned his usual rounds and slept outside the Hall of Founders.

The groundskeeper frowned.

“He hasn’t done that in fifteen years.”

⸻

Professor Helena found Headmaster Aldren standing alone outside the eastern tower.

“The wards shifted.”

“They did.”

“How much?”

He rested his hand against one of the ancient foundation stones.

“They’re still holding.”

She noticed the way he had answered.

Not strong.

Holding.

“They’ve never behaved like this.”

“No.”

“They haven’t.”

He looked toward the mountain.

“They’re listening.”

⸻

Sleep came uneasily across Blackwood Academy.

Not because nightmares spread.

Because almost everyone dreamed.

One student dreamed of endless corridors beneath the academy that seemed to rearrange themselves whenever he blinked.

Another dreamed of the old oak growing roots deep into crimson stone.

A young first-year woke convinced someone had been softly knocking beneath the floorboards all night.

None of the dreams were identical.

All of them carried the same feeling.

Waiting.

Morning conversations filled the dining hall.

“…Did you dream too?”

“I thought it was just me.”

“It felt so real.”

“No monsters.”

“Just…”

“I don’t know.”

Aidan listened quietly.

He had dreamed.

Not of corridors.

Not of voices.

He dreamed of standing beneath the old stone arch while people he loved walked freely through the academy around him.

They smiled.

Talked.

Laughed.

None of them could hear the heartbeat beneath their feet.

Only he could.

He hadn’t feared it.

He had feared being the only one carrying its weight.

⸻

Cassian slid into the seat beside him.

“You look terrible.”

“You too.”

“I barely slept.”

“I know.”

Cassian looked around before lowering his voice.

“I had a weird dream.”

Aidan met his eyes.

“So did I.”

For several moments neither spoke.

Finally Cassian shrugged.

“Whatever’s happening…”

He tore a piece of bread in half and handed one piece to Aidan.

“…you’re not dealing with it alone.”

The gesture was so ordinary…

That it struck Aidan harder than any dramatic promise ever could.

He accepted the bread.

“Neither are you.”

Cassian smiled.

“There he is.”

⸻

After breakfast…

Master Rowan requested Aidan’s company.

The two walked beyond the western gardens until the sounds of the academy faded behind them.

The old traveler carried no books.

No staff lessons.

Only two smooth stones collected from the stream.

He handed one to Aidan.

“Throw it.”

Aidan skipped it across the water.

Five skips.

The stone disappeared beneath the current.

Rowan nodded.

“Again.”

Aidan threw the second.

Three skips.

Then silence.

The old traveler remained watching the water.

“What do you see?”

“Ripples.”

“And after they disappear?”

“The river.”

Rowan smiled.

“Exactly.”

He finally looked toward Aidan.

“People spend their lives chasing ripples.”

“They celebrate them.”

“They fear them.”

“They fight them.”

He pointed toward the flowing current beneath the surface.

“But wisdom…”

“…belongs to those who learn to see the river.”

Aidan listened carefully.

“The strange dreams.”

“The shifting wards.”

“The heartbeat.”

“They’re ripples.”

The old traveler’s expression grew serious.

“The river…”

He rested a hand over his heart.

“…is deciding who you become while all this happens.”

The words settled into Aidan more deeply than any lesson before them.

“When hardship comes…”

Rowan continued quietly.

“…never ask first…”

“‘How do I stop this?’”

He met Aidan’s eyes.

“Ask…”

“‘Who will I become if I walk through it well?’”

For a long time…

Neither spoke.

The stream continued flowing exactly as it always had.

Aidan watched it differently now.

Not as water moving around stones.

But as something that remained itself…

Even while everything within it changed.

Even while everything within it changed.

The lesson remained with Aidan long after he and Rowan returned to the academy.

Students hurried between lectures.

The bell rang.

Someone laughed across the courtyard.

Nothing outwardly had changed.

Yet he found himself paying less attention to the strange tension beneath the mountain…

And more attention to the people walking beside him.

The river.

Not the ripples.

⸻

Professor Bennett dismissed formal drills that afternoon.

Instead…

He divided the students into small work details across the academy.

Repair stone walls.

Restore damaged practice circles.

Reorganize the old archives.

No one complained.

The work mattered.

Aidan, Cassian, Malik, Elias, and Lyra were assigned to repair a weathered section of the eastern garden wall.

The stones themselves were sound.

Time had simply loosened them.

Cassian lifted the largest block.

“I’ve got it.”

Malik immediately stepped beside him.

“No.”

“We’ve got it.”

Without another word…

The two lifted together.

Aidan steadied the foundation while Elias scraped away old mortar.

Lyra carefully reset each stone.

No one gave orders.

Everyone simply found the work that needed doing.

An hour later…

The wall stood straighter than before.

Professor Bennett inspected it in silence.

Then nodded once.

“You repaired more than the wall.”

Cassian looked confused.

“We did?”

“You trusted one another’s strengths.”

The instructor turned to leave.

“Remember that.”

⸻

As the group gathered their tools…

The ground trembled again.

Slightly stronger this time.

Several birds exploded from the eastern trees.

A nearby lantern hanging from a stone post swung gently despite the still air.

Students fell quiet.

No one panicked.

But everyone noticed.

Professor Helena immediately looked toward the mountain.

Headmaster Aldren was already doing the same.

Their eyes met across the courtyard.

Neither needed to speak.

It was happening again.

Closer.

⸻

That evening…

The old oak stood strangely silent.

No birds nested in its branches.

No insects sang nearby.

The five friends sat beneath it anyway.

Cassian leaned back against the trunk.

“I don’t like it.”

“The silence?”

Lyra asked.

He nodded.

“It feels…”

He searched for the word.

“…wrong.”

Aidan rested his hand against the bark.

The tree felt alive.

Steady.

Unafraid.

“The tree isn’t running.”

Everyone looked at him.

He smiled faintly.

“Neither should we.”

Malik laughed softly.

“That sounded like Rowan.”

“It did.”

Elias nodded.

“I was about to say the same thing.”

Cassian grinned.

“Congratulations.”

“You’re becoming old.”

Aidan rolled his eyes.

“I’ll try to recover.”

They laughed.

Not because the danger had disappeared.

Because none of them wanted fear to decide who they would become.

High above them…

Master Rowan watched from the library steps.

He said nothing.

He simply smiled.

The lesson had taken root.

Not in Aidan’s mind.

In his choices.

The Crimson Legacy appeared.

Home.

The second heartbeat pulsed once.

The ancient wards answered.

This time…

The mountain was no longer merely remembering.

It had begun…

To awaken.”
