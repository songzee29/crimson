Blackwood had barely returned from the river when another rider arrived.

His horse stumbled through the gate, foam covering its neck.

The rider slid to the ground before anyone reached him.

“They’re sick.”

“Who?” Helena asked.

“The children…”

He coughed violently.

“…Stonebrook.”

Helena’s expression changed immediately.

“Sick how?”

“I don’t know.”

“They just…”

He swallowed hard.

“…stopped waking up.”

No hunters.

No claws.

No ambush.

Only another village needing help.

Again.

Aidan looked toward Helena.

“I’ll go.”

She shook her head immediately.

“No.”

“They need you.”

“So do you.”

His side throbbed beneath fresh bandages.

His shoulder had stiffened after the reservoir.

His ribs still shifted painfully whenever he breathed too deeply.

He ignored all of it.

“I can still move.”

Helena looked into his tired eyes.

“I know.”

That answer hurt more than refusing him would have.

…

Stonebrook lay east of Blackwood.

The children still breathed.

Their hearts remained steady.

They simply…

Wouldn’t wake.

Helena moved from bed to bed.

Nothing explained it.

No fever.

No poison she recognized.

No visible wounds.

Parents stood helplessly beside sleeping sons and daughters.

Aidan quietly walked through the village.

Listening.

The silence felt wrong.

Not because no one spoke.

Because every sleeping child breathed in almost the same rhythm.

He stopped outside the final house.

His head tilted.

One heartbeat.

Different.

He entered.

A little boy lay asleep beside the window.

Unlike the others…

His breathing wasn’t steady.

It was slowing.

“Aidan?”

Helena looked up.

“What is it?”

He knelt beside the bed.

“I don’t know.”

His hand hovered over the child’s wrist.

Not touching.

Listening.

The second heartbeat answered beneath his own.

For one impossible moment…

He thought he heard another heartbeat trying to fade away.

His instincts screamed.

“Helena.”

She was already beside him.

“What?”

“He needs you.”

She didn’t question why.

She immediately began weaving healing magic around the boy.

His breathing steadied.

Color slowly returned to his face.

The room released a collective breath.

Helena looked at Aidan.

“You knew.”

He looked at the sleeping child.

“No.”

He spoke honestly.

“I only knew…”

His voice grew quieter.

“…he didn’t have much time.”

Outside…

Parents continued waiting beside children who still refused to wake.

Inside…

Another burden settled onto Aidan’s shoulders.

He had helped save one.

Too many still remained.

And before the sun could set…

A scout rode through Stonebrook carrying another sealed message from Blackwood.

Another patrol had gone missing.

The nightmare had already moved somewhere else.
