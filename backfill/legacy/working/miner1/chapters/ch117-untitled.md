Silence settled over the ruined cellar.

Bodies lay scattered across broken stone.

No one celebrated.

Aidan examined the battlefield.

“They left intentionally.”

Cassian nodded.

“They learned what they came for.”

Draco knelt beside one of the fallen vampires.

Inside the man’s coat rested a folded piece of black parchment.

No name.

No seal.

Only one sentence.

Bring the Heir alive.

Nothing else.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

“They don’t want you dead,” Rowan said.

“They want control.”

Aidan burned the parchment between his fingers.

“They’ll fail.”

“They may.”

Draco rose.

“But they’ll keep sending people until they don’t.”

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

As they climbed from the cellar toward the street, the Mississippi flowed beyond the old buildings.

Moonlight reflected across its surface.

For a single moment…

The current bent quietly around the place where Aidan emerged before continuing downstream.

No one noticed.

The city remained alive around them.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Outside, emergency crews filled the streets.

The smell of blood still lingered.

A frightened paramedic rushed toward them.

“Please!”

“My partner’s trapped!”

“The building’s coming down!”

Before anyone else moved…

Aidan was already there.

He tore aside a collapsed support beam with impossible strength.

The trapped medic crawled free.

“Thank you!”

The man reached instinctively for Aidan’s arm.

Aidan stepped back before the hand touched him.

His eyes lingered on the medic’s pulse.

On the blood running from a cut across his forehead.

The hunger surged.

Stronger than before.

His jaw tightened.

Without a word, he turned and walked away.

Lyra watched him leave.

She didn’t follow immediately.

Instead, she looked back at the rescued medic.

Then at Aidan disappearing into the night.

She finally understood something she had refused to admit.

Every time he saved someone…

He was also forcing himself to stand within arm’s reach of the very thing he wanted most.

Not gratitude.

Blood.

The cost of his control was rising.

And each victory over the hunger left him more isolated than the last.

Far ahead, Aidan stopped on a rooftop overlooking the river.

He didn’t look back.

The Inner Circle followed anyway.

None of them believed they could reach him anymore.

But none of them were willing to stop trying.
