The eastern courtyard slowly emptied as students made their way toward the morning’s first lecture.

Aidan and the others lingered near the old stone arch connecting the academic halls.

Cassian stretched his arms over his head.

“If Helena starts another lecture with, ‘Today will be simple,’ I’m leaving.”

“You’ve threatened that every week,” Elias replied.

“And yet…”

Cassian smiled.

“…I’m still here.”

“Mostly because she scares you.”

“I respect her.”

“You fear her.”

“I respectfully fear her.”

Lyra laughed.

“I think that’s the closest you’ve ever come to honesty.”

⸻

The academy bell rang once.

Students began moving.

As Aidan stepped beneath the arch…

He stopped.

Not because of the Legacy.

Not because of magic.

Because someone had spoken.

“Keeper.”

The voice was barely above a whisper.

Ancient.

Calm.

Aidan turned immediately.

The corridor behind him stood empty.

Students continued walking past.

No one had reacted.

Malik noticed him stop.

“What?”

“I…”

Aidan frowned.

“I thought someone called me.”

Cassian looked around dramatically.

“I didn’t hear anything.”

Elias shrugged.

“Maybe somebody did.”

Aidan slowly nodded.

“Maybe.”

But deep inside…

He knew.

No one at Blackwood Academy called him Keeper.

⸻

History class began quietly.

Professor Helena stood before an enormous map of the old kingdoms.

Today…

No chalk waited in her hand.

Instead…

She carried an aged journal bound in faded leather.

Without speaking…

She placed it upon the desk.

“I have a question.”

The room settled.

“If every written record of this academy disappeared today…”

She looked slowly across the students.

“…what would remain?”

Hands rose.

“The buildings.”

“The teachers.”

“The students.”

“The mountain.”

Helena nodded politely after each answer.

Then…

She shook her head.

“Those would remain…”

“For a while.”

She rested one hand upon the journal.

“What truly remains…”

“…is what one generation successfully teaches the next.”

Her eyes settled briefly upon Aidan.

“The rest…”

“…eventually becomes dust.”

⸻

The lesson continued.

Students discussed kingdoms.

Wars.

Empires.

Libraries lost to fire.

Knowledge buried beneath time.

But Aidan found himself distracted.

Keeper.

The word continued echoing quietly inside his thoughts.

Not loudly.

Patiently.

As though someone had simply reminded him of a name he hadn’t yet earned.

⸻

After class…

Master Rowan approached Professor Helena.

“You heard it too.”

She didn’t ask what he meant.

“I did.”

“He isn’t ready.”

“No.”

The old traveler sighed.

“But something beneath the mountain…”

He looked toward the western cliffs.

“…has begun recognizing him.”

⸻

That afternoon…

Training resumed beneath the old oak instead of the eastern grounds.

Mr. Bennett placed a single candle upon a flat stone.

Its flame burned steadily despite the afternoon breeze.

He looked toward every student gathered.

“Today…”

“You will protect this flame.”

Confused expressions spread immediately.

Cassian blinked.

“…From what?”

Bennett quietly stepped backward.

“You’ll see.”

Without warning…

Professor Helena waved one hand.

A sudden gust of wind swept across the clearing.

Leaves exploded into the air.

Several candles immediately went out.

Others flickered wildly.

Students instinctively cupped their hands around the flames.

Some used their bodies as shields.

Others tried blocking the wind itself.

Aidan did neither.

He watched.

The wind came in waves.

Never randomly.

Every stronger gust followed a brief lull.

Instead of fighting continuously…

He moved only when necessary.

Shielding.

Waiting.

Adjusting.

The candle never went out.

Mr. Bennett noticed.

So did Master Rowan.

⸻

When the exercise ended…

Bennett walked toward Aidan.

“Why didn’t you fight the wind?”

Aidan looked down at the candle.

“Because…”

He smiled faintly.

“…the wind wasn’t trying to destroy it.”

“It was only doing what wind does.”

Bennett nodded once.

“And?”

“I only needed to protect the flame.”

The instructor quietly looked toward Rowan.

Neither said anything.

Yet both carried the same thought.

The Founder would have approved.

⸻

Far beyond the academy…

The silver-haired traveler finally reached the crest of a lonely hill.

Blackwood Academy rested in the distance.

Smaller than he remembered.

He stood there for a long time.

Then quietly removed an old pendant from beneath his cloak.

A crimson crown.

Worn smooth by countless years.

He closed his hand around it.

“So…”

He whispered.

“You’ve finally returned.”

The wind carried his words toward the academy.

And somewhere beneath the mountain…

The ancient heartbeat answered.

The ancient heartbeat answered.

Boom…

…

Boom…

The silver-haired traveler slowly opened his eyes.

For a brief moment…

His expression softened.

Then it was gone.

He continued walking toward Blackwood Academy.

Not hurriedly.

Every step carried the certainty of someone who had already accepted whatever waited at the end of the road.

⸻

Back at the academy…

The students remained beneath the old oak.

Professor Helena relit every extinguished candle.

She looked across the clearing.

“What happened?”

Cassian answered first.

“The wind.”

“No.”

She shook her head.

“The wind revealed something.”

Malik spoke next.

“Some of us reacted.”

“Good.”

Elias added,

“Some of us panicked.”

“Better.”

The class looked confused.

Professor Helena smiled slightly.

“You cannot improve what you refuse to see.”

She pointed toward the candles.

“The flame wasn’t today’s lesson.”

“What was?”

Lyra asked.

Helena’s answer came quietly.

“Your first instinct.”

⸻

The students slowly dispersed.

Some discussed the exercise.

Others argued over what they should have done differently.

Aidan remained behind.

The candle still burned quietly atop the stone.

Master Rowan approached.

“What do you see?”

“A flame.”

“And beyond that?”

Aidan studied it.

“It keeps burning…”

“…even after the wind passes.”

The old traveler nodded.

“Exactly.”

He gently extinguished it between his fingers.

“Character works the same way.”

⸻

As they walked back toward the academy…

Aidan finally asked the question that had remained with him all day.

“Master Rowan…”

“Yes?”

“Has anyone ever called you…”

He hesitated.

“…Keeper?”

The old man stopped walking.

Only for a heartbeat.

Then…

“No.”

“You heard that word?”

Aidan slowly nodded.

“This morning.”

Rowan became unusually quiet.

“When?”

“Beneath the eastern arch.”

“Did anyone else hear it?”

“I don’t think so.”

The old traveler looked toward the mountain.

His expression revealed nothing.

Yet his voice carried unmistakable gravity.

“If it happens again…”

“Tell me immediately.”

Aidan noticed the change.

“You know what it means.”

“I know…”

Rowan answered carefully.

“…that some names are spoken only after they’ve been remembered.”

He continued walking.

Leaving Aidan with more questions than answers.

⸻

Far beneath Blackwood Academy…

Behind the ancient sealed gate…

Silence returned.

But now…

Within the darkness…

A faint smile appeared.

Not cruel.

Not welcoming.

Patient.

Very…

Very patient.”
