The Hunt Beneath Hollow Ridge

Rain hammered the black pine forest as crimson lightning split the clouds above Hollow Ridge.

The ground shook.

Ancient stone pillars erupted from beneath the mud, each carved with symbols older than Blackwood Academy itself.

Three creatures burst from the shattered earth.

They weren’t wolves.

They weren’t vampires.

They weren’t witches.

Whatever they had once been had long since disappeared beneath layers of twisted evolution.

One opened four jaws.

Another crawled across the trees upside down.

The last had no eyes at all.

Only mouths.

Too many mouths.

“…Target acquired.”

The calm voice came through the crystal communicator clipped beneath a dark cloak.

“Aidan.”

No answer.

The creatures suddenly stopped moving.

Every one of them.

Their heads slowly turned toward the darkness between the trees.

They could smell him.

One of the monsters smiled.

Then—

A blur.

CRACK.

The head disappeared from its shoulders before anyone actually saw the strike.

The body remained standing for half a second.

Then collapsed.

Blood sprayed across the rain-soaked forest.

The remaining two screamed.

A crimson-coated figure stepped from the darkness.

Long black coat.

Dark crimson lining.

Black combat armor scarred by hundreds of battles.

A sword rested across one shoulder.

Its crimson runes pulsed like a heartbeat.

Aidan Darkheart looked older.

His face had become sharper.

Harder.

Several thin scars crossed his jaw and neck.

One disappeared beneath the collar of his coat.

His crimson-gold eyes were completely still.

No rage.

No excitement.

Only calculation.

One monster lunged.

Aidan disappeared.

The creature crashed into empty air.

A hand caught its throat from behind.

“…Too slow.”

CRACK.

Its spine folded backwards.

He released the corpse before it hit the ground.

The last creature exploded toward him.

Claws.

Teeth.

Magic.

Everything at once.

Aidan calmly raised one hand.

“Hold.”

Crimson runes flashed across the forest.

The creature froze in midair as though reality itself refused to let it move.

The Law had become effortless.

Aidan walked forward through the rain.

The monster struggled.

Its muscles tore trying to escape.

It couldn’t.

He stared into its twisted face.

“Who sent you?”

The creature laughed.

“We finally found—”

It suddenly convulsed.

Black veins raced across its body.

Its flesh collapsed inward.

Aidan’s eyes narrowed.

“…Again.”

The monster dissolved into black ash before another word escaped.

Silence.

Only rain remained.

Several cloaked hunters emerged from the forest.

None approached too closely.

Not because they feared the battlefield.

Because they feared him.

One young hunter swallowed.

“…That was the seventh one this week.”

Another quietly whispered, “It killed an entire coven before we arrived…”

Aidan wiped black blood from his blade with one smooth motion.

“They’re adapting.”

The hunters exchanged uneasy looks.

Not one questioned him.

A woman with silver hair stepped forward.

Commander Selene.

She had once been considered one of the strongest battle witches outside Blackwood.

Now—

Even she looked toward Aidan before making decisions.

“Our scouts confirmed another breach farther east.”

Aidan looked toward the mountains.

“…How many civilians?”

“Forty-two.”

“Distance.”

“Twelve miles.”

He nodded once.

“I’ll go.”

“We can send—”

“No.”

There was no arrogance in his voice.

Only certainty.

“They won’t survive long enough.”

Without another word—

He vanished.

The young hunters blinked.

“…He’s gone.”

Commander Selene sighed.

“No.”

She looked toward the distant mountains.

“He’s already halfway there.”

⸻

Far above the forest…

Something enormous moved behind the clouds.

It never descended.

It simply watched.

Then disappeared.

⸻

Twelve miles away…

An abandoned mining town burned beneath crimson-black fire.

People ran through collapsing streets.

A massive creature smashed through the center of the village.

Eight legs.

Stone-like skin.

Rows of glowing crimson eyes.

Its roar shattered windows.

A child stumbled.

The monster raised one claw.

The mother screamed.

Before the strike landed—

A crimson blur crashed through the creature’s chest.

BOOM!!

The beast flew through three buildings before stopping.

Everyone froze.

The child looked upward.

Aidan stood between them and the monster.

His coat slowly settled behind him.

Blood dripped from one hand.

Not his.

The creature roared again.

It regenerated.

Fast.

Faster than anything from two years ago.

Aidan noticed.

“…Interesting.”

The monster charged.

He didn’t.

Instead…

He walked toward it.

Every step perfectly measured.

Every movement economical.

The monster swung first.

He tilted his head.

Missed.

Second claw.

He caught the wrist.

The ground exploded beneath their feet.

The creature screamed as Aidan twisted.

Its entire arm tore free.

Before it could regenerate—

He thrust his hand into the wound.

Crimson energy erupted through its body.

Every vein glowed.

The monster froze.

Then shattered from the inside out into thousands of crimson-black fragments.

Silence.

The survivors stared.

An old man whispered with trembling lips.

“…The Crimson Heir…”

Another person quickly bowed their head.

“He really exists…”

Aidan ignored them.

Instead he knelt beside the broken remains of the creature.

His fingers brushed the strange black crystal buried inside its chest.

Again.

The same crystal.

The same impossible material.

He pulled it free.

The moment air touched it—

Cracks spread across its surface.

Aidan crushed it in his hand before it could react.

Dust.

Nothing remained.

Whoever was creating these things had learned not to leave evidence.

He stood.

Commander Selene arrived minutes later with the rest of the hunters.

She looked around the destroyed battlefield.

“…Finished already.”

One hunter quietly muttered, “How many is that now?”

Another answered under his breath.

“I stopped counting after a hundred.”

Aidan sheathed his sword.

“Evacuate the survivors.”

“What about you?”

He looked toward the dark horizon.

The rain had stopped.

Night had become unnaturally quiet.

“They wanted me here.”

“You think this was a distraction?”

“I know it was.”

As if answering him—

Every crystal communicator activated at once.

Static.

Then a desperate voice.

“Emergency… Blackwood Academy… eastern defenses breached…”

Another voice cut in.

“No… multiple breaches…”

Then another.

“We’ve already lost the outer barrier…”

Silence.

Commander Selene’s face paled.

“…How?”

Blackwood’s defenses had only grown stronger over the past two years.

They weren’t supposed to fail.

Aidan’s expression never changed.

He simply turned toward the distant silhouette of the Academy.

For the first time all night…

His crimson-gold eyes narrowed.

“They finally made their move.”

Without another word—

He vanished into the darkness.

Behind him, the survivors watched the empty road where he had been standing only moments before.

No one spoke.

No one needed to.

The stories had been true.

The Crimson Heir no longer fought like someone trying to survive.

He fought like the thing every nightmare hoped would never find it.

And somewhere deep beneath Blackwood Academy…

Something ancient smiled.
