The old oak screamed.

Not with a voice.

With magic.

Every branch twisted toward the sky as crimson-black veins spread across its trunk.

The protective runes carved into the bark shattered one after another.

Professor Helena immediately raised both hands.

“All witches!”

“Barrier positions!”

Golden circles erupted throughout the courtyard.

Hundreds of runes intertwined into one immense protective formation surrounding the academy.

For a single heartbeat…

The Curse paused.

Then…

It reached outward.

Not toward the barriers.

Toward the people.

A first-year student suddenly gasped.

Then another.

Then dozens.

Their eyes became unfocused.

Each stared toward someone only they could see.

Parents.

Friends.

Loved ones.

Voices no one else could hear.

“It’s inside the students!” Helena shouted.

“No.”

Rowan answered immediately.

“It is inside their grief.”

“Dad…”

Elias caught him before he could walk away.

“No.”

Malik struggled.

“He’s alive.”

“You know he isn’t.”

“I heard him.”

Elias gripped his shoulders.

“I know.”

“My brother called me too.”

Their eyes met.

Both were shaking.

Both were crying.

Neither let go.

The whispers faded.

Across the courtyard…

Cassian stood frozen.

The cemetery gates had opened.

A lone figure stood inside.

Waiting.

Arms outstretched.

Cassian took one step.

Then another.

Aidan intercepted him.

“Cassian.”

“I failed her.”

“No.”

“I left.”

“You survived.”

“I should’ve died.”

Aidan didn’t argue.

Instead…

He embraced him.

Without another word.

Cassian broke.

Years of guilt escaped in silent tears.

The figure inside the cemetery dissolved into crimson mist.

The Curse lost another path.

⸻

The crimson-black sky trembled.

For the first time…

The Curse sounded angry.

The whisper reached only Aidan.

Why…

…do they keep choosing each other?

Aidan quietly answered.

“Because you can’t understand love.”

Not before Aidan’s eyes.

Above the entire courtyard.

Every witch saw it.

Every barrier immediately steadied.

Every trembling ward regained its shape.

Aidan looked toward the old oak.

Toward the heart of the Curse.

He finally understood.

Hold had taught him to preserve.

Bind had taught him to reconnect.

Stand taught him what remained when preservation and connection were no longer enough.

Rowan smiled with quiet pride.

“The Crimson Witch…”

“…is finally born.”

The old oak split down its center with a deafening crack.

A wave of crimson-black magic erupted across the academy.

No longer testing.

No longer waiting.

The Witch Curse had begun its final attempt to break the Heir.

And this time…

Aidan stepped forward instead of back.
