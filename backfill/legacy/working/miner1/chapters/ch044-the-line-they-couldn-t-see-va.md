The charcoal map remained spread across the strategy table long after everyone else had stopped speaking.

No names.

No landmarks anyone recognized.

Only lines.

Some ended abruptly.

Some crossed each other.

One ended at Blackwood Academy.

Another simply stopped in the middle of nowhere.

Elias leaned closer.

“It isn’t a trade map.”

“No.”

Rowan traced one finger along the charcoal path.

“It isn’t military either.”

Cassian looked toward Aidan.

“What do you think?”

Aidan didn’t answer immediately.

He wasn’t looking at the map.

He was listening.

The academy breathed around them.

Students changing watches.

Healers crossing the courtyard.

Rain striking the western roofs.

Then—

A single unfamiliar footstep.

Outside the hall.

It stopped.

His head turned instantly.

“So did yours,” Cassian said quietly.

Aidan nodded once.

Without another word, both men crossed the room.

The hallway stood empty.

Only a rain-soaked window remained slightly open.

Nothing else.

Cassian looked back.

“You heard someone.”

“I did.”

“No one was there.”

Aidan wasn’t so certain.

…

Before dawn, Rowan changed Blackwood’s procedures again.

Every messenger now traveled with a sealed phrase known only to Rowan and the receiving instructor.

No one objected.

No one even asked why.

The academy had learned that certainty could no longer be assumed.

…

The northern patrol departed before sunrise.

Aidan walked near the rear this time.

Not because he was tired.

Because he wanted to watch everyone else.

The thought unsettled him.

He hated it.

Every laugh between apprentices.

Every whispered conversation.

Every delayed response.

His mind examined each one.

Then he deliberately stopped himself.

The hunter wanted this.

He refused to give it more than caution.

Not suspicion.

Never suspicion.

…

Halfway through the patrol…

Aidan froze.

Not because of movement.

Because something was missing.

“The river.”

Malik frowned.

“What about it?”

“I can’t hear it.”

Everyone listened.

Nothing.

The river should have been loud enough to reach the trail.

Instead…

Silence.

They hurried forward.

When they reached the riverbank, every member of the patrol stopped.

The river still flowed.

Strong.

Fast.

Yet somehow…

Its sound vanished beneath an unnatural stillness.

Even the current seemed quieter than it should have been.

Lyra whispered,

“This place feels…”

She couldn’t finish.

Aidan stepped closer to the water.

His reflection looked back at him.

Thinner.

Older.

The scars along his jaw caught the morning light.

For one heartbeat…

The reflection didn’t move when he did.

His breath caught.

Then the surface rippled.

The illusion disappeared.

He stepped back immediately.

No one else had seen it.

He said nothing.

Some questions weren’t ready to be spoken aloud.

…

As they turned away…

His hearing caught another sound.

A child crying.

Very faint.

Across the river.

He looked.

Empty woods.

No movement.

Then…

The crying stopped.

Replaced by laughter.

The same laughter.

Exactly the same.

He recognized it.

The abandoned village.

Without another word…

He led the patrol away from the river.

No one argued.

No one looked back.
