Yet he would never see it the same way again.

Morning arrived beneath low gray clouds.

No rain fell.

Even the wind seemed hesitant.

The academy grounds stirred awake more slowly than usual.

Students spoke in quieter voices.

Not because they knew what was happening.

Because they felt it.

The mountain had become impossible to ignore.

Not through fear.

Through presence.

⸻

The messenger ravens refused to fly east.

Every release ended the same way.

They circled once above the academy.

Then returned to the western towers.

The stable horses repeatedly turned toward the mountain before settling again.

Professor Bennett quietly dismissed the handlers.

“I’ll take care of them.”

He remained with the animals for nearly an hour.

When he finally emerged…

His sleeves carried fresh dust.

As though he had been somewhere beneath the stables.

⸻

Throughout the day…

Faculty members appeared where students had never seen them before.

Professor Helena inspected old archways between classes.

Headmaster Aldren quietly walked the perimeter walls himself.

Master Rowan disappeared into the archives twice.

Returned with nothing.

Said nothing.

Only Aidan noticed.

Not because he was searching for secrets.

Because Rowan had taught him to observe the river.

Not the ripples.

⸻

After the afternoon lesson…

Cassian found Aidan repairing a loose practice rack.

Without a word…

He picked up the opposite side.

Aidan looked over.

“I didn’t ask.”

“I know.”

“You’ve got your own work.”

Cassian shrugged.

“So?”

They lifted together.

The damaged beam settled back into place.

Neither spoke until the repair was finished.

Cassian dusted off his hands.

“You remember the bridge?”

“I do.”

“I’ve been thinking.”

“That’s dangerous.”

Cassian laughed.

“It is.”

Then his expression became more serious.

“I keep saying you catch me.”

He looked down for a moment.

“But…”

“You know…”

“…you’ve also made me stop pretending I don’t need people.”

Silence settled between them.

Aidan stepped forward.

Without ceremony…

He gripped Cassian’s forearm.

Cassian returned the gesture instantly.

No dramatic oath.

No grand speech.

Only understanding.

Brotherhood strengthened by a hundred ordinary moments.

⸻

Later…

Lyra found Aidan beneath the old oak.

She carried two cups of tea.

“I figured Rowan would approve.”

Aidan accepted one.

“He probably would.”

They sat together beneath the broad branches.

Neither rushed to speak.

Finally…

Lyra rested her shoulder lightly against his.

“You don’t have to carry every quiet thing alone.”

He looked toward her.

“I know.”

“You’ve actually started believing that.”

“I have.”

She smiled.

“I’m glad.”

The words were simple.

The comfort they carried was not.

For the first time since inheriting the Legacy…

Aidan realized there was one place where he no longer felt the need to hide every burden.

Beside her.

He didn’t need to become smaller.

Or stronger.

Only honest.

⸻

From the library steps…

Master Rowan watched them both.

Headmaster Aldren approached quietly.

“You’ve noticed.”

“I have.”

“They steady one another.”

Rowan nodded.

“The strongest anchors rarely announce themselves.”

He looked toward the old oak.

“When storms come…”

“…people don’t remember who spoke the loudest.”

“They remember…”

“…who helped them remain themselves.”

⸻

As evening settled over Blackwood Academy…

The ancient wards shimmered once.

Visible.

Only for an instant.

Thin crimson lines spread through the oldest stones beneath the academy before disappearing again.

Students gasped.

Faculty remained perfectly still.

No one spoke.

The silence itself became the answer.

Far beneath them…

The first heartbeat answered.

The second heartbeat answered.

For the first time…

A third pulse echoed between them.

Not separate.

Not approaching.

Connecting.

The convergence had begun.
