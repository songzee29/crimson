The answer came the following morning.

Not at Blackwood.

Three miles away.

Smoke rose above the village of Grey Hollow.

One of the settlements supplying the academy.

A messenger arrived on horseback.

His horse collapsed before reaching the gate.

“They’re burning everything…”

He struggled to breathe.

“They never came near the academy…”

“They waited for us…”

“…then attacked the village…”

The room fell silent.

The hunters had evolved again.

They had stopped attacking where Blackwood was strongest.

They were attacking what Blackwood couldn’t protect.

…

Within minutes Rowan assembled a rescue force.

Not everyone.

Half remained behind.

“They want us divided,” Rowan said.

“We won’t give them the whole academy.”

Aidan looked at the map.

Grey Hollow.

Narrow roads.

Dense forest.

Limited visibility.

Perfect ground for an ambush.

“We move fast.”

“No.”

Rowan shook his head.

“We move carefully.”

The lesson had finally been learned.

…

Grey Hollow was already burning when they arrived.

Homes collapsed.

Families fled toward the river.

The hunters weren’t killing indiscriminately.

They were driving people.

Herding them.

Exactly as they had done with Blackwood’s students.

“They’re controlling movement,” Elias realized.

“They’re deciding where everyone runs.”

Aidan’s hearing caught something no one else noticed.

Not screams.

Silence.

One street.

Completely empty.

He turned immediately.

“Not there.”

Malik looked toward the deserted lane.

“Why?”

“Because they want us to.”

The words had barely left his mouth when the empty street collapsed into a hidden pit lined with sharpened stakes.

The Trapper.

It had adapted.

The hunters no longer relied on claws alone.

They reshaped battlefields before the fight even began.

“They’re forcing us to waste time,” Cassian said.

“They’re forcing us to doubt every step,” Aidan corrected.

The fire spread.

Children cried from another building.

Aidan ran.

Not recklessly.

Purposefully.

His body slipped through collapsing beams with almost unnatural precision.

He reached a frightened little girl trapped beneath a burning support.

He lifted the beam despite the protest from his healing ribs.

Pain flared white across his vision.

Still…

He carried her outside.

Behind him…

The house collapsed.

His breathing became ragged.

He smelled smoke.

Ash.

Burning wood.

Then…

Blood.

The girl’s scraped arm.

Just a little.

His eyes found it immediately.

He looked away before she noticed.

“Find Helena,” he told her quietly.

She nodded and ran.

Across the square…

A pale figure stood upon the roof of a burning house.

It didn’t attack.

Its black-marked arm calmly carved another line into the charred wood.

The Observer.

It watched Aidan rescue the child.

It watched him ignore his own injuries.

It watched where his eyes lingered for one heartbeat too long before he turned away.

Then it disappeared into the smoke.

The village would survive.

Barely.

But the hunters had learned another lesson.

If Blackwood would not leave its walls willingly…

Its heart could be drawn outside them.

And for the first time…

The battlefield was no longer the academy.

It was the entire kingdom around it.
