The hunt did not end.

It simply stopped counting days.

Spring became summer.

Summer surrendered to autumn.

Autumn froze beneath winter.

Then the seasons repeated.

Again.

And again.

The years disappeared between patrol reports, bloodstained maps, emergency bells, and exhausted footsteps crossing Blackwood Academy before dawn.

No one remembered the last truly peaceful morning.

No one remembered the last uneventful week.

The hunt became life.

…

The villages learned a familiar sight.

A young witch with crimson-gold eyes arriving long after sunrise.

Sometimes covered in blood.

Sometimes limping.

Sometimes with fresh bandages beneath his coat.

Always too tired.

Always asking the same question.

“How many?”

Sometimes the answer was one.

Sometimes ten.

Sometimes everyone.

Those were the days he carried home in silence.

…

The hunters evolved.

The Observer no longer worked alone.

Its carvings spread across abandoned towers, ruined bridges, cave walls, and forgotten shrines.

Every battlefield became another page in its record.

The Trapper reshaped roads before caravans arrived.

The Divider mastered breaking rescue formations.

The Manipulator never needed to fight.

It only needed frightened civilians.

A burning home.

A child calling for help from the wrong direction.

An impossible decision.

The Executioner grew stronger with every encounter.

It never wasted energy anymore.

It waited until exhaustion had already begun the battle.

The Commander…

Was never seen.

Only felt.

Every attack carried its patience.

…

Blackwood adapted.

Again.

Dormitories moved underground.

Signal runes changed monthly.

Hidden shelters spread beneath the academy.

Students graduated already knowing battlefield medicine.

Every first-year learned evacuation before advanced spellcraft.

Every teacher carried weapons.

Every healer learned combat.

The academy survived.

It no longer resembled a school.

It resembled a nation preparing for endless war.

…

The inner circle endured together.

Cassian’s damaged arm never fully recovered.

He simply learned to fight differently.

Malik’s limp became permanent after collapsing beneath a shattered watchtower to save trapped apprentices.

He joked about it.

Less often than he once had.

Elias abandoned pride and accepted the enchanted brace on his injured leg.

He became Blackwood’s greatest strategist because running into battle was no longer always possible.

Lyra accumulated scars she rarely spoke about.

She smiled.

She laughed.

But only when she believed Aidan was watching.

Because she wanted him to remember how.

Rowan’s hair turned noticeably whiter.

Professor Helena slept in the infirmary more often than in her own quarters.

No one escaped untouched.

…

Aidan survived.

Again.

And again.

And again.

The Crimson System never celebrated.

It observed.

It archived.

It recorded.

Sometimes after a siege.

Sometimes after a rescue.

Sometimes after surviving wounds that should have ended him.

No explanations.

No promises.

Only preparation.

…

The scars multiplied.

The one beneath his ribs.

The claw marks across his back.

The wound along his side.

The broken shoulder that ached whenever storms approached.

The faint line beneath his jaw earned while shielding children during the Siege of Ash Hollow.

The burn across his forearm from the Fall of Red Mill.

The fractured knuckles that never healed perfectly.

Each one remembered something.

Each one cost something.

…

He slept less.

Sometimes because alarms woke him.

Sometimes because dreams refused to let him rest.

Sometimes because silence itself felt suspicious.

Meals became shorter.

Then forgotten.

Helena stopped asking if he had eaten.

She simply handed him food whenever she found him.

Sometimes he remembered.

Sometimes the plate returned untouched.

…

He smiled less.

Then laughed less.

Then stopped noticing the difference.

…

He still saved people.

He still knelt beside frightened children.

He still carried the wounded.

He still stood between monsters and strangers.

That part never changed.

What changed…

Was how quietly he did it.

…

Rumors spread through the surrounding villages.

The Crimson Witch always came.

Sometimes late.

Never willingly.

Always bleeding.

Always leaving before dawn.

Children drew pictures of crimson barriers protecting burning homes.

Parents whispered prayers that Blackwood would arrive before the hunters.

No one called him a hero.

They called him hope.

A title far heavier than either crown or sword.

He carried it anyway.

…

Exactly two years passed.

The calendar declared Aidan Darkheart twenty years old.

No celebration marked the day.

No cake.

No laughter.

No gathering beneath the old oak.

His birthday ended the same way the previous hundreds of days had.

On patrol.

Standing atop Blackwood’s eastern wall beneath a cold autumn sky.

His coat hid fresh bandages across old scars.

Dark circles rested permanently beneath tired eyes.

His movements had become quiet enough that new students often failed to hear him approach.

His hearing reached beyond the academy walls.

His instincts rarely proved wrong.

His body survived injuries that should have ended ordinary witches.

His soul carried every battle.

Every name.

Every failure.

Every life he had been unable to reach in time.

He was still Aidan.

He still cared.

He still chose people before himself.

But surviving had taken almost everything else.

…

The academy bell rang.

Once.

Not an alarm.

A warning.

A rider approached Blackwood alone.

His horse staggered through the gate before collapsing.

The rider rolled onto the stone courtyard.

Covered in blood.

Not all of it his own.

He looked up at Aidan.

His lips trembled.

“They’re back…”

Aidan didn’t ask who.

He already knew.

The second heartbeat answered beneath his own.

Steady.

Patient.

Waiting.

He looked toward the northern horizon.

Somewhere beyond the forests…

The Commander had begun the next hunt.

And before Aidan could catch his breath from the last two years…

The nightmare started again.
