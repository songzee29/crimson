Morning never came for Aidan.

The darkness remained perfect.

His eyes no longer needed dawn.

Every shadow welcomed him.

Every silence belonged to him.

He stood atop the roof of the abandoned stable overlooking the inn.

Nine heartbeats.

He could distinguish each one.

The child’s pulse fluttered weakly.

The old man’s heart skipped every twelfth beat.

One merchant carried a healing wound across his shoulder.

Fresh blood.

Faint.

His head turned immediately toward it.

The scent nearly pulled him from the roof.

He gripped the weathered timber beneath his hands.

It splintered effortlessly.

He stared at the broken wood.

His own strength surprised him.

The power settled naturally into his body.

No practice.

No instruction.

Only instinct.

The hunger immediately wanted to use it.

He disappeared.

The roof cracked where he had been standing.

A heartbeat later he stood at the edge of the road over a hundred paces away.

His own movement startled him.

He had arrived before conscious thought finished forming.

The world felt slower.

Smaller.

Fragile.

It preserved.

It did not empower.

Inside the inn, the coughing child finally fell asleep.

The mother quietly stroked his hair.

She never knew crimson eyes had watched through the rain-streaked window only moments before.

Aidan stood beyond the edge of the lantern light.

The hunger urged him forward.

Another instinct—new, faint, but growing—held him still for one heartbeat longer.

Then he turned.

South.

Toward New Orleans.

Toward ancient doors waiting beneath a city that had never known his name.

Behind him, the inn remained untouched.

Ahead of him, the night stretched endlessly onward.

And for the first time since his rebirth, the Crimson System had become more than a witness to his transformation.

It had become the path he would walk.
