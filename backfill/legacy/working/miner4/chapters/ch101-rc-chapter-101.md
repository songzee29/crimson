# Chapter One Hundred One

## Two Years Later

Rain hammered the rooftops of the abandoned riverfront district.

Lightning flashed over the Mississippi, illuminating broken warehouses covered in crimson warning runes.

The silence lasted only a heartbeat.

Then something screamed.

A black shape burst through the shattered doors of the largest warehouse.

Cassian met it head-on.

Steel crashed against claw.

The impact threw both of them across the concrete floor.

“Left side!” Lyra shouted.

A massive silver wolf exploded through a broken wall, tackling the creature before it reached Cassian's throat.

The beast slammed into stacked shipping containers, sending twisted metal crashing into the darkness.

Malik raised both hands.

Golden sigils spiraled around his arms.

“Seal!”

Chains of glowing witchcraft erupted from the floor.

The creature tore through the first three as though they were thread.

The fourth lasted less than a second.

“It adapted again,” Malik growled.

“It always does,” Elias answered calmly as he drew another crimson-inscribed blade.

None of them sounded surprised anymore.

Two years earlier…

They would have celebrated surviving monsters like these.

Now…

This was Tuesday.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Three more creatures emerged from the darkness.

No eyes.

Too many mouths.

Bodies that shifted every few seconds as if refusing to settle into one shape.

Nobody asked what they were.

Nobody even tried naming them anymore.

Kill.

Move.

Survive.

That had become enough.

Lyra's crimson wolf disappeared beneath one monster before erupting upward, ripping straight through its chest.

It dissolved into black ash before hitting the floor.

Another rushed Elias.

He sidestepped without hesitation.

One strike.

Its head rolled across the concrete.

Cassian didn't even watch.

He trusted Elias completely now.

Behind them…

Heavy footsteps echoed through the warehouse.

Slow.

Measured.

Almost lazy.

Every monster froze.

For the briefest instant…

Fear filled the room.

Not fear of the hunters.

Fear…

Of something else.

Aidan Darkheart stepped through the shattered entrance.

His black coat dripped with rain.

Longer crimson-black hair rested against his shoulders.

Fresh scars crossed his jaw and neck.

Others disappeared beneath the sleeves covering both arms.

His crimson-gold eyes glowed quietly in the darkness.

The second heartbeat echoed once.

Then silence.

No expression crossed his face.

No anger.

No excitement.

Only the cold patience of something that had hunted far longer than anything standing before him.

The nearest creature lunged.

It never reached him.

A crimson blur crossed the room.

Aidan's hand closed around its throat.

One sharp twist.

Its neck shattered.

Before the body touched the floor…

His other hand pierced straight through its chest.

The crimson glow vanished from his fingers.

The creature collapsed into ash.

No wasted movement.

No flourish.

No spell.

Just efficiency.

Cassian sheathed one sword.

“You took your time.”

“I finished another hunt.”

“How many this week?”

“I stopped counting.”

Nobody questioned the answer.

Nobody laughed.

Two years had changed that too.

The screen disappeared.

Aidan barely glanced at it.

System messages had become another part of breathing.

Useful.

Nothing more.

Malik looked toward the warehouse entrance.

“There were supposed to be four.”

“There were.”

“We killed four.”

“There were five.”

Everyone immediately turned.

Aidan was already looking upward.

The ceiling.

One heartbeat.

Two.

A faint scratching echoed through rusted support beams.

Lyra's ears flattened.

“It's waiting.”

“No,” Aidan answered quietly.

“It's watching.”

The beam above them exploded.

The largest creature yet crashed downward.

Twice the size of the others.

Its body pulsed with crimson veins.

Before anyone moved—

Aidan vanished.

Concrete shattered beneath where he had been standing.

The monster swung.

It struck nothing.

Aidan appeared beside its shoulder.

One punch.

The creature flew through three concrete pillars before crashing into the opposite wall.

The entire warehouse shook.

Dust filled the air.

It stood again.

Aidan nodded once.

“Better.”

The creature roared.

For the first time in months…

Something had survived his opening attack.

His lips curled upward.

Not into a smile.

Into anticipation.

He disappeared again.

The battle became impossible to follow.

Concrete exploded.

Steel bent.

The creature's claws carved trenches through warehouse walls.

Every strike missed.

Aidan never hurried.

Never overextended.

Every dodge placed him exactly where he wanted to be.

Every counterattack broke another piece of the monster apart.

Cassian watched quietly.

“He got faster.”

Rowan stepped from the shadows near the entrance, his weathered staff resting against one shoulder.

“He stopped relying on speed.”

Another explosion echoed through the building.

The creature staggered backward.

One arm missing.

Half its face gone.

Still…

It refused to die.

Rowan's old eyes remained fixed on Aidan.

“Watch carefully.”

Aidan raised one hand.

Not toward the creature.

Toward the blood scattered across the floor.

The crimson droplets trembled.

Then rose.

Thousands of floating crimson needles formed around him.

No System announcement.

No dramatic display.

Only absolute control.

The creature charged.

Aidan lowered two fingers.

The storm descended.

Every crimson needle struck simultaneously.

Silence.

The monster stopped moving.

Tiny holes covered every inch of its body.

One second later…

It collapsed into dust.

The warehouse became still once more.

His gaze shifted toward the broken ceiling.

The rain continued falling.

The night smelled of wet stone…

And blood.

His hunger stirred.

Constant.

Patient.

Never gone.

He closed his eyes.

One slow breath.

When they opened again…

Control remained.

For now.

Lyra approached until she stood beside him.

“You okay?”

Aidan answered without looking away from the storm.

“I'm hungry.”

Cassian sighed.

“Again.”

“I didn't say I was losing control.”

“I know.”

That answer alone revealed how much time had passed.

No panic.

No fear.

No reaching for weapons.

His friends no longer doubted his control.

They simply accepted the burden he carried every waking moment.

A sudden chime echoed inside Aidan's mind.

Not loud.

Not urgent.

Different.

Something ancient opened its eyes.
