# Chapter Twenty-One: The Shape of Exhaustion

The journey back from the southern bridge was silent.

No one celebrated saving the convoy.

Not after watching Aidan cough blood into the rain.

By the time Blackwood Academy's gates came into view, Malik was supporting half of Aidan's weight while Lyra stayed close enough to catch him if he stumbled.

"I'm walking," Aidan muttered.

"Barely," Malik replied.

"I've been worse."

"You keep saying that."

Cassian looked ahead without slowing.

"One day it won't be true."

No one answered.

...

The infirmary had become too small.

Rows of temporary beds filled adjoining classrooms.

Merchants.

Students.

Guards.

Every battle added names.

Every victory demanded space.

Professor Helena examined Aidan in complete silence.

She unwrapped the bandages around his ribs.

The room grew colder.

Rowan stood behind her.

"What is it?"

Helena didn't answer immediately.

She gently pressed one side of Aidan's chest.

Pain exploded through him.

He gritted his teeth but refused to cry out.

"The fourth rib never healed."

She moved lower.

"The fifth fractured again."

Another touch.

"The sixth is cracked now."

Malik slowly exhaled.

Helena looked directly at Aidan.

"You're surviving injuries faster than anyone should..."

She paused.

"...but you aren't recovering from them."

The distinction settled over the room.

His body kept him alive.

It did not erase the damage.

Every battle remained.

Layer upon layer.

Scar upon scar.

...

That evening Rowan dismissed everyone except Aidan.

The old master placed two cups of tea between them.

"You've stopped asking me questions."

Aidan stared into the steam.

"I don't have answers anymore."

"No."

Rowan nodded.

"You have responsibilities."

Silence lingered.

Then Rowan quietly continued.

"You know what worries me?"

Aidan looked up.

"You aren't afraid for yourself."

"I'm not."

"I know."

The old master leaned forward.

"You're beginning to believe your body will always survive."

Aidan opened his mouth to deny it.

Nothing came.

Because somewhere deep inside...

Part of him already believed it.

Not consciously.

Instinctively.

That realization frightened him.

...

Near midnight...

Aidan left the infirmary alone.

Again.

His footsteps crossed the academy grounds without disturbing so much as a loose pebble.

The old oak stood against the stars.

He rested one hand against its trunk.

The second heartbeat answered.

THUMP.

Then...

Another sound.

Not beneath him.

Above.

His head lifted.

Someone stood atop the eastern watchtower.

Not moving.

Watching.

"Aidan."

The voice drifted across the night.

Cassian.

"I wondered how long it would take."

Aidan climbed the tower.

Cassian never looked away from the forest.

"You've been coming out here every night."

"I couldn't sleep."

"No."

Cassian's voice remained calm.

"You've been listening."

Aidan froze.

Cassian continued.

"I've watched you stop before attacks."

"You hear things none of us hear."

"You react before anyone else."

He finally turned.

"And every day..."

His eyes settled on Aidan's exhausted face.

"...you look more tired."

For a long moment...

Neither spoke.

Finally Cassian asked the question no one else had dared.

"What are you hiding from us?"

Aidan searched for words.

Only one came.

"Myself."

# Chapter Twenty-Two: The Hunter's Choice

The attack began before sunrise.

Not with claws.

Not with screams.

With fire.

The western grain stores erupted into flames.

Students rushed with water.

Faculty reinforced nearby buildings.

Smoke swallowed half the academy.

"They're trying to burn us out!" Malik shouted.

"No."

Aidan was already moving.

"They're moving us."

His body turned toward the eastern wall before his thoughts caught up.

"They want everyone looking west."

The mountain exploded.

Stone burst upward beneath the eastern shelters.

Nine pale hunters erupted through the ground simultaneously.

Not random.

Perfectly timed.

Children screamed.

The hunters ignored armed instructors.

Ignored Rowan.

Ignored Helena.

Every one of them sprinted toward the evacuation shelters.

"They're after the students!" Lyra cried.

"No."

Aidan's voice was frighteningly calm.

"They're after me."

The hunters knew exactly where he would run.

The first creature slashed through a support beam.

The roof began collapsing.

Aidan was already there.

"Hold!"

The structure froze inches above dozens of terrified children.

Another hunter attacked his exposed side.

Cassian intercepted it.

His damaged arm buckled under the impact.

Bone cracked.

Still...

He stood.

Malik dragged children from beneath the suspended roof despite his injured leg threatening to give out.

Elias reinforced escape paths one trembling rune at a time.

The hunters adapted immediately.

Two disengaged.

Not retreating.

Circling.

Separating.

One deliberately knocked over a burning wagon.

Flames raced toward another shelter.

Too many crises.

Too many choices.

Exactly as they intended.

Aidan's breathing became uneven.

Every instinct screamed different answers.

Save the roof.

Stop the fire.

Protect Lyra.

Help Cassian.

His body chose before his mind.

He threw one hand toward the flames.

"Still!"

The fire stopped spreading for a single heartbeat.

Just enough.

Helena flooded the area with a wave of conjured water.

The blaze died.

The roof finally settled safely as the last child escaped.

Only then did Aidan release the Law.

His knees buckled.

Blood splashed across broken stone.

One hunter stood only a few feet away.

It did not attack.

It watched him.

Studied him.

Then...

Slowly...

It looked toward Lyra.

The meaning was unmistakable.

Next time.

It vanished beneath the academy before anyone could pursue.

Silence spread across Blackwood.

The shelters still stood.

The students lived.

But the message had been delivered.

The hunters no longer tested Blackwood's defenses.

They tested Aidan's heart.

Later...

Long after the wounded had been carried away...

Aidan stood alone in the ruined shelter.

His breathing remained shallow.

His body ached from head to toe.

The scent of blood lingered in the air from dozens of minor injuries.

His eyes kept drifting toward it.

Again.

And again.

He forced himself to leave.

Behind him...

Hidden beneath shattered floorboards...

A single pale claw quietly carved one final mark into the stone before disappearing into the tunnels below.

The enemy no longer measured walls.

It no longer measured magic.

It measured sacrifice.

And each time Aidan chose someone else's life over his own...

The hunters drew one step closer to learning exactly how to kill the Crimson Witch.
