## Chapter One Hundred Fifty-Six — The Departure

The decision was made before Aidan admitted it to himself.

He was leaving.

Not because the Living Legacy commanded him.

It never had.

Not because the Guardian ordered him.

He never would.

He was leaving because history had finally become larger than the walls of House Darkheart.

---

Dawn broke quietly over the estate.

Mist rolled across the gardens while servants began another ordinary morning.

To everyone else...

Nothing had changed.

To Aidan...

Everything had.

He packed lightly.

The sketchbook Lyra had given him.

The journal from Blackwood Library.

The First Keeper's key.

A change of clothes.

Nothing more.

The Guardian's words echoed through his thoughts.

A Founder carries knowledge...

Not possessions.

He closed his travel bag.

It felt almost empty.

Yet somehow...

He had never carried so much before.

---

Before leaving, Aidan walked one final time through the western wing.

The empty portrait waited exactly where he had first discovered it.

He stood before it in silence.

This time...

He noticed something he had missed before.

Along the bottom edge of the frame...

Tiny letters had been carved into the wood.

Not a name.

A promise.

**May every generation surpass the one before it.**

Aidan smiled.

Whoever the Founder had been...

He hadn't wanted followers.

He had wanted successors.

That thought stayed with him as he quietly left the room.

---

Outside...

Malik waited beside his motorcycle.

Took you long enough.

Aidan laughed.

How did you—

You really think I wouldn't notice my best friend planning a mysterious trip?

Fair point.

Malik tossed him a small compass.

Old-fashioned brass.

Scratched from years of use.

My grandfather gave it to me.

Now I'm giving it to you.

I can't take this.

You can.

Malik folded Aidan's fingers around it.

A compass isn't valuable because it tells you where north is.

It's valuable because it reminds you not to get lost.

Promise you'll bring it back.

Aidan nodded.

I promise.

The two embraced briefly.

No dramatic speeches.

Some friendships didn't need them.

---

As Aidan reached the academy one last time before leaving...

Lyra was already waiting beneath the old oak.

She wasn't surprised to see the travel bag over his shoulder.

You really are going.

Aidan nodded.

For a while.

She handed him a rolled piece of paper.

Another drawing?

No.

A map.

He unrolled it.

It showed the forests east of the city.

Several paths had been crossed out.

One remained highlighted in charcoal.

How did you—

I've hiked those mountains since I was little.

She smiled softly.

Everyone uses the southern trail.

Don't.

Take the old northern path instead.

It's longer...

But safer.

Aidan carefully folded the map.

Thank you.

She looked toward the mountains.

When you come back...

I want to hear every story.

Aidan smiled.

Only if you promise to draw them.

Deal.

Neither noticed...

The crimson lily pressed between the pages of her sketchbook gently glowing for a single heartbeat before returning to normal.

---

Far beneath Blackwood Library...

The old bookseller extinguished one lantern.

Then lit another.

The new flame burned crimson instead of gold.

He whispered into the empty chamber.

The Pilgrim has departed.

From distant tunnels...

Three bells answered.

Not loud.

Just enough.

The Keepers had heard.

---

Beyond the eastern mountains...

The Hunter stood atop a weathered cliff overlooking a forgotten valley.

He watched Aidan's tiny figure leave the city far below.

Finally.

The Heir has taken the first true step.

He lifted his spear.

Not in challenge.

In salute.

Every journey begins with leaving home.

Every civilization begins with someone willing to walk where no map can guide them.

Then...

Without another word...

The Hunter disappeared into the forest below.

He would not lead Aidan.

He would not protect him.

From this point forward...

He would only witness whether the Heir truly possessed the judgment to become the Founder history had been waiting for.

As the sun climbed above the eastern peaks...

Aidan crossed the old stone bridge marking the boundary of the city.

He stopped only once.

Turning back...

He looked toward House Darkheart.

Toward the academy.

Toward Blackwood Library.

Toward everything that had shaped the person he was.

Then he faced the mountains.

Beyond them waited the Second Foundation.

Unknown Keepers.

Ancient truths.

The old races.

And a world far older than the one he had grown up believing existed.

The road stretched endlessly ahead.

For the first time...

The Crimson Legacy was no longer the story of a boy discovering his inheritance.

It had become the story of a young man walking toward the future he would one day build.

The First Pilgrim Road...

Had begun.
