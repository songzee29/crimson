# Chapter Thirty-Two: Before the Wounds Could Close

The stitches across Aidan's side lasted less than twenty-four hours.

Not because they failed.

Because another alarm split the morning before sunrise.

Three sharp pulses.

North.

Then one pulse.

West.

A pause.

Then the eastern signal rune flared crimson.

Not another attack.

Three attacks.

At once.

The strategy hall erupted into motion.

Messengers crossed the room carrying reports faster than anyone could read them.

"The northern patrol lost contact."

"Grey Hollow's warning rune activated again."

"The western supply road has collapsed."

Rowan spread the reports across the table.

"They're forcing decisions."

Helena immediately answered.

"We don't have enough people."

"No."

Aidan's voice remained quiet.

"They know that."

The room fell silent.

The hunters weren't becoming stronger.

They were becoming patient.

...

"We split."

"No."

Every eye turned toward Aidan.

"They expect that."

Cassian folded his arms.

"Then what?"

Aidan stared at the map.

His hearing drifted beyond the room.

Students moving supplies.

Guards changing positions.

The wind against the western towers.

Then...

A heartbeat.

Not inside Blackwood.

Outside the eastern wall.

Waiting.

Watching.

"They're counting."

Rowan noticed Aidan's focus.

"What did you hear?"

"They're waiting to see who leaves."

No one questioned him.

Not anymore.

...

Blackwood changed its response.

Instead of sending one large rescue force...

Small teams departed in different directions.

Some were real.

Others carried empty wagons.

Some left through the gates.

Others through hidden tunnels.

The academy adapted again.

...

The hunters adapted too.

Aidan realized it first.

"They ignored the decoys."

Cassian looked toward him.

"How do you know?"

"They never moved."

A minute later...

A messenger arrived.

"The false convoy reached Grey Hollow untouched."

Another followed.

"The real supply wagon was attacked in the southern valley."

The room became silent.

"They watched long enough to recognize the difference," Elias whispered.

"They're learning faster."

"No."

Aidan looked toward the southern valley.

"They're watching longer."

...

By midday...

The rescue force reached the southern road.

The wagons still stood.

The horses did not.

The drivers had survived.

Barely.

No hunters remained.

Only another message.

This one carved into the wagon itself.

One black line.

Then another.

Then another.

Thirty-two.

Cassian frowned.

"They're counting the hunts."

Aidan shook his head.

"They're counting me."

The realization lingered between them.

The Observer wasn't recording battles anymore.

It was recording Aidan.

...

As they searched the surrounding forest...

Aidan stopped.

Not because he saw something.

Because he smelled it.

Fresh blood.

Very little.

Hidden.

His feet carried him toward the scent before he consciously chose a direction.

He froze halfway there.

No.

He forced himself to stop.

Cassian watched quietly.

"You found something."

Aidan nodded.

"Over there."

Cassian discovered an injured merchant concealed beneath fallen branches.

Alive.

Bleeding.

Breathing.

He looked back at Aidan.

"How?"

"I..."

The answer stayed trapped inside him.

"I don't know."

But he was beginning to.

His body knew before his mind accepted it.

...

They returned to Blackwood after sunset.

No sooner had they crossed the gate than another messenger arrived.

"The northern watchtower has gone silent!"

Malik let out a tired breath.

"We just got back."

"We're going," Rowan answered immediately.

No one protested.

No one had the energy.

Still...

They went.

Because someone always needed them.

# Chapter Thirty-Three: One Step Behind

The northern watchtower stood intact.

That was the first thing that felt wrong.

No smoke.

No blood.

No signs of battle.

Only silence.

Aidan slowly climbed the wooden stairs.

Each step sent pain through his ribs.

The wound across his side pulled against fresh stitches.

He ignored both.

At the top...

The guards remained alive.

Sleeping.

Every one of them.

Helena examined the nearest.

"They aren't injured."

"What happened?" Malik asked.

Aidan knelt.

He smelled bitter herbs.

Not enough to notice before.

Enough now.

"They were drugged."

Elias looked surprised.

"The hunters didn't kill them?"

"No."

Rowan's expression hardened.

"They wanted the tower."

The realization came too late.

Aidan's hearing caught distant movement.

South.

Fast.

Not one runner.

Many.

He turned sharply.

"They're attacking Blackwood."

Everyone sprinted.

The hunters had never intended to take the tower.

Only to pull the academy's strongest defenders away.

By the time Blackwood came into view...

Smoke already rose above the eastern training grounds.

Students fought beside instructors.

Barrier circles flickered.

The hunters were already retreating.

Again.

Another lesson learned.

Another wound left behind.

They had struck the academy...

While its protectors chased shadows.

...

The eastern training grounds lay in ruins.

No students had died.

But three instructors had fallen protecting them.

Helena silently knelt beside the wounded.

Her hands never stopped moving.

Malik carried broken stone from collapsed buildings despite his worsening limp.

Cassian ignored the pain in his damaged arm while helping evacuate apprentices.

Lyra organized frightened first-years into healing circles.

Everyone kept moving.

Only Aidan stood still.

His eyes drifted across the battlefield.

The hunters hadn't stayed long enough to win.

Only long enough to prove they could reach Blackwood whenever they chose.

His fists slowly tightened.

The second heartbeat answered.

THUMP.

The scent of blood surrounded him.

Not one source.

Dozens.

He closed his eyes.

Focused on breathing.

One breath.

Then another.

The hunger eased.

Barely.

Lyra quietly stepped beside him.

"You found them too late."

He nodded.

"They're always one step ahead."

She looked toward the broken training grounds.

"No."

Her voice remained gentle.

"They're making you believe that."

He wanted to believe her.

He truly did.

But somewhere deep inside...

The hunter's whisper returned.

*You have your traitor to thank for this.*

He immediately pushed the thought away.

Not Lyra.

Not Rowan.

Not Cassian.

Not Malik.

Not Elias.

Never.

Still...

He found himself looking over the ruined academy with different eyes.

Not searching for enemies.

Searching for answers.

And while Blackwood rebuilt once more...

Far beyond the academy...

The Commander quietly erased the thirty-third mark from a weathered stone.

In its place...

It carved a different symbol.

A single circle.

Closing.

The hunt was no longer tightening around Blackwood.

It was tightening around Aidan himself.
