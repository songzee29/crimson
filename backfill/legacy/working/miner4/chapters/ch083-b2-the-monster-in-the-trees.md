Not because he ruled it.

Because nothing else dared breathe too loudly within it.

Thousands.

No...

Tens of thousands.

Every living heartbeat echoed through the mountains.

Birds nesting inside hollow trees.

Foxes hunting beneath fallen leaves.

Villagers sleeping behind locked doors.

Children crying in distant cabins.

Each heartbeat carried its own rhythm.

Its own scent.

Its own promise.

His throat burned.

Not with pain.

With need.

# Chapter Eighty-Four: The Living Remember

The academy gates remained shut.

Students no longer trained beyond the walls.

Every patrol returned before sunset.

Every child traveled with an escort.

The academy had survived the Witch Curse.

Now...

It lived in fear of the one who had saved it.

...

Rowan stood alone inside the abandoned training yard.

Aidan's practice circle remained exactly as it had been the week before.

Cracked stone.

Scorch marks.

The outline of countless lessons.

He rested his hand against one weathered pillar.

"You learned faster than anyone."

The empty yard offered no reply.

"I always thought your final lesson would be mastery."

A slow breath escaped him.

"It was sacrifice."

For the first time since Aidan's death...

The old master allowed himself to grieve.

Not loudly.

Not publicly.

Simply as a teacher who had outlived his greatest student.

...

Helena moved through the infirmary.

Every injured traveler asked the same question.

"Is it true?"

She never answered immediately.

Instead she changed bandages.

Mixed medicine.

Closed wounds.

Finally...

One frightened woman whispered,

"Will the monster come here?"

Helena looked toward the forest beyond Blackwood's walls.

She remembered the young boy who had once apologized every time she stitched him back together.

She remembered the exhausted young man who never stopped asking whether everyone else had survived.

She closed her eyes.

"I don't know."

It was the hardest truth she had ever spoken.

...

Cassian remained inside the Great Hall.

Aidan's broken crimson staff still rested where Elias had placed it.

Cassian reached toward it.

Then stopped.

His own hands shook.

He could still feel the weight of the final moment.

The curse spoken against him.

The final breath.

The impossible choice.

He whispered into the empty hall,

"I ended your life..."

The words echoed back unchanged.

No forgiveness came.

None was expected.

...

Far beyond Blackwood...

Aidan stood knee-deep in a cold river.

The flowing water carried dozens of scents.

Deer.

Fish.

Humans.

All distinct.

All alive.

His reflection stared back at him.

Crimson eyes.

Pale skin.

Fangs resting just beyond closed lips.

He looked nothing like the exhausted young witch who had collapsed beneath the mountain.

He looked stronger.

Faster.

Perfect.

Only the fire inside his throat betrayed the truth.

It had not eased.

It had grown.
