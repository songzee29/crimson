## Chapter One Hundred Forty-One — The First Trial of Judgment

Rain began falling through the broken ceiling of Ashfall Abbey.

Each drop echoed against the ancient stone floor.

Neither Aidan nor the Hunter moved.

The silence between them felt deliberate.

Measured.

The Hunter's black eyes never left Aidan.

You're younger than I expected.

Aidan remained calm.

You're older than I expected.

A faint smile crossed the Hunter's face.

Good.

You're not afraid.

I didn't say that.

The Hunter nodded approvingly.

Even better.

Fear acknowledged...

Is far more useful than fear denied.

Aidan glanced toward the ancient book resting upon the altar.

Who are you?

Someone history chose to remember...

For all the wrong reasons.

The answer settled heavily in the ruined abbey.

The Hunter slowly removed the weathered cloth covering the weapon across his back.

Not to threaten.

To reveal.

A long spear emerged.

Its shaft was forged from black metal unlike anything Aidan had ever seen.

Ancient crimson runes had been carved into its length.

Not glowing.

Sleeping.

The blade reflected no light.

Only shadows.

That weapon...

Aidan whispered.

Has taken many lives.

The Hunter looked down at it.

Too many.

His voice carried no pride.

Only regret.

Then why keep it?

Because remembering my failures...

Makes it harder to repeat them.

The words struck Aidan harder than any display of power could have.

The Hunter gently rested the spear against one of the broken pillars.

I wasn't sent to fight you.

I was sent to judge whether you're worthy of surviving what's coming.

The air inside the abbey grew colder.

Not from magic.

From truth.

## Chapter One Hundred Forty-Two — The Measure of Judgment

The Hunter walked toward the altar.

His footsteps echoed across stone worn smooth by centuries.

He stopped beside the ancient book.

Do you know why kingdoms fall?

Aidan thought for a moment.

War.

Sometimes.

Greed.

Often.

Power.

Occasionally.

The Hunter looked directly into Aidan's crimson-and-gold eyes.

Certainty.

Aidan frowned.

Certainty?

The Hunter nodded.

The moment a ruler believes he can no longer be wrong...

History begins preparing his replacement.

The ruined abbey fell silent.

The Hunter placed one hand upon the ancient book.

This place once trained judges.

Not kings.

Not warriors.

Judges.

People entrusted with deciding when power should...

And should not...

Be used.

Aidan slowly approached the altar.

Why bring me here?

Because the Law accepted you.

Now we must discover whether your judgment deserves the Law.

He stepped aside.

Open the book.

Aidan reached toward the crimson cover.

The moment his fingertips touched the leather...

The abbey disappeared.

## Chapter One Hundred Forty-Three — The Trial of One Life

Aidan stood in the center of a burning village.

Smoke filled the air.

Homes collapsed around him.

People screamed for help.

A child cried from beneath a fallen beam.

Across the village...

A tower burned fiercely.

From within it...

Dozens of people pounded desperately against locked doors.

One voice echoed through the smoke.

Choose.

Aidan turned.

The Hunter stood untouched by the flames.

If you save the child...

The people inside the tower die.

If you save the tower...

The child dies.

No third option exists.

Aidan's heart pounded.

There has to be another way.

The Hunter remained expressionless.

There isn't.

Judgment does not wait for perfect choices.

It waits for your choice.

The cries grew louder.

The flames spread.

Time was running out.

Aidan closed his eyes.

The Law of Protection stirred within his soul.

Not telling him what to do.

Simply reminding him...

That every choice carried a burden.

He opened his eyes.

Instead of running...

He looked upward.

The bell tower above the village had already begun collapsing.

Its massive bronze bell hung by a single cracked chain.

The Hunter noticed his gaze.

Interesting...

Aidan sprinted.

Not toward the child.

Not toward the tower.

Toward the bell.

He climbed the burning staircase two steps at a time.

Heat scorched his hands.

Smoke filled his lungs.

With everything he had...

He drove his shoulder into the support beam.

The chain snapped.

The enormous bell crashed through the burning building.

Its weight shattered the locked doors of the tower.

The falling stone blocked the spreading flames long enough for people to escape.

The collapsing debris also crushed the beam trapping the child.

The little girl crawled free moments before the roof gave way.

Silence.

The village dissolved into crimson mist.

The Hunter slowly smiled.

You refused the question.

Aidan steadied his breathing.

No.

I refused to believe those were the only choices.

For several long moments...

The Hunter simply watched him.

Then he nodded.

Good.

Very good.

The greatest judges question impossible choices...

Before accepting them.

## Chapter One Hundred Forty-Five — The Name Never Spoken

The Hunter lifted the ancient book from the altar.

For the first time...

He looked almost relieved.

You passed the First Trial.

First?

There are others.

Of course there are.

He gently returned the book to its resting place.

Aidan looked around the ruined abbey.

Who built this place?

The Hunter's expression became unreadable.

A man...

Whose name should never have been forgotten.

Yet...

Whose greatest wish...

Was that it would be.

Aidan's heart quickened.

The Founder?

The Hunter remained silent.

Silence itself became the answer.

Outside...

Lightning split the night sky.

For a single heartbeat...

The broken stained-glass windows illuminated an ancient mural hidden beneath centuries of dust.

A crowned figure stood beside three towering symbols.

A circle of runes.

A crimson moon.

A great wolf.

The figure's face...

Had been deliberately erased.

Only one inscription beneath the mural remained untouched by time.

**A house may begin with one Founder.**

**A civilization begins with one worthy successor.**

Before Aidan could read another word...

Darkness reclaimed the abbey.

When the lightning faded...

The mural had disappeared beneath shadow once more.

Some truths...

History revealed only a heartbeat at a time.
