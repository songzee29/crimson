# Chapter Fifty: The Horn in the South

The horn never sounded a second time.

One blast.

Then silence.

Somehow...

That frightened Aidan more.

He and Lyra reached the southern gate before the guards finished opening it.

Rowan, Cassian, Malik, Elias, and Helena were already there.

No one asked whether Aidan intended to come.

That question had disappeared years ago.

The messenger waiting beyond the gate struggled to remain standing.

His clothes were soaked.

Not from rain.

From river water.

"They took..."

He fought for breath.

"...the ferry."

"The southern crossing?" Rowan asked.

The man nodded weakly.

"No one can cross."

"How many?"

"I don't know."

"Were they attacked?"

"I..."

The ferryman looked lost.

"I never saw anyone."

The words settled over the group.

Again.

No witnesses.

No certainty.

Only consequences.

...

The southern ferry crossed the widest section of the Black River.

Without it...

Five villages were effectively cut off from Blackwood.

Food.

Medicine.

Healers.

Everything stopped.

Aidan looked toward the map Elias quickly unfolded.

"If they wanted to destroy it..."

"They would have burned it," Cassian finished.

"They want us delayed," Rowan said.

"They always do."

...

The journey south took most of the morning.

No voices carried through the trees.

No birds.

Only the steady rhythm of boots against damp earth.

Halfway there...

Aidan stopped.

His head turned toward the ridgeline.

"What?"

Malik asked.

He listened.

Very faint.

Wood creaking.

Then...

Splashing.

Not one splash.

Several.

"They're already on the river."

No one else heard it.

They increased their pace.

...

The ferry remained tied to its dock.

Untouched.

No signs of battle.

The rope hadn't even been cut.

The ferryman's hut stood open.

Breakfast still rested on the table.

Cold.

Hours old.

Helena slowly looked around.

"No blood."

Cassian pushed open another door.

"Nothing."

Aidan stepped onto the ferry itself.

The wooden deck groaned beneath his weight.

His eyes drifted toward the far bank.

Then farther.

Mist rolled across the river.

Beyond it...

He thought he saw movement.

A shadow.

Gone before he focused.

He frowned.

Too far.

Or perhaps...

Never there.

His hearing reached across the water.

The river swallowed most sounds.

Then...

A child cried.

Once.

Very faint.

Across the river.

Lyra immediately looked toward him.

"You heard it."

He nodded.

"Yes."

She listened.

"I didn't."

The cry came again.

Then...

Stopped.

No splash.

No footsteps.

Nothing.

Aidan slowly backed away from the ferry.

"We're leaving."

Cassian trusted him immediately.

No one stepped aboard.

Minutes later...

The ferry drifted away from the dock on its own.

No wind.

No current strong enough to break the mooring.

The rope...

Had been cut from beneath the water.

The empty vessel floated into the center of the river.

Then exploded upward as hidden supports beneath it collapsed.

The river swallowed the wreckage.

No one spoke.

Had they boarded...

No one would have reached the opposite shore alive.

...

On the far bank...

Something moved behind the mist.

Only once.

Then vanished.

Aidan stared until his eyes hurt.

Nothing returned.

# Chapter Fifty-One: The Burden That Waited

Blackwood had barely returned from the river when another rider arrived.

His horse stumbled through the gate, foam covering its neck.

The rider slid to the ground before anyone reached him.

"They're sick."

"Who?" Helena asked.

"The children..."

He coughed violently.

"...Stonebrook."

Helena's expression changed immediately.

"Sick how?"

"I don't know."

"They just..."

He swallowed hard.

"...stopped waking up."

No hunters.

No claws.

No ambush.

Only another village needing help.

Again.

Aidan looked toward Helena.

"I'll go."

She shook her head immediately.

"No."

"They need you."

"So do you."

His side throbbed beneath fresh bandages.

His shoulder had stiffened after the reservoir.

His ribs still shifted painfully whenever he breathed too deeply.

He ignored all of it.

"I can still move."

Helena looked into his tired eyes.

"I know."

That answer hurt more than refusing him would have.

...

Stonebrook lay east of Blackwood.

The children still breathed.

Their hearts remained steady.

They simply...

Wouldn't wake.

Helena moved from bed to bed.

Nothing explained it.

No fever.

No poison she recognized.

No visible wounds.

Parents stood helplessly beside sleeping sons and daughters.

Aidan quietly walked through the village.

Listening.

The silence felt wrong.

Not because no one spoke.

Because every sleeping child breathed in almost the same rhythm.

He stopped outside the final house.

His head tilted.

One heartbeat.

Different.

He entered.

A little boy lay asleep beside the window.

Unlike the others...

His breathing wasn't steady.

It was slowing.

"Aidan?"

Helena looked up.

"What is it?"

He knelt beside the bed.

"I don't know."

His hand hovered over the child's wrist.

Not touching.

Listening.

The second heartbeat answered beneath his own.

For one impossible moment...

He thought he heard another heartbeat trying to fade away.

His instincts screamed.

"Helena."

She was already beside him.

"What?"

"He needs you."

She didn't question why.

She immediately began weaving healing magic around the boy.

His breathing steadied.

Color slowly returned to his face.

The room released a collective breath.

Helena looked at Aidan.

"You knew."

He looked at the sleeping child.

"No."

He spoke honestly.

"I only knew..."

His voice grew quieter.

"...he didn't have much time."

Outside...

Parents continued waiting beside children who still refused to wake.

Inside...

Another burden settled onto Aidan's shoulders.

He had helped save one.

Too many still remained.

And before the sun could set...

A scout rode through Stonebrook carrying another sealed message from Blackwood.

Another patrol had gone missing.

The nightmare had already moved somewhere else.
