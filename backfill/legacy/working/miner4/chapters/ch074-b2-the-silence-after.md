# Chapter Seventy-Four: The Silence After

No one moved.

The mountain pass had become impossibly quiet.

Wind drifted through broken stone.

Dust settled across crimson-stained rock.

Aidan's hand remained where it had fallen.

Still.

Helena kept her trembling hands against his chest long after his final breath had faded.

Healing magic continued pouring from her palms.

Nothing answered.

Nothing changed.

At last...

The light around her hands vanished.

She lowered her head.

"I..."

Her voice broke.

"...I can't."

No one blamed her.

No one spoke.

...

Lyra knelt beside Aidan.

She reached for his face with shaking fingers.

His skin had already begun losing its warmth.

"You promised..."

The words escaped before she realized she had spoken them.

"You always came back."

No answer came.

Only silence.

...

Malik turned away.

His damaged leg finally gave out beneath him.

He caught himself against a broken boulder and remained there, unable to look toward the center of the pass.

Elias quietly removed Aidan's shattered staff from beneath fallen stone.

The polished crimson wood had split nearly in half.

He held it carefully.

As though it might still matter.

...

Rowan stood with both hands resting on his own staff.

Years of lessons.

Years of impossible expectations.

Years spent forging a boy into the Crimson Witch.

All of it had ended beneath a mountain.

His shoulders seemed older than they had that morning.

"He saved them."

The words were barely above a whisper.

No one disagreed.

Around them...

The rescued families slowly lowered their heads.

One by one.

Without instruction.

Without ceremony.

Simple gratitude mixed with unbearable grief.

A little girl stepped forward.

The same wildflower Aidan had accepted earlier still rested in her hands.

She quietly laid it beside him.

Then stepped back into her mother's embrace.

...

Cassian had not moved.

He still held Aidan's hand.

He waited.

For another breath.

Another heartbeat.

Anything.

Nothing came.

His grip tightened.

"I'm sorry."

No one knew whether he spoke to Aidan...

Or to himself.

...

The journey back to Blackwood began at sunset.

There were no victory banners.

No celebration.

Only a wagon moving slowly beneath a darkening sky.

No one rode beside it.

Everyone walked.

Students who met them along the road understood before anyone spoke.

The silence told them everything.

Some wept openly.

Others simply stood aside as the procession passed.

The academy gates opened.

No bell rang.

No order was given.

Blackwood did not know how to announce the loss of the one who had answered every alarm.

# Chapter Seventy-Five: The Impossible Choice

Night covered Blackwood Academy.

The Great Hall had become a place of quiet mourning.

Candles burned along every wall.

No one spoke above a whisper.

Aidan rested at the center of the hall beneath the banner of Blackwood.

His broken staff lay across folded hands.

The crimson cloak clasp rested beside him.

Students came one by one.

Some bowed.

Some cried.

Some simply stood in silence before leaving.

...

Long after midnight...

Only the inner circle remained.

Helena sat nearby, exhausted beyond tears.

Lyra refused to leave.

Malik leaned heavily on the wall, staring at the floor.

Elias still held the cracked journals he had hoped Aidan would one day finish reading.

Rowan stood watch.

Cassian remained closest.

He had not left Aidan's side.

Not once.

...

Outside...

The academy wards flickered.

Only for an instant.

Then again.

Several instructors immediately looked toward the windows.

"Did you feel that?"

One quietly nodded.

"The wards shifted."

No alarm followed.

The lights steadied.

The academy settled once more.

Inside the Great Hall...

Nothing appeared to change.

...

Rowan's eyes slowly lifted toward the ancient crimson crest hanging above the hall.

His expression hardened.

"Everyone outside."

Lyra looked up sharply.

"What?"

"Now."

Helena frowned.

"Rowan—"

"Outside."

There was something in his voice none of them had heard before.

Not fear.

Recognition.

Reluctantly...

They obeyed.

Cassian stood last.

He looked down at Aidan one final time before stepping backward toward the doors.

The hall emptied.

Heavy wooden doors closed.

Silence returned.

Outside...

No one spoke.

The air itself felt different.

Still.

Waiting.

Cassian stood motionless before the closed doors.

His hand rested unconsciously against the hilt at his side.

Not drawn.

Not ready.

Simply there.

Inside the hall...

Beyond the ancient doors...

The candles flickered.

Once.

Then all at once...

Every flame bent toward the same point in the darkness.

And the night held its breath.
