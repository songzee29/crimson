# Chapter Fifty-Eight: The Weight of Returning

The walk back to Blackwood was slower than the journey out.

Not because the path had changed.

Because Aidan had.

Every few hundred steps he leaned a little harder on Rowan's staff.

He tried to hide it.

No one pretended not to notice.

Malik quietly shortened the pace.

Cassian walked close enough to catch him if he stumbled.

Lyra never drifted more than a few feet away.

No one mentioned why.

...

The academy gates appeared through the fading fog.

Before anyone could breathe with relief—

The eastern warning rune flashed once.

Then the western.

Then the northern.

Not an alarm.

Three separate reports arriving at once.

Aidan closed his eyes.

"Again..."

A messenger reached Rowan first.

"The northern bridge held."

A second interrupted him.

"The western grain convoy is overdue."

A third stumbled into the courtyard.

"The eastern watchfire never lit."

Three problems.

None could wait.

Elias spread another map across the nearest table.

"If we divide again—"

"They'll expect it," Cassian finished.

"If we don't divide," Malik said quietly, "someone goes without help."

There was no right answer.

Only different losses.

...

Aidan's hearing drifted across the courtyard.

Students.

Rainwater dripping from the roofs.

A frightened horse.

Then—

Someone crying.

A little girl.

Near the infirmary.

He turned before realizing he had moved.

A young apprentice sat beside her injured brother.

The boy's arm hung awkwardly inside a splint.

"I'm sorry," she whispered to him.

"I shouldn't have asked you to climb the wall."

The boy forced a smile.

"It isn't your fault."

Aidan stood watching for a moment.

Neither child noticed him.

His chest tightened.

Not from broken ribs.

From memory.

Every apology sounded the same now.

...

He continued toward the strategy hall.

Helena intercepted him halfway.

"You're bleeding again."

He looked down.

The bandages around his side had turned dark.

"I didn't feel it."

"I know."

She guided him toward the infirmary.

He resisted for exactly three steps.

Then stopped.

Not because he agreed.

Because he suddenly felt tired enough to fall where he stood.

...

Helena cut away the soaked bandages.

The room fell silent.

The wound hadn't merely reopened.

The stitches had torn completely.

She looked at him for a long time before speaking.

"You've stopped noticing injuries."

"I notice."

"No."

She gently cleaned dried blood from his side.

"You notice when other people are hurt."

Her voice became quieter.

"Your own body has become background noise."

He had no answer.

Because she was right.

...

That evening Rowan made a decision no one expected.

"Aidan stays inside the academy tomorrow."

The room immediately objected.

"Aidan—"

"I'll go," he interrupted.

"No."

Rowan's voice carried the same calm certainty it always had.

"This is not punishment."

"It feels like it."

"It is necessity."

Silence.

"You cannot protect anyone if you die walking to the gate."

Aidan looked away.

His hands slowly tightened.

He understood.

He hated it.

Because somewhere...

Someone needed help.

And he wouldn't be there.

# Chapter Fifty-Nine: The Burden of Staying

Morning arrived without Aidan leaving Blackwood.

It felt wrong.

Every instinct urged him toward the gates.

Toward another patrol.

Another village.

Another burden.

Instead...

He stood inside the eastern training grounds helping reinforce defensive barriers with the apprentices.

His movements were slower than before.

His breathing shallower.

Yet his instructions remained steady.

"Again."

The students repeated the formation.

"No."

He quietly adjusted one apprentice's stance.

"Anchor with your whole body."

She tried again.

The barrier held.

Better.

A faint smile crossed her face.

"You remembered."

"You taught us."

The words lingered.

He wondered how many lessons he had forgotten teaching.

...

By midday...

The first rescue team returned.

Not everyone came back.

A wagon rolled through the gates carrying two wounded guards.

Another arrived carrying one covered body.

The courtyard became silent.

Aidan's hands stopped moving.

He had obeyed Rowan.

He had stayed.

Someone had died anyway.

The thought settled heavily inside him.

Would he have changed the outcome?

He didn't know.

The uncertainty hurt more than certainty ever could.

...

Cassian found him alone beneath the old oak.

"You heard the wagons."

Aidan nodded.

"I counted them."

Cassian wasn't surprised.

"You couldn't have been everywhere."

"I know."

"You keep saying that."

"Because it's true."

Cassian looked toward the fresh grave already being prepared.

"And you still blame yourself."

Neither denied it.

Neither could.

...

As evening settled over Blackwood...

Aidan remained beneath the branches of the old oak.

The academy slowly quieted.

His hearing stretched beyond the walls.

Toward distant villages.

Toward empty forests.

Toward places he couldn't reach.

Then...

One heartbeat.

Very faint.

Far away.

It lasted only an instant before disappearing.

He opened his eyes.

The second heartbeat answered beneath his own.

Steady.

Patient.

Waiting.

He didn't follow.

He couldn't.

Not tonight.

For the first time in years...

The burden wasn't another battle.

It was remaining where he was while not knowing what might be happening somewhere else.

And somehow...

That hurt even more than the wounds still stitched across his body.
