# Chapter Eighteen: The Weight of Mercy

The western wall remained broken.

Blackwood Academy no longer attempted to hide the damage.

Stone masons worked without pause.

Students carried supplies through the night.

Every repair circle was surrounded by armed guards.

The academy had accepted a new truth.

The enemy was beneath them.

Watching.

Waiting.

Learning.

...

Lyra refused to remain in the infirmary.

"The wound isn't deep enough to stop me."

Professor Helena folded her arms.

"It is if I say it is."

Lyra smiled faintly.

"Then I'll heal while walking."

Helena sighed.

"You spend too much time around Aidan."

Across the room, Aidan quietly tightened the fresh bindings around his ribs.

Every movement still hurt.

The reopened claw wounds across his back had finally begun closing.

His shoulder remained stiff.

His face had grown noticeably thinner from weeks of blood loss and interrupted sleep.

Helena looked at him next.

"And you..."

"I'm staying."

"I hadn't even spoken."

"You were going to."

"I was."

The professor shook her head.

"I've never treated so many stubborn people."

...

By midday, Rowan called another council.

This time...

No maps covered the table.

Only names.

Every patrol.

Every instructor.

Every student assigned to the walls.

"They're studying our people now," Rowan said quietly.

"They've learned our defenses."

"They've learned our formations."

"They've learned our priorities."

Cassian finished the thought.

"The next thing they'll learn..."

"...is who matters most to Aidan."

Silence settled over the room.

Nobody dismissed the possibility.

Aidan looked down at the list of names.

His eyes stopped on Lyra.

Then Malik.

Then Elias.

His family.

His weakness.

The realization settled heavily in his chest.

"They're going to make me choose."

No one argued.

Because they all knew he was right.

...

That evening Aidan abandoned the patrol route.

Not because he was careless.

Because he had noticed something.

Every attack happened after they became predictable.

So he became unpredictable.

No route.

No destination.

Only instinct.

His footsteps drifted naturally through the ruined outskirts of the academy.

Silent.

Even he barely heard himself moving.

He paused.

The wind shifted.

Smoke.

Wet earth.

Old stone.

Then—

Blood.

His head turned immediately.

Not toward the academy.

Toward the forest.

His stomach tightened.

Fresh.

Close.

His body had already started walking before he realized what he was doing.

"Aidan."

Cassian's voice stopped him.

He looked back.

Cassian stood several yards away.

"I've been following you."

"Why?"

"Because you changed direction three times without looking."

Cassian studied him carefully.

"You weren't tracking footprints."

"No."

"What were you following?"

Aidan opened his mouth.

Nothing came.

He didn't know.

"I..."

The answer refused to form.

Cassian said nothing.

But his concern deepened.

...

The scream came from the western woods.

Both men ran.

Not toward a battlefield.

Toward a single apprentice trapped beneath a fallen tree.

She wasn't alone.

One of the pale hunters stood over her.

It didn't attack.

It watched.

Waiting.

The apprentice struggled to free herself.

The creature looked directly at Aidan.

Then...

It deliberately raised one claw.

Slowly.

It could have killed her instantly.

Instead...

It waited.

Testing him.

Aidan understood immediately.

It wanted to know what he would save first.

The creature.

Or the girl.

He never hesitated.

"Still."

The Law froze the collapsing tree instead of the enemy.

Cassian understood instantly.

He lunged for the creature while Aidan tore the apprentice free.

The hunter smiled.

Actually smiled.

Then retreated before Cassian's blade reached it.

Gone.

Again.

It had never intended to fight.

It had only wanted the answer.

"They know now," Cassian said quietly.

Aidan looked toward the empty forest.

"They already did."

# Chapter Nineteen: The Hunter's Answer

Rain fell before dawn.

Not enough to wash away blood.

Only enough to blur tracks.

Blackwood's western forest had become eerily quiet.

Too quiet.

Aidan stood at the repaired section of wall.

Something felt wrong.

Not because he saw anything.

Because he heard...

Nothing.

No birds.

No insects.

No small animals moving through wet leaves.

The forest had become silent.

His heartbeat slowed.

The second heartbeat answered.

THUMP.

His eyes narrowed toward the trees.

"They're here."

Malik looked around.

"I don't see—"

The attack came from behind.

Not from the forest.

From beneath the wall.

Three pale hunters erupted through hidden tunnels inside the academy grounds.

Not one charged Aidan.

Instead...

Each sprinted toward a different group of students.

"They changed tactics!" Rowan shouted.

"They're splitting us!"

Aidan moved.

Threat Assessment unfolded almost instinctively.

Three attacks.

One group of first-years.

One wounded instructor.

One collapsed section of wall ready to fall.

He couldn't save all three alone.

For the first time...

He froze.

One heartbeat.

One terrible heartbeat.

The creatures had learned.

They had created too many choices.

"Aidan!"

Lyra's voice cut through the chaos.

"Trust us!"

She didn't wait for his answer.

She and Helena turned toward the students.

Malik intercepted the wounded instructor.

Cassian vanished toward the nearest hunter despite the pain in his damaged arm.

They weren't waiting for Aidan anymore.

They had adapted too.

Realizing that...

Freed him.

His eyes shifted upward.

The wall.

If it collapsed...

Everyone died.

He raised both hands.

"Hold!"

The fractured battlements froze once again.

Every cracked stone hung suspended.

Pain ripped through his chest.

Blood seeped through the bindings around his ribs.

His knees buckled.

Still...

He held.

Below him, his friends fought without hesitation.

Not because they expected him to save everyone.

Because they trusted each other.

One hunter looked up at Aidan.

Then...

For the first time...

It retreated before the battle was over.

Not defeated.

Satisfied.

It had learned something else.

The Crimson Witch would always sacrifice his own body before allowing another wall to fall.

The academy survived.

Again.

But when the dust settled...

Aidan collapsed to one knee, coughing blood onto broken stone.

The metallic scent filled the air.

His eyes locked onto the crimson droplets before they struck the ground.

The hunger surged harder than ever.

His throat tightened.

His body leaned forward.

Just slightly.

Then...

Lyra's hand rested on his shoulder.

"I'm here."

The words reached him before the scent did.

He shut his eyes.

The hunger receded.

Not gone.

Only waiting.

Far beneath Blackwood Academy...

The hunters gathered once more.

Their leader slowly carved new marks into the stone.

Not random scratches.

A record.

One symbol beneath another.

Each encounter.

Each lesson.

Each weakness.

Each adaptation.

The hunt was no longer about killing the Crimson Witch.

It was becoming an education.

For both sides.
