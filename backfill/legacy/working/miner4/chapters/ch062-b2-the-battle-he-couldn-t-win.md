# Chapter Sixty-Two: The Battle He Couldn't Win

The western alarm bell was still ringing when Aidan reached Blackwood's outer gate.

His side had already soaked through another set of bandages.

He ignored it.

The road west was crowded with terrified villagers.

Children cried.

Parents carried whatever they could save.

Nobody looked back.

"Aidan!"

An old woman grabbed his sleeve as he passed.

"They're still trapped!"

"Where?"

She pointed toward the hills.

"Our people never made it out."

He looked toward Rowan.

The old master had already begun directing evacuations.

"Get everyone inside the walls."

Cassian and Malik immediately took charge.

Lyra helped the elderly and wounded.

Helena transformed the entrance hall into another emergency infirmary.

Everyone had a task.

Aidan had one too.

The hills.

...

The climb became steeper with every step.

His breathing grew uneven.

His shoulder throbbed.

Every stride sent fresh pain through fractured ribs.

He kept climbing.

Halfway up...

He stopped.

The forest was wrong.

No wind.

No birds.

No insects.

Only silence.

Then...

A scream.

A child.

Without thinking...

He ran.

Branches tore across his face.

Loose stones rolled beneath his boots.

He burst into a narrow ravine.

Empty.

No child.

No footprints.

Nothing.

His instincts screamed.

Too late.

The hillside exploded.

Stone and fallen trees crashed down from above.

Aidan threw both hands upward.

"Hold!"

Crimson light slowed the avalanche.

Not stopped.

Slowed.

The weight was too great.

The mountain itself pushed against him.

Blood streamed from his nose.

His vision blurred.

One boulder struck his left shoulder.

Another slammed into his ribs.

His barrier shattered.

The remaining stones buried him beneath a wave of earth.

...

Cassian heard the collapse from below.

"Aidan!"

Without hesitation he sprinted toward the ravine.

Rowan grabbed his arm.

"No!"

"He's alive."

"How do you know?"

Cassian looked toward the mountain.

"Because he'd still be fighting."

No one argued.

They climbed.

...

Beneath the rubble...

Aidan forced one arm free.

Every breath felt smaller than the last.

His hearing barely reached beyond the stone surrounding him.

Then...

Voices.

Not close.

His friends.

Digging again.

Always digging.

He almost laughed.

Instead...

He coughed blood.

His hand found his staff.

He drove its end into the packed earth above him.

Once.

Twice.

A third time.

A faint knock echoed upward.

Cassian stopped instantly.

"There!"

Every shovel turned.

Every spell shifted.

Every ounce of strength focused toward that single sound.

...

When they finally pulled him free...

He couldn't stand.

His left shoulder hung unnaturally low.

Helena looked once before speaking.

"It's dislocated."

She reset it immediately.

The crack echoed through the ravine.

Aidan didn't cry out.

He simply closed his eyes until the pain settled enough to breathe again.

"You need rest," Helena whispered.

He looked past her toward the frightened villagers.

"They need homes."

Helena lowered her head.

She no longer knew how to argue with someone who had forgotten how to choose himself.

# Chapter Sixty-Three: The Cost of Another Tomorrow

Blackwood's walls filled beyond capacity.

The refugees from the western hills slept wherever space remained.

Classrooms.

Hallways.

Training yards.

Even the Great Hall became a shelter.

Students gave up their beds without complaint.

No one believed the displacement would last only a few days.

...

Aidan spent the night carrying supplies.

Not because he had recovered.

Because there weren't enough hands.

His shoulder remained wrapped tightly against his chest.

His side bled whenever he lifted anything heavier than a sack of grain.

He carried it anyway.

Malik finally stepped in front of him.

"Enough."

"I'm almost done."

"You've said that since yesterday."

"There are still people waiting."

"There always will be."

The words struck harder than Malik intended.

Because they were true.

There would always be another person waiting.

Another village.

Another burden.

Another reason to keep walking.

...

Near dawn...

Elias entered the strategy hall carrying a bundle of weathered patrol journals recovered from an abandoned outpost.

"I thought these might help."

Aidan reached toward the first journal.

Before he could open it—

The southern warning rune ignited.

Then the eastern.

Then the western.

Three directions.

Three separate emergencies.

Again.

Aidan slowly withdrew his hand from the unopened journal.

He didn't even sigh anymore.

He simply stood.

Cassian watched him quietly.

"You were trying to find answers."

"I know."

"You never get the chance."

"I know."

The journal remained unopened on the table.

Another truth postponed.

Another mystery left waiting.

Because somewhere outside Blackwood...

Someone needed saving first.

...

As the rescue parties assembled...

Lyra stepped beside Aidan.

She looked carefully at the fresh blood spreading through the bandages around his side.

"You haven't healed from the mountain."

"No."

"You haven't healed from Red Creek."

"No."

"You haven't healed from the reservoir."

"No."

She swallowed.

"You haven't healed in years."

For the first time...

Aidan didn't answer immediately.

His tired eyes drifted toward the academy gates already opening for another mission.

Finally...

He spoke.

"I don't think I've had time."

Lyra looked away before he could see the tears forming in her eyes.

She remembered the boy who had laughed through Rowan's impossible lessons.

Who complained about long training sessions.

Who argued with Malik over the last piece of bread.

Who smiled without effort.

Standing beside her now...

He was still Aidan.

Still kind.

Still willing to give everything for someone else.

But every rescue took something that never came back.

The gates opened fully.

Another messenger stumbled through.

Another desperate plea.

Another impossible choice.

Aidan adjusted the sling supporting his injured shoulder.

Tightened the bandages around his ribs.

Ignored the blood staining his side.

Then walked toward the next battle before the last one had even finished costing him.
