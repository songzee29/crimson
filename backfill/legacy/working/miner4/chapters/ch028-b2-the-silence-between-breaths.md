# Chapter Twenty-Eight: The Silence Between Breaths

Blackwood Academy stopped reacting.

It began anticipating.

The old patrol bells were abandoned.

Instead, Rowan introduced silent signal runes hidden throughout the academy. A flash of blue beneath a window. A crimson ember along a wall. A pulse through the wards themselves.

No shouted orders.

No warning the hunters could overhear.

"They've been listening to us," Rowan told the instructors. "From now on... we listen better than they do."

The academy adapted.

Again.

...

Aidan stood among the instructors.

He spoke little.

Not because he had nothing to say.

Because he had begun weighing every word before speaking it.

He hated himself for it.

The hunter's voice still echoed inside his thoughts.

*You have your traitor to thank for this.*

No.

He refused to believe it.

Yet...

He also stopped announcing every instinct the moment he felt it.

Not because he distrusted anyone.

Because if someone truly was watching Blackwood...

Less information meant fewer opportunities.

It was caution.

He hoped.

...

Cassian noticed first.

"You stopped finishing your thoughts."

They walked the northern wall together.

"I have?"

"You tell us where to go."

Cassian looked ahead.

"But not why."

Aidan didn't answer immediately.

"I've been wrong before."

"No."

Cassian stopped walking.

"You've changed."

The words weren't accusing.

They hurt anyway.

"I know."

"You don't laugh anymore."

Silence.

"You don't argue with Malik."

Silence.

"You don't complain about Rowan's training."

A faint smile almost appeared.

Almost.

"I still complain."

"Only in your head."

Cassian watched him carefully.

"And you keep looking toward the trees every few minutes."

Aidan hadn't realized he was doing it.

...

That afternoon Helena found him studying reports from every recent attack.

She quietly sat beside him.

"You've read those three times."

"I'm looking for something."

"What?"

"I don't know yet."

She waited.

Then softly asked,

"When was the last time you slept through the night?"

He couldn't remember.

That answer alone worried her.

"You aren't healing."

"I'm alive."

"Those aren't the same thing."

Her hand rested gently on one of the reports.

"I've treated witches for thirty years."

She looked directly into his eyes.

"I've never seen someone survive so much while becoming more exhausted."

He looked away.

Neither had he.

...

That evening the mountain became quiet.

Too quiet.

Aidan stood beneath the old oak.

He listened.

Nothing.

No scraping stone.

No distant heartbeats.

No movement.

The silence itself felt wrong.

His breathing slowed.

His eyes narrowed.

"They're gone."

Rowan stepped beside him.

"Gone?"

"They left the tunnels."

The old master trusted the statement immediately.

Within the hour...

Search teams confirmed it.

The deepest passages beneath Blackwood stood empty.

No fresh tracks.

No sounds.

No hunters.

Malik smiled for the first time in days.

"Maybe we finally scared them off."

Aidan looked toward the dark forest.

"No."

His voice remained calm.

"They're changing the battlefield."

# Chapter Twenty-Nine: The First Outside Attack

The answer came the following morning.

Not at Blackwood.

Three miles away.

Smoke rose above the village of Grey Hollow.

One of the settlements supplying the academy.

A messenger arrived on horseback.

His horse collapsed before reaching the gate.

"They're burning everything..."

He struggled to breathe.

"They never came near the academy..."

"They waited for us..."

"...then attacked the village..."

The room fell silent.

The hunters had evolved again.

They had stopped attacking where Blackwood was strongest.

They were attacking what Blackwood couldn't protect.

...

Within minutes Rowan assembled a rescue force.

Not everyone.

Half remained behind.

"They want us divided," Rowan said.

"We won't give them the whole academy."

Aidan looked at the map.

Grey Hollow.

Narrow roads.

Dense forest.

Limited visibility.

Perfect ground for an ambush.

"We move fast."

"No."

Rowan shook his head.

"We move carefully."

The lesson had finally been learned.

...

Grey Hollow was already burning when they arrived.

Homes collapsed.

Families fled toward the river.

The hunters weren't killing indiscriminately.

They were driving people.

Herding them.

Exactly as they had done with Blackwood's students.

"They're controlling movement," Elias realized.

"They're deciding where everyone runs."

Aidan's hearing caught something no one else noticed.

Not screams.

Silence.

One street.

Completely empty.

He turned immediately.

"Not there."

Malik looked toward the deserted lane.

"Why?"

"Because they want us to."

The words had barely left his mouth when the empty street collapsed into a hidden pit lined with sharpened stakes.

The Trapper.

It had adapted.

The hunters no longer relied on claws alone.

They reshaped battlefields before the fight even began.

"They're forcing us to waste time," Cassian said.

"They're forcing us to doubt every step," Aidan corrected.

The fire spread.

Children cried from another building.

Aidan ran.

Not recklessly.

Purposefully.

His body slipped through collapsing beams with almost unnatural precision.

He reached a frightened little girl trapped beneath a burning support.

He lifted the beam despite the protest from his healing ribs.

Pain flared white across his vision.

Still...

He carried her outside.

Behind him...

The house collapsed.

His breathing became ragged.

He smelled smoke.

Ash.

Burning wood.

Then...

Blood.

The girl's scraped arm.

Just a little.

His eyes found it immediately.

He looked away before she noticed.

"Find Helena," he told her quietly.

She nodded and ran.

Across the square...

A pale figure stood upon the roof of a burning house.

It didn't attack.

Its black-marked arm calmly carved another line into the charred wood.

The Observer.

It watched Aidan rescue the child.

It watched him ignore his own injuries.

It watched where his eyes lingered for one heartbeat too long before he turned away.

Then it disappeared into the smoke.

The village would survive.

Barely.

But the hunters had learned another lesson.

If Blackwood would not leave its walls willingly...

Its heart could be drawn outside them.

And for the first time...

The battlefield was no longer the academy.

It was the entire kingdom around it.
