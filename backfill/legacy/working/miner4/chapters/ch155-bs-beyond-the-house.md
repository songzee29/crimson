## Chapter One Hundred Fifty-Five — Beyond the House

The eastern road lingered in Aidan's thoughts long after he returned to the estate.

For years...

House Darkheart had been his entire world.

Its halls.

Its hidden passages.

Its forgotten secrets.

Everything had pointed inward.

Now...

For the first time...

History pointed outward.

He stood on the eastern balcony overlooking the forests beyond the city.

The mountains rested against the horizon like silent giants.

Somewhere beyond them...

The Second Foundation waited.

Not calling.

Waiting.

There was a difference.

---

Breakfast was unusually quiet.

His aunt noticed him staring through the kitchen window.

You're leaving.

It wasn't a question.

Aidan looked up in surprise.

How did you know?

She smiled sadly.

The same way mothers know.

The same way fathers know.

The same way homes know.

One day...

The people they love stop looking at the walls...

And start looking beyond them.

She placed another cup of coffee beside him.

I don't know where you're going.

I won't ask.

But promise me something.

Aidan nodded.

Come home.

No matter how long it takes...

Come home.

His throat tightened.

I promise.

She reached across the table and squeezed his hand.

Then wherever you're going...

Go without regret.

---

Later that afternoon...

Malik found Aidan sitting beneath the old oak at the academy.

You look like someone planning something dangerous.

Aidan laughed.

Dangerous?

Maybe.

Important?

Definitely.

Malik sat beside him.

You know...

When we first met...

I figured you'd be one of those quiet guys who disappeared after graduation.

Aidan smiled.

And now?

Now I think you're the kind of person who disappears...

Then comes back with stories nobody believes.

The two laughed.

The moment faded into comfortable silence.

Then Malik became serious.

Whatever happens...

Don't try carrying everything alone.

Friends exist for a reason.

Aidan looked at him.

I'll remember that.

Neither of them realized...

Those words would one day save far more than a friendship.

---

That evening...

Lyra waited outside the academy art room.

She held a small leather sketchbook.

I wanted to give you something.

Aidan accepted it carefully.

Inside...

Every page contained a drawing.

The old oak.

The academy courtyard.

The bridge where the truck had nearly fallen.

Blackwood Library.

Even the sunrise viewed from the eastern hill outside the city.

You drew all of these?

Lyra nodded.

Places matter.

People forget that.

Years from now...

You'll look at them differently.

Aidan slowly turned another page.

The final sketch wasn't a place.

It was him.

Standing alone...

Looking toward distant mountains.

When did you draw this?

Yesterday.

She smiled.

I had a feeling...

You're about to go somewhere.

Aidan looked up.

Did I tell you that?

No.

She closed the sketchbook.

You didn't have to.

Artists notice when someone starts looking toward the horizon instead of the road beneath their feet.

For a long moment...

Neither spoke.

Finally...

Aidan thanked her.

Not just for the drawings.

For understanding something he hadn't yet said aloud.

---

Night settled over House Darkheart.

The Guardian stood before the great map of the Seven Foundations.

The eastern road now glowed with a steady crimson light.

Not bright enough to command attention.

Only bright enough to guide.

Aidan approached.

If I leave...

Can I come back?

The Guardian looked at him.

Every road changes the traveler.

No one returns exactly as they left.

He rested one hand upon the carved map.

But yes...

House Darkheart will always remain your beginning.

Never mistake your beginning...

For your destination.

Aidan nodded slowly.

He understood.

The House had raised him.

The Sanctuary had prepared him.

The Living Legacy had recorded him.

But none of them could walk the road for him.

---

Deep beneath the mountain...

The Second Foundation awakened a little more.

Ancient braziers lit themselves one by one.

Stone guardians opened eyes that had remained closed for centuries.

Roots withdrew from forgotten halls.

An enormous circular chamber slowly emerged from darkness.

At its center stood no throne.

No crown.

Only a single stone table.

Upon it rested an unopened scroll sealed with crimson wax.

The seal bore no family crest.

Only one word.

**Pilgrim.**

Somewhere within the silent halls...

Footsteps echoed.

Not Aidan's.

Someone else had already entered the mountain.

Someone who had been waiting long before the Heir began his journey.

The Second Foundation...

Was no longer empty.
