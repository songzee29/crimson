# Chapter Sixty: The Price of Remaining

The grave had not been filled before the next alarm sounded.

One long bell.

Then another.

Not from Blackwood.

From the southern ridge.

A signal fire.

Rowan looked toward the smoke.

"No."

His voice was almost weary.

"Already..."

No one asked if they were leaving.

Everyone simply moved.

Aidan reached for his coat.

Helena caught his wrist.

"You are not fit to fight."

"I know."

"You've reopened every major wound."

"I know."

"You can barely stand."

"I know."

He gently pulled free.

"They still lit the signal."

There was nothing Helena could say that outweighed those words.

...

The southern ridge had become a battlefield before Blackwood arrived.

Wagons lay overturned.

Trees had been split apart.

The survivors huddled beneath shattered carts while instructors from a nearby outpost fought desperately to keep them alive.

Aidan entered the fight already exhausted.

His first barrier held.

The second nearly failed.

By the third...

His vision blurred.

Something slammed into him from the side.

He never saw it coming.

His body twisted on instinct, sparing his chest.

The blow still hurled him through a stone wall.

The impact shattered what little healing his ribs had managed.

He hit the ground hard enough that breathing became impossible.

For several terrifying seconds...

He couldn't move.

Someone screamed his name.

Another impact.

Another collapse.

Cassian appeared over him, intercepting another attack before it reached Aidan.

His damaged arm folded beneath the force.

He stayed on his feet anyway.

"Move!"

Aidan forced air back into his lungs.

Pain flooded every nerve.

He stood.

Not because he had recovered.

Because other people still needed him standing.

The battle continued.

When it finally ended...

Three more defenders would never return to Blackwood.

The caravan survived.

The people survived.

The cost remained behind on the hillside.

...

As the wounded were loaded onto wagons, Malik quietly sat beside Aidan.

Neither spoke.

After a long silence, Malik finally asked,

"Do you remember when we used to think passing Rowan's exams was hard?"

Aidan looked toward the stretchers.

"I don't remember much before this."

Malik didn't laugh.

Neither of them could.

# Chapter Sixty-One: The Weight That Never Lifted

Blackwood Academy no longer measured time by weeks.

It measured it by casualties.

Another name joined the memorial wall.

Students walked past it in silence.

No one had room for fresh grief before another responsibility arrived.

...

Helena removed Aidan's blood-soaked bandages once again.

This time...

She stopped halfway through.

The room remained quiet.

Aidan looked up.

"What?"

She traced the scar tissue surrounding older wounds.

"There isn't enough healthy tissue left."

He frowned.

"What do you mean?"

"Every wound keeps reopening before the last one finishes healing."

She looked into his tired eyes.

"Your body is surviving."

Her voice broke almost imperceptibly.

"But it has stopped remembering what uninjured feels like."

The words lingered.

Neither of them spoke again.

...

That afternoon, Aidan finally sat down to study the strange charcoal map recovered days earlier.

He had barely unfolded it—

The western alarm bell rang.

Once.

Twice.

Then continuously.

Aidan closed the map without reading another line.

Another crisis.

Another choice already made.

He was halfway across the courtyard before anyone called after him.

"Aidan!"

He never slowed.

His side tore open before he reached the gate.

Warm blood spread beneath fresh bandages.

He didn't stop.

He didn't even look down.

By the time Rowan caught up beside him, Aidan's breathing had already become uneven.

"You should still be in the infirmary."

"So should half this academy."

Neither man said another word.

They ran together toward another emergency.

Behind them...

The unfinished map remained on the strategy table.

Its unanswered questions would have to wait.

Again.

Because every time Aidan reached toward the truth...

Someone else needed saving first.

And with every life he chose over himself...

Another piece of the Crimson Witch was left behind on the road.
