# Chapter Three  
## Hairline Cracks

Rain settled over Blackwood Academy before dawn.

Not a storm.

Just a slow, steady rain that washed soot from shattered stone and carried the scent of wet earth through the recovering grounds.

Students continued rebuilding despite it.

They no longer waited for perfect days.

None of them believed perfect days existed anymore.

Aidan stood beneath the covered training pavilion, one hand extended toward a cluster of floating stone fragments.

They hovered effortlessly around him.

Not a single piece wavered.

His breathing remained slow.

Measured.

"Again."

Rowan's voice echoed through the empty pavilion.

Aidan nodded.

His fingers shifted.

The floating stones spun into a perfect circle before separating into dozens of smaller pieces, each following a different path without touching another.

Every movement demanded impossible precision.

Every fragment obeyed.

Rowan watched quietly.

"You've stopped thinking before you cast."

"I don't need to anymore."

"No."

The old master's expression remained unreadable.

"You've become the kind of witch whose instincts are beginning to surpass conscious thought."

Aidan lowered his hand.

The stones settled gently onto the floor without a sound.

"Is that dangerous?"

"It can be."

Rowan walked toward him.

"Power becomes most dangerous the moment it feels natural."

Aidan absorbed the lesson without answering.

That had always been Rowan's way.

Never a lecture.

Only truth.

...

The afternoon passed peacefully.

Blackwood's infirmary finally stood nearly empty.

Damaged classrooms reopened one by one.

Students returned to lessons, though every lecture now carried a quiet seriousness that hadn't existed before the Witch Curse.

No one wasted time anymore.

No one assumed tomorrow was guaranteed.

Aidan found Lyra helping younger students redraw damaged runic circles across the library entrance.

She looked up as he approached.

"You've been impossible to find."

"I was training."

"You always say that."

"I usually am."

She laughed softly before handing him a brush dipped in shimmering crimson ink.

"Then help."

For the next hour they worked side by side without speaking much.

Simple work.

Careful work.

The kind that reminded Aidan why magic existed in the first place.

Not only to destroy.

To preserve.

A first-year student watched nervously from nearby.

"Master Aidan..."

He looked over.

"Yes?"

"How do you know if your spell is strong enough?"

Aidan studied the unfinished rune beneath his hand.

Then smiled.

"If you're asking how much power to use..."

He gently completed the final line.

"...use the least amount needed."

The rune ignited with flawless light.

No explosion.

No dramatic surge.

Only quiet perfection.

"The strongest spell isn't the loudest one."

The young witch stared at the glowing rune before smiling.

"I understand."

"I hope you do."

...

Evening settled across the academy.

Students gathered for dinner.

Stories replaced fearful whispers.

Plans replaced survival.

Hope slowly reclaimed the halls.

Malik challenged Elias to another rebuilding contest.

Cassian silently joined one side.

Lyra joined the other.

Before long half the hall had become involved.

Arguments erupted over whose wall stood straighter.

Professor Helena declared every one of them equally terrible builders.

Laughter filled the room.

Aidan watched from the end of the table.

A small smile tugged at the corner of his mouth.

His family.

His home.

The second heartbeat answered.

Thump.

His smile faded.

Without warning...

The rich scent of roasted meat drifting through the hall disappeared.

Something else reached him instead.

A faint copper scent.

Barely noticeable.

Someone at another table had nicked a finger while carving bread.

One crimson drop landed against white cloth.

Aidan's eyes found it instantly.

The world seemed to narrow.

The laughter around him grew distant.

The single drop of blood looked...

Bright.

Warm.

Alive.

His fingers tightened around the edge of the table.

A strange emptiness stirred deep inside him.

Not pain.

Not illness.

Something quieter.

Something that whispered without words.

Take another breath.

He did.

The scent became stronger.

"...Aidan?"

Lyra's voice broke through the silence inside his mind.

He blinked.

The hall returned.

Students laughed.

Malik complained loudly that Helena had intentionally judged his wall unfairly.

The drop of blood had already been wiped away.

"What happened?" Lyra asked.

He looked at her.

"I..."

For a moment he couldn't find the answer.

"I don't know."

She searched his face.

"You look tired."

He wanted to disagree.

Instead...

He realized she was right.

Not physically.

Something deeper.

As if part of him had remained awake while the rest of him slept.

"I'm alright."

She didn't seem entirely convinced.

...

That night sleep refused to come.

Aidan stood outside his dormitory overlooking the quiet academy.

Lanterns flickered beneath the rain.

Everything appeared peaceful.

His thoughts did not.

The second heartbeat remained.

Steady.

Patient.

Never matching his own.

He placed a hand over his chest.

"My heart..."

Beat.

"...or yours?"

No answer came.

Only silence.

Then—

**Crimson Law Blueprint**

**LAW: STILL**

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The blueprint dissolved into countless patterns before settling beyond his current understanding.

Aidan exhaled slowly.

Another lesson to earn.

Another step forward.

Yet even as he focused on the Law...

His eyes drifted toward the dark forest beyond Blackwood's walls.

He wasn't sure why.

Nothing moved there.

Nothing called to him.

Still...

For the briefest instant...

He found himself wondering what hunted beneath those trees after midnight.

The thought vanished as quickly as it had come.

Aidan frowned.

That wasn't like him.

He turned away from the forest and walked back inside, never noticing that his footsteps lingered for just a heartbeat at the doorway...

As though some unseen part of him had wanted to keep walking into the darkness.
