# Chapter Two  
## Echoes Beneath the Skin

Morning routines slowly returned to Blackwood Academy.

Not because life had become normal.

Because everyone had decided it had to continue.

The eastern training grounds rang with the sound of practice staffs striking enchanted targets. Stone masons repaired fractured towers while witches carefully rewove protective circles into the academy walls. Fresh runes shimmered across repaired archways, brighter than the old ones ever had been.

The academy was healing.

So were its people.

Aidan crossed the courtyard before sunrise, his hands tucked into the pockets of his black coat.

For the first time since arriving at Blackwood, no one stopped him with frightened questions.

No emergency demanded his attention.

No curse waited around the next corner.

Only the quiet rhythm of rebuilding.

"You were late."

Rowan stood in the center of the practice field with his hands folded behind his back.

Aidan glanced toward the rising sun.

"By thirty seconds."

"You noticed."

"I notice everything now."

A faint smile crossed Rowan's weathered face.

"Good."

He tossed a smooth wooden staff toward Aidan.

"Then prove it."

The staff spun through the air.

Aidan caught it without looking.

Rowan moved instantly.

The old master crossed the distance between them in a blur, striking from the left before shifting his momentum into a sweeping kick aimed at Aidan's legs.

The staff moved almost on instinct.

Wood collided with wood.

Aidan redirected the strike, stepped aside, and rested the tip of his weapon against Rowan's shoulder before the older witch could recover.

Silence settled over the field.

Then Rowan lowered his own staff.

"Again."

For nearly an hour they moved across the training grounds.

No magic.

Only discipline.

Every exchange became faster.

Cleaner.

More precise.

Eventually Rowan stepped back and lowered his weapon completely.

"Enough."

Aidan blinked.

"That's it?"

"You no longer need me to teach you how to fight."

The words landed harder than any blow.

Rowan looked across the academy.

"My job has changed."

"What is it now?"

"To remind you that strength isn't measured by how often you win."

Aidan lowered the staff.

"And if I forget?"

"You have enough people around you now to remind you."

For a moment neither of them spoke.

Then Rowan placed one hand on Aidan's shoulder.

"I'm proud of you."

The words were simple.

They carried more weight than any title Aidan had ever earned.

...

Later that afternoon the Great Hall buzzed with conversation.

Malik somehow convinced half the students to compete in rebuilding one of the collapsed gardens.

Elias immediately declared Malik's plan structurally impossible.

Ten minutes later both of them were covered in dirt after attempting it anyway.

Lyra laughed so hard she nearly dropped the basket she was carrying.

Cassian quietly picked it up before it touched the floor.

"Thank you."

He nodded once.

"You don't have to thank me."

"I know."

She smiled.

"But I wanted to."

Cassian hesitated before returning the smile.

Small.

Uncertain.

Real.

Across the room Aidan watched them together.

Family.

Not by blood.

By choice.

Something inside his chest warmed.

Then...

It stopped.

Without warning...

His heartbeat skipped.

Once.

A strange pressure spread through his ribs before disappearing almost immediately.

Aidan frowned.

"You alright?"

Lyra had noticed.

"I'm fine."

"You sure?"

"It was nothing."

He believed it.

Mostly.

...

That evening Aidan climbed to the highest tower overlooking Blackwood Academy.

Wind rolled across the rooftops, carrying the scent of rain and fresh stone.

He closed his eyes.

The Crimson Witch Authority unfolded effortlessly.

Invisible currents of magic spread through the academy.

He sensed repaired wards.

Students practicing spells.

Professors reinforcing ancient barriers.

Birds nesting within broken towers.

Even water flowing beneath the academy's foundations.

Everything felt connected.

Everything...

Except one place.

Deep beneath his own heartbeat...

There remained that strange silence.

Like standing before a sealed door.

He reached toward it with careful curiosity.

Nothing answered.

Only that same distant...

Steady...

Second heartbeat.

Thump.

...

Thump.

...

Thump.

Not louder.

Only clearer.

Aidan opened his eyes.

The sensation vanished immediately.

His breathing remained calm.

His magic remained perfect.

Yet something about the silence unsettled him.

It felt less like an illness...

And more like patience.

It became possibility.

Thousands of delicate patterns unfolded through his thoughts before quietly settling beyond his current understanding.

He smiled.

It wasn't giving him power.

It was giving him something to earn.

A cool breeze swept across the tower.

For just a heartbeat...

The wind carried an unfamiliar scent.

Warm.

Metallic.

Sharp.

Aidan instinctively inhaled again.

The scent disappeared.

He frowned toward the darkening horizon.

"I know you're there," he whispered to the empty night.

Only silence answered.

Far below, life continued inside Blackwood Academy.

Laughter echoed through restored halls.

Lanterns glowed behind repaired windows.

For the first time in generations...

Hope had returned.

And somewhere beneath Aidan's calm heartbeat...

Something ancient waited...

Growing just a little hungrier.
