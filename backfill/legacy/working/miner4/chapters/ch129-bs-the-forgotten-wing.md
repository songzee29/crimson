## Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Nine — The Forgotten Wing

The western wing of the Darkheart estate had remained locked for as long as Aidan could remember.

No one spoke about it.

Servants quietly avoided the corridor.

Even visitors somehow overlooked the massive oak doors at its entrance, as though the house itself encouraged people to forget they existed.

On a quiet Sunday afternoon, curiosity drew Aidan there.

The doors stood slightly open.

Just enough.

He frowned.

They had always been locked.

Pushing them wider, he stepped into a long hallway draped in white sheets that covered forgotten furniture. Dust floated lazily through shafts of afternoon light, untouched for decades.

Portraits lined both walls.

Unlike those in the main estate, these paintings carried no names beneath their frames.

Only dates.

Some stretched back hundreds of years.

Every generation of House Darkheart seemed to be represented.

Men.

Women.

Children.

Scholars.

Warriors.

Yet one detail united them all.

Every member of the family wore the same expression.

Not pride.

Responsibility.

At the very end of the hall rested the largest portrait of all.

Its frame had been carved from black wood inlaid with crimson crystal.

The canvas...

Was empty.

No throne.

No figure.

Nothing.

Only an untouched field of aged parchment.

Aidan stepped closer.

Why would anyone preserve an empty painting?

His crimson-and-gold eyes lingered upon it.

For the briefest moment...

A faint outline appeared.

Not a person.

A crown.

Then it faded.

The empty canvas remained.

Some memories...

Even history refused to reveal before their proper time.

## Chapter One Hundred Thirty — The Keeper of the Estate

As Aidan left the forgotten wing, he nearly collided with the estate's oldest groundskeeper.

Mr. Elias had served House Darkheart for longer than anyone could remember.

His hair had long since turned silver, but his back remained perfectly straight.

I was wondering when you'd find that hallway.

Aidan blinked.

You knew it was open?

The old man smiled.

Some doors don't open because the lock changes.

They open because the person does.

Aidan looked back toward the empty corridor.

Whose portrait was that?

Mr. Elias remained silent for several moments.

Finally, he answered.

The only portrait in this house that was never meant to remember a face.

Why?

Because...

The old man looked toward the setting sun.

Some people become so important...

That remembering only their face causes everyone to forget their purpose.

Before Aidan could ask another question, Mr. Elias tipped his hat and quietly continued tending the gardens.

His answer lingered far longer than the conversation itself.

## Chapter One Hundred Thirty-One — The Crimson Oak

That evening, the Guardian led Aidan somewhere new within the Living Legacy.

An enormous oak tree stood alone atop a hill overlooking the Valley of Beginnings.

Its leaves shimmered crimson beneath a sky painted gold.

Unlike every other tree in the valley...

This one possessed roots that stretched across the landscape like rivers.

What is this place?

The Guardian rested one hand upon the ancient trunk.

Every civilization begins with a seed.

This...

Was the first tree planted in the Sanctuary.

Aidan looked upward.

The branches seemed to touch the clouds.

It's beautiful.

The Guardian nodded.

It has survived storms.

Wars.

Silence.

Generations.

Not because it was the strongest tree.

Because its roots always grew deeper than its branches reached higher.

He looked directly at Aidan.

Never chase a taller crown before growing deeper roots.

Many kingdoms have fallen because their rulers forgot that lesson.

Aidan gently placed his hand against the bark.

The tree felt warm.

Alive.

Its heartbeat matched his own.

## Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Two — The Old Races

The next afternoon, Aidan wandered through Blackwood Library while the old bookseller organized newly returned volumes.

One shelf immediately caught his attention.

Its books bore familiar symbols.

A crescent moon.

A raven.

A wolf.

He looked toward the bookseller.

May I?

The old man nodded.

Knowledge belongs to those willing to treat it with respect.

Aidan opened the first volume.

It described ancient covens of witches whose Laws shaped villages instead of battlefields.

The second recorded forgotten vampire houses that ruled through fear, loyalty, and blood.

The third preserved the stories of werewolf packs that protected forests long before kingdoms existed.

None of the books mentioned the Crimson Bloodline.

Interesting.

The bookseller quietly approached.

Notice anything?

Aidan nodded.

They're all still here.

Exactly.

The old man smiled.

History rarely replaces itself.

It grows.

One age builds upon another.

The old races are not ending.

Their stories simply continue beside new ones.

Aidan carefully returned each book to its shelf.

The Crimson Bloodline wasn't meant to erase history.

It was destined to become another chapter within it.

Steady.

Patient.

Waiting.
