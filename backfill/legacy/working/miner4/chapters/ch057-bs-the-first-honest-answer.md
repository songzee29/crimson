## Chapter Fifty-Seven — The First Honest Answer

The rain steadily drummed against the tall library windows.

The fire crackled softly between them.

The old bookseller never rushed him.

He simply waited.

Aidan lowered his eyes to the cup of tea resting in his hands.

At first...

I thought it was curiosity.

He paused.

Then shook his head.

No...

That's not true.

The old man remained silent.

I think...

I was tired.

Tired of feeling like something was missing.

Everyone always looked at me like they expected something.

Like I was supposed to become someone I didn't know.

The old man nodded almost imperceptibly.

Go on.

When I found that room...

I thought maybe...

Maybe it held an answer.

I didn't know it would change my life.

He laughed quietly.

Honestly...

If I had known...

I probably would've walked away.

The old man smiled.

No.

You wouldn't have.

Aidan looked up.

Why are you so sure?

Because...

The bookseller leaned back in his chair.

The Legacy does not choose the curious.

It chooses those who cannot live while ignoring the truth.

Silence settled over the room.

The words struck deeper than Aidan expected.

The old man continued.

Curiosity fades.

Truth doesn't.

You would've opened that door today...

Tomorrow...

Or twenty years from now.

Because some questions refuse to leave certain people alone.

The fire suddenly flared.

Its crimson light danced across thousands of books lining the endless shelves.

You were never running toward the Book.

The bookseller whispered.

The Book was patiently waiting for you to become ready.

No title.

Nothing.

But absolute.

Because the book does.

Everything that remembers...

Lives in some form.

Knowledge has always carried a price.

They're wrong.

Responsibility.

## Chapter Sixty — The Name That Was Erased

The old man led Aidan toward a portrait hanging alone at the end of the chamber.

Unlike every other painting...

This one had been covered with black cloth.

The bookseller carefully removed it.

Aidan's breathing stopped.

The portrait showed a magnificent throne room.

Knights stood proudly on either side.

Crimson banners filled the hall.

At the center stood a single man.

Tall.

Powerful.

A king.

But...

His face had been completely erased.

Only empty canvas remained where his features should have been.

The rest of the painting was untouched.

Who did this?

The bookseller quietly folded the black cloth.

He did.

Aidan stared.

The king erased himself?

Yes.

Why?

The old man's eyes softened.

Because he loved the future more than he loved being remembered.

The words hung heavily in the air.

Most rulers spend their lives trying to make history remember them.

The Founder...

Spent his making sure history wouldn't become trapped by him.

He wanted the next generation...

To build something greater.

Not worship something older.

Aidan couldn't take his eyes off the faceless king.

For some reason...

The empty space where the face should have been felt more powerful than any portrait he had ever seen.

## Chapter Sixty-One — The Question Left Behind

The rain had finally stopped.

Moonlight spilled through the towering windows of Blackwood Library.

The bookseller walked Aidan back toward the entrance.

Before opening the door...

He reached into his coat and removed a small wooden token.

On one side...

The same crimson symbol as the Book.

On the other...

A simple key.

This isn't a key that opens doors.

He placed it into Aidan's hand.

It's a reminder.

The greatest locks...

Exist inside people.

Not buildings.

Aidan carefully slipped it into his pocket.

Thank you.

The bookseller smiled warmly.

One final question before you leave.

Aidan nodded.

The old man looked toward the stars.

When the day comes...

And you finally learn the Founder's real name...

Will you protect it...

Or will you tell the world?

Aidan stood silently beneath the moonlight.

He had never considered that question.

The bookseller opened the library door.

Don't answer today.

The Legacy already knows.

It simply wants to know...

When you do.
