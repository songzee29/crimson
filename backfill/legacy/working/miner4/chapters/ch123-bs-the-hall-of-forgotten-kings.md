## Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Three — The Hall of Forgotten Kings

Three nights passed before Aidan returned to the hidden chamber beneath House Darkheart.

Nothing called him there.

No notification appeared.

No mysterious vision interrupted his sleep.

He simply felt...

Drawn.

The Living Legacy had taught him one lesson above all others.

Some truths revealed themselves only to those willing to seek them without being summoned.

The forgotten corridor greeted him with the same ancient silence.

Portraits watched from both walls.

The air smelled faintly of old parchment and polished wood.

As he passed the faceless portrait once again, he noticed something new.

The throne behind the erased figure wasn't empty.

Resting upon one arm of the throne sat a crown.

Unlike the jeweled crowns found in history books...

This one possessed no gold.

No silver.

It appeared to have been carved from crimson crystal.

Its shape resembled intertwining branches that rose into sharp points like flames reaching toward the heavens.

Beautiful.

Yet strangely solemn.

Aidan found himself staring at it.

Not because it represented power.

Because it looked unbearably heavy.

His crimson-and-gold eyes reflected the ancient painting.

For the briefest instant...

The empty canvas where the King's face had once existed shimmered.

Not revealing features.

Only two crimson-and-gold eyes.

Watching.

Then...

The vision disappeared.

Aidan instinctively stepped backward.

Silence returned.

The portrait became ordinary once more.

The Legacy had shown him nothing.

It had merely reminded him...

History was still watching.

## Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Four — The Three Seals

The Guardian waited beside the Chamber of Threads.

Without greeting Aidan, he turned toward a corridor the Heir had never seen before.

Walk with me.

Massive stone pillars stretched into darkness.

Ancient carvings covered every wall.

Aidan immediately noticed something different.

Until now...

Every carving throughout the Sanctuary had told stories.

These...

Contained only symbols.

Three symbols.

Repeated endlessly.

A circle surrounded by intricate runes.

A crescent moon resting above flowing crimson lines.

The head of a great wolf gazing toward the horizon.

They appeared separately.

Never together.

Aidan finally spoke.

I've seen these before.

The Guardian nodded.

You have.

What do they mean?

The old warrior stopped walking.

Their names...

Have not yet been earned.

He gently placed one hand against the first symbol.

The stone briefly glowed crimson.

Not brightly.

Respectfully.

The Guardian lowered his hand.

Some inheritances must first become questions...

Before they are allowed to become answers.

He resumed walking.

Aidan followed.

He no longer felt frustrated by incomplete explanations.

He had begun trusting the pace of the Legacy.

## Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Five — Blackwood's Secret

Saturday afternoon brought Aidan back to Blackwood Library.

The old bookseller looked up from an enormous volume resting upon the counter.

Welcome back.

Aidan smiled.

You knew I was coming.

The bookseller chuckled.

No.

I hoped.

There's a difference.

The old man carefully closed the book.

Its cover bore no title.

Only a crimson tree whose roots stretched beneath mountains.

Curious...

Aidan looked toward it.

The bookseller noticed immediately.

Would you like to know the oldest lie ever written?

Aidan nodded.

The old man smiled sadly.

History belongs to the victors.

He shook his head.

No...

History belongs to whoever survives long enough to preserve it.

He rested one wrinkled hand upon the book.

That...

Is why the Legacy exists.

Not to change history.

To remember it faithfully.

The words lingered between them.

The Living Legacy was never created to control the future.

It existed because someone believed the truth deserved to survive.

Even if its author did not.
