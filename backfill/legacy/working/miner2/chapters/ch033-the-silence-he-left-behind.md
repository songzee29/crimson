The tunnel above the folded corridor felt normal again.

That was the first lie.

Normal didn’t exist anymore—not after something like that.

Lyra stayed leaned against the wall for a long time, watching Aidan without speaking. Not because she didn’t want to. Because she was trying to understand what she was seeing.

Aidan stood a few feet away, staring at his own hands like they belonged to someone else.

Not confusion anymore.

Absence.

Like a room after something precious had been removed and the air still hadn’t decided how to behave.

“You’re quieter,” Lyra said finally.

Aidan blinked slowly.

“I am?”

It wasn’t defensive. It wasn’t unsure.

It was neutral.

And that scared her more than panic would have.

Lyra pushed herself off the wall.

“Aidan… do you feel anything right now?”

He paused too long before answering.

“I think… I’m supposed to.”

That sentence shouldn’t have made her chest tighten as much as it did.

But it did.

Because he wasn’t lying.

He just didn’t have access to the part of himself that would normally explain why that was wrong.

A faint System notice flickered in the corner of his vision.

---

> STABILITY UPDATE  
> CORE INTEGRITY: DEGRADED  
> ADAPTATION CONTINUES

---

Lyra noticed his eyes shift slightly.

“What did it say?”

Aidan looked at her.

“I don’t know how to explain it,” he said. “It feels like… instructions without meaning.”

Lyra’s stomach sank.

The System wasn’t gone.

It was compensating.
