They moved deeper into the city tunnels, away from the folded space collapse point.

Every sound felt louder than it should’ve been.

Dripping water. Distant metal groans. Footsteps that might’ve belonged to them or something else entirely.

Lyra kept watching Aidan’s reactions.

Or lack of them.

At one point, a loose pipe fell behind them, slamming into the ground with a sharp metallic crack.

Aidan didn’t flinch.

Not even slightly.

Lyra stopped walking.

“Aidan.”

He turned.

“Yes?”

She studied him carefully.

“That didn’t scare you?”

He thought about it.

“I don’t think it was supposed to.”

Lyra stepped closer.

“It was loud.”

Aidan nodded.

“I noticed that.”

“And you didn’t react.”

Another pause.

“I didn’t know I needed to.”

That was the moment Lyra understood something deeply wrong had happened.

It wasn’t just memory loss.

It was **emotional instruction loss.**

Something inside Aidan no longer told him what mattered.
