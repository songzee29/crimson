Midnight settled over the swamp.

Mist drifted across the dark water.

Ancient cypress trees stretched overhead, their branches hanging like skeletal hands.

Aidan stepped onto an old wooden dock.

The Guardian was already waiting.

"You came."

"You asked."

The Guardian nodded approvingly.

"Good."

Then—

He threw Aidan a wooden staff.

No magic.

No enchantment.

Just wood.

Aidan caught it.

"...What's this for?"

The Guardian drew another staff.

"For the next lesson."

"You've relied on your curse."

"You've relied on instinct."

"You've relied on luck."

He pointed the staff toward Aidan.

"Now..."

"...learn to fight."

---

For the next two hours...

Aidan never landed a single hit.

Every swing missed.

Every attack was countered.

Every mistake punished.

The Guardian wasn't cruel.

He was patient.

When Aidan rushed...

He was knocked into the mud.

When Aidan relied on Hex Step...

The Guardian anticipated where he would appear.

When Aidan tried Crimson Shackles...

The Guardian tapped his wrist before the spell could form.

"Again."

Aidan stood.

Again.

"Again."

He stood.

Again.

"Again."

By sunrise...

His arms trembled from exhaustion.

His knuckles bled.

His body ached.

The Guardian finally smiled.

"You've stopped trying to win."

Aidan looked confused.

"What?"

"Now..."

"...you're trying to learn."
