They ran.

But the corridor no longer felt like something they were moving through.

It felt like something deciding where they were allowed to be.

Doors appeared and disappeared.

Paths extended and collapsed.

At one point, Aidan ran straight into a wall that hadn’t existed a second earlier.

He stumbled back.

Panting.

“This place is alive,” he said.

“No,” Lyra corrected. “It’s being edited.”

The System flickered again in his vision.

---

> EMERGENCY EXIT UPDATE  
> SECONDARY COST DETECTED  
> STABILITY LOSS: INCREASING

---

Aidan looked up.

“What now?”

Lyra hesitated.

“That means it’s escalating.”

“Escalating into what?”

She didn’t answer right away.

Because she didn’t want to say it out loud.

Behind them, footsteps multiplied.

Not just hunters now.

More presence.

Heavier presence.

Aidan felt it before he saw it.

The air changed.

Pressure dropped.

Like reality itself was making space for something larger than the scouts.

Lyra whispered:

“…Execution class.”

Aidan frowned.

“What does that mean?”

But the answer came from the corridor itself.

A new figure stepped forward.

Not masked like the others.

This one wore a smooth, featureless helm that reflected nothing at all.

No face.

No cracks.

No movement.

Just absence shaped like authority.

The moment it appeared, even the scouts stopped.

Lyra’s voice went tight.

“We are not supposed to be seen by that level.”

Aidan’s chest tightened.

“Then what do we do?”

Lyra looked at him.

For the first time, she didn’t have an answer immediately.

Because there wasn’t one.

The Execution-class figure raised one hand slightly.

And the corridor *stopped rewriting randomly.*

It stabilized.

Not for them.

For it.

Then it spoke.

And the voice didn’t echo.

It simply existed everywhere at once.

“You are incomplete.”

Aidan froze.

The figure continued.

“You are not fully erased. Not fully recorded. Not fully classified.”

A pause.

Then:

“You should not exist in this layer.”

Aidan’s heart pounded harder.

Lyra stepped in front of him again, but her body was shaking now.

The Execution-class tilted its head slightly.

“Return the deviation.”

The System responded instantly.

---

> WARNING  
> EXECUTION AUTHORITY ENGAGED  
> HOST SURVIVAL: UNSTABLE

---

Aidan felt it again.

That pull inside his mind.

Another memory anchor being targeted.

Lyra grabbed his arm tighter.

“Aidan, don’t let it take anything else. If it finishes, you’ll start losing yourself completely.”

He looked at her.

“I don’t even know what I already lost,” he said quietly.

And that was the problem.

He didn’t.

The System was already rewriting him in pieces.

Behind them, the scouts began to move again.

The Execution-class didn’t rush.

It didn’t need to.

It simply raised its hand again.

And the corridor began collapsing inward.

Not breaking.

**Condensing.**

Space tightening.

Reality compressing toward Aidan and Lyra like pressure in the lungs.

Lyra grabbed his wrist.

“There’s one way out,” she said quickly.

Aidan looked at her.

“But you won’t like it.”

The System flickered again.

---

> EMERGENCY EXIT: FINAL OFFER  
> COST: CORE MEMORY ANCHOR (PRIMARY)

---

Aidan swallowed.

Core memory.

Not partial.

Not secondary.

Core.

Something in him already understood what that meant.

And for the first time—

he felt something inside him resist the System directly.

Not power.

Not magic.

Something deeper.

A name he couldn’t remember but still felt attached to.

The Execution-class stepped forward.

The corridor closed tighter.

Lyra whispered:

“If you lose this one, you won’t come back the same.”

Aidan stared at the System prompt.

Then at Lyra.

Then at the collapsing space around them.

And finally—

he made his decision.
