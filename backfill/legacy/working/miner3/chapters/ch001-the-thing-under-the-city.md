The city didn’t feel alive anymore.

It felt occupied.

Like something had moved in after humanity stopped paying attention and decided it liked the space.

Aidan Vale moved through the lower ruins beneath New Orleans without a light.

He didn’t need one.

Not anymore.

That was new.

And he didn’t know when it started being new.

The tunnels had once been part of a transit system—rails, stations, movement, life. Now it was all collapsed geometry and drowned concrete, swallowed by time and water that refused to leave.

Every step echoed too far.

Too clean.

Like the darkness was listening.

Aidan stopped walking.

He didn’t know why.

He just… did.

Silence stretched.

Then—

a drop of water hit stone somewhere behind him.

*Drip.*

He turned.

Nothing.

But his throat tightened anyway.

A feeling he had no name for yet.

Something between warning and memory.

He kept moving.

But slower now.

More aware of himself than the world.

That was the problem lately.

He was becoming aware of things he never used to notice.

Heartbeat rhythms in distant structures.

Air pressure shifts when living things moved nearby.

Warmth where there shouldn’t be warmth.

He didn’t like it.

Because it meant the world wasn’t simple anymore.

And neither was he.

---

Something deeper in the tunnel shifted.

Not sound.

Presence.

Aidan stopped again.

This time he *knew* he didn’t choose it.

His body chose it first.

His breath slowed.

His muscles tightened.

Like something inside him had just recognized something else.

Not danger.

Something worse.

Opportunity.

Aidan swallowed.

“What is this…” he muttered.

His voice sounded wrong in the tunnel.

Too quiet.

Too controlled.

Too unfamiliar.

Then—

a second presence appeared behind his eyes.

Not in the room.

Inside him.

Rez.

But not fully.

Not stable.

Just a flicker of awareness.

> “Don’t go further.”

Aidan froze.

“…Rez?”

No answer.

Just that same pressure in his mind.

Like someone speaking through a wall instead of a voice.

Then it faded again.

Gone.

Aidan exhaled slowly.

“What the hell is happening to me…”

He kept walking.

---

The tunnel widened into a collapsed station.

Broken pillars.

Flooded platforms.

Old advertisements still clinging to cracked walls, faces smiling from a world that didn’t exist anymore.

Aidan stepped into the space—

—and his body reacted instantly.

Hunger.

Not sudden.

Not sharp.

*Deep.*

Like something had been waiting for him to enter this exact place.

His stomach twisted.

His throat tightened.

His teeth ached faintly.

Aidan backed up slightly.

“No… no, not again.”

He pressed a hand against his chest like that would stop anything.

His heartbeat was steady.

Too steady.

That was the problem.

Because something inside him was listening to it.

Measuring it.

Matching it.

Then—

footsteps.

Aidan’s head snapped up before he thought to move.

A figure appeared on the far platform.

A scavenger.

Covered in patched clothing. Carrying supplies. Moving carefully through the ruins like they belonged to the dead more than the living.

Alive.

Warm.

Human.

Aidan didn’t realize he was stepping forward until he had already moved.

Just one step.

Then another.

He stopped himself.

Forcing it.

His breath hitched.

“What… no.”

The hunger didn’t speak.

It didn’t need words.

It just became louder.

The scavenger noticed him.

“Hey—” the voice called. “You lost or somethin’?”

Normal tone.

Normal concern.

That was the problem.

Aidan’s vision sharpened slightly.

Too much detail.

Too much information.

He could see the pulse in the scavenger’s neck now.

He shouldn’t have been able to see that.

He stepped back.

Hard.

Hit the wall behind him.

His breathing quickened.

“No,” he whispered. “I’m not—”

The word didn’t finish.

Because something inside him leaned forward.

Rez flickered again.

> “Aidan…”

But the voice cut out mid-syllable.

Like the system didn’t want it interfering.

Aidan shook his head.

“I’m fine,” he lied.

The scavenger took a cautious step closer.

That was all it took.

The scent hit him.

Warm blood under skin.

Alive systems.

Heartbeat rhythm.

Everything inside Aidan tightened violently.

His nails dug into his palms.

“No… no, don’t do this…”

The hunger didn’t rise.

It *opened.*

Like a door that had been locked for too long finally remembering how to exist.

Aidan’s body moved forward—

then stopped.

Violently.

Like he was fighting himself in real time.

“I said stop,” he hissed through clenched teeth.

The scavenger paused.

“Hey, man, I don’t wanna—”

Aidan flinched.

The sound became too loud.

Too personal.

Everything blurred for half a second.

Then snapped back.

Clear again.

Too clear.

And that clarity was worse than confusion.

Because now he understood what he was looking at.

Not a person.

A system of blood and breath.

A living thing that could be… stopped.

Consumed.

Aidan shook.

His control was breaking in layers now.

Not all at once.

Slow.

Methodical.

“I can’t… I can’t…”

Rez’s voice returned—lower than before.

Barely there.

> “If you don’t stop it now… you won’t get another chance to choose.”

Aidan’s eyes widened slightly.

That landed somewhere deep.

Choice.

He still had that.

For now.

His hand trembled violently.

He forced it down.

Tried to step back again.

But the hunger surged harder this time.

Not anger.

Not rage.

Just inevitability.

Aidan’s voice cracked.

“Please…”

And then—

he moved.

Not decision.

Collapse.

The tunnel filled with motion too fast to follow clearly.

Aidan crossed the space between them like the world forgot to enforce distance.

The scavenger barely had time to turn before Aidan reached them.

There was a moment—

a fraction of awareness—

Aidan’s eyes widening as he realized what was about to happen.

“No—”

But it was already too late.

The rest wasn’t choice anymore.

It was consequence finishing itself.

---

Silence returned to the tunnel slowly.

Not immediately.

Slowly.

Like the world was waiting to see if it needed to react further.

Aidan stood in the center of it.

Unsteady.

Shaking.

Blood on his mouth.

Hands trembling like they didn’t belong to him anymore.

He stared down at what remained.

Then stepped back.

One step.

Then another.

Until his back hit the wall again.

He slid down it.

Slow.

Like gravity had just remembered him.

His breath came in uneven pieces.

“I didn’t…” he tried.

But the sentence broke.

Rez didn’t speak right away.

When he finally did, his voice wasn’t sharp.

Wasn’t guiding.

It was quiet.

Heavy.

> “Yeah…”

A pause.

> “You did.”

Aidan closed his eyes.

And somewhere deep inside him—

something new quietly finished waking up.
