Aidan turned toward the far wall of the subway room.

Lyra immediately moved between him and it.

“No,” she said.

Aidan frowned slightly.

“I didn’t say anything.”

“You didn’t have to.”

Behind them, the flickering light dimmed.

The Witch Sight layer in Aidan’s vision expanded without permission.

The wall wasn’t a wall anymore.

It was a boundary.

And beyond it—

something was waiting.

Aidan stepped closer despite Lyra blocking him.

“I think it’s important,” he said quietly.

Lyra’s voice sharpened.

“You don’t know that.”

Aidan stopped.

That word—*know*—hit differently now.

He blinked.

“I don’t know how I know it,” he admitted.

That was worse.

Because it meant the System wasn’t just guiding him.

Something deeper than the System was still inside him.

Lyra reached out and grabbed his wrist.

“Aidan, listen to me,” she said firmly. “Whatever that feeling is—it’s not safe.”

He looked at her hand.

Then at her face.

Then said something that made her chest tighten:

“I don’t think I have a word for safe anymore.”
