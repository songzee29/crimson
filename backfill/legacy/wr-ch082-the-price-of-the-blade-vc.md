Aidan hit Cassian before he could raise his hands.

The punch drove him across the stone.

Cassian struck the ground, rolled once, and came up on one knee.

Aidan was already there.

His fist crashed into Cassian’s face.

Bone broke.

Blood sprayed across the rock.

Cassian fell sideways.

Aidan grabbed him by the collar and hauled him upright.

“You killed her.”

He drove his forehead into Cassian’s nose.

Cassian staggered.

Aidan hit him again.

And again.

Every blow carried the force of the Wolf.

Every strike was guided by the Vampire’s precision.

Every movement was sharpened by the Witch’s awareness.

Cassian tried to block.

Aidan tore through his guard.

Cassian tried to retreat.

Aidan followed.

Cassian tried to speak.

Aidan punched him in the mouth.

“Don’t.”

Cassian crashed into a broken pillar.

The stone fractured behind him.

Aidan seized his throat and slammed him against it.

“You don’t get to explain.”

Cassian’s blood ran down his chin.

“I wasn’t—”

Aidan struck him in the ribs.

The impact folded him.

“You don’t get to apologize.”

Another blow.

Cassian’s shoulder dislocated.

“You don’t get to say her name.”

Aidan drove his knee into Cassian’s stomach.

Cassian vomited blood.

The others stood frozen around them.

Rowan watched with grim silence.

Elias looked away.

Malik’s fists tightened at his sides.

Draco did not move.

Lyra stood beneath Rowan’s cloak, her crimson eyes fixed on the two men.

She did not stop Aidan.

She did not ask him to.

Cassian dropped to one knee.

Aidan grabbed the back of his head and forced him to look at Lyra.

“Look at her.”

Cassian’s eyes found hers.

Lyra did not flinch.

Cassian’s face collapsed.

Aidan slammed him into the ground.

The stone cracked beneath his skull.

“You watched her die.”

Cassian tried to push himself up.

Aidan kicked him back down.

“You heard her stop breathing.”

Another kick.

“You felt her blood on your hands.”

Cassian curled around the pain.

Aidan seized his ankle and dragged him across the stone.

“You knew exactly what you were doing.”

Cassian’s fingers clawed at the ground.

“Yes.”

Aidan stopped.

The word hung between them.

Cassian rolled onto his back.

His face was already swelling.

One eye had closed.

Blood covered his mouth.

“Yes,” he repeated. “I knew.”

Aidan stared down at him.

The Wolf wanted him dead.

The Vampire calculated how quickly it could happen.

The Witch showed him every vulnerable point in Cassian’s body.

Aidan could end it with one strike.

Cassian knew that too.

“Then say it,” Aidan said.

Cassian breathed through broken ribs.

“I killed Lyra.”

Aidan’s fist tightened.

“Again.”

“I killed Lyra.”

“Again.”

Cassian’s voice cracked.

“I killed Lyra.”

Aidan hit him.

The blow lifted Cassian from the ground and sent him skidding across the plateau.

Lyra’s eyes closed briefly.

Not from pity.

From memory.

Aidan saw it.

He saw the moment she had died.

Saw her body go limp.

Saw the blood leave her.

Saw Cassian standing over her.

The rage became something larger than anger.

It became grief with fists.

Aidan crossed the distance.

Cassian tried to rise.

Aidan kicked his knee sideways.

The joint bent wrong.

Cassian screamed.

Aidan grabbed him by the hair and pulled him upright.

“You don’t get to scream louder than she did.”

He punched him in the stomach.

Cassian folded.

Aidan struck his back.

Then his ribs.

Then his jaw.

Cassian’s body stopped defending itself.

Aidan kept hitting him.

The Wolf did not take control.

It did not need to.

Aidan knew exactly what he was doing.

He was not lost inside the violence.

He was choosing it.

Every blow was deliberate.

Every strike carried the memory of Lyra’s death.

Cassian’s blood splashed across Aidan’s hands.

The same hands that had held Lyra.

The same hands that had brought her back.

The same hands now breaking the man who had killed her.

Cassian collapsed again.

Aidan stood over him, breathing hard.

Lyra’s voice reached him.

“Aidan.”

He looked at her.

She had not said stop.

She had only said his name.

That was enough to make him pause.

Cassian lay motionless at his feet.

Aidan’s claws remained extended.

His eyes burned crimson.

Lyra stepped closer.

The runes beneath her skin glowed in answer to his power.

“He needs to hear me,” she said.

Aidan looked down at Cassian.

“He heard you die.”

“I know.”

“He watched it happen.”

“I know.”

“He chose it.”

“I know.”

Lyra stopped beside Aidan.

She looked at Cassian.

“You don’t get forgiveness because I’m alive.”

Cassian’s remaining eye opened.

“I know.”

“You don’t get to call this a mistake.”

“I know.”

“You don’t get to pretend you saved me by bringing me back.”

Cassian swallowed blood.

“I know.”

Lyra’s claws emerged.

She crouched beside him.

“I died.”

Cassian stared at her.

“I felt it.”

His expression twisted.

“I know.”

“No,” Lyra said. “You don’t.”

She touched two fingers to the wound beneath her ribs.

The crimson runes there brightened.

“I felt my heart stop. I felt the dark take me. I heard Aidan calling for me, and I couldn’t answer him.”

Cassian’s face went pale.

“I’m sorry.”

Lyra’s hand snapped forward.

Her claws stopped a breath from his throat.

“Don’t say that yet.”

Cassian froze.

Lyra rose.

Aidan looked at her.

“What do you want?”

She met his eyes.

“I want him alive.”

Aidan’s jaw tightened.

“Why?”

“Because I want him to remember.”

Cassian closed his eyes.

Lyra continued.

“I want him to remember the sound of my heart stopping. I want him to remember Aidan holding me. I want him to remember that he killed me, and that my return does not change it.”

Aidan stared at her.

The rage inside him did not disappear.

It settled.

Not forgiven.

Not finished.

Directed.

He turned back to Cassian.

“You heard her.”

Cassian tried to move.

Aidan placed one boot against his chest and pressed him into the stone.

“You live.”

Cassian’s eyes opened.

Aidan leaned closer.

“You live with every second of this.”

He lifted his foot and drove it into Cassian’s ribs.

A crack echoed across the plateau.

Cassian screamed.

Aidan grabbed him again and pulled him upright.

“You live knowing she came back because of me.”

A punch.

“You live knowing you were the reason she died.”

Another.

“You live knowing she looked at you and chose not to kill you.”

Another.

Cassian’s head snapped back.

Aidan released him.

Cassian collapsed onto the stone.

The Darkheart Curse stirred.

Black veins spread across Cassian’s throat.

The air around him darkened.

Aidan felt the curse recognize the violence.

Recognize the blood.

Recognize the judgment.

The curse had wanted blood to answer blood.

It had wanted Aidan to become executioner.

Instead, Aidan had beaten Cassian nearly to death and left him alive to carry the consequence.

The black veins tightened.

A mark burned across Cassian’s chest.

Aidan recognized the shape.

A broken heart surrounded by thorns.

Cassian screamed again as the curse branded him.

The Crimson System appeared.

CRIMSON SYSTEM

Darkheart Curse:

Judgment Phase Advanced

Recorded Transgression:

Murder of Lyra

Recorded Consequence:

Aidan’s Chosen Punishment

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The interface vanished.

Cassian lay bleeding beneath the mark.

Aidan stood over him.

The beating was over.

The anger was not.

Lyra came to stand beside him.

Her shoulder brushed his.

Aidan looked at her.

“You should rest.”

“I died and came back as something no one has ever been before.”

“Yes.”

“I’m not resting.”

Despite the blood on his hands, despite the broken mountain, despite everything that had happened, Aidan almost laughed.

Almost.

Lyra looked down at Cassian.

“He’s still breathing.”

“Barely.”

“Good.”

Cassian’s eye opened.

Lyra crouched beside him once more.

“You will never be my brother again.”

Cassian absorbed the words without protest.

“You will never be his brother again either.”

Aidan’s expression hardened.

Cassian looked at him.

“I know.”

“You will not ask us to forgive you.”

“I won’t.”

“You will not call what happened necessary.”

“I won’t.”

“You will not pretend my return makes it less real.”

Cassian’s voice was barely audible.

“I won’t.”

Lyra stood.

Aidan looked toward the collapsing mountain.

“We have to move.”

Rowan nodded.

The others began gathering their weapons.

Aidan did not move immediately.

He looked at his hands.

Lyra’s blood had dried across one palm.

Cassian’s blood covered the other.

The two stains had mixed.

He hated that.

He hated that the same hands had carried both of them through death and violence.

Lyra reached for him.

Aidan took her hand.

Her fingers were warm.

Her pulse was strong.

The Wolf inside him recognized her.

The Wolf inside her answered.

Crimson light moved between their joined hands.

Not ownership.

Not control.

Bloodline.

Choice.

Aidan looked at her transformed eyes.

“You’re really here.”

Lyra squeezed his hand.

“I’m really here.”

“You’re the first Crimson Runefang.”

“I know.”

“You have a Wolf form.”

“I know.”

“You died.”

Her expression softened.

“I know.”

Aidan’s voice broke.

“I watched you.”

Lyra stepped into him and pressed her forehead against his.

“I know.”

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then Lyra whispered, “You beat him.”

Aidan looked past her at Cassian.

“Yes.”

“Did it help?”

Aidan considered the question.

“No.”

Lyra nodded.

“It wasn’t supposed to.”

Behind them, Cassian tried to push himself upright.

He failed.

Draco moved toward him, but Aidan raised a hand.

“Leave him.”

Cassian looked at him.

Aidan’s voice was cold.

“He can crawl.”

Cassian lowered his head.

He began to crawl.

No one helped him.

No one stopped him.

The mountain continued to collapse around them, but the central truth had already settled into the world.

Lyra was alive.

She was changed forever.

She was the first Crimson Runefang.

Cassian had killed her.

Aidan had beaten him for it.

And none of those truths erased the others.

The Crimson System appeared one final time.

Returned

FIRST CRIMSON RUNEFANG

Race:

WITCH + WOLF

Aidan’s Response:

Retribution Delivered

Cassian:

Alive

Cassian’s Crime:

Unforgiven

Darkheart Curse:

Active

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The interface disappeared.

Aidan tightened his grip around Lyra’s hand.

Together, they walked away from the broken mountain.

Behind them, Cassian crawled through the blood and stone.

Alive.

Marked.

Broken.

And forced to remember that the woman he had murdered had returned as something greater than death.
