Blackwood’s walls filled beyond capacity.

The refugees from the western hills slept wherever space remained.

Classrooms.

Hallways.

Training yards.

Even the Great Hall became a shelter.

Students gave up their beds without complaint.

No one believed the displacement would last only a few days.

…

Aidan spent the night carrying supplies.

Not because he had recovered.

Because there weren’t enough hands.

His shoulder remained wrapped tightly against his chest.

His side bled whenever he lifted anything heavier than a sack of grain.

He carried it anyway.

Malik finally stepped in front of him.

“Enough.”

“I’m almost done.”

“You’ve said that since yesterday.”

“There are still people waiting.”

“There always will be.”

The words struck harder than Malik intended.

Because they were true.

There would always be another person waiting.

Another village.

Another burden.

Another reason to keep walking.

…

Near dawn…

Elias entered the strategy hall carrying a bundle of weathered patrol journals recovered from an abandoned outpost.

“I thought these might help.”

Aidan reached toward the first journal.

Before he could open it—

The southern warning rune ignited.

Then the eastern.

Then the western.

Three directions.

Three separate emergencies.

Again.

Aidan slowly withdrew his hand from the unopened journal.

He didn’t even sigh anymore.

He simply stood.

Cassian watched him quietly.

“You were trying to find answers.”

“I know.”

“You never get the chance.”

“I know.”

The journal remained unopened on the table.

Another truth postponed.

Another mystery left waiting.

Because somewhere outside Blackwood…

Someone needed saving first.

…

As the rescue parties assembled…

Lyra stepped beside Aidan.

She looked carefully at the fresh blood spreading through the bandages around his side.

“You haven’t healed from the mountain.”

“No.”

“You haven’t healed from Red Creek.”

“No.”

“You haven’t healed from the reservoir.”

“No.”

She swallowed.

“You haven’t healed in years.”

For the first time…

Aidan didn’t answer immediately.

His tired eyes drifted toward the academy gates already opening for another mission.

Finally…

He spoke.

“I don’t think I’ve had time.”

Lyra looked away before he could see the tears forming in her eyes.

She remembered the boy who had laughed through Rowan’s impossible lessons.

Who complained about long training sessions.

Who argued with Malik over the last piece of bread.

Who smiled without effort.

Standing beside her now…

He was still Aidan.

Still kind.

Still willing to give everything for someone else.

But every rescue took something that never came back.

The gates opened fully.

Another messenger stumbled through.

Another desperate plea.

Another impossible choice.

Aidan adjusted the sling supporting his injured shoulder.

Tightened the bandages around his ribs.

Ignored the blood staining his side.

Then walked toward the next battle before the last one had even finished costing him.
