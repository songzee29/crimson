## Chapter One Hundred Fifty-Eight — The Guardian of the Pass

Morning sunlight filtered through the towering pines.

The silver stag was gone.

Only a single hoofprint remained in the soft earth beside Aidan's campfire.

He knelt beside it.

The print was deeper than it should have been.

Not because the animal was heavy.

Because it had stood there for a long time.

Watching.

Judging.

He smiled to himself.

Then quietly packed his belongings.

The fire was extinguished.

The campsite looked untouched.

The Guardian had taught him well.

Leave every place better than you found it.

---

The northern trail climbed steadily into the mountains.

The forest changed as Aidan walked.

The trees grew older.

Their trunks widened until it took four or five people to wrap their arms around them.

Ancient crimson moss covered broken stones scattered throughout the woods.

Some stones had clearly once belonged to buildings.

Others...

To something much larger.

Civilizations had existed here long before the modern world.

Nature had simply reclaimed them.

Hours passed.

Near midday...

The path ended.

Not because it disappeared.

Because an enormous stone gate blocked the mountain pass.

Two statues stood on either side.

Both wore hooded robes.

Both held open books instead of weapons.

Neither face had been erased.

Instead...

Their features had simply worn away beneath centuries of wind and rain.

Carved across the arch above them were unfamiliar words.

Aidan couldn't read them.

Yet somehow...

He understood them.

**Knowledge without character becomes ruin.**

He stood beneath the archway.

The mountain remained silent.

No hidden mechanism.

No glowing runes.

Nothing happened.

He waited.

Still nothing.

Then he remembered the First Trial at the academy.

The answer had never been speed.

It had been understanding.

Instead of searching for a way to force the gate open...

He simply sat.

The wind whispered through the mountain pass.

Birds landed upon the statues.

Time passed peacefully.

Nearly an hour later...

A quiet voice echoed from somewhere beyond the stone gate.

Why didn't you try to open it?

Aidan stood.

Because...

If someone built this place...

They probably had a reason for closing it.

Silence.

Then...

A deep rumble echoed beneath his feet.

The stone gate slowly parted.

Not because of strength.

Because of patience.

## Chapter One Hundred Fifty-Nine — The Forest Keeper

The passage beyond the gate opened into a vast mountain valley untouched by modern civilization.

Crystal-clear streams wound through emerald fields.

Ancient cedar trees stood like silent guardians.

Stone paths connected buildings crafted entirely from mountain rock and polished timber.

No electricity.

No vehicles.

No noise.

Only life.

Waiting near the entrance stood an elderly man dressed in weathered green robes.

A longbow rested across his back.

His white hair reached his shoulders.

His eyes...

Were kind.

You've grown since I last saw you.

Aidan frowned.

We've met?

The old man smiled.

No.

But I've watched.

He extended one hand.

My name is Rowan.

Keeper of the Eastern Pass.

Aidan accepted the handshake.

Aidan Darkheart.

I know.

The answer carried no arrogance.

Only certainty.

Rowan began walking deeper into the valley.

Come.

You've traveled far enough.

It's time someone finally explains why the Pilgrim Roads were built.

---

As they walked...

Aidan noticed children tending gardens.

Young men and women repairing stone walls.

Others studied beneath ancient trees while elderly teachers quietly guided them.

No one appeared hurried.

No one appeared idle.

Everyone worked.

Everyone learned.

Everyone taught.

This isn't a village.

Aidan whispered.

Rowan nodded.

No.

It's a living archive.

A place where knowledge survives...

Even if kingdoms don't.

The words immediately reminded Aidan of the Living Legacy.

The old man noticed.

Yes.

The two were built upon the same philosophy.

History survives...

Only if someone chooses to carry it.

## Chapter One Hundred Sixty — The Second Foundation

At the center of the valley stood a circular temple built into the mountainside.

Unlike the Sanctuary beneath House Darkheart...

This place felt open.

Sunlight poured through massive skylights.

Water flowed gently beneath polished stone floors.

In the middle of the chamber rested the great stone table Aidan had glimpsed in the distance.

Upon it...

The sealed scroll still waited.

Exactly as before.

Rowan stopped several steps away.

This...

Is the Second Foundation.

Not a fortress.

Not a palace.

A school.

Aidan looked around.

I thought Foundations protected relics.

They do.

Rowan smiled.

Knowledge is the greatest relic ever entrusted to mankind.

He looked toward the sealed scroll.

That document has remained unopened for nine hundred and twelve years.

Why?

Because no one worthy has stood before it.

Aidan's heart skipped.

I'm not—

Rowan gently raised one hand.

Whether you are worthy...

Is not for me to decide.

He stepped aside.

Nor for you.

The scroll...

Will decide.

The chamber fell silent.

Sunlight illuminated the crimson wax seal.

Unlike every seal Aidan had encountered before...

This one bore no family crest.

Only three simple words.

**Serve Before Self.**

For the first time since leaving House Darkheart...

Aidan realized the journey ahead wasn't about inheriting ancient power.

It was about proving he deserved ancient trust.

Outside the temple...

The mountain wind carried the sound of a distant bell.

Not a warning.

Not a celebration.

A welcome.

Somewhere beyond the valley...

Unseen eyes quietly turned toward the Second Foundation.

The Pilgrim had arrived.

Now...

History would decide whether it would continue opening its doors.
