## Chapter One — The Room That Time Refused to Touch

There were rooms inside the Darkheart estate that had not been opened in generations.

Dust rested upon ancient furniture like snow that had forgotten how to melt. Portraits of forgotten ancestors watched silently from the walls, their eyes forever frozen between pride and warning. Every hallway carried the weight of history, yet one door remained untouched by servants, family, and even time itself.

No lock protected it.

No chains sealed it.

Fear had done that long ago.

People whispered that whoever entered never returned the same. Some claimed they heard footsteps behind the door on nights when the moon disappeared. Others swore they had seen crimson light escape beneath it before fading into darkness as though the house itself had taken another breath.

Aidan Darkheart had heard every story.

He believed none of them.

At least, that was what he kept telling himself.

The estate had become quieter since his father's disappearance years before. Conversations ended when he entered a room. Older relatives looked at him with pity mixed with something far more unsettling.

Expectation.

As though they were waiting for him to become someone he did not yet understand.

He hated that look.

Today the silence felt different.

He could not explain why, but every step through the abandoned corridor felt guided by something invisible.

His heartbeat slowed.

The air became colder.

Even the dust drifting through the narrow beams of sunlight seemed suspended in place.

The old wooden door waited at the end of the hallway.

Unlike everything surrounding it, the wood had never rotted.

The brass handle had never tarnished.

Time had ignored it.

Aidan reached toward the handle before stopping.

His instincts screamed that opening this door would divide his life into two parts.

Before.

After.

He laughed quietly at himself.

Just an old room.

Nothing more.

His fingers wrapped around the handle.

The door opened without resistance.

The room beyond was impossibly clean.

Shelves stretched from floor to ceiling, overflowing with ancient journals, maps, and relics from civilizations that should have existed only in legends. Strange symbols were carved into the stone floor, faintly glowing beneath centuries of silence.

At the center of the room rested a single pedestal.

Upon it lay an ancient crimson book.

Its cover looked alive.

Dark crimson veins pulsed beneath weathered leather as though blood still flowed inside it.

No title decorated its surface.

No author's name.

Only a symbol unlike anything Aidan had ever seen.

The room fell completely silent.

Even the wind outside vanished.

Every instinct told him to leave.

Instead, he walked forward.

One step.

Then another.

The closer he came, the heavier the air became until every breath felt like lifting stone.

When he finally stood before the pedestal, the book remained perfectly still.

Waiting.

For him.

Aidan hesitated.

His hand slowly reached toward the cover.

The instant his fingertips touched it, a sharp pain shot across his finger.

He jerked back.

A single drop of blood fell through the silent room.

It landed upon the crimson cover.

Nothing happened.

Then the book inhaled.

The blood vanished into the leather.

Ancient symbols ignited across the cover in brilliant crimson light, racing like lightning through every carved line.

The entire room trembled.

Shelves shook violently.

Dust exploded into the air.

The symbols covering the floor awakened one after another until the chamber became a circle of living crimson light.

Aidan stumbled backward.

The book rose from the pedestal without anyone touching it.

Its pages turned on their own.

Thousands.

Millions.

Every page flipping faster than the human eye could follow.

Voices echoed from nowhere and everywhere at once.

Not one voice.

Countless.

Kings.

Queens.

Warriors.

Scholars.

People who had lived and died long before history remembered their names.

Then, just as suddenly—

Silence.

The book shot forward like a crimson star.

It struck Aidan directly in the chest.

There was no impact.

No pain.

The book dissolved into countless streams of crimson light that poured into his body.

The room went dark.

Every symbol disappeared.

The pedestal stood empty.

The book was gone.

Aidan collapsed to one knee, clutching his chest as an unbearable pressure spread through his entire body.

Something had entered him.

Not a curse.

Not a blessing.

An inheritance.

Deep within the darkness behind his closed eyes, countless crimson symbols assembled themselves into an endless horizon of floating light.

Something ancient had awakened.

Something patient.

Something that had been waiting for only one person.

Far beyond the reach of ordinary sight, where history itself seemed to sleep, an unseen presence stirred for the first time in ages.

The Legacy had found its Heir.

And somewhere in the darkness beyond human understanding, unseen eyes slowly opened.
