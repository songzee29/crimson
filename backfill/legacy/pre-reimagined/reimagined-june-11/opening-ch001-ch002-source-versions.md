# The Crimson Legacy  
## Chapter 1 — “The Silent Chamber”

The ruins beneath New Orleans didn’t feel abandoned. They felt sealed—like a wound the city kept hidden beneath layers of concrete, water, and time. Aidan moved through the flooded subway tunnels with a quiet purpose, the kind that comes from being driven by something he couldn't name.

He wasn’t supposed to be down here. That was the first thing he told himself as he stepped through a cracked wall, past old rail ties, and into a chamber that shouldn’t exist.

At the center of that chamber, sitting on a stone pedestal, was a crimson book. Not dusty. Not worn. Just crimson, like fresh blood spilled on perfect leather. His pulse quickened without a reason he could explain. He hesitated, but his fingers reached out anyway.

The moment his fingertips brushed the cover, a sharp, almost electric sting flared across his palm. A thin line of crimson blood welled up, and as it dropped onto the book, the air shifted. Not with a breeze—just an absence of sound, like the world was holding its breath.

Aidan stepped back, heart pounding, as the book began to open slowly—no wind, no magic trick—just the weight of inevitability settling around him. And then, from nowhere, a voice—low, distant, like a fragment of a dream trying to take form.

“BLOODLINE DETECTED.”

Aidan staggered back, eyes wide. “Who’s there?” he whispered, but the voice didn’t come from a place outside—this voice was inside him, like a shadow left over from a dream.

He shook his head, trying to dismiss the fear. “No, no, this can’t be real.” But the book didn’t stop. It opened wider, and in that moment, Aidan saw something he couldn’t name—lines of power, invisible threads stitched through life, through blood, waiting.

And then it closed again.

Aidan sank to his knees, hand still shaking from the cut, and for the first time in his life, he felt like something had chosen him—not as a gift, but as a judgment.

He didn’t know it yet, but he had just stepped into a system that would demand something from him he wasn’t ready to give.

And Rez? He stayed asleep inside the book, waiting for the moment when Aidan would need him most—after the line was crossed.
