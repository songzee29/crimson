## Chapter 113 — The Black Gate

Aiden didn’t hesitate. The vision from the stone still burned in his mind: that black gate, that symbol—the missing branch erased from the Hall of Names. He stood, his spine straight, and resolved: he had to go back to the forgotten trail—no detours.

Rez appeared beside him, seriousness in his eyes. “Aiden, the Black Gate was never meant to be found. Whoever erased it, they wanted it buried forever.”

Aiden didn’t look away. “I’m done letting people hide what matters. I’m not afraid of what’s waiting behind that gate.”

They moved fast. The trail south twisted through overgrown brush, but Aiden’s steps were sure. The Codex barely flickered, and Rez stayed quiet, watching the shadows. Finally, after hours, the trees parted, and there it was—a gate carved in obsidian stone, ancient symbols still faintly glowing.

Aiden stepped forward, and the gate creaked open—no magic, no fanfare—just an invitation to truth. And as Aiden crossed the threshold, he knew this wasn’t just a trial of power—it was a trial of memory. And he was ready.

## Chapter 114 — The Gatekeeper’s Secret

As Aiden stepped inside the black gate, the air shifted. A cold mist clung to his skin, and the symbols on the stone began to glow, pulsing like a heartbeat. Rez hovered beside him, eyes narrow, whispering, “This is where the Purifiers left their mark—this is the place they almost buried the truth.”

Aiden nodded, his fingers tightening around the Codex. The mist parted, revealing a narrow hallway, walls lined with symbols that seemed to whisper their own warnings. Every step echoed like a heartbeat. Aiden felt a strange pull in his chest—a familiar tug—like someone was calling him forward.

At the end of the hallway, a figure emerged. Cloaked in shadow, the Gatekeeper stood tall, his face hidden beneath a hood, but his voice rang out clear: “The truth you seek was never meant for human eyes, but you will choose whether it rises or falls.”

Aiden didn’t flinch. He stepped forward. “I don’t fear the truth. I fear never knowing.”

The Gatekeeper nodded once, and with a wave of his hand, the darkness behind him twisted, revealing a memory—not a legend, but a moment Aiden had never seen: Zee as a boy, sitting alone beneath a tree, practicing small spells—just a child learning not to be afraid.

Tears stung Aiden’s eyes. For the first time, he saw that he wasn’t alone. The past wasn’t a burden; it was a path. And the Gatekeeper faded, leaving him in that memory—just a boy with a book, still finding his power.
