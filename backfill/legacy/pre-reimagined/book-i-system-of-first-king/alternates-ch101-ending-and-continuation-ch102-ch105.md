### Chapter 102 — Return to Ember Hollow

The journey down from the floating island felt longer than Aiden expected. Each step off the sky staircase brought him back to the real world—a world that, despite its quiet, was still filled with challenges.

When he arrived at Ember Hollow, the village was still waking up. Smoke curled from chimneys. The boy he saved, Eli, ran past, his wooden sword still in hand. Aiden crouched down, ruffled Eli’s hair, and smiled. “You’ve been practicing, huh?”

Eli nodded, wide-eyed. “I’m going to be a protector like you someday.”

Something swelled in Aiden’s chest—a mixture of pride and fear. He hadn’t just saved the village; he had reminded someone else that courage could be learned.

Rez floated beside him, his usual grin gone, but his eyes steady. “You did good, kid,” Rez said quietly. “But remember: this isn’t about being perfect. It’s about what you do when the world looks away.”

Aiden stood up, feeling the weight of his next steps. He didn’t need a crown or a title—he needed to choose, every day, who he was becoming.

### Chapter 103 — The Ashborn Bloodline’s Warning

That night, as Aiden sat by the fire, the Codex hummed again, but this time the message was different. It wasn’t a quest. It wasn’t a reward. It was a warning. The Ashborn line was being watched. Not just by him—by something older, something waiting in the shadows.

Rez’s expression darkened. “They’re still out there, Aiden. The Purifiers. The ones who tried to erase your line.”

### Chapter 105 — The Ashfire Pact

By nightfall, Aiden reached the edge of the forest. The trees stood tall, ancient, and silent. As he lit a small fire, the Codex flickered again. This time, a new quest appeared, but it wasn’t just about leveling—it was a pact. Aiden was asked to bind himself to the legacy—not as a king, but as a bridge. To remember that the Ashfire he wielded was never just for destruction—it was for balance.

Aiden took a deep breath. He touched the Codex, and a warm pulse ran up his arm. In that moment, he made a promise—not to conquer fate, but to walk beside it, guiding others when he could, and never forgetting that he was still a boy who once had nothing but a book.
