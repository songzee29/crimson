Grey Hollow burned long after the hunters disappeared.

Villagers formed desperate lines, passing buckets from the river while Blackwood’s witches fought to keep the flames from spreading into the surrounding forest.

The battle had ended.

The damage had not.

Aidan stood in the center of the ruined village.

His coat was blackened with soot.

The bandages beneath it had turned crimson again.

Every breath scraped against fractured ribs.

He barely noticed.

A child tugged gently on his sleeve.

“Mister…”

He looked down.

The little girl he had pulled from the burning house held a carved wooden horse with one broken leg.

“My daddy…”

Her voice trembled.

“…he didn’t make it.”

Aidan looked toward the rows of bodies covered with blankets.

His answer caught in his throat.

He knelt despite the pain.

“I’m sorry.”

The words felt empty.

The girl nodded anyway.

As she walked away…

Aidan remained kneeling in the ash.

The hunters had not destroyed Grey Hollow.

They had left enough survivors to remember.

That realization settled heavily inside him.

…

Blackwood returned before sunset.

No one spoke during the journey home.

Not because they had failed.

Because saving people no longer felt like victory.

Only surviving another day.

As they crossed the academy gates, Helena intercepted Aidan before he reached the infirmary.

“Sit.”

“I’m fine.”

“You collapsed twice in Grey Hollow.”

“I stood back up.”

“You shouldn’t have been able to.”

She pulled away the bandages around his ribs.

Fresh blood seeped through reopened wounds.

The bruising had spread farther across his chest.

His shoulder still hadn’t fully healed.

Helena looked genuinely unsettled.

“Your body refuses to stop…”

She whispered.

“…but it also refuses to recover.”

Aidan looked away.

He had begun noticing that too.

…

That night Rowan ordered every patrol doubled.

Every supply route changed.

Every village surrounding Blackwood received emergency warning runes.

The academy adapted again.

Yet Rowan knew.

The hunters would adapt too.

…

Near midnight…

Aidan stood atop the southern wall.

His hearing reached far beyond the academy.

Wind.

Branches.

Owls.

Running water.

Then…

Footsteps.

One set.

Moving deliberately through the forest.

Not hiding.

Watching.

Aidan slipped from the wall without alerting anyone.

His footsteps crossed the earth almost silently.

He followed.

Not recklessly.

Carefully.

The trail led to an ancient standing stone overlooking the valley.

No hunter waited there.

Only another carving.

Fresh.

Seven symbols.

Then nine.

Now…

Ten.

Each beneath the last.

The final mark had only just been carved.

The hunters weren’t recording victories.

They were recording evolution.

A faint sound reached him.

Stone against stone.

Behind him.

He turned.

Nothing.

Too slow.

Something struck the back of his injured shoulder.

Pain exploded through his body.

He stumbled forward.

Thin black cords tightened around both wrists.

A trap.

The Trapper had returned.

The cords weren’t trying to kill him.

Only delay him.

Aidan twisted.

The bindings tightened.

His breathing became uneven.

The second heartbeat answered.

THUMP.

His body reacted.

He dropped his weight instead of pulling away.

The cords loosened for one heartbeat.

Enough.

He slipped free.

The moment he did…

Three pale hunters stepped from the darkness.

Not attacking.

Observing.

The Observer.

The Trapper.

Another he had never seen before.

It carried no claws.

Only long bone-like hooks attached to chains wrapped around its arms.

It slowly dragged one hook across the ground.

Studying distance.

Timing.

Spacing.

Aidan understood immediately.

It wasn’t measuring him.

It was measuring where he would dodge.

The hunters had begun predicting his instincts.

Without warning the chained hooks flew toward him.

Not where he stood.

Where he was about to move.

The first hook tore through his sleeve.

The second ripped across his side.

The third barely missed his throat.

He changed direction instantly.

The fourth hook was already waiting.

His body rolled beneath it by instinct.

The hunter tilted its head.

Learning.

Again.

Far away…

A crimson signal rune ignited atop Blackwood’s wall.

Someone had noticed he was gone.

The hunters noticed it too.

They withdrew immediately.

No pursuit.

No final attack.

Only another lesson collected.

Aidan stood alone in the forest, blood running steadily from a fresh wound across his side.

He pressed one hand against it.

Warm.

The scent reached him.

For one dangerous heartbeat…

He wanted to leave his hand there.

Instead he tore it away.

His breathing became ragged.

“You don’t control me.”

The words sounded less certain than before.
