They emerged into a maintenance corridor that smelled like rust and old water.

The moment Lyra stepped inside, she stopped.

“This is wrong,” she said.

“What do you mean wrong?”

“This section shouldn’t exist.”

Aidan looked around.

It looked like every other maintenance tunnel he had seen so far.

But Witch Sight disagreed.

The walls flickered faintly, like the memory of them wasn’t fully stable.

Aidan placed a hand on the wall.

It felt real.

And unreal at the same time.

Behind them, the corridor entrance sealed shut.

Not slammed.

Not closed.

Simply no longer there.

Lyra turned slowly.

“They’ve locked us in a folded space.”

Aidan stared at her.

“A what?”

“A rewritten layer,” she said. “A place the city used to be before it got erased.”

The air grew colder.

A faint sound echoed ahead.

Footsteps.

Slow.

Measured.

Unhurried.

Aidan exhaled sharply.

“They’re here.”

Lyra didn’t answer.

Because she was already looking at something further down the corridor.

Something Aidan couldn’t see at first.

Then Witch Sight activated again.

And the corridor split open in his perception.

Dozens of layered versions of the same hallway overlapped.

And in each one—

a hunter stood.

All waiting.

All watching.

Then, one voice spoke from everywhere at once.

“You cannot leave a place that remembers you incorrectly.”

Aidan’s chest tightened.

Lyra whispered, barely audible:

“They’re not hunting you.”

A pause.

“They’re cataloging you.”
