The ruins beneath the city had started to feel quieter.

Not peaceful—just wrong in a different way.

Like the world had learned Aidan was listening now… and decided to whisper instead of shout.

He sat alone in the remains of an old transit tunnel, where broken concrete ribs arched overhead like the skeleton of something that once carried thousands of lives at once. Water dripped somewhere far in the dark, each drop landing like a metronome counting down nothing in particular.

Aidan pressed his back against the wall.

He didn’t move much anymore.

Moving made it worse.

Breathing made it worse.

Even thinking made it worse.

Because thinking always eventually led to the same place.

*Hunger.*

Not the kind that came from missing meals.

This wasn’t stomach hunger.

This was deeper.

Like something inside him had learned a new language and refused to stop speaking it.

Rez’s voice had been quieter lately. Not gone—just… careful.

> “You’re suppressing it too hard.”

Aidan didn’t answer.

His jaw tightened instead.

Because Rez was right, and he hated that Rez was right.

A faint sound echoed through the tunnel.

Footsteps.

Aidan’s head lifted slightly before he even decided to move.

That was new.

His body reacted before he did now.

A slow figure appeared at the edge of the broken light—someone from the surface tunnels. A scavenger, maybe. Hood up. Carrying supplies. Just another survivor trying not to become a corpse in a world that didn’t care about survivors anymore.

A normal person.

That should have meant nothing.

But it didn’t.

Because the moment Aidan saw them, his throat tightened.

Not emotionally.

Physically.

Like something inside him had just recognized a meal.

Aidan stood up too fast.

The movement made his vision sharpen.

Too sharp.

He could hear the person’s heartbeat now.

Not metaphorically.

Actually hear it.

Thump… thump… thump…

And beneath it, something else—

Warm.

Alive.

*Calling.*

Aidan took a step back instinctively.

“No,” he whispered under his breath. “No—no, I’m not doing this.”

Rez didn’t respond immediately.

That silence was worse than anything.

The scavenger paused.

They noticed him now.

“Hey—are you alright?” the stranger called out cautiously. Not fear yet. Just concern. The kind people still had before the world trained it out of them.

Aidan swallowed hard.

His hands were shaking.

He clenched them into fists so tight his nails dug into his palms.

“I’m fine,” he said.

His voice didn’t sound like his anymore.

It sounded… strained. Controlled. Fragile.

The heartbeat got louder.

Thump. Thump. Thump.

Aidan turned his head slightly away, like that would help.

It didn’t.

Because now the scent was there too.

Blood beneath skin.

Life beneath warmth.

His body reacted with a sudden, violent clarity.

Not hunger anymore.

*Need.*

Aidan stepped backward again.

His back hit the wall.

Trapped.

“No,” he said again, sharper this time. “Stay back.”

The scavenger hesitated.

“I’m just trying to—”

Aidan flinched.

Because the words blurred.

Not what they said.

What they *meant.*

His mind translated them wrong now.

Friendly words became noise.

Noise became weakness.

Weakness became something his body didn’t want to tolerate anymore.

Rez’s voice finally cut in—but it was different now.

Lower.

Careful.

> “Aidan… don’t push it down too far.”

“I’m not—” Aidan started.

But he stopped.

Because his teeth ached.

That was new.

A sharp, unbearable pressure behind his jaw like something trying to form itself through bone.

The hunger surged.

Not like a wave.

Like a break.

Something inside him stopped asking permission.

The scavenger took a cautious step forward.

“Do you need help?”

That was the moment.

Not because it was dangerous.

Because it was kind.

And kindness, right now, felt unbearable.

Aidan’s vision snapped slightly out of focus.

Then back in.

Too clear.

Too alive.

The heartbeat became everything.

Everything else disappeared behind it.

Rez’s voice cracked slightly:

> “Aidan—fight it.”

“I am,” Aidan whispered.

But his body was already moving.

One step forward.

Then another.

Slow at first.

Like he was walking through water.

His hand lifted without his permission.

He saw it happen like it belonged to someone else.

The scavenger finally looked afraid.

“Hey—what are you—”

Aidan stopped.

For a fraction of a second.

He was still there.

Still him.

Still fighting.

His eyes widened slightly as realization hit full force.

“No,” he whispered again—but this time it wasn’t denial.

It was pleading.

His hand trembled mid-air.

Every muscle in his body shaking like it was trying to hold two versions of him together at once.

The hunger screamed.

Not sound.

Instinct.

*Take. Take. Take.*

Rez’s voice almost broke:

> “Aidan, don’t let it decide for you—”

That’s when it happened.

Not a choice.

Not a decision.

A collapse.

His body moved first.

Faster than thought.

Faster than regret.

Aidan crossed the distance in an instant he didn’t experience like motion—only like *arrival.*

The scavenger barely had time to gasp before Aidan’s hand caught them.

And then—

everything inside him snapped into focus.

Too late.

Too clear.

He felt the pulse under skin like a drum against his palm.

Warm.

Alive.

And the hunger finally stopped being something he resisted.

It became something he understood.

Aidan’s breath hitched.

“I’m sorry,” he tried to say.

But it didn’t come out as words.

It came out as something fractured.

Then he bit down.

The world didn’t go silent.

It went *loud.*

Heartbeat exploding into sensation.

Blood rushing like fire through every sense he had.

The scavenger struggled at first—shock, confusion, pain—but it didn’t last long.

Not because Aidan was strong.

Because he couldn’t stop.

Not anymore.

Not once it started.

Rez didn’t speak.

Not immediately.

For the first time, there was no commentary.

Just silence.

Aidan felt everything.

Too much.

Too real.

The moment stretched—wrong, endless, irreversible.

Until finally—

nothing was left moving.

The body in his arms went still.

Not faint.

Not unconscious.

Still.

Aidan stepped back slowly.

His hands dropped.

He stared at them like they didn’t belong to him anymore.

Blood dripped from his mouth onto the cracked concrete.

One drop.

Then another.

Then silence.

He couldn’t breathe right.

Not because of damage.

Because his body didn’t know what to do after crossing something it had no name for before now.

Rez’s voice returned—but it wasn’t guiding him anymore.

It was quieter.

Heavier.

> “That’s it…”

A pause.

Long.

> “…that’s the line.”

Aidan stumbled backward until his back hit the wall again.

He slid down slowly, like his legs forgot how to exist.

His hands shook.

Not from hunger now.

From understanding.

“I didn’t—” he whispered.

He tried again.

“I didn’t mean—”

But the sentence broke apart halfway through.

Because his body remembered.

Even if his mind didn’t want to.

Rez finally spoke again.

Not soft.

Not kind.

Just real.

> “I know.”

Another pause.

> “But it still happened.”

Aidan looked up at the tunnel ceiling.

Like maybe if he looked long enough, the world would reset itself.

It didn’t.

Instead, something else arrived.

Not sound.

Not light.

Just presence.

Deep inside his mind, like the system had been waiting for the moment he stopped pretending he was safe.

And then it spoke.

Cold.

Final.

> **“VAMPIRE CURSE CONDITION SATISFIED.”**

Aidan closed his eyes.

And for the first time since this began…

he didn’t feel like he was becoming something.

He felt like something had already finished deciding what he was.
