The masked hunter didn’t move like a man preparing to fight.

He moved like something that already knew the outcome.

Slow. Certain. Patient.

The black blade in his hand hummed softly, etched symbols pulsing faintly as if recognizing Aidan on sight.

Lyra stepped in front of Aidan again, but this time her stance was different. Not protective in a simple way—protective like someone who had done this before and knew exactly how badly it could end.

“Aidan,” she said under her breath, “don’t look into their markings.”

“Why?” he asked.

“Because they don’t just cut flesh,” she whispered. “They overwrite memory.”

Aidan swallowed.

The Veil hunter tilted his head slightly.

“Subject is stabilized,” he said calmly. “Erasure resistance confirmed.”

The second hunter stepped forward.

“Primary target deviation detected,” he added. “System response active.”

Aidan frowned.

System response?

That wasn’t his System.

Or at least it didn’t feel like it.

A cold line of text flickered in his vision.

> SYSTEM NOTICE  
> UNKNOWN AUTHORITY INTERFERENCE DETECTED

Then it vanished.

Lyra noticed his expression change.

“They’re talking to something inside you,” she said quietly.

“I don’t know what they’re talking to,” Aidan replied.

The first hunter raised his blade.

And the station shifted.

Not physically—but conceptually.

The lights above them dimmed like reality itself was lowering its attention.

“This is your only warning,” the hunter said. “Come quietly, or be rewritten.”

Aidan took a step back without realizing it.

Not fear.

Instinct.

Something deep in him reacted to the word rewritten more than anything else.

Lyra grabbed his wrist.

“We run,” she said.

Aidan hesitated.

And that hesitation was enough.

The third hunter moved.

He didn’t cross the space between them.

He erased it.

One moment he was across the platform.

The next he was in front of Aidan.

The blade came down.
