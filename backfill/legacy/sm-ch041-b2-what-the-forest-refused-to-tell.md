# Chapter Forty-One: What the Forest Refused to Tell

No one spoke on the journey back.

The crater remained behind them.

The empty villages remained behind them.

The questions did not.

Aidan walked at the front without realizing it.

Not because he wanted to lead.

Because every few minutes...

His head turned before anyone else's.

Listening.

Waiting.

The northern forest no longer felt like wilderness.

It felt like something watching without revealing itself.

...

Blackwood Academy received them after sunset.

No celebration waited.

Only more reports.

Another caravan overdue.

A patrol that had missed two scheduled signals.

Three villages requesting healers after an unknown sickness spread through livestock.

None of it appeared connected.

Yet...

None of it felt accidental.

The strategy hall slowly filled.

Maps spread farther than ever before.

The attacks no longer formed a simple pattern.

They overlapped.

Interrupted one another.

Forced impossible priorities.

Elias quietly moved carved markers across the table.

"If these reports are related..."

He stopped.

"They shouldn't make sense."

Cassian frowned.

"If they aren't related..."

"They shouldn't happen this close together," Rowan finished.

Silence settled over the room.

No one knew which answer frightened them more.

...

Aidan studied every report.

Not reading.

Looking.

Something felt...

Wrong.

He couldn't explain it.

Only feel it.

His eyes stopped on one forgotten note written in the margin of an old patrol report.

**No signs of struggle.**

Another.

**Nothing stolen.**

Another.

**Everyone simply... gone.**

He closed his eyes.

The words refused to fit together.

Not because they were impossible.

Because they were incomplete.

Someone...

Or something...

Was always one step ahead.

But how?

He immediately hated the direction of his thoughts.

The whisper returned.

*You have your traitor to thank for this.*

No.

He folded the report closed.

He would not allow doubt to become accusation.

Not without proof.

Never without proof.

...

That night...

The academy slept in uneasy silence.

Aidan did not.

He walked alone beyond the outer wall.

Not far.

Just enough to hear the forest without hearing the academy.

Wind moved through ancient branches.

Leaves shifted.

Somewhere distant...

A branch snapped.

His eyes opened.

Nothing.

He waited.

Minutes passed.

No second sound came.

He almost turned back.

Then...

He noticed footprints.

One set.

Fresh.

Leading toward the trees.

None returning.

He followed.

Not because curiosity won.

Because someone might still be alive.

The trail ended beside an abandoned shrine swallowed by moss and vines.

No body.

No struggle.

Only a weathered stone bowl resting at the center.

Inside it...

A single academy badge.

Clean.

Untouched.

He recognized the crest.

Northern patrol.

Missing three days.

No blood.

No answer.

Only another question.

His hearing stretched through the darkness.

Nothing answered.

Even the forest seemed unwilling to speak.

Aidan slowly picked up the badge.

It was still warm.

His heartbeat quickened.

Someone...

Had left only moments before.

He searched the trees.

The shadows remained still.

Too still.

His instincts screamed.

Leave.

Now.

He obeyed without understanding why.

The instant he stepped away from the shrine...

Something heavy struck exactly where he had been standing.

Stone shattered.

Dust erupted.

By the time he looked back...

Whatever had fallen had already vanished into the darkness.

No roar.

No footsteps.

No pursuit.

Only silence.

Waiting.

Watching.

Learning.

Aidan backed away slowly.

For the first time in years...

He chose not to follow.

Not because he lacked courage.

Because he had finally learned that not every unanswered question could be chased.

Some wanted to be chased.

And that made them the most dangerous of all.

As he disappeared back toward Blackwood...

The abandoned shrine stood empty once more.

A cool wind turned the forgotten academy badge over in the dirt.

By morning...

Even it would be gone.
