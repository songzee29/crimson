# Chapter Fifty-Two: The Village That Could Not Be Saved

The ride back to Blackwood never happened.

A second messenger found them before they reached the academy.

His horse stumbled through the mud, collapsed, and slid several feet before stopping.

The young scout rolled free.

His face was gray.

"Master Rowan..."

He could barely breathe.

"...Red Creek..."

Aidan's chest tightened.

"What happened?"

The scout swallowed hard.

"We were too late."

Silence.

No one asked another question.

They already understood.

...

Red Creek lay another hour west.

The smell reached them long before the village came into view.

Smoke.

Wet ash.

Burned timber.

Then...

Blood.

Too much.

Aidan's stomach twisted.

His body instinctively turned toward the strongest scent before he forced himself to look away.

The village gates hung open.

Homes still smoldered.

No voices.

No cries for help.

Only the crackle of dying fire.

Malik stepped over a shattered cart.

"We missed them..."

Cassian's jaw tightened.

"No."

His eyes swept the empty streets.

"They wanted us to."

Helena hurried from house to house.

Searching.

Calling.

Finding no answers.

Only the wounded who had hidden beneath collapsed floors and inside root cellars.

Too frightened to emerge.

Too late to save those who hadn't.

Aidan knelt beside an elderly man leaning against a burned wall.

The man's breathing came in shallow gasps.

He reached weakly for Aidan's sleeve.

"You came..."

His voice barely carried.

"...just..."

A cough interrupted him.

"...too late."

The words struck harder than any blow.

Aidan lowered his head.

"I'm sorry."

The old man smiled sadly.

"I know..."

His hand slipped from Aidan's sleeve.

It never moved again.

Aidan remained kneeling long after Helena quietly closed the man's eyes.

Another village.

Another apology.

Another life that had ended before he arrived.

The cost of surviving today...

Was knowing survival hadn't been enough.

...

They buried the dead before sunset.

No one spoke during the work.

The silence felt heavier than grief.

As they prepared to leave...

Aidan noticed something carved into the blackened remains of the village well.

Not words.

Only another symbol.

An eye.

Watching.

He stared at it for a long moment.

The second heartbeat answered beneath his own.

Steady.

Patient.

Waiting.

He struck the carving once with the end of his staff.

The stone cracked.

The symbol disappeared.

It changed nothing.

# Chapter Fifty-Three: The Breaking Point That Wasn't

Blackwood Academy received them after dark.

No sooner had the gates closed than the eastern warning runes flared crimson.

Not one.

All of them.

The alarm bell thundered across the academy.

Students ran toward shelters.

Instructors rushed for the walls.

Rowan looked toward the exhausted rescue party.

"You've barely returned."

Aidan drew a slow breath.

"I know."

His body protested the movement.

His ribs burned.

His side throbbed.

The wound in his shoulder had started bleeding again during the ride home.

He tightened the bandage with one hand and kept walking.

...

The eastern wall shook.

Not from impact.

From below.

Stone cracked beneath the defenders' feet.

Everyone spread out instinctively.

Good.

They had learned.

The ground erupted anyway.

Not beneath the largest group.

Beneath the smallest.

Three young apprentices disappeared into collapsing earth before anyone could reach them.

"Aidan!"

He was already moving.

The hole widened as loose stone continued falling inward.

He dove without thinking.

Cassian cursed.

"No!"

Too late.

Aidan vanished into the darkness.

The fall ended hard.

His injured shoulder struck first.

Pain exploded across his chest.

His vision blurred.

He forced himself upright.

The apprentices were alive.

Terrified.

One had a broken ankle.

Another bled from the forehead.

The third simply stared at him in shock.

"It's alright."

His voice remained calm.

"We're getting out."

Above...

The opening continued collapsing.

Aidan looked around.

No obvious way back.

No easy climb.

He heard Rowan shouting somewhere far overhead.

Too distant.

The apprentices looked to him.

Waiting.

Trusting.

His body had begun trembling from exhaustion.

He ignored it.

He found a narrow crack leading upward.

Too small for adults.

Large enough for children.

"You first."

They hesitated.

"What about you?"

"I'll be right behind you."

It wasn't a promise he knew he could keep.

One by one, he lifted them toward the opening.

Each movement pulled against torn muscles and fractured ribs.

By the time the last apprentice reached Cassian's outstretched hand above...

Aidan's strength failed.

The wall gave way.

Stone crashed down.

He threw one arm over his head.

Rock struck his back.

Another slammed into his side.

The tunnel collapsed around him.

Darkness swallowed everything.

For a long moment...

There was no sound.

Then...

Very faintly...

He heard voices above.

Digging.

Calling his name.

Again.

His eyes slowly closed.

He wanted to stand.

His body wouldn't answer.

His thoughts drifted.

*You came... just... too late.*

The old man's voice returned.

Then another.

The frightened child from Grey Hollow.

The instructors buried at Blackwood.

The ferryman.

The missing patrols.

The sleeping children.

Every face.

Every failure.

Every apology.

His breathing slowed.

The second heartbeat remained.

Steady.

Patient.

Waiting.

Far above...

Cassian tore at the fallen stone with his bare hands.

Malik ignored the agony in his ruined leg.

Lyra's voice broke as she called Aidan's name again and again.

Rowan never stopped giving orders.

Helena prepared the infirmary before they had even found him.

No one left.

No one surrendered.

But beneath the mountain...

For the first time in years...

Aidan allowed himself one terrifying thought.

Maybe surviving wasn't enough anymore.
