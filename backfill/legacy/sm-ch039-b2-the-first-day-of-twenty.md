# Chapter Thirty-Nine: The First Day of Twenty

The rider died before sunrise.

Helena did everything she could.

She sealed the wounds.

Stopped the bleeding.

Pushed restorative magic beyond what she normally dared.

It wasn't enough.

His last breath escaped while Rowan quietly closed his eyes.

The room remained silent.

No one asked for his name.

They already knew what mattered.

His message.

"They're back."

...

The map stretched across the strategy table once more.

This time...

The northern frontier looked different.

Seven villages had stopped responding within the past week.

Not destroyed.

Silent.

Supply caravans never returned.

Messengers vanished.

Watchtowers reported strange lights moving through the forests after sunset.

No bodies.

No survivors.

Only absence.

"They're changing again," Elias said quietly.

"They've stopped announcing themselves."

Cassian rested his good hand on the edge of the table.

"They want us guessing."

"No."

Aidan's voice remained calm.

"They want us searching."

Everyone looked toward him.

He traced a slow line across the northern map.

"They're pulling us farther from Blackwood."

Rowan nodded.

"But if we ignore it..."

"People die."

The answer came before the question finished.

...

By midday...

The rescue force crossed into the northern woods.

The trees felt older here.

Denser.

Even sunlight struggled to reach the ground.

Aidan stopped walking.

His head tilted slightly.

"What?"

Malik whispered.

"No birds."

Everyone listened.

Nothing.

Not a single song.

Not a single insect.

Only wind.

Cassian slowly unsheathed his sword.

"They cleared the forest."

Aidan shook his head.

"No."

His eyes drifted toward the branches.

"The forest cleared itself."

The realization chilled them.

The wildlife had fled.

Long before Blackwood arrived.

...

Half an hour later...

They found the first village.

Untouched.

Doors stood open.

Meals remained on tables.

Water still boiled inside abandoned kettles.

Blankets lay scattered across floors.

Families had vanished in the middle of ordinary life.

No blood.

No struggle.

Nothing.

Lyra knelt beside a child's wooden toy.

"It doesn't make sense."

"It does."

Everyone turned toward Aidan.

"They wanted everyone alive."

No one wanted to ask why.

...

A faint cry echoed through the empty streets.

Help.

Malik immediately ran toward it.

"Wait!"

Aidan shouted.

Too late.

The ground disappeared beneath Malik's feet.

The concealed pit opened without warning.

Malik twisted instinctively, catching the edge with both hands instead of falling onto the sharpened stakes below.

Cassian lunged forward.

His injured arm buckled beneath Malik's weight.

Aidan reached them first.

His body reacted before thought.

One hand caught Cassian's shoulder.

The other seized Malik's wrist.

Every muscle screamed.

His ribs felt as though they were tearing apart.

Still...

He pulled.

Together they dragged Malik onto solid ground.

No one spoke.

They simply stared into the trap.

It hadn't been built to kill one person.

It had been built to make someone else save them.

The hunters had started predicting compassion itself.

...

As they left the village...

Aidan glanced once over his shoulder.

High above...

Carved into the trunk of an ancient oak...

Another symbol waited.

An eye.

Watching.

# Chapter Forty: The Village That Was Waiting

Night fell before they reached the second village.

Unlike the first...

Lights still burned.

Smoke drifted peacefully from chimneys.

Someone laughed inside one of the homes.

Malik frowned.

"They're alive."

"No."

Aidan stopped walking.

"They want us to think they are."

His hearing stretched through the darkness.

The laughter repeated.

Exactly the same.

Not similar.

Identical.

Again.

Then again.

A recording.

Not a person.

The hunters had learned something else.

Hope could be bait.

Cassian quietly circled left.

Lyra and Elias moved right.

Rowan and Helena remained with the rear guard.

No one approached the houses directly.

Aidan advanced alone.

Each silent step carried him closer to the village square.

The laughter stopped.

Every door opened at once.

Not by hands.

By hidden ropes.

Wind carried them inward.

Every home stood empty.

The tables remained set.

Beds remained warm.

Dinner still waited inside bowls.

As if every family had simply...

Stood up.

And walked away together.

The smell reached Aidan first.

Not blood.

Fear.

Old fear.

Enough people had panicked here to leave it lingering in the air.

"They were taken yesterday."

Helena looked toward him.

"You can tell?"

Aidan hesitated.

"I..."

He couldn't explain it.

He simply knew.

The realization unsettled him more each time.

A sharp whistle echoed across the rooftops.

One.

Then another.

Not hunters.

Signals.

The Commander was directing something.

Aidan's instincts screamed.

"Out!"

The order came before he consciously understood why.

Everyone moved.

Instantly.

The entire center of the village collapsed inward.

Hidden tunnels swallowed the square in a deafening roar.

Homes slid into the earth.

The church bell tower cracked in half.

Dust erupted toward the night sky.

Aidan shoved Lyra clear of the collapsing ground.

The effort twisted his side violently.

Fresh pain tore through old scars.

His bandages immediately grew warm.

Blood again.

Always blood.

When the dust settled...

The village no longer existed.

Only an enormous crater remained.

The hunters had not hidden beneath it.

They had hollowed it out.

Months earlier.

Waiting.

Planning.

Learning.

The Commander had thought far beyond the next battle.

Rowan stared into the abyss.

"This wasn't for us."

Aidan slowly shook his head.

"No."

His tired eyes followed the tunnel leading north beneath the collapsed village.

"It was built long before we arrived."

Cassian's expression hardened.

"They're not reacting to us anymore."

"They're preparing for us."

Far beyond the ruined village...

Standing upon a ridge beneath the moon...

The Commander watched Blackwood's rescue force leave another battlefield alive.

It never smiled.

It never celebrated.

It simply turned...

And walked deeper into the northern wilderness.

The next stage of the hunt had already begun before this one ended.

And for the first time in two years...

Aidan realized something that stole another piece of certainty from him.

The hunters were no longer merely surviving alongside Blackwood.

They were building a war.
