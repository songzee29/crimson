# Chapter Forty-Eight: The Bell Before Dawn

The emergency bell did not stop.

One strike.

Then another.

Then another.

Not frantic.

Measured.

Urgent.

The sound followed Blackwood's defenders all the way back through the academy gates.

Students were already evacuating toward reinforced shelters.

Healers hurried stretchers across the courtyard.

No one panicked anymore.

They had learned how to survive fear.

Rowan intercepted the first messenger.

"What happened?"

The young guard struggled to catch his breath.

"The western reservoir..."

He swallowed.

"...it's breaking."

Helena's face hardened.

"The reservoir?"

"If it goes..."

Elias looked toward the academy map.

"...half the lower district floods."

No hunters.

No monsters.

No visible enemy.

Only another disaster demanding every remaining ounce of strength.

...

The western reservoir had been carved into the mountainside generations before Blackwood Academy existed.

Now...

A jagged fracture split its ancient retaining wall.

Water forced itself through the crack with increasing pressure.

Engineers shouted over one another.

"If this section fails—"

"It all goes!"

Aidan climbed the damaged wall without waiting for permission.

His ribs protested immediately.

The wound across his side burned beneath fresh bandages.

He ignored both.

He pressed one hand against the fractured stone.

The crack continued spreading beneath his fingertips.

"This isn't natural."

Rowan looked up.

"What?"

"The pressure."

Aidan closed his eyes.

The stone vibrated differently along different sections.

One place...

The wall sounded hollow.

He struck it once with the butt of his staff.

The sound echoed far deeper than solid stone should have.

"There."

Malik and Cassian immediately moved beside him.

Together they cleared away several loose blocks.

Behind them...

An empty cavity stretched into darkness.

Not ancient erosion.

Something had been dug.

Recently.

No tracks remained.

No tools.

Only another tunnel disappearing beneath the mountain.

Elias looked down into the opening.

"They weakened it from underneath."

No one answered.

There was nothing to add.

The reservoir had not simply begun failing.

Someone...

Or something...

Had made sure it would.

...

Water burst through another section.

The wall lurched violently.

Students cried out below.

Aidan raised both hands.

"Hold."

Crimson light spread across fractured stone.

The crack stopped.

Only barely.

The pressure drove pain through his chest.

His old shoulder injury flared.

Blood trickled from the corner of his mouth.

Still...

He held.

Malik organized workers into evacuation lines.

Lyra directed frightened families away from the valley below.

Cassian descended into the newly exposed tunnel with two instructors.

Not to chase.

To make sure nothing remained beneath them.

Minutes stretched into an hour.

Every heartbeat hurt.

The strain never eased.

Finally...

Engineers reinforced the damaged section with fresh support pillars.

Rowan rested one hand against Aidan's shoulder.

"You can let go."

The Law dissolved.

Aidan staggered backward.

His legs nearly folded beneath him.

He caught himself against broken stone.

The wall stood.

The reservoir held.

Another disaster survived.

Another price paid.

...

Cassian climbed back from the tunnel.

"Nothing."

"No one?"

"No one."

Only silence.

Always silence.

# Chapter Forty-Nine: What Silence Takes

The infirmary smelled of herbs.

Clean bandages.

Fresh rain.

And blood.

Too much blood.

Helena silently cleaned the dried crimson from Aidan's side.

"You tore every stitch."

"I know."

"You've reopened your shoulder."

"I know."

"You've strained the fractures in your ribs again."

"I know."

She finally stopped.

Her hands rested motionless.

"You never ask if you're getting better anymore."

Aidan looked toward the ceiling.

"I don't think that's the question anymore."

The words settled heavily between them.

...

He left before dawn.

As always.

The academy remained asleep.

Or pretending to be.

Aidan crossed the courtyard so quietly that the guards on the western wall never noticed him.

His footsteps no longer announced him.

He rarely noticed that anymore.

The old oak waited beneath a sky without stars.

Clouds hid everything.

He rested one hand against the scarred bark.

Closed his eyes.

Listened.

The academy.

The wind.

Distant rivers.

The forest.

Then...

Nothing.

Again.

That unnatural silence.

Not empty.

Watching.

His breathing slowed.

The second heartbeat answered beneath his own.

THUMP.

Another heartbeat echoed somewhere beyond the northern trees.

Then it vanished.

Not retreating.

Simply...

Gone.

As if whoever had been there had realized Aidan was listening.

He opened his eyes.

Someone else spoke.

Not from the forest.

From behind him.

"You left before breakfast."

Lyra.

He hadn't heard her approach.

She carried two wrapped pieces of bread.

She held one toward him.

"You'll forget again."

He accepted it.

This time...

He actually ate.

Slowly.

Mechanically.

She watched until the last bite disappeared.

Only then did she smile.

"There."

He looked at the empty cloth in his hand.

"I almost forgot."

"I know."

She hesitated.

"You don't have to remember everything alone."

He wanted to answer.

Instead...

His hearing caught another distant sound.

A horn.

Far away.

South this time.

Only one blast.

Then silence.

He and Lyra looked toward the horizon together.

Neither spoke.

They didn't need to.

Another burden had already begun.

Before the wounds from the reservoir could close...

Before the exhaustion could ease...

Before breakfast had even settled...

Blackwood would answer another call.

Aidan quietly adjusted the fresh bandages beneath his coat.

The movement reopened one of the smaller wounds across his side.

He didn't even flinch anymore.

He simply turned toward the southern gate.

And started walking.

Lyra fell into step beside him.

Neither asked whether they were ready.

Readiness had stopped mattering a long time ago.
