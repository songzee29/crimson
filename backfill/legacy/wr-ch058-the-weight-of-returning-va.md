The walk back to Blackwood was slower than the journey out.

Not because the path had changed.

Because Aidan had.

Every few hundred steps he leaned a little harder on Rowan’s staff.

He tried to hide it.

No one pretended not to notice.

Malik quietly shortened the pace.

Cassian walked close enough to catch him if he stumbled.

Lyra never drifted more than a few feet away.

No one mentioned why.

…

The academy gates appeared through the fading fog.

Before anyone could breathe with relief—

The eastern warning rune flashed once.

Then the western.

Then the northern.

Not an alarm.

Three separate reports arriving at once.

Aidan closed his eyes.

“Again…”

A messenger reached Rowan first.

“The northern bridge held.”

A second interrupted him.

“The western grain convoy is overdue.”

A third stumbled into the courtyard.

“The eastern watchfire never lit.”

Three problems.

None could wait.

Elias spread another map across the nearest table.

“If we divide again—”

“They’ll expect it,” Cassian finished.

“If we don’t divide,” Malik said quietly, “someone goes without help.”

There was no right answer.

Only different losses.

…

Aidan’s hearing drifted across the courtyard.

Students.

Rainwater dripping from the roofs.

A frightened horse.

Then—

Someone crying.

A little girl.

Near the infirmary.

He turned before realizing he had moved.

A young apprentice sat beside her injured brother.

The boy’s arm hung awkwardly inside a splint.

“I’m sorry,” she whispered to him.

“I shouldn’t have asked you to climb the wall.”

The boy forced a smile.

“It isn’t your fault.”

Aidan stood watching for a moment.

Neither child noticed him.

His chest tightened.

Not from broken ribs.

From memory.

Every apology sounded the same now.

…

He continued toward the strategy hall.

Helena intercepted him halfway.

“You’re bleeding again.”

He looked down.

The bandages around his side had turned dark.

“I didn’t feel it.”

“I know.”

She guided him toward the infirmary.

He resisted for exactly three steps.

Then stopped.

Not because he agreed.

Because he suddenly felt tired enough to fall where he stood.

…

Helena cut away the soaked bandages.

The room fell silent.

The wound hadn’t merely reopened.

The stitches had torn completely.

She looked at him for a long time before speaking.

“You’ve stopped noticing injuries.”

“I notice.”

“No.”

She gently cleaned dried blood from his side.

“You notice when other people are hurt.”

Her voice became quieter.

“Your own body has become background noise.”

He had no answer.

Because she was right.

…

That evening Rowan made a decision no one expected.

“Aidan stays inside the academy tomorrow.”

The room immediately objected.

“Aidan—”

“I’ll go,” he interrupted.

“No.”

Rowan’s voice carried the same calm certainty it always had.

“This is not punishment.”

“It feels like it.”

“It is necessity.”

Silence.

“You cannot protect anyone if you die walking to the gate.”

Aidan looked away.

His hands slowly tightened.

He understood.

He hated it.

Because somewhere…

Someone needed help.

And he wouldn’t be there.
