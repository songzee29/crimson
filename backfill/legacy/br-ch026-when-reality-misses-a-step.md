Aidan didn’t think.

His body reacted before his mind caught up.

A flicker of Witch Sight cracked open across his vision.

The world split into layered fractures—paths of movement, pressure points, intention threads.

Everything slowed.

Everything except the blade.

It wasn’t just fast.

It didn’t belong to the same layer of motion.

Lyra shouted something, but it was distant now, like sound underwater.

Aidan twisted, barely avoiding the strike. The blade scraped past his side, leaving a burning sensation—not pain exactly, but something colder. Like the moment the world decided he had been marked.

The hunter didn’t look surprised.

“That level of perception… confirmed,” he said.

Aidan staggered back.

“What are you?” Aidan breathed.

The hunter’s mask didn’t move, but the voice behind it felt almost… clinical.

“We are correction,” he said. “You are deviation.”

The station trembled slightly.

Not from force.

From recognition.

Like something inside the underground system was acknowledging a breach.

Lyra pulled Aidan toward the tunnels.

“Now!” she snapped.

They ran.

But as they moved, Aidan noticed something horrifying.

The tunnels behind them were changing.

Doors that weren’t there before appeared.

Old signage rewrote itself.

Paths shifted.

The station was adapting.

Or rewriting itself around them.

“They’re changing the layout,” Aidan said.

“No,” Lyra replied, breath tight. “They’re rewriting what the station was.”

Aidan looked back once.

The hunters weren’t chasing.

They were walking.

Because they didn’t need to run.
