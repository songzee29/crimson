## Continuity decisions supported by the source

### 1. Activation is causal, not clairvoyant

The book is in an untouched room. Aidan must physically touch it and blood must enter the interaction before activation. Only then can the system identify him. “Bloodline detected” cannot appear before a plausible trigger.

### 2. The book is crimson

The black-book wording belongs to superseded early prose. The intended image is an old **crimson** book. Do not normalize the current manuscript back to black because it appears in the earliest draft.

### 3. Curse sequence is Witch → Vampire → Wolf

- **First:** Zee's Witch Curse/System layer.
- **Second:** Vampire Curse, triggered by Aidan's death; its price includes blood hunger and the struggle to retain control.
- **Third:** Wolf Curse, triggered later through a bond/loss event. The author specifically wanted the trigger to be emotionally devastating rather than convenient.

A draft that starts with vampire hunger or skips directly to the vampire event is an alternate or an error relative to this correction.

### 4. Rez remains dormant until the Vampire Curse

Rez is trapped in the book at the beginning. The author repeatedly corrects versions that let him speak too early. Later rules specify that Rez is inside Aidan's head and no one else can see him; Aidan should communicate with Rez mentally so bystanders do not assume he is speaking aloud. The locked timing later named for Rez's awakening is the Vampire Arc, Chapter 7.

### 5. System visibility and identity

The system is intended for Aidan alone. Later refinements allow Lyra to notice that Aidan is looking at something, but no one else should literally see the system. The desired feel is between a game UI and a living legacy built specifically for the heir—not a generic level 1–100 ladder.

### 6. Aidan's inheritance and the hidden-heir reveal

On June 12, the author develops the line “hide him” into the idea that Aidan is Zee's secretly hidden son and heir. A repeated physical tell is Aidan's right leg shaking when he is angry, upset, or stressed, echoing Zee. The author then emphasizes that the power is dangerous: one person should not be able to hold all of it and remain wholly good. The system is a guide because Aidan would not understand the inheritance without it.

These later ideas should be traced to their own source messages before being promoted to the locked canon file; this report records their development, not an independent decree.

## What must not be merged

- The first Chapter 1 versions (“The Boy Who Had Nothing,” “The Thing Under the City,” “The Verdict in the Dark,” “The Thing That Should Not Wake”) are distinct drafts.
- The crimson-book version is a correction to the earlier black-book imagery.
- Chapter 2 versions differ on whether Rez speaks. The speaking version is not current timing.
- Chapter 3 has two source versions with the same title.
- Preserve incomplete source text as incomplete. Do not reconstruct missing endings from memory or a different draft.
- This June 11 restart is not the May 31–June 1 **System of the First King** track and not the historical **The Forgotten Blood** continuity. The latter's House Drakhar belongs to that alternate track; current canon uses **House Darkheart**.

## Broader timeline cues discovered while scanning the same source conversation

The story changes substantially after the opening:
- **June 12:** hidden-son/heir idea and the memory-erasure mystery deepen; the author asks about Lyra discovering what memory was taken.
- **June 16–21:** the reveal and Aidan's movement toward the place where Zee hid him before erasure become active story threads.
- **June 26:** the author refines system visibility and the multi-evolution structure; explicitly warns not to forget Zee's later evolutions and another son after Witch/Vampire/Wolf.
- **June 27:** new friends are introduced to make the Witch Curse trigger gradually; Lyra must not die first; the Vampire Curse is intended to be Aidan's darkest experience, including a humanity-off turn; Rez must remain invisible and be addressed mentally.
- **June 27:** the author notes the story has reached roughly chapters 70–80 while still in the Witch Arc and asks to accelerate pacing without changing the curse mechanics. This is a major pacing directive, not permission to reorder the curses.

These cues are signposts for the next extraction pass. They are not a substitute for archiving each later chapter response and each user correction.
