# Chapter Thirty-Six: The Weight He Chose

Rain fell for six straight days.

Not enough to flood the villages again.

Enough to keep the roads heavy.

Enough to slow supply wagons.

Enough to make every patrol longer.

Enough to remind Blackwood Academy that survival demanded effort even when the hunters remained unseen.

The silence should have brought relief.

Instead...

It only made everyone wait.

...

Aidan had not entered his room in two days.

Rowan found him asleep in the strategy hall.

Not in a bed.

At the table.

Still sitting upright.

Maps spread beneath one arm.

His other hand still rested on a report from Grey Hollow.

The candle beside him had burned itself out hours earlier.

Rowan quietly draped a blanket over his shoulders.

He did not wake.

When Aidan finally opened his eyes...

The first thing he did was reach for the reports.

Not the blanket.

Not food.

Work.

Rowan watched without speaking.

Another piece of the boy had disappeared.

...

Breakfast passed.

Aidan never came.

Lunch arrived.

His seat remained empty.

By evening...

Malik found him repairing barrier runes along the southern wall.

"You skipped every meal."

"I lost track."

"You always lose track now."

Aidan tightened another rune into place.

"If I stop..."

He looked toward the repaired wall.

"...something gets missed."

Malik leaned beside him.

"And if you collapse?"

Aidan didn't answer.

Because he didn't know.

...

The answer came before sunset.

One apprentice screamed.

A section of the southern wall gave way beneath weeks of rain.

Not because of hunters.

Because stone eventually breaks.

Aidan moved before anyone shouted.

His body reached the collapsing section first.

"Hold."

Crimson light anchored the fractured wall.

Workers escaped.

Students stumbled backward.

The collapse stopped.

His ribs screamed beneath the strain.

Fresh blood spread beneath the bindings around his chest.

The wall remained standing.

Barely.

By the time Rowan reinforced the structure...

Aidan could no longer feel his hands.

...

Helena found him afterward sitting alone behind the wall.

Blood stained the cuffs of his sleeves.

She quietly knelt.

"You reopened your ribs."

"I know."

"You tore the muscles around your shoulder."

"I know."

"You've lost enough blood this month to kill an ordinary witch."

"I know."

Her hands stopped moving.

"You answer everything the same way."

He looked down.

"I don't know what else to say."

For a long time...

Neither spoke.

Finally Helena whispered,

"I'm starting to forget what your healthy voice sounds like."

The words lingered long after she walked away.

...

That night...

The academy finally slept.

Aidan didn't.

He walked the empty halls.

One quiet step after another.

Without realizing it...

His feet made almost no sound.

He paused outside the first-year dormitories.

Inside...

Children slept peacefully.

He listened to every heartbeat.

Every steady breath.

Every quiet dream.

Satisfied...

He continued walking.

He never noticed that he had begun guarding them before guarding himself.

# Chapter Thirty-Seven: The Cost of One More Day

The messenger arrived before dawn.

Out of breath.

Bleeding from one arm.

"They took the mill."

Not Grey Hollow.

Not Blackwood.

The only grain mill supplying three nearby villages.

"They burned the grain?"

"No."

The messenger swallowed hard.

"They're waiting."

Rowan's expression darkened.

"They want us to come."

Cassian looked toward Aidan.

"They always do."

...

The rescue force left immediately.

Nobody complained.

Nobody asked why it was always them.

It simply had become their life.

The mill stood untouched.

Smoke drifted lazily from its chimney.

The waterwheel still turned.

Nothing moved.

Too perfect.

Aidan stopped fifty yards away.

His hearing reached ahead.

No workers.

No voices.

No animals.

Then...

One heartbeat.

Inside.

Alone.

The messenger.

Alive.

His breathing was frantic.

"They left someone alive," Lyra whispered.

"No."

Aidan's gaze narrowed.

"They left someone for us."

He circled the building instead of entering.

The others followed without question.

Behind the mill...

The earth had been hollowed out.

Hidden beneath loose timber.

A pit.

Large enough to swallow the entire rescue party.

The Trapper.

Again.

Only bigger.

Cassian looked at Aidan.

"You saved us."

Aidan shook his head.

"No."

His eyes scanned the surrounding hills.

"They wanted us to find this."

Almost immediately...

A distant horn echoed across the valley.

Not Blackwood's.

Another village.

West.

The hunters had counted on them stopping here.

While they delayed...

Another attack had already begun.

Malik cursed under his breath.

"We're behind again."

"No."

Aidan looked toward the sound.

"We were always meant to be."

The realization settled over everyone.

The mill had never been the target.

It had been the cost.

Every minute spent surviving one trap...

Was another minute someone else fought alone.

...

They ran.

Every step pulled against Aidan's side.

His shoulder burned.

His ribs felt as though they were grinding together beneath the bandages.

Still...

He never slowed.

The second heartbeat remained steady beneath his own.

Patient.

Waiting.

The western village finally appeared through the trees.

Smoke.

Screams.

Collapsed fences.

Families fleeing.

Hunters moving among them.

Not killing.

Driving.

Separating.

Learning.

Again.

Aidan raised his hands.

Pain shot through every healing wound.

He ignored it.

The battle had already begun before they arrived.

It would continue after it ended.

That was their life now.

Not finishing battles.

Arriving before they became tragedies.

Sometimes succeeding.

Sometimes failing.

Always paying.

As he stepped forward...

His eyes caught a streak of blood across the ground.

For the briefest instant...

Everything else disappeared.

The world narrowed to that single crimson line.

His breathing slowed.

His body leaned toward it.

Then he heard a child crying.

The sound cut through the hunger.

His head snapped toward the voice.

He ran.

Not toward the blood.

Toward the child.

The choice still belonged to him.

For now.

Far away...

Hidden atop a ruined bell tower...

The Observer carved another mark.

Not beneath the others.

Beside them.

A new symbol.

A droplet.

The Commander studied it in silence.

The Crimson Witch still chose people over instinct.

But the hesitation before each choice...

Had grown just a little longer.

The hunt did not need him to fail today.

It only needed tomorrow's choice...

To cost a little more than today's.
