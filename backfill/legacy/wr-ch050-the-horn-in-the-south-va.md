The horn never sounded a second time.

One blast.

Then silence.

Somehow…

That frightened Aidan more.

He and Lyra reached the southern gate before the guards finished opening it.

Rowan, Cassian, Malik, Elias, and Helena were already there.

No one asked whether Aidan intended to come.

That question had disappeared years ago.

The messenger waiting beyond the gate struggled to remain standing.

His clothes were soaked.

Not from rain.

From river water.

“They took…”

He fought for breath.

“…the ferry.”

“The southern crossing?” Rowan asked.

The man nodded weakly.

“No one can cross.”

“How many?”

“I don’t know.”

“Were they attacked?”

“I…”

The ferryman looked lost.

“I never saw anyone.”

The words settled over the group.

Again.

No witnesses.

No certainty.

Only consequences.

…

The southern ferry crossed the widest section of the Black River.

Without it…

Five villages were effectively cut off from Blackwood.

Food.

Medicine.

Healers.

Everything stopped.

Aidan looked toward the map Elias quickly unfolded.

“If they wanted to destroy it…”

“They would have burned it,” Cassian finished.

“They want us delayed,” Rowan said.

“They always do.”

…

The journey south took most of the morning.

No voices carried through the trees.

No birds.

Only the steady rhythm of boots against damp earth.

Halfway there…

Aidan stopped.

His head turned toward the ridgeline.

“What?”

Malik asked.

He listened.

Very faint.

Wood creaking.

Then…

Splashing.

Not one splash.

Several.

“They’re already on the river.”

No one else heard it.

They increased their pace.

…

The ferry remained tied to its dock.

Untouched.

No signs of battle.

The rope hadn’t even been cut.

The ferryman’s hut stood open.

Breakfast still rested on the table.

Cold.

Hours old.

Helena slowly looked around.

“No blood.”

Cassian pushed open another door.

“Nothing.”

Aidan stepped onto the ferry itself.

The wooden deck groaned beneath his weight.

His eyes drifted toward the far bank.

Then farther.

Mist rolled across the river.

Beyond it…

He thought he saw movement.

A shadow.

Gone before he focused.

He frowned.

Too far.

Or perhaps…

Never there.

His hearing reached across the water.

The river swallowed most sounds.

Then…

A child cried.

Once.

Very faint.

Across the river.

Lyra immediately looked toward him.

“You heard it.”

He nodded.

“Yes.”

She listened.

“I didn’t.”

The cry came again.

Then…

Stopped.

No splash.

No footsteps.

Nothing.

Aidan slowly backed away from the ferry.

“We’re leaving.”

Cassian trusted him immediately.

No one stepped aboard.

Minutes later…

The ferry drifted away from the dock on its own.

No wind.

No current strong enough to break the mooring.

The rope…

Had been cut from beneath the water.

The empty vessel floated into the center of the river.

Then exploded upward as hidden supports beneath it collapsed.

The river swallowed the wreckage.

No one spoke.

Had they boarded…

No one would have reached the opposite shore alive.

…

On the far bank…

Something moved behind the mist.

Only once.

Then vanished.

Aidan stared until his eyes hurt.

Nothing returned.
