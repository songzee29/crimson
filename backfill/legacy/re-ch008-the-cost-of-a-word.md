Aidan didn't sleep.

Not after the notebook.

Not after the message.

Not after reality had answered him.

He spent the rest of the night sitting on the floor with his back against the wall, staring at the notebook lying where it had fallen.

Every few minutes he expected it to move again.

It never did.

That somehow made it worse.

Because if it had kept moving, he could blame a ghost.

A trick.

A hallucination.

Anything.

Instead, the room sat perfectly still.

As if reality had proven its point and no longer needed to convince him.

---

Morning arrived slowly.

The sky over New Orleans was gray.

Heavy clouds hung low over the city.

Aidan stood in front of the bathroom mirror, looking at himself.

Same dark circles.

Same tired eyes.

Same face.

Yet something felt wrong.

Not physically.

Deeper.

Like there was now a second version of himself standing just behind the first.

Watching.

Waiting.

Learning.

He splashed water on his face.

The feeling remained.

---

School was worse.

Much worse.

Because now he wasn't looking for weird things.

The weird things were finding him.

Every time someone spoke, Aidan noticed things he had never noticed before.

Tiny pauses.

Tiny hesitations.

Fear hidden beneath smiles.

Anger buried beneath jokes.

It wasn't mind reading.

It was something stranger.

As if he could see the pressure beneath people.

The cracks they tried to hide.

---

Halfway through history class it happened again.

A teacher dropped a stack of papers.

The room barely reacted.

Most students ignored it.

But Aidan's eyes locked onto the falling papers.

For a split second—

everything slowed.

Not time.

Perception.

Every sheet floating separately.

Every movement visible.

Every detail sharp.

Then it ended.

The papers hit the floor.

Reality snapped back.

The classroom continued.

Nobody noticed.

Except him.

---

His pulse raced.

"What was that?"

No answer came.

The System remained silent.

Which somehow felt worse than hearing it.

Because silence meant it was watching.

Not helping.

Not explaining.

Just observing.

Like a judge waiting to see what kind of person he would become.

---

After school Aidan skipped the bus.

He walked.

The city helped him think.

Or at least it used to.

Today it only made things stranger.

Rainwater flowed through gutters.

People moved through crowded sidewalks.

Cars passed.

Everything looked normal.

Yet hidden beneath it all—

he kept seeing flashes.

Small things.

Tiny things.

A woman arguing with someone on the phone.

For half a second Aidan saw black threads wrapped around her wrist.

Then they disappeared.

A man sitting alone outside a convenience store.

For an instant Aidan saw something broken hanging over him like shattered glass.

Then it vanished.

Aidan stopped walking.

His chest tightened.

The world wasn't changing.

His eyes were.

---

He reached the old tunnel entrance before sunset.

Not because he planned to.

His feet simply brought him there.

Again.

The abandoned station greeted him with silence.

The deeper he walked, the heavier the air became.

Like the ruins remembered him.

Like the chamber knew he was returning.

---

Eventually he reached the room where the book had been.

Empty.

Still empty.

Yet it no longer felt abandoned.

It felt occupied by absence.

The kind of absence left behind by something enormous.

---

Aidan sat in the center of the chamber.

Cross-legged.

Alone.

For several minutes he simply listened.

Dripping water.

Distant echoes.

His own heartbeat.

Then he spoke.

Quietly.

Carefully.

"System."

Nothing happened.

Aidan exhaled.

Of course not.

Why would it—

Cold.

Precise.

Emotionless.
