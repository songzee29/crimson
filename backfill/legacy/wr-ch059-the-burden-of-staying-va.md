Morning arrived without Aidan leaving Blackwood.

It felt wrong.

Every instinct urged him toward the gates.

Toward another patrol.

Another village.

Another burden.

Instead…

He stood inside the eastern training grounds helping reinforce defensive barriers with the apprentices.

His movements were slower than before.

His breathing shallower.

Yet his instructions remained steady.

“Again.”

The students repeated the formation.

“No.”

He quietly adjusted one apprentice’s stance.

“Anchor with your whole body.”

She tried again.

The barrier held.

Better.

A faint smile crossed her face.

“You remembered.”

“You taught us.”

The words lingered.

He wondered how many lessons he had forgotten teaching.

…

By midday…

The first rescue team returned.

Not everyone came back.

A wagon rolled through the gates carrying two wounded guards.

Another arrived carrying one covered body.

The courtyard became silent.

Aidan’s hands stopped moving.

He had obeyed Rowan.

He had stayed.

Someone had died anyway.

The thought settled heavily inside him.

Would he have changed the outcome?

He didn’t know.

The uncertainty hurt more than certainty ever could.

…

Cassian found him alone beneath the old oak.

“You heard the wagons.”

Aidan nodded.

“I counted them.”

Cassian wasn’t surprised.

“You couldn’t have been everywhere.”

“I know.”

“You keep saying that.”

“Because it’s true.”

Cassian looked toward the fresh grave already being prepared.

“And you still blame yourself.”

Neither denied it.

Neither could.

…

As evening settled over Blackwood…

Aidan remained beneath the branches of the old oak.

The academy slowly quieted.

His hearing stretched beyond the walls.

Toward distant villages.

Toward empty forests.

Toward places he couldn’t reach.

Then…

One heartbeat.

Very faint.

Far away.

It lasted only an instant before disappearing.

He opened his eyes.

The second heartbeat answered beneath his own.

Steady.

Patient.

Waiting.

He didn’t follow.

He couldn’t.

Not tonight.

For the first time in years…

The burden wasn’t another battle.

It was remaining where he was while not knowing what might be happening somewhere else.

And somehow…

That hurt even more than the wounds still stitched across his body.
