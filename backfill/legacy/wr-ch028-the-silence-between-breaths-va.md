Blackwood Academy stopped reacting.

It began anticipating.

The old patrol bells were abandoned.

Instead, Rowan introduced silent signal runes hidden throughout the academy. A flash of blue beneath a window. A crimson ember along a wall. A pulse through the wards themselves.

No shouted orders.

No warning the hunters could overhear.

“They’ve been listening to us,” Rowan told the instructors. “From now on… we listen better than they do.”

The academy adapted.

Again.

…

Aidan stood among the instructors.

He spoke little.

Not because he had nothing to say.

Because he had begun weighing every word before speaking it.

He hated himself for it.

The hunter’s voice still echoed inside his thoughts.

You have your traitor to thank for this.

No.

He refused to believe it.

Yet…

He also stopped announcing every instinct the moment he felt it.

Not because he distrusted anyone.

Because if someone truly was watching Blackwood…

Less information meant fewer opportunities.

It was caution.

He hoped.

…

Cassian noticed first.

“You stopped finishing your thoughts.”

They walked the northern wall together.

“I have?”

“You tell us where to go.”

Cassian looked ahead.

“But not why.”

Aidan didn’t answer immediately.

“I’ve been wrong before.”

“No.”

Cassian stopped walking.

“You’ve changed.”

The words weren’t accusing.

They hurt anyway.

“I know.”

“You don’t laugh anymore.”

Silence.

“You don’t argue with Malik.”

Silence.

“You don’t complain about Rowan’s training.”

A faint smile almost appeared.

Almost.

“I still complain.”

“Only in your head.”

Cassian watched him carefully.

“And you keep looking toward the trees every few minutes.”

Aidan hadn’t realized he was doing it.

…

That afternoon Helena found him studying reports from every recent attack.

She quietly sat beside him.

“You’ve read those three times.”

“I’m looking for something.”

“What?”

“I don’t know yet.”

She waited.

Then softly asked,

“When was the last time you slept through the night?”

He couldn’t remember.

That answer alone worried her.

“You aren’t healing.”

“I’m alive.”

“Those aren’t the same thing.”

Her hand rested gently on one of the reports.

“I’ve treated witches for thirty years.”

She looked directly into his eyes.

“I’ve never seen someone survive so much while becoming more exhausted.”

He looked away.

Neither had he.

…

That evening the mountain became quiet.

Too quiet.

Aidan stood beneath the old oak.

He listened.

Nothing.

No scraping stone.

No distant heartbeats.

No movement.

The silence itself felt wrong.

His breathing slowed.

His eyes narrowed.

“They’re gone.”

Rowan stepped beside him.

“Gone?”

“They left the tunnels.”

The old master trusted the statement immediately.

Within the hour…

Search teams confirmed it.

The deepest passages beneath Blackwood stood empty.

No fresh tracks.

No sounds.

No hunters.

Malik smiled for the first time in days.

“Maybe we finally scared them off.”

Aidan looked toward the dark forest.

“No.”

His voice remained calm.

“They’re changing the battlefield.”
