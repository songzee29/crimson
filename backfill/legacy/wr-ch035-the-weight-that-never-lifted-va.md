That night Blackwood Academy remained awake.

Not because another attack had come.

Because everyone expected one.

The waiting itself had become exhausting.

Students startled at every shifting shadow.

Faculty slept in armor.

Lanterns remained lit until dawn.

Fear had become routine.

…

Aidan sat alone inside the strategy hall.

Maps covered every table.

Villages.

Supply routes.

Collapsed tunnels.

Missing patrols.

Destroyed bridges.

Every solved problem had created another.

He quietly moved another marker across the map.

Grey Hollow.

Safe.

For now.

A knock interrupted his thoughts.

Cassian entered.

“You’ve been in here for four hours.”

“I lost track.”

“I know.”

Cassian sat across from him.

Neither man spoke for several minutes.

Finally Cassian asked,

“Do you remember the first day we met?”

Aidan smiled faintly.

“You challenged me.”

“You looked terrified.”

“I was.”

“You hid it badly.”

Silence.

Cassian’s expression softened.

“I don’t remember the last time I saw that version of you.”

The words struck harder than any hunter’s claw.

“I don’t either.”

It was the first completely honest answer Aidan had given in days.

…

Long after Cassian left…

Aidan remained alone.

His hearing stretched across the academy.

Every patrol.

Every conversation.

Every heartbeat.

Then…

One heartbeat faltered.

A young apprentice collapsed from exhaustion during the midnight watch.

Aidan was already standing before anyone called for help.

He reached the courtyard moments before Helena.

The apprentice looked up weakly.

“I’m sorry…”

“You don’t have to apologize,” Aidan said quietly.

“You were trying.”

The apprentice smiled.

“So are you.”

The simple words left Aidan unexpectedly speechless.

…

Later…

Standing once more beneath the old oak…

He rested his forehead against its ancient trunk.

He didn’t ask for strength.

He didn’t ask for victory.

He asked for one thing.

“Just…”

His voice barely carried above the wind.

“…let them survive.”

No answer came.

Only the familiar second heartbeat beneath his own.

Steady.

Patient.

Waiting.

Far beyond Blackwood…

The Commander stood atop a ridge overlooking the sleeping academy.

The Observer carved another symbol into black stone.

Not a victory.

Not a defeat.

A burden.

The Commander watched the exhausted Crimson Witch standing alone beneath the old oak.

It had learned something new.

The fastest way to weaken Aidan was no longer through battle.

It was through responsibility.

If every problem became his to solve…

If every life became his to carry…

Then one day…

When the impossible choice finally came…

He would already be too exhausted to survive it.

And somewhere deep inside Aidan…

Without anyone realizing it…

Another quiet piece of the boy he used to be slipped away.
