One ordinary day at a time.

The ordinary days were becoming harder to protect.

Just after sunrise…

A low vibration rolled beneath Blackwood Academy.

Not violent.

Not loud.

Every cup in the dining hall trembled once.

Water rippled inside every basin.

The banners hanging from the central hall stirred without wind.

Then…

Everything stopped.

Students slowly looked around.

No one spoke.

Professor Helena rose from her chair before anyone else.

“Remain seated.”

Her voice remained calm.

That calmness mattered more than the words themselves.

The conversations slowly resumed.

Not naturally.

Deliberately.

⸻

High within the Headmaster’s Tower…

A messenger reached Aldren before breakfast had ended.

“The eastern foundation.”

“What about it?”

“The Third Archive corridor shifted.”

Mr. Bennett immediately stood.

“Collapsed?”

The messenger shook his head.

“No.”

“It moved.”

Silence filled the room.

Rowan closed the journal he had been reading.

“Show us.”

⸻

The corridor had existed for nearly three centuries.

Every instructor knew it.

Every maintenance worker had walked it.

Every academy map recorded it.

Now…

It no longer matched the maps.

The hallway had curved.

Only slightly.

Barely enough for an ordinary person to notice.

Yet the old lantern hooks no longer aligned with the walls.

The stone floor no longer met the corners it had met yesterday.

Professor Helena measured it herself.

“Three degrees.”

Mr. Bennett checked twice.

“She’s right.”

Aldren rested one hand against the wall.

Cold.

Solid.

Real.

The academy itself had shifted.

Not broken.

Changed.

Rowan quietly looked down the length of the corridor.

“The mountain isn’t trying to destroy the academy.”

“No.”

Helena answered.

“It’s trying to remember what stood here before.”

⸻

The decision came immediately.

Without debate.

“The Third Archive is closed.”

Aldren looked toward Helena.

“Move every historical volume before nightfall.”

She nodded.

“Bennett.”

“Relocate all practical classes away from the eastern halls.”

Already moving.

“Rowan.”

The old traveler smiled faintly.

“I’ll help the students understand why change isn’t always loss.”

⸻

By midday…

Books filled carts throughout the academy.

Students volunteered without being asked.

Some carried stacks of journals.

Others carefully boxed fragile scrolls.

No one complained.

The work felt important.

Though few understood why.

Aidan lifted another crate beside Malik.

“You think they’re telling us everything?”

Malik shook his head.

“No.”

“You?”

“No.”

Cassian adjusted a heavy shelf with Bennett.

“I don’t think they’re lying.”

“They’re protecting us.”

Lyra quietly added.

“There’s a difference.”

Everyone fell silent.

Because they knew she was right.

⸻

Hours later…

The final shelf stood empty.

The Third Archive waited in complete silence.

Professor Helena locked its doors herself.

She rested her hand against the weathered wood.

For only a moment.

Then turned away.

She never looked back.

⸻

That evening…

Master Rowan found Aidan watching students carry the final cart across the courtyard.

“It feels strange.”

“It should.”

Rowan stood beside him.

“What do you see?”

“The academy changing.”

“No.”

The old traveler smiled gently.

“The people.”

Aidan watched again.

First-years helping seniors.

Instructors lifting crates beside groundskeepers.

Students carrying books they would never read simply because someone needed help.

The building had changed.

The people had responded.

Together.

“I understand.”

Rowan nodded.

“When something precious changes…”

“The first question should never be…”

“‘What have we lost?’”

He looked across the courtyard.

“It should be…”

“‘What must we protect now?’”

⸻

That night…

The Crimson Legacy appeared.
