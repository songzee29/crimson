The road never ended.

Moonlight slid across abandoned highways and forgotten forests as Aidan moved south with impossible speed.

He no longer counted miles.

Only heartbeats.

The world unfolded around him in layers.

The insects beneath fallen leaves.

The birds sleeping high in ancient oaks.

The steady pulse of rivers.

The racing hearts of frightened travelers hiding behind locked doors.

Every sound reached him.

Every scent carried meaning.

His throat burned again.

Not as fiercely as it had after awakening.

Worse.

Deeper.

A slow, relentless fire.

The notifications dissolved into darkness.

His body answered before his thoughts.

Every muscle tightened.

Every heartbeat nearby became louder.

The hunger had returned.

Not because his first feeding had failed.

Because one feeding could never satisfy what he had become.

…

A narrow bridge crossed a swollen creek ahead.

Three highway robbers stepped from the shadows.

Each carried a blade.

Each heartbeat raced with confidence.

One pointed toward Aidan.

“Leave everything you’ve got.”

Aidan stopped.

He tilted his head slightly.

The men mistook the silence for fear.

The leader charged.

His knife never reached its target.

Aidan caught his wrist.

The bones shattered instantly.

The robber screamed.

The other two froze.

Aidan looked down at his own hand.

He had barely tightened his grip.

The man collapsed, clutching his ruined arm.

Blood soaked through torn cloth.

The scent hit Aidan like lightning.

His fangs descended.

The leader’s heartbeat exploded with terror.

Aidan stepped forward.

The hunger surged.

Then—

A distant heartbeat.

A child.

Inside a wagon hidden farther down the road.

Small.

Weak.

The sound cut through the hunger for only an instant.

Aidan’s head turned.

Not toward compassion.

Toward calculation.

More heartbeats.

Too many.

The scent of fresh blood scattered among them.

The odds shifted.

He released the broken man.

The three robbers stumbled backward in disbelief before fleeing into the darkness.

Aidan never watched them leave.

He was already moving south.

The hunger had chosen not mercy—

Only a different trail.

Waiting for blood enough to fuel the change.

He continued south.

The fire in his throat only grew.
