The ruins beneath New Orleans did not feel empty.

They felt *contained*.

Like something was buried so deeply beneath the city that even time avoided speaking about it.

Aidan Vale stood in the collapsed underground chamber, staring at a structure that should not have survived history.

Stone walls too clean.

Air too still.

A circle carved into the floor with precision no ancient civilization should have had.

And at the center—

a black book.

Resting.

Not glowing.

Not moving.

Just waiting.

Aidan frowned.

“…This doesn’t make sense.”

His voice sounded too loud in the chamber.

He stepped forward.

Then stopped.

Something felt wrong about approaching it directly.

Like instinct was warning him before thought could.

Still—

he crouched slowly and reached out.

The moment his fingers touched the surface—

the world reacted.

Not the book.

The world.

A pressure snapped through the chamber like reality had been pinched.

Aidan recoiled instantly.

“What the—”

A thin line of blood had formed on his finger.

He hadn’t noticed it before.

A cut from the stone edge.

One drop fell.

It landed on the book.

---

Silence.

Not normal silence.

Complete absence of expectation.

Then—

the book opened.

Slowly.

Like it had been waiting specifically for that exact moment of contact.

Aidan stumbled back.

“I didn’t— I didn’t mean to—”

The pages inside were not pages.

They were layered darkness.

And then—

something spoke.

Not from outside.

From *inside the act of reading itself*.

> **“BLOOD CONTACT CONFIRMED.”**

Aidan froze.

“…What?”

The book did not move.

But something in it *looked at him*.

Not a face.

A recognition.

Then—

a second presence stirred.

Weak.

Fragmented.

Like a voice trying to wake up after being buried for centuries.

> “…finally…”

Aidan’s eyes widened.

“Who’s there?”

The voice cracked slightly.

> “…you’re louder than the last one…”

A pause.

Then:

> “…name’s Rez…”

Aidan stepped back immediately.

“Where are you?”

Rez didn’t answer cleanly.

Instead, his voice flickered like it was coming through something thick.

> “…don’t panic…”

> “…you’re doing that already, aren’t you…”

Aidan swallowed hard.

“This is insane.”

Rez let out something like a tired breath.

> “Yeah… that’s usually step one.”

---

The book tightened.

Not physically.

Conceptually.

Like something inside it had just confirmed identity.

Then the system responded:

> **“BLOODLINE LINK ESTABLISHED.”**

Aidan’s stomach dropped.

“Bloodline…?”

Rez went quiet.

Longer this time.

Then, carefully:

> “…oh.”

That one word carried weight.

Aidan frowned.

“What does that mean?”

Rez’s voice lowered.

> “It means you’re not supposed to be random.”

A pause.

> “And I really wish you were.”

---

The symbols in the chamber began to faintly respond now—not glowing yet, but acknowledging presence.

The book opened further.

And the system began its first real process:

Not giving power.

Not explaining.

Just **judging compatibility**.

> **“INITIALIZATION: CURSE AUTHORITY LAYER…”**

Aidan stepped back again.

“I didn’t ask for any of this.”

Rez responded immediately this time:

> “No one does.”

---

And then—

the book stopped.

Completely.

Like it had reached a decision.

A final line appeared—not visible to Aidan’s eyes, but *felt* in his mind:

> **“HEIR ACCEPTED.”**

Then—

the book vanished.

No explosion.

No light.

Just absence.

Like it had never existed in physical space at all.

---

Aidan stood alone.

Breathing hard.

Hands shaking.

“…Rez?”

Silence.

Then—

not from the book anymore.

From inside his mind.

Faint.

Real.

Present.

> “…yeah…”

Aidan froze.

“…you’re in my head.”

Rez didn’t argue.

> “…yeah.”

A long pause.

Then quieter:

> “…don’t let it scare you too much.”

Aidan whispered:

“It already does.”
