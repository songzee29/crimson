The old path had nearly vanished beneath time.

Leaves covered weathered stones.

Tree roots curled across broken steps that had once been carefully laid by forgotten hands.

No signs marked the trail anymore.

Nature had slowly reclaimed it.

Yet…

Someone had built it.

Aidan walked at the front.

Not because he intended to lead.

Because somehow…

He had already begun.

The others followed without discussion.

Without hesitation.

⸻

“This place feels…”

Lyra searched for the word.

“…older.”

“It is.”

Elias brushed moss away from a half-buried stone marker.

“This academy used to extend farther east.”

Malik looked around.

“I’ve never heard anyone mention it.”

“They probably forgot.”

Aidan answered quietly.

Forgot.

Professor Helena’s lesson returned to him.

Civilizations rarely died when walls fell.

They began dying when people forgot why they had been built.

The thought lingered.

⸻

The trail curved around a rocky hillside before opening into a small clearing.

Nothing remarkable stood there.

Only a weathered stone bench.

An ancient ash tree.

And…

The sixth marker.

Cassian threw both hands into the air.

“I knew we’d find it.”

“You said there were seven.”

“There are spiritually seven.”

“No.”

“There are.”

“No.”

Lyra laughed so hard she nearly dropped the map.

⸻

As Elias carefully copied the final location…

The ground trembled.

Barely.

Just enough to disturb a few leaves from the ash tree above.

Everyone froze.

The tremor ended almost immediately.

Malik looked toward Aidan.

“You felt that?”

“Yeah.”

Cassian scanned the surrounding woods.

“No storm.”

“No landslide.”

Elias slowly rolled up the map.

“We should head back.”

No one argued.

⸻

High above the clearing…

Master Rowan stood hidden among the trees.

He had followed from a distance all afternoon.

Not to protect them.

To observe them.

He had watched Aidan instinctively take the forgotten trail.

Watched the others trust him without question.

Watched them return together after every obstacle.

The old traveler quietly smiled.

“They’ve stopped becoming companions.”

He whispered to himself.

“They’ve become a family.”

The smile faded.

“And families…”

He lowered his eyes toward the trembling earth.

“…are what the Witch Curse always demands the most.”

The words disappeared into the wind.

⸻

The group reached the academy just before sunset.

Professor Bennett accepted their completed map.

“You found the eastern marker.”

“We did.”

“It hasn’t been used in many years.”

Aidan looked toward him.

“Why?”

Bennett’s expression softened.

“The trail became unnecessary.”

Rowan’s lesson echoed within him.

Nothing truly became unnecessary.

People simply forgot.

Professor Bennett studied Aidan for a long moment.

Then…

The smallest hint of a smile appeared.

“You’ll make a good teacher someday.”

Aidan blinked.

“I’ve still got a lot to learn.”

“Exactly.”

Bennett nodded.

“The best ones always believe that.”

As the five friends walked back toward the old oak together…

The Crimson Legacy appeared.

Aidan looked ahead.

The forgotten trail.

The old bridge.

The laughter.

The shared map.

None of it had felt extraordinary.

Yet somehow…

He knew Rowan would tell him those were the days that shaped a lifetime.

Far beneath Blackwood Academy…

The second heartbeat stopped.

Not because its journey had ended.

Because…

It had reached its destination.
