Blackwood Academy no longer waited for dawn.

The wounded arrived before it.

The dead arrived after.

The pattern had become so familiar that no one looked surprised when another wagon rolled through the gates beneath a sky still filled with stars.

Helena walked toward it.

She already knew what she would find.

Bandages.

Blood.

Silence.

…

Aidan stood nearby helping unload the survivors.

His left arm remained secured against his side beneath a leather brace.

Every movement tugged against the torn muscles across his ribs.

He never reached with that arm anymore.

His body had quietly learned its limits.

His heart had not.

A frightened boy stumbled while climbing from the wagon.

Before anyone else moved…

Aidan caught him with his good arm.

“Easy.”

Only one word.

The boy nodded.

“Thank you.”

Aidan simply guided him toward the infirmary.

He never mentioned the fresh blood now soaking through his own bandages.

…

That afternoon Rowan assembled the remaining patrol captains.

The map had changed again.

Three roads were crossed out.

Two villages had been evacuated permanently.

One watchtower would not be rebuilt.

“We’re shrinking our line,” Rowan said.

No one objected.

Every argument had already been lost weeks earlier.

Aidan studied the map.

Not searching for another solution.

Accepting the one before him.

“We can’t hold everything.”

His voice was calm.

Quiet.

Matter-of-fact.

The room fell silent.

Cassian looked at him.

Months ago…

Aidan would have searched for another way.

Now…

He simply measured what could still be saved.

…

The meeting ended.

Everyone left.

Cassian remained behind.

“Aidan.”

He stopped.

“When did you stop arguing with Rowan?”

Aidan considered the question.

“I don’t know.”

“You always challenged him.”

“I was wrong.”

“No.”

Cassian stepped closer.

“You believed impossible things.”

Aidan looked toward the academy courtyard.

Children carried water between buildings.

Students repaired shattered stone.

Life continued because someone chose to keep it moving.

“I still do.”

Cassian frowned.

“Then why don’t you fight anymore?”

Aidan answered without turning.

“I do.”

His hand rested lightly against the map.

“I just stopped fighting reality.”

The words stayed with Cassian long after Aidan walked away.

…

That night…

Lyra found Aidan beneath the old oak.

He wasn’t watching the forest.

He wasn’t listening.

He simply sat with his back against the ancient trunk.

Eyes closed.

Breathing slowly.

“You fell asleep here.”

He opened one eye.

“For a minute.”

“It was almost an hour.”

“I didn’t mean to.”

She sat beside him.

Neither spoke.

The silence wasn’t uncomfortable.

It was simply all either of them had left.

Finally Lyra leaned her head gently against his shoulder.

“You don’t have to carry everything.”

He didn’t answer immediately.

When he finally spoke…

His voice was almost too quiet to hear.

“I know.”

She waited.

“But if I put it down…”

He looked toward the lights of Blackwood.

“…someone else picks it up.”

He closed his eyes again.

“I’d rather it stay with me.”

Lyra reached for his hand.

He squeezed hers once.

Then they sat beneath the old oak until dawn found them both still awake.
