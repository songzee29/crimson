The Wolf opened its eyes.

And Aidan felt everything.

Not more power.

Not at first.

Everything.

Lyra’s blood cooling against his hands.

The faint scent of rain caught in her hair.

Cassian’s blood on the stone twenty feet away.

Elias’s uneven breathing.

Malik’s heartbeat hammering behind him.

Draco’s claws scraping unconsciously against rock.

Rowan’s grief buried beneath disciplined silence.

Every living thing on the mountain suddenly existed inside Aidan’s awareness.

Too close.

Too loud.

Too alive.

While Lyra…

Wasn’t.

Aidan stared down at her.

His thumb moved slowly across her cheek.

“Lyra.”

Nothing.

He waited.

As though his body had not yet accepted what his eyes already knew.

“Lyra.”

The second time broke something.

A sound escaped his chest.

Not a howl.

Not a scream.

Her name.

Raw.

Small.

Wrong coming from him.

Aidan bent over her body and pressed his forehead against hers.

“No.”

His shoulders shook.

“No, no, no…”

He had survived death.

He had awakened through death.

He had watched blood become power and pain become evolution.

Some irrational part of him kept waiting for Lyra to do the same.

For her eyes to open.

For her fingers to tighten around his.

For her to inhale sharply and complain that everyone was staring.

Nothing happened.

Death remained death.

Then Aidan felt it.

A faint pulse beneath Lyra’s skin.

So weak it could have been memory.

He froze.

The Wolf surged through him—not as a separate voice, not as a monster clawing for control, but as instinct sharpened by everything Aidan had already survived.

There.

Again.

A pulse.

Aidan pressed two fingers against Lyra’s throat.

Nothing.

Then—

A single, fragile beat.

His breath caught.

“Rowan.”

Rowan moved immediately.

“What?”

“She’s not gone.”

Rowan knelt beside him and placed his hand over Lyra’s chest.

His expression changed.

“Her heart stopped.”

“I know.”

“Then what—”

Aidan did not answer.

The Crimson Blood inside him had begun to move.

It gathered beneath his skin, hot and dense, answering the Wolf now filling his senses. His veins glowed faintly red. The blood in his hands, Lyra’s blood, and the broken magic of the Sacred Seal all seemed to recognize one another.

The shattered runes around them began to burn.

Cassian looked up from the stone.

“What are you doing?”

Aidan ignored him.

He lifted Lyra carefully against his chest.

Her head rested beneath his chin.

Her body was still limp.

Still cold.

Still dead by every ordinary measure.

But the pulse came again.

One beat.

Then another.

Aidan pressed his mouth to the wound beneath her ribs.

The place where Cassian’s blade had entered her.

His fangs descended.

The Wolf did not resist.

The Vampire did not hunger.

The Witch within him opened.

Aidan bit into his own wrist.

True Crimson Blood welled instantly.

It was darker than ordinary blood, threaded with a red light that seemed to contain every foundation inside him—the Witch, the Vampire, and the Wolf he had already earned through bloodshed, hunting, survival, and war.

He pressed his wrist to Lyra’s lips.

“Drink.”

Nothing happened.

Aidan tightened his hold around her.

“Lyra, drink.”

Her lips remained still.

The mountain groaned around them.

The broken Seal released another wave of crimson force.

Aidan felt the truth of his blood.

It did not need permission from another seal.

It did not require a trial.

It did not wait for a percentage.

He had already become what he needed to become.

The blood answered him because it was his.

Because Lyra was his.

Because the bond between them had been forged long before this moment.

Aidan opened his palm and pressed it against the wound.

“Come back.”

The Crimson Blood entered her.

Lyra’s body arched violently.

A shockwave exploded across the plateau.

Rowan threw up a barrier.

Elias shouted.

Malik stumbled backward.

Cassian was driven onto one knee.

The runes surrounding them ignited.

Aidan held Lyra tighter as crimson light poured through her veins.

Her body convulsed again.

The wound in her side began to close.

Not heal.

Change.

The flesh drew together beneath Aidan’s hand, but dark crimson lines spread outward from the wound like living runes. They crossed her ribs, climbed her throat, and traced themselves beneath her skin.

Lyra’s hair lifted in the force gathering around her.

Her fingers curled.

Aidan felt her spirit brush against his.

Not a memory.

Not an echo.

Lyra.

Frightened.

Furious.

Lost.

Aidan?

His eyes burned.

“I’m here.”

I died.

“I know.”

It hurts.

“I know.”

Why can I hear your heart?

“Because I’m bringing you back.”

The Wolf wrapped around her presence.

Not consuming it.

Recognizing it.

Protecting it.

Aidan felt Lyra’s magic awaken inside the blood he had given her. Her Witch foundation reached for the Wolf, and the Wolf answered without resistance.

The two powers fused.

Not cleanly.

Not gently.

The transformation tore through her.

Lyra screamed.

The sound ripped across the mountain.

Aidan held her through every convulsion.

Her spine arched.

Her nails lengthened into black-red claws.

Her teeth sharpened.

The runes beneath her skin brightened until her entire body seemed carved from moonlight and blood.

A second heartbeat began beneath her ribs.

Then a third rhythm joined it.

Aidan’s blood.

Lyra’s magic.

The Wolf.

The Sacred Seal shattered completely.

A column of crimson light erupted around them.

Within it, Lyra rose from Aidan’s arms.

Her body hung suspended above the stone.

Her eyes opened.

They were no longer only Lyra’s eyes.

The irises had become luminous crimson, ringed with silver-white runes.

Her pupils narrowed into predatory slits.

A howl formed in her throat.

But beneath it remained her voice.

Her soul.

Her fury.

Her love.

The light collapsed.

Lyra fell.

Aidan caught her.

For one terrible second, she did not move.

Then she inhaled.

The breath was sharp enough to cut.

Her fingers closed around Aidan’s shirt.

“Aidan.”

He bowed over her.

“Lyra.”

She opened her eyes fully.

The first thing she saw was him.

The second was the blood covering his hands.

The third was Cassian.

Her expression changed.

Not confusion.

Recognition.

Memory returned in fragments.

The blade.

Cassian’s face.

Aidan screaming.

The darkness.

Her death.

Lyra’s hand tightened against Aidan’s chest.

“He killed me.”

Aidan’s jaw clenched.

“Yes.”

Cassian lowered his head.

Lyra looked down at herself.

The crimson runes moved beneath her skin like living script.

Her claws slowly withdrew.

Then returned.

She stared at them.

“What did you do?”

Aidan touched her cheek.

“I gave you my blood.”

“That isn’t all.”

“No.”

The Wolf stirred inside him.

Lyra felt it.

Her eyes widened.

“You released it.”

“It was already released.”

“And you put it in me.”

Aidan’s voice broke.

“I couldn’t lose you.”

Lyra stared at him for a long moment.

Then she placed her palm against his chest.

The instant she touched him, the bond ignited.

Crimson light flashed between them.

Aidan felt her heartbeat synchronize with his.

Not identical.

Not swallowed.

Two hearts.

Two wills.

One bloodline.

Lyra gasped.

A vision struck them both.

A vast wolf beneath a red moon.

A witch standing beside it.

Runes burning across her skin.

Aidan at the center of the bond, not as master, but as origin.

The vision vanished.

Lyra’s eyes remained fixed on him.

“What am I?”

The Crimson System appeared.

The interface faded.

Lyra looked at Aidan.

He answered before she could ask.

“You’re the first.”

Her breathing slowed.

“The first what?”

“The first Crimson Runefang.”

The words settled over the mountain.

Historic.

Permanent.

Impossible to undo.

Lyra closed her eyes.

When she opened them again, the grief of death remained there.

So did the memory of the blade.

So did the knowledge that she had crossed a line no one had crossed before.

She pushed herself upright.

Aidan tried to support her.

“I can stand.”

“You just died.”

“And now I’m standing.”

She rose.

Her legs trembled once.

Then steadied.

The Wolf inside her answered the ground.

Lyra’s body changed.

Crimson fur erupted across her arms.

Her spine lengthened.

Her hands became claws.

Her face shifted, not into a mindless beast, but into something powerful and unmistakably hers.

A true Wolf form.

A crimson runic wolf stood where Lyra had been.

Silver-white markings burned across its forehead and ribs.

The eyes remained hers.

Intelligent.

Furious.

Alive.

The wolf lowered its head toward Aidan.

He placed his hand against its muzzle.

The bond pulsed.

Lyra shifted back.

She stood naked beneath the fading crimson light, breathing hard, her body covered only by the glowing runes that now belonged to her.

Rowan removed his cloak and placed it around her shoulders.

Lyra accepted it without looking away from Cassian.

Cassian remained on his knees.

He had watched her die.

He had watched her return.

Neither sight absolved him.

Lyra’s voice was quiet.

“You killed me.”

Cassian’s face twisted.

“Yes.”

“You knew what that would do to him.”

“Yes.”

“You knew what it would do to me.”

Cassian looked at her.

“I didn’t know you would come back.”

“That isn’t an answer.”

“No.”

Lyra stepped closer.

Aidan caught her wrist.

Not to stop her.

To remind himself she was real.

Lyra looked down at his hand.

Then back at Cassian.

“I died because of you.”

Cassian lowered his eyes.

“I know.”

Lyra’s claws emerged again.

Aidan felt the Wolf inside him respond to hers.

Not with command.

With recognition.

She was not fragile now.

She was not untouched.

She was alive, changed, and carrying death inside her memory.

Lyra turned toward Aidan.

“I remember everything.”

“I know.”

“I remember you holding me.”

His throat tightened.

“I know.”

“I remember thinking I would never see you again.”

Aidan pulled her against him.

She held him just as tightly.

For a moment, the mountain disappeared.

There was only her heartbeat against his.

Her breath against his throat.

The warmth of her body.

The impossible fact of her return.

Then Lyra whispered against his chest.

“I’m not the same.”

Aidan closed his eyes.

“Neither am I.”

Behind them, the mountain cracked.

The transformation had not stopped the collapse.

The Sacred Seal was gone.

The world had changed.

But Lyra was alive.

The first Crimson Runefang stood beneath the broken sky.

And Cassian still had to face what he had done.

Aidan released Lyra slowly.

His eyes moved to Cassian.

The grief remained.

The love remained.

The betrayal remained.

Nothing had been erased.

Lyra’s return had not undone her murder.

It had made the consequences permanent.

Aidan stepped between them.

“Stay behind me.”

Lyra’s eyes narrowed.

“I can fight.”

“I know.”

“Then don’t order me around.”

Despite everything, the corner of Aidan’s mouth moved.

“There you are.”

Lyra’s expression softened for half a second.

Then she looked at Cassian again.

The softness vanished.

Aidan turned toward him.

The Wolf, the Vampire, and the Witch answered together beneath his skin.

Cassian slowly rose.

He did not reach for his sword.

He did not run.

He knew what was coming.

Aidan’s hands curled into fists.

Lyra stood behind him, alive in a body that had been born from death.

The Crimson System appeared one final time.

This time, no one stopped him.
