# Chapter Forty-Two: Echoes Without Answers

By the time Aidan reached Blackwood Academy, dawn had already begun breaking across the mountains.

He said nothing about the shrine.

Nothing about the academy badge.

Nothing about the stone that had shattered the ground where he had been standing.

Not because he wanted secrets.

Because he had nothing useful to report.

Only questions.

Questions got people killed if they sounded like certainty.

...

The gates opened before he knocked.

Rowan was already waiting.

"You left the walls."

"I couldn't sleep."

"I know."

The old master's eyes settled briefly on the dirt covering Aidan's boots.

"You found something."

Aidan hesitated.

"Maybe."

The single word weighed heavier than any explanation.

Rowan didn't press.

Not yet.

...

The strategy hall filled before sunrise.

Fresh reports covered the table.

Another supply route had gone quiet.

A healer returning from the southern villages had disappeared.

Two shepherds claimed they heard voices calling their names from empty hillsides after sunset.

No bodies.

No witnesses.

No proof.

Only frightened people who no longer trusted the darkness.

Elias rubbed tired eyes.

"We're chasing smoke."

"No."

Aidan quietly spread the reports farther apart.

"They want us looking everywhere."

Cassian frowned.

"So we stop looking?"

"No."

Aidan shook his head.

"We stop reacting the same way every time."

...

Blackwood changed again.

Small patrols no longer answered distress calls alone.

No messenger traveled without another.

Supply routes changed every morning instead of every week.

Students were forbidden from leaving the academy after dusk.

The academy adapted.

Again.

Whether it would be enough...

No one knew.

...

Late that afternoon, Aidan inspected the northern watch line.

Not because someone ordered him to.

Because he couldn't silence the feeling that something had already changed.

Halfway through the patrol, he stopped.

His head tilted slightly.

"What is it?" Lyra asked.

He raised one hand.

Silence.

Wind moved through the pines.

Leaves whispered overhead.

Then...

A voice.

Faint.

Almost carried away by the wind.

"...help..."

Aidan's heartbeat quickened.

"There."

He ran.

The others followed.

The cry came again.

Closer.

"...please..."

Malik pointed ahead.

"I heard it!"

They broke through the trees into a narrow clearing.

Empty.

No footprints.

No blood.

No one.

Only an abandoned lantern still burning despite the afternoon breeze.

The voice came again.

This time...

From somewhere behind them.

Cassian immediately turned.

Nothing.

The sound moved.

Always just beyond reach.

Aidan slowly backed toward the center of the clearing.

"No."

Everyone looked at him.

"It isn't calling us."

Another whisper drifted through the trees.

"...Aidan..."

His stomach tightened.

No one else reacted.

They hadn't heard his name.

Only he had.

His breathing slowed.

The second heartbeat answered beneath his own.

He closed his eyes.

Listened.

The whisper came again.

Not from one direction.

From several.

Too perfect.

Too deliberate.

"Leave."

No one questioned him.

They withdrew without another word.

The moment they crossed back beneath the tree line...

A deafening crash shook the clearing behind them.

Ancient branches collapsed exactly where they had been standing.

Lyra stared back through the settling dust.

"If we'd stayed..."

Aidan never answered.

He was listening to something else.

For one brief instant...

He thought he heard quiet footsteps walking away through the forest.

Then even that disappeared.

# Chapter Forty-Three: The Weight of Uncertainty

That night...

Rain returned.

Not heavy.

Enough to wash away tracks before morning.

Enough to erase answers.

Aidan stood beneath the covered walkway outside the infirmary.

He watched the rain strike the stone courtyard.

His shoulder ached.

The old break beneath his ribs pulsed with every change in the weather.

The wound along his side had begun healing.

He knew better than to trust it.

Another battle would open it again.

It always did.

...

Helena found him there.

"You've been standing in the rain for twenty minutes."

"I didn't notice."

"I know."

She folded her arms.

"You also forgot your medicine."

"I forgot."

"You don't forget."

Her voice softened.

"You stop thinking about yourself."

He looked away.

"There are more important things."

"To everyone else."

She stepped closer.

"But not to me."

Silence settled between them.

Finally she reached into her satchel and handed him fresh bandages.

"You've started bleeding through the old ones again."

He looked down.

She was right.

He hadn't even felt it.

...

Later...

Cassian joined him atop the eastern wall.

Neither man spoke for several minutes.

Below them, the academy moved through another sleepless night.

Patrols rotated.

Healers crossed between buildings.

Students reinforced barriers that had already been repaired three different times.

Cassian finally broke the silence.

"You don't tell us everything anymore."

Aidan didn't deny it.

"I don't always know what's important."

Cassian studied him.

"Or you don't know who might hear."

The words struck harder than they should have.

Aidan's jaw tightened.

"I trust you."

"I know."

Cassian's reply came immediately.

"But I don't think you trust what's happening anymore."

That...

Was true.

Not his friends.

Not Rowan.

Not Lyra.

Not Malik.

Not Elias.

What he no longer trusted...

Was certainty.

Every answer uncovered another question.

Every victory uncovered another trap.

Every trail ended where someone wanted it to end.

He had begun questioning his own instincts.

And that frightened him.

...

The academy bell rang once.

Not an alarm.

A summons.

A scout had returned from the western hills.

Exhausted.

Shaking.

He spread a weathered map across the table.

"I found this."

The map wasn't theirs.

No Blackwood markings.

No village names.

Only charcoal lines crossing forests and rivers.

Several had been scratched out.

Others circled.

One path...

Led directly toward Blackwood Academy.

Rowan frowned.

"Who made it?"

The scout swallowed.

"I don't know."

"Where did you find it?"

"In an empty camp."

"Were there bodies?"

"No."

"Supplies?"

"No."

"Anything?"

The scout hesitated.

"Just..."

He looked uncertain.

"It felt like whoever had been there knew I was coming."

Silence.

No one answered.

There was nothing to answer.

Aidan looked at the charcoal path ending at Blackwood.

His fingers rested lightly on the edge of the map.

Not because he understood it.

Because he didn't.

The second heartbeat echoed quietly beneath his own.

Steady.

Patient.

Waiting.

Outside...

Rain continued washing away the world.

Inside...

The questions remained.

And for the first time since the hunt had begun...

Aidan wondered whether every answer he found had been left for him on purpose.
