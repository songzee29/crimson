The ride back to Blackwood never happened.

A second messenger found them before they reached the academy.

His horse stumbled through the mud, collapsed, and slid several feet before stopping.

The young scout rolled free.

His face was gray.

“Master Rowan…”

He could barely breathe.

“…Red Creek…”

Aidan’s chest tightened.

“What happened?”

The scout swallowed hard.

“We were too late.”

Silence.

No one asked another question.

They already understood.

…

Red Creek lay another hour west.

The smell reached them long before the village came into view.

Smoke.

Wet ash.

Burned timber.

Then…

Blood.

Too much.

Aidan’s stomach twisted.

His body instinctively turned toward the strongest scent before he forced himself to look away.

The village gates hung open.

Homes still smoldered.

No voices.

No cries for help.

Only the crackle of dying fire.

Malik stepped over a shattered cart.

“We missed them…”

Cassian’s jaw tightened.

“No.”

His eyes swept the empty streets.

“They wanted us to.”

Helena hurried from house to house.

Searching.

Calling.

Finding no answers.

Only the wounded who had hidden beneath collapsed floors and inside root cellars.

Too frightened to emerge.

Too late to save those who hadn’t.

Aidan knelt beside an elderly man leaning against a burned wall.

The man’s breathing came in shallow gasps.

He reached weakly for Aidan’s sleeve.

“You came…”

His voice barely carried.

“…just…”

A cough interrupted him.

“…too late.”

The words struck harder than any blow.

Aidan lowered his head.

“I’m sorry.”

The old man smiled sadly.

“I know…”

His hand slipped from Aidan’s sleeve.

It never moved again.

Aidan remained kneeling long after Helena quietly closed the man’s eyes.

Another village.

Another apology.

Another life that had ended before he arrived.

The cost of surviving today…

Was knowing survival hadn’t been enough.

…

They buried the dead before sunset.

No one spoke during the work.

The silence felt heavier than grief.

As they prepared to leave…

Aidan noticed something carved into the blackened remains of the village well.

Not words.

Only another symbol.

An eye.

Watching.

He stared at it for a long moment.

The second heartbeat answered beneath his own.

Steady.

Patient.

Waiting.

He struck the carving once with the end of his staff.

The stone cracked.

The symbol disappeared.

It changed nothing.
