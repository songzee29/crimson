The ripple should have disappeared.

That was what bothered Aidan most.

Not that it happened.

That it stayed.

For three days he tried to convince himself he imagined it. The flickering streetlight. The shifting shadows. The strange feeling that words were becoming heavier inside his mind.

But every time he closed his eyes, he remembered the water.

The glass had moved.

Not fallen.

Not shaken.

Moved.

As if something invisible had touched it.

And deep down, Aidan knew he hadn't been alone in that moment.

---

The following night rain fell over New Orleans.

The city sounded different in the rain.

Quieter.

Older.

Aidan sat at his desk staring at an open notebook.

Blank pages.

Blank thoughts.

Blank excuses.

He picked up a pen.

Wrote a sentence.

Crossed it out.

Wrote another.

Crossed that out too.

Nothing felt right anymore.

Ever since the book disappeared, reality felt thinner.

Like a wall separating him from something vast had started to crack.

His eyes drifted toward the cut on his palm.

The wound was gone.

Completely healed.

Yet he could still feel where it had been.

Sometimes it burned.

Sometimes it felt cold.

Tonight it felt warm.

Almost alive.

Aidan clenched his fist.

"You're losing it."

The words sounded weak.

Even to him.

---

Thunder rolled outside.

A sudden gust of wind struck his bedroom window.

The glass rattled violently.

Aidan flinched.

The noise scraped against his nerves.

Again.

The window rattled.

Again.

Harder.

His frustration rose.

Not anger.

Just exhaustion.

Weeks of confusion.

Weeks of fear.

Weeks of pretending everything was normal.

The rattling continued.

Something inside him finally snapped.

"Stop."

The word left his mouth without thought.

Without intent.

Without power.

Or at least—

it should have.

The room instantly fell silent.

Completely silent.

No rain.

No wind.

No rattling glass.

Nothing.

Aidan froze.

His heartbeat thundered in his ears.

The silence lasted exactly three seconds.

Then—

everything returned at once.

Rain.

Wind.

Thunder.

Sound.

As though reality itself had paused.

---

Aidan stood up so fast his chair fell backward.

"What the hell..."

His breathing quickened.

No.

No.

No.

That wasn't possible.

He stared at the window.

Nothing unusual.

Everything looked normal.

Yet he knew.

He knew what happened.

Because for three seconds the world obeyed.

---

The notebook on his desk trembled.

Just slightly.

Aidan's eyes locked onto it.

The notebook slid half an inch across the desk.

Then stopped.

His stomach dropped.

He hadn't touched it.

The room suddenly felt much smaller.

Much colder.

He backed away from the desk.

Every instinct screamed at him to leave.

To run.

To pretend none of this existed.

But another feeling rose beneath the fear.

Curiosity.

Dangerous curiosity.

---

Slowly—

Aidan approached the desk again.

His hands shook.

He stared at the notebook.

Nothing happened.

Several seconds passed.

Then he whispered:

"Move."

Nothing.

Aidan exhaled.

Relief flooded through him.

See?

Maybe he imagined—

The notebook shot across the desk and slammed into the wall.

CRACK.

Aidan stumbled backward.

His heart nearly stopped.

The notebook fell to the floor.

Silence.

Complete silence.

---

Then something appeared.

Not in the room.

Inside his mind.

Words.

Cold.

Ancient.

Emotionless.

The same presence from the chamber.

The System.

For the first time since the book vanished.

---

> **BLOODBOUND VERDICT SYSTEM ONLINE**

Aidan couldn't breathe.

The words continued.

---

> **FIRST HEX REGISTERED**

> **AUTHORITY COMPATIBILITY: ACCEPTED**

> **CURSE AUTHORITY LAYER: STABLE**

> **WARNING**

> **UNTRAINED COMMAND USAGE DETECTED**

---

The words vanished.

Just like that.

Leaving Aidan alone.

Shaking.

Terrified.

And for the first time—

certain.

The book had been real.

The system was real.

And whatever Zee had left behind...

was starting to wake up.

---

Far beneath the city...

deep inside a place beyond dreams and memory...

something sleeping shifted slightly.

Not enough to wake.

Not yet.

But enough to notice.

And somewhere in that darkness...

a faint voice almost formed.

Then silence returned.

Rez slept on.

Waiting.

For death.

For hunger.

For the moment Aidan would finally trigger the next curse.
