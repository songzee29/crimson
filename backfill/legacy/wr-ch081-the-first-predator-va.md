The forest had become unbearable.

Not because it was loud.

Because it wasn’t.

Every heartbeat rose above the silence like a drum.

Every frightened breath carved a path through the darkness.

Every pulse called to him.

The Crimson Vampire no longer searched.

He sensed.

Miles away…

A frightened rabbit.

Closer…

A wounded deer.

Closer still…

Human blood.

Fresh.

Warm.

Alive.

His throat burned.

The pain spread from his fangs into his jaw until every instinct demanded the same answer.

Feed.

Now.

His body changed direction before thought followed.

The forest blurred.

Ancient trees became streaks of shadow.

He crossed streams without seeing them.

Stone shattered beneath each landing.

Nothing existed except the heartbeat ahead.

The notification vanished.

No explanation.

Only necessity.

…

Blackwood Academy stood at the edge of the forest.

No one entered.

No one left.

The gates remained barred.

Students stood atop the walls with shaking hands.

Not because an army surrounded them.

Because something worse wandered beyond the trees.

Helena quietly lowered her head.

“He isn’t choosing this.”

“No.”

Rowan never looked away from the forest.

“But everyone he meets will believe he is.”

The words settled over the walls.

Heavy.

Final.

…

Cassian sat alone beside the broken remains of the Great Hall.

His sword rested across his knees.

He had cleaned the blood from its blade.

It changed nothing.

He still felt Aidan’s final breath.

Still heard his apology.

Still felt the curse settle into his soul.

He closed his eyes.

“I killed him.”

No one answered.

There was nothing anyone could say.

…

Lyra refused to remain inside.

She stood beyond the walls, staring into the forest.

“He looked at me.”

Malik joined her quietly.

“He didn’t know you.”

“I know.”

“He nearly killed Cassian.”

“I know.”

“He chased an innocent man.”

Her voice broke.

“I know.”

She wiped away another tear.

“But for one second…”

She looked into the darkness.

“…I thought he heard my voice.”

No one told her she was wrong.

No one told her she was right.

Hope had become too fragile to argue with.

…

Far deeper within the forest…

Aidan stopped.

The heartbeat.

Closer.

Only one.

His head slowly turned.

An old woodcutter staggered through the trees carrying firewood on his back.

The man’s hand had been cut by a splintered branch.

Blood ran slowly across rough fingers.

He never noticed the crimson eyes watching from the darkness.

Aidan did.

Every drop carried its own scent.

Age.

Fear.

Life.

His fangs ached.

His vision narrowed.

The heartbeat became everything.

One step.

Another.

The woodcutter finally looked up.

His bundle fell from trembling hands.

“M-monster…”

The word echoed through the trees.

Aidan hesitated.

Not because compassion returned.

Because the sound meant nothing.

The heartbeat meant everything.

He moved.

The woodcutter turned and ran.

Branches whipped across his face.

He screamed for help.

No answer came.

Aidan reached him before the third scream ended.

His hand closed around the man’s shoulder.

The woodcutter collapsed backward.

“No… please…”

The pulse hammered wildly beneath thin skin.

The scent overwhelmed every remaining thought.

Aidan lowered his head.

His fangs hovered only inches away.

The burning inside his throat became unbearable.
