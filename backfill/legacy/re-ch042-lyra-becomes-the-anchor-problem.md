Back in the subway room, Lyra noticed something she hadn’t before.

Every time Aidan looked toward the wall—

he looked at *her afterward.*

Like he was comparing something.

Not consciously.

Instinctively.

She stepped closer again.

“Aidan,” she said quietly.

He turned.

“Tell me what you’re thinking.”

He hesitated.

Then:

“I think… you’re stopping me from going somewhere.”

Lyra froze.

“That’s not true.”

Aidan tilted his head.

“I didn’t say you were doing it on purpose.”

That answer hurt more than it should have.

Because it meant he was starting to form conclusions without emotional grounding.

Lyra lowered her voice.

“If I step out of the way, will you go?”

A long pause.

Then:

“I think I will.”

Neither of them moved.

The silence between them stretched.

Then Aidan added something quieter:

“But I don’t know why I should.”

Lyra’s breath caught.

Because that was the first time he had shown uncertainty—not about facts—

but about *choice.*
