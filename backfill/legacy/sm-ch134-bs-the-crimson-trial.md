## Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Four — The Crimson Trial

Monday began like any other.

Students hurried through the academy gates.

Teachers prepared their lessons.

The morning announcements echoed through the halls.

Nothing appeared unusual.

Yet before Aidan reached his classroom, the crimson-and-gold in his eyes intensified for only an instant.

Not from fear.

Instinct.

He stopped walking.

The hallway continued moving around him.

Someone was watching.

Not the curious glances of classmates.

Not the quiet admiration of younger students.

Something older.

Something patient.

Across the crowded courtyard...

A man in a charcoal coat stood perfectly still.

Their eyes met for less than a heartbeat.

Black.

Completely black.

The stranger nodded once.

Not respectfully.

Not mockingly.

As though confirming an answer only he understood.

Then he disappeared into the sea of students.

By the time Aidan reached the courtyard...

The man was gone.

No footsteps.

No trace.

Only an uneasy silence remained.

Exactly.

Silence returned.

The Hunter closed his hand.

Permission to begin?

Not his strength.

At last...

The first real trial.

## Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Five — Blood on the Bridge

Rain began falling just after sunset.

Aidan left Blackwood Library carrying two borrowed books about forgotten kingdoms and ancient witch covens.

Neither mentioned House Darkheart.

Neither mentioned the Crimson Bloodline.

Yet both hinted that history contained more gaps than answers.

Halfway across an old stone bridge...

He heard shouting.

Help!

A delivery truck had struck the guardrail.

The front wheels hung over the rushing river below.

Inside...

A father struggled to free himself from the driver's seat while desperately reaching toward a little girl trapped in the passenger side.

The damaged bridge groaned.

Cracks spread beneath the truck.

People gathered.

Phones appeared.

Someone yelled for emergency services.

Nobody moved closer.

The truck shifted another inch.

Without thinking...

Aidan ran.

The warmth of the Law of Protection stirred within his soul.

Not as power demanding release.

As conviction demanding action.

He reached the driver's door.

It's jammed!

The father slammed against it from inside.

Please!

My daughter!

Aidan grabbed the twisted metal with both hands.

Every instinct told him he wasn't strong enough.

He pulled anyway.

Nothing.

The truck lurched.

The river roared beneath them.

The little girl cried.

The bridge cracked again.

Not a spell.

A Law.

Protect.

Into the damaged steel itself.

Just enough.

The crimson light disappeared.

The truck plunged into the river below.

Aidan collapsed onto one knee.

His breathing became ragged.

The Law had answered.

Exhaustion spread through every muscle.

Not punishment.

Cost.

The father rushed back toward him.

Anyone would have.

But deep inside...

He already knew that wasn't true.

## Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Seven — The Crimson Crown

That night...

The faceless Founder appeared once more within Aidan's dream.

This time...

The throne room was empty.

The ancient King stood alone before the Crimson Throne.

Upon the throne rested the crimson crown Aidan had glimpsed inside the forgotten portrait.

The Founder did not wear it.

Instead...

He gently lifted it with both hands.

He looked toward Aidan.

Never seek this.

His voice echoed throughout the endless hall.

Carry it...

Only when those beside you ask you to.

The Founder slowly returned the crown to the throne.

Then the entire throne room dissolved into crimson ash.

Aidan awoke before sunrise.

His heart raced.

He remembered every word.

Not because they were loud.

Because they felt true.

Outside his bedroom window...

A raven landed upon the ancient oak overlooking the estate.

It stared directly at him.

Then flew toward the western forest.

On its leg...

Tied with crimson thread...

Was a small strip of weathered parchment.

The Hunter had made his move.

And for the first time...

History had invited Aidan to leave the safety of everything he had ever known.
