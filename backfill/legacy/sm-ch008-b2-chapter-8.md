# Chapter Eight  
## Blood Debt

The infirmary doors stood open before sunrise.

Aidan was already awake.

He had not slept.

Every time he closed his eyes...

He heard the bell.

Dong.

He felt invisible claws tearing across his back.

He tasted blood.

His own.

Professor Helena entered carrying fresh bandages.

"I told you to rest."

"I did."

"You sat in the same chair all night."

"I wasn't walking."

She sighed.

"I suppose that's progress."

Carefully she removed the blood-soaked wrappings from his ribs.

The room fell quiet.

"The wounds should have closed more."

Aidan looked down.

"They haven't?"

"No."

Helena frowned.

"They're healing..."

She paused.

"...just slower than I expected."

Neither of them understood why.

She finished replacing the bandages before quietly leaving.

Outside the infirmary...

Lyra had been waiting.

"You heard."

"I did."

Neither said another word.

They simply walked together through the waking academy.

...

Students had returned to training.

Repair crews had become faster.

Life refused to surrender.

Watching them should have brought Aidan comfort.

Instead...

He noticed something else.

Tiny cuts.

Scraped knuckles.

Bruised fingers.

His eyes found every drop of blood before he consciously realized anyone was injured.

He immediately looked away.

Too late.

The scent lingered.

Warm.

Copper.

Inviting.

His jaw tightened.

"What is wrong with me..."

Lyra glanced toward him.

"What?"

"Nothing."

He hated how quickly the lie came.

...

By midday Rowan summoned the senior group to the strategy hall.

Ancient maps covered the central table.

The eastern forest had been marked with fresh crimson ink.

"The bell wasn't random," Rowan said.

"It pushed us apart."

Cassian nodded.

"It hunted isolation."

"It also studied us," Elias added.

"It never repeated the same attack."

Aidan remained silent.

His mind replayed the battle.

Not the creature.

The feeling.

His body had reacted before thought again.

That bothered him more than broken ribs.

"If it comes back..." Malik said.

"...we stay together."

"No."

Everyone looked toward Aidan.

"If it wants us separated..."

He rested one hand against the map.

"...then next time we'll make it believe it succeeded."

Rowan slowly smiled.

"Explain."

For the next hour Aidan outlined the plan.

Smaller formations.

Hidden support.

Layered barriers.

Signals instead of shouting.

Each role depended on the others.

Not one person fought alone.

By the time he finished...

No one questioned who was leading.

...

The alarm came before sunset.

Not the bell.

Screams.

A supply caravan approaching Blackwood had been attacked along the northern pass.

Only two survivors reached the academy.

"They're still out there..."

One of the merchants collapsed to his knees.

"They're killing everyone..."

Rowan didn't hesitate.

"Move!"

...

The northern pass cut between towering cliffs where the road narrowed enough for only two wagons.

Broken crates littered the ground.

Dead horses.

Shattered wheels.

No bodies.

"They took them," Cassian said.

Aidan knelt beside a deep groove carved through stone.

Not claws.

Something heavy had been dragged.

"They're alive."

"How do you know?" Malik asked.

"There's almost no blood."

The answer chilled everyone.

Whoever had attacked...

Wanted prisoners.

A faint cry echoed from deeper within the canyon.

Then another.

Aidan's heartbeat accelerated.

The second heartbeat answered.

THUMP.

His senses sharpened instantly.

"There."

He pointed toward a narrow crack hidden between massive rocks.

Everyone followed.

The passage opened into a vast cavern lit by strange pale crystals.

Chains hung from ancient pillars.

Captured merchants knelt in terrified silence.

Standing among them...

Were three towering figures wrapped in gray skin marked with black runes.

Each carried massive hooked blades fashioned from dark stone.

Their empty white eyes turned toward the intruders.

One smiled.

Its mouth opened far wider than any human's should.

Then it screamed.

The sound shattered crystal.

Blood trickled from Elias's ears.

Malik dropped to one knee.

Lyra nearly lost her balance.

Only Aidan remained standing.

Not because he resisted.

Because something inside him recognized danger before fear could.

The creatures charged.

"Formation!"

The team moved instantly.

Exactly as they had practiced.

Cassian intercepted the first.

Malik anchored the center.

Lyra and Elias reinforced barriers around the prisoners.

Aidan met the largest creature alone.

Its hooked blade crashed downward.

He caught it with a Crimson Law.

Stone exploded beneath his feet.

His broken ribs screamed.

The creature pushed harder.

Stronger.

Far stronger than the forest beasts.

His knees buckled.

The Law cracked.

For the first time since mastering the Witch Authority...

Aidan felt his magic losing ground.

The creature smiled again.

It knew.

His body reacted.

Not his mind.

His left hand abandoned the collapsing barrier.

Instead...

It reached directly toward the creature's throat.

No Law.

No spell.

Pure instinct.

The creature froze.

Not from magic.

From surprise.

That heartbeat was enough.

Cassian's blade pierced through its shoulder.

Malik slammed into its side.

Together they drove it backward.

"Aidan!"

Lyra's voice pulled him back.

He stared at his own hand.

Why...

Why had he reached like that?

The battle raged around him.

Another creature broke through Elias's barrier.

One merchant screamed.

Without hesitation Aidan cast.

"Hold!"

Invisible force slammed the creature into a cavern wall.

Rock collapsed around it.

The prisoners escaped.

The final enemy fled deeper into darkness rather than die.

No one pursued.

Saving lives mattered more.

...

They returned to Blackwood long after nightfall.

Every prisoner survived.

Malik's shoulder had been dislocated.

Cassian suffered another deep wound across his side.

Elias carried fresh burns.

Lyra's magic reserves were nearly empty.

Aidan...

Collapsed just inside the academy gates.

...

When consciousness returned...

He found himself lying in the infirmary again.

His ribs had shifted.

One had pierced muscle.

Breathing felt like swallowing knives.

Lyra sat beside him.

"You saved them."

He stared at the ceiling.

"We all did."

She smiled softly.

"You don't have to carry every victory alone."

His eyes closed.

For the first time in days...

He almost believed her.

Then—
