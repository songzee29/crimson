## Chapter Sixteen — Back to Reality

The beam of sunlight stretched across the valley like a bridge between two worlds.

Aidan took one final look at the Sanctuary.

The Guardian stood at the entrance to the golden gate, his broken sword resting against his shoulder. Though separated by hundreds of yards, he raised a single fist to his chest.

A silent farewell.

A silent promise.

Aidan returned the gesture.

The moment he stepped into the light, the entire world disappeared.

...

Cold air struck his face.

He stumbled forward and caught himself against the stone pedestal inside the hidden room beneath the Darkheart estate.

The chamber was quiet once again.

The passages had sealed themselves.

The hidden city...

The Guardian...

The Valley of Beginnings...

All of it had vanished.

Only the empty pedestal remained.

Had it been real?

Aidan looked down.

The weathered wooden practice staff was still in his hand.

His answer.

The Legacy had never been a dream.

It had become part of his life.

He climbed the long staircase leading back toward the estate.

Each step felt lighter than before.

Not because he had become stronger.

Because he had become certain.

His life would never be ordinary again.

When he reached the old hallway, the grandfather clock near the entrance chimed softly.

Three times.

Aidan froze.

He had spent what felt like days inside the Sanctuary.

Yet only three minutes had passed.

The Legacy existed beyond time.

He slowly walked through the mansion.

Servants hurried through the halls without paying him any attention.

No one knew where he had been.

No one noticed the faint crimson glow hidden beneath his eyes.

For now...

The Legacy remained his secret.

## Chapter Seventeen — The Ordinary World

Morning sunlight spilled across the city as students filled the sidewalks, buses rumbled through traffic, and shop owners prepared for another ordinary day.

To everyone else...

Nothing had changed.

To Aidan...

Everything had.

He sat quietly on the bus ride to school, resting the practice staff across his knees. Wrapped in cloth, it looked like nothing more than an old walking stick.

His classmates laughed in the seats behind him.

Someone complained about homework.

Another argued about basketball.

Life continued exactly as it had yesterday.

Yet Aidan couldn't stop noticing things.

The rhythm of people's footsteps.

The way emotions subtly changed their breathing.

The tiny movements before someone turned their head.

Lessons from the Guardian replayed inside his mind.

Observe.

Understand.

Then act.

Without realizing it, he had already begun seeing the world differently.

Aidan stepped onto the campus just as someone collided with his shoulder.

Sorry!

She quickly knelt to gather her notebooks before noticing Aidan helping her.

Their hands reached for the same book.

For a brief second...

Time seemed to pause.

The air shifted.

A faint warmth spread through Aidan's chest.

The young woman blinked.

Her eyes lingered on him with the same confusion he felt.

Thank you.

Aidan.

She waved.

See you around, Aidan.

History.

Mathematics.

Literature.

When the teacher spoke about ancient civilizations disappearing without explanation, Aidan couldn't help wondering how many truths history had forgotten.

Or hidden.

At lunch, he found a quiet bench beneath an old oak tree.

He unwrapped the practice staff and balanced it across his hands.

One breath.

One movement.

Exactly as the Guardian had demonstrated.

Again.

Again.

Again.

Every repetition became smoother.

More natural.

He didn't notice someone watching him.

Across the courtyard stood a tall student with sharp eyes and an athletic build.

His name was Malik.

Unlike everyone else, he wasn't looking at the staff.

He was studying Aidan's movements.

Interesting...

Malik muttered.

Nobody practices like that anymore.

Not unless someone taught them.

Their eyes met.

Neither looked away.

Aidan nodded politely.

Malik returned the gesture before walking off.

Money.

Phone.

Please...

Aidan stopped walking.

His heartbeat quickened.

Protect others.

Not because a notification demanded it.

Because it was right.

The three teenagers turned toward him.

Observe.

Understand.
