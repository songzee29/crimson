## Chapter One Hundred Seventy-Two — The Keeper of the Broken Bridge

The Guardian of the Bridge rose from the depths of the canyon with the slow certainty of a mountain waking from sleep.

Stone cracked.

Ancient dust cascaded from its shoulders.

Crimson vines hung from weathered armor forged long before any kingdom now standing had been born.

Its face resembled neither man nor beast.

Instead, it wore a smooth stone helm crowned by three ancient symbols.

A circle of runes.

A crimson crescent.

A wolf's head.

The same three symbols Aidan had seen since entering the Living Legacy.

The giant's crimson eyes swept across the travelers.

One by one.

Malik instinctively reached for his weapon.

Cassian's hand rested near the hilt of his sword.

Lyra quietly stepped closer to Kael.

Elias...

Stepped in front of Aidan without hesitation.

The movement was so natural that Aidan almost smiled.

Even after years apart...

Some instincts never changed.

The giant's voice echoed through the canyon.

Pilgrims...

State your purpose.

Its words sounded like boulders grinding together.

Not hostile.

Ancient.

Rowan had warned them.

The Foundations were never conquered.

They were approached with respect.

Aidan stepped beside Elias.

We seek passage.

Not conquest.

Not ownership.

Only understanding.

The Keeper remained motionless.

Understanding...

Must be proven.

Not spoken.

## Chapter One Hundred Seventy-Three — The Trial of Trust

The stone beneath their feet trembled.

The crimson pathway extending across the canyon slowly widened.

Then...

Sections of the bridge vanished.

Only scattered platforms remained suspended above the endless abyss.

The Keeper spoke once more.

One may cross alone.

Or...

Many may cross together.

Choose wisely.

Silence settled over the company.

Cassian studied the broken path.

If one person crosses...

The rest remain safe.

Malik frowned.

That sounds too easy.

Elias quietly shook his head.

Nothing about the Foundations has ever been easy.

Aidan stared at the bridge.

The gaps were impossible to jump.

Unless...

He suddenly understood.

It's not a bridge.

Everyone looked toward him.

It's a test.

The Keeper did not move.

Aidan continued.

No one gets across alone.

The crimson stones aren't broken...

They're waiting.

He turned toward his friends.

Take each other's hands.

Cassian raised an eyebrow.

Seriously?

Trust me.

Without another word...

Elias extended his hand first.

Aidan took it.

Malik grabbed Aidan's other hand.

Lyra reached for Kael.

Darius linked with Cassian.

The seven formed a single chain.

The moment the final hands joined...

Every missing section of the bridge reappeared.

Solid.

Whole.

The Keeper slowly lowered its head.

Trust...

Recognized.

The first trial had never been about strength.

## Chapter One Hundred Seventy-Four — The Forgotten Crossing

Together they crossed the ancient bridge.

Each step caused crimson lines hidden within the stone to awaken beneath their feet.

Not magical.

Historical.

The bridge was remembering.

Halfway across...

Aidan noticed carvings running along the ancient railings.

Thousands of names.

Not kings.

Not generals.

Pilgrims.

Every person who had crossed the bridge before them.

Each name ended with the same inscription.

**Walk well.**

Elias smiled.

They're not recording greatness.

They're recording the journey.

Exactly.

Rowan's lessons echoed within Aidan's memory.

History belongs to those who preserve it.

Not those who seek glory.

Near the center of the bridge...

Cassian quietly stopped walking.

Everyone noticed.

His expression had changed.

He was staring at one name.

His own.

Impossible...

It had been carved into the bridge centuries before he was born.

Before anyone could ask what was wrong...

The name disappeared.

As though it had never existed.

Cassian quickly continued walking.

No one mentioned it.

But Aidan saw the fear in his eyes.

For the first time...

Cassian looked less like a confident traveler...

And more like someone running from a truth he could no longer outrun.
