# Chapter One Hundred Two

The warehouse settled into silence.

Ash drifted through broken shafts of moonlight like black snow.

No one spoke.

No one celebrated.

Another mission had ended.

Another threat had disappeared.

There would be another before sunrise.

Cassian wiped dark blood from the edge of his sword before sliding it into its sheath.

"We're finished here."

Aidan knelt beside the largest pile of ash.

His crimson-gold eyes remained fixed on the floor.

"No."

Everyone looked toward him.

He reached down and rubbed a small amount of black residue between his fingers.

"It died too quickly."

Malik frowned.

"What does that mean?"

"It means something killed its life before I did."

The others exchanged uneasy glances.

Lyra stepped closer.

"You've seen this before."

Aidan stood.

"Twice."

"When?"

"The Appalachian Mountains."

"The Everglades."

"And now here."

His voice never changed.

No emotion.

Only observation.

"They're getting closer."

A cold breeze swept through the shattered warehouse.

Rowan walked forward, studying the strange black ash.

"For two years we've hunted creatures that shouldn't exist."

He looked toward Aidan.

"And for two years you've said the same thing."

"They aren't invading."

"They're running."

No one argued.

Not anymore.

Two years ago...

That theory would have sounded impossible.

Now...

It was becoming the only explanation that fit.

No insignias.

None of them appeared frightened.

Toward the river.

The rain soaked through his coat without slowing him.

Slow.

Heavy.

Always there.

His hunger followed.

Never screaming.

Never fading.

Simply waiting.

Tourists.

Workers.

Families.

Children.

His throat tightened.

For one brief second...

His instincts whispered.

Just one.

No one would know.

His fingers slowly curled into a fist.

"No."

The word left his mouth quietly.

The hunger obeyed.

Not because it was gone.

Because he had become stronger than it.

For now.

Behind him, soft footsteps approached.

Lyra stopped beside him without speaking.

For several moments they simply watched the river.

"You still hear them?"

"Every heartbeat."

"Does it ever stop?"

"No."

She nodded as though she had expected the answer.

"I stopped asking because I hoped one day you'd tell me it had become easier."

Aidan stared into the darkness.

"It didn't."

"It became familiar."

There had once been warmth in his eyes.

Laughter.

Frustration.

Hope.

Not emptiness.

Control.

Perfect, exhausting control.

"I sleep."

"When?"

"I figured."
