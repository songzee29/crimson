# Chapter Four  
## Quiet Preparations

The rain finally broke.

Sunlight poured across Blackwood Academy, revealing fresh stone where ruined walls had once stood and new runes glowing along repaired towers.

For the first time in weeks...

Students complained about ordinary lessons.

Professor Helena smiled when she heard them.

Normal complaints meant normal lives.

The academy had earned that much.

Aidan walked the outer perimeter before classes began, inspecting the restored defensive circles surrounding the grounds. His hands remained behind his back while crimson runes shimmered beneath his feet.

Every barrier answered him.

Every ward recognized him.

The Crimson Witch needed no introductions anymore.

He merely listened.

The academy spoke through its magic.

Satisfied, he continued walking.

...

Far below the western cliffs, several younger students struggled to reinforce a collapsed watchtower.

Their spellwork buckled under the weight of cracked stone.

"It won't hold!"

"It keeps slipping!"

Aidan raised one hand.

No words.

No display.

The fractured stones simply stopped falling.

Hundreds of pounds of shattered masonry floated motionless in the air.

The students froze.

Their eyes widened.

"You have enough time now," Aidan said calmly.

"Repair the foundation first."

The apprentices hurried into motion.

Only after the new support pillars had settled into place did Aidan lower his hand.

Each stone returned exactly where it belonged.

Not one landed out of place.

When the final block settled...

The tower stood stronger than before.

One of the younger witches stared at him in amazement.

"You didn't even look..."

Aidan smiled faintly.

"The magic already knew where it belonged."

He continued down the path before they could ask another question.

...

Later that afternoon Rowan found him beneath the old oak.

"You've started fixing problems before anyone asks."

"I noticed they needed help."

"I know."

Rowan leaned against the ancient tree.

"I also noticed you didn't solve everything for them."

"They needed to finish it themselves."

The old master nodded.

"You've learned."

Silence settled comfortably between them.

Eventually Rowan spoke again.

"I don't have many lessons left."

Aidan looked toward him.

"I figured."

"You've surpassed the stage where I teach you how to become a witch."

A gentle breeze stirred the branches overhead.

"My remaining lessons have nothing to do with magic."

Aidan waited.

"They're about surviving the person you'll become."

The words lingered long after Rowan walked away.

...

Training resumed before sunset.

Not against monsters.

Against one another.

Friendly duels.

Reflex drills.

Barrier control.

Precision.

Control over power.

Malik charged first.

Predictably.

Aidan sidestepped without casting.

Malik laughed.

"I almost had you."

"You almost ran into me."

"Same thing."

Before Malik could recover, Elias struck from behind.

Aidan reacted automatically.

His body moved before conscious thought.

He caught Elias's wrist.

Twisted.

Swept his legs.

Pinned him to the ground.

The movement ended in less than a heartbeat.

Silence.

Aidan blinked.

He hadn't meant to use that much force.

Elias lay staring up at him.

"...Well."

He exhaled.

"That answered my question."

Aidan immediately released him.

"Sorry."

"You almost broke my shoulder."

"I misjudged."

Malik helped Elias stand.

"You've never misjudged before."

Aidan frowned.

Neither had he.

The thought unsettled him.

Only briefly.

Training continued.

No one mentioned it again.

But Rowan had seen everything.

...

That evening the Great Hall buzzed with conversation.

Repairs had progressed faster than expected.

Several classrooms would reopen tomorrow.

Students applauded.

Professor Bennett claimed it was because everyone had finally started listening to him.

Nobody believed him.

Laughter echoed through the hall.

Aidan reached for a loaf of bread.

His hand paused.

Someone several tables away accidentally cut their thumb while slicing vegetables.

One crimson bead welled against dark skin.

His eyes found it instantly.

Again.

The scent reached him before anyone reacted.

Warm.

Metallic.

Fresh.

For one impossible heartbeat...

Everything else tasted dull.

His fingers tightened around the bread until the crust cracked.

"...Aidan?"

Cassian's voice.

Aidan looked over.

"You crushed it."

He looked down.

The loaf had collapsed beneath his grip.

"I didn't realize."

Malik laughed.

"Guess you're stronger than you thought."

Everyone chuckled.

The moment passed.

Only Aidan remained unsettled.

...

Night returned.

Sleep did not.

He stood once more atop Blackwood's highest tower.

Below him, lanterns glowed across the academy.

Safe.

Peaceful.

His chest tightened.

Thump.

...

Thump.

The second heartbeat.

Closer now.

Not louder.

Simply...

Nearer.

He closed his eyes.

The Crimson Witch Authority unfolded across the academy.

Everything answered.

Every rune.

Every barrier.

Every living spark of magic.

Then—

Something answered that wasn't magic.

His head turned sharply toward the eastern forest.

Nothing stood there.

No movement.

No presence.

Only darkness.

Yet every instinct insisted something had been watching him.

The feeling vanished before he could investigate.

A cool sensation spread across the back of his neck.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

**CRIMSON SYSTEM**

**Behavioral Analysis Complete.**

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The notification faded.

Another replaced it.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

**Observation Recorded.**

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Nothing else.

No explanation.

No reward.

No quest.

Aidan waited.

Silence.

Then—

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

**Unknown Blueprint Archived.**

**Access Restricted.**

**Authority Requirement Unmet.**

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The messages disappeared before he could think to question them.

His brow furrowed.

"What authority?"

There was no reply.

Only the quiet wind crossing Blackwood Academy.

Only the steady pulse beneath his own heartbeat.

Only the ancient patience of something preparing for a day that had not yet come.

Far beneath conscious thought...

His body took one slow breath.

And for the briefest instant...

It wished the night carried the scent of blood.
