Something ancient…

Began walking.

The heartbeat beneath Blackwood Academy did not grow louder.

It grew closer.

Stone remembered.

Ancient corridors, sealed long before the academy had ever existed, answered with faint vibrations that no ordinary ear could detect.

Dust slipped from forgotten ceilings.

Hairline cracks spread through walls that had not moved in centuries.

Nothing broke.

Not yet.

The mountain itself seemed to take one slow breath.

Then…

It became still again.

⸻

Morning arrived beneath heavy clouds.

Students crossed the courtyard wrapped in conversation, completely unaware that anything had changed.

To them…

Blackwood Academy remained exactly as it had been yesterday.

To Master Rowan…

It did not.

He paused beside the eastern arch before anyone else had awakened.

His weathered fingers rested against the stone.

He closed his eyes.

The stone answered.

Not with words.

With memory.

A second rhythm pulsed deep below.

Steady.

Deliberate.

Walking.

The old traveler slowly lowered his hand.

“So…”

“You’ve reached the academy.”

⸻

Professor Helena approached from behind.

“You came here first.”

“I always do.”

She studied his face.

“It moved.”

Rowan nodded once.

“It did.”

“Should we seal the lower passages?”

“No.”

She looked surprised.

“No?”

“If we begin fearing every shadow…”

“We’ll teach the students to fear them too.”

He looked toward the awakening campus.

“They deserve one more ordinary day.”

⸻

Breakfast passed with unusual energy.

A regional inter-academy exhibition had been announced for the following month, and nearly every conversation revolved around predictions and friendly rivalries.

Cassian slammed both hands onto the table.

“We’re winning.”

Malik looked at him.

“You don’t even know what the events are.”

“Doesn’t matter.”

“It matters a little.”

“It absolutely does not.”

Lyra laughed.

“I admire your confidence.”

Elias took another bite of breakfast.

“I admire his ability to ignore reality.”

Cassian pointed dramatically.

“Reality has never supported greatness.”

Aidan shook his head.

“That’s definitely something you made up.”

“It sounded wise.”

“It sounded like you.”

The table erupted into laughter.

Nearby…

Master Rowan quietly watched from across the hall.

He wasn’t watching Aidan.

He was watching how often Aidan laughed now.

How easily.

The Founder would have smiled.

⸻

After breakfast…

Professor Bennett led several classes toward the northern training woods.

Today’s lesson required teamwork.

No individual scores.

No rankings.

Only shared objectives.

The students spread through the forest in groups of five.

Aidan’s group naturally remained together.

The assignment appeared simple.

Locate six carved markers hidden throughout the woodland.

Map their positions.

Return before sunset.

No magic.

No competition.

Just observation.

⸻

The deeper they walked…

The quieter the forest became.

Cassian stretched.

“I still think one of these markers is fake.”

“There are only six.”

“So seven.”

“There can’t be seven.”

“That’s exactly what someone hiding a seventh marker would want us to think.”

Malik laughed.

“I’ve missed this.”

“You saw him yesterday.”

“I’ve missed him since breakfast.”

Lyra smiled without looking back.

“You two really are impossible.”

“No.”

Elias answered.

“Just one of them.”

⸻

They reached a narrow stream crossing.

The wooden bridge looked old.

Not dangerous.

Just old.

Cassian stepped forward first.

The bridge groaned beneath his weight.

“See?”

He looked back proudly.

“Perfectly—”

The center plank cracked.

Not enough to collapse.

Enough for Cassian’s foot to slip through.

His balance disappeared instantly.

Before anyone could react…

Aidan moved.

Not quickly.

Correctly.

He didn’t grab Cassian’s arm.

He caught the back of his jacket and shifted his own weight backward exactly as Rowan had once shown him with the practice staff.

The bridge steadied.

Cassian regained his footing.

Silence.

Then…

Cassian looked down through the broken plank.

“…I’m never making fun of bridges again.”

“You’ll forget by tomorrow.”

“I probably will.”

He turned toward Aidan.

“Thanks.”

Aidan smiled.

“You’ve caught me before.”

Cassian frowned.

“When?”

“You don’t even realize you do.”

Cassian thought for a long moment.

Then quietly nodded.

“…Fair enough.”

They crossed together.

This time…

One at a time.

⸻

Far beneath the stream…

Beyond the reach of sunlight…

The second heartbeat paused.

As though listening.

Then…

It continued walking.

One step.

Another.

Ancient stone answered each movement with whispers too old to become words.

⸻

By late afternoon…

The group had located five of the six markers.

Only one remained.

Elias crouched beside an old oak studying the map.

“We’ve searched everywhere except…”

He stopped.

“The eastern ridge.”

Malik looked up.

“That’s outside the usual trail.”

“Exactly.”

Aidan studied the sketch.

“It isn’t outside.”

He pointed toward an almost-forgotten path hidden by years of fallen leaves.

“It used to be the trail.”

Everyone looked.

The old path had nearly vanished beneath time.

Cassian grinned.

“Guess we’re taking the scenic route.”
