# Chapter Forty-Four: The Burden of Knowing

The charcoal map remained spread across the strategy table long after everyone else had stopped speaking.

No names.

No symbols anyone recognized.

Only lines.

Some ended abruptly.

Others circled the academy as though someone had walked the surrounding forests over and over again.

Rowan finally rolled the map closed.

"We don't know what it means."

"No," Aidan said quietly.

"But someone wanted it found."

The room fell silent again.

Nobody argued.

Because no one could explain why an empty camp would leave behind the only thing worth finding.

...

Blackwood adapted once more.

No patrol used the same route twice.

Messengers traveled in groups of three.

The old watchtowers were abandoned in favor of concealed observation posts.

Even instructors no longer announced where they were going until moments before they left.

Not because they distrusted each other.

Because certainty had become dangerous.

...

That afternoon, Aidan stood on the northern wall watching apprentices reinforce the barriers.

His ribs still ached whenever he reached overhead.

The wound across his side had finally begun closing again.

His shoulder remained stiff from the trap in the forest.

He rolled it once.

Pain answered immediately.

He ignored it.

Below him...

A first-year struggled to lift a supply crate nearly half his size.

Without thinking, Aidan climbed down and took the weight.

The apprentice smiled.

"Thank you, sir."

Aidan carried the crate to the wall before realizing how light it felt.

Not effortless.

Lighter.

He frowned.

The thought lingered only a moment before another sound stole his attention.

Someone breathing too quickly.

His head turned.

Across the courtyard, an older groundskeeper had collapsed against a stone bench.

Before anyone shouted...

Aidan was already moving.

Helena arrived seconds later.

"How did you know?"

"I heard him."

She looked toward the bench.

It stood nearly a hundred yards away.

She said nothing.

But the concern in her eyes deepened.

...

That evening...

Lyra found him sitting alone inside the abandoned archive.

Not reading.

Simply staring at an unopened book.

"You've been here an hour."

"I know."

"You haven't turned a page."

He looked down.

"I forgot why I came."

She sat beside him.

For several quiet moments neither spoke.

Then she reached into her satchel.

A small wrapped pastry.

"You skipped lunch."

He accepted it automatically.

Took one bite.

Forced another.

Then stopped.

"I'm sorry."

She looked at the untouched half still in his hand.

"You don't have to apologize."

"I used to be hungry all the time."

"I remember."

"I don't anymore."

The words frightened him more than he intended them to.

Lyra gently took the remaining pastry.

Not because she was offended.

Because she knew he wouldn't finish it.

She leaned her shoulder lightly against his.

Neither tried to fill the silence.

For the first time that day...

Aidan allowed himself to close his eyes.

Only for a minute.

Only because someone else was watching.

# Chapter Forty-Five: The Voice That Knew

The alarm came after midnight.

Not bells.

A single crimson flare rising beyond the eastern forest.

One of Blackwood's hidden observation posts.

Rowan was already moving before the flare faded.

Within minutes, Aidan, Lyra, Cassian, Malik, Elias, Helena, and a small response team disappeared into the trees.

No one ran blindly anymore.

Every step was measured.

Every shadow questioned.

The observation post came into view.

Nothing had been destroyed.

The barrier remained intact.

The lantern still burned.

One sentry stood where he should have been.

Motionless.

"Aidan..."

Cassian whispered.

The sentry wasn't moving.

Aidan approached carefully.

The young guard was alive.

Eyes open.

Breathing.

But utterly unresponsive.

He stared into the darkness beyond the trees as though he still saw something no one else could.

Helena examined him.

"No wounds."

"No poison."

"No spell I recognize."

The guard suddenly whispered.

"So close..."

Then fell silent again.

"So close to what?" Rowan asked.

No answer came.

Aidan looked toward the forest.

His hearing stretched outward.

Branches.

Wind.

Owls.

Rainwater dripping from leaves.

Then...

Footsteps.

Very light.

Already retreating.

He started after them.

Only three steps.

Then stopped himself.

Not this time.

Not alone.

Not because something wanted him to.

He stepped back.

"We stay together."

Cassian glanced at him and gave a slight nod.

The lesson had finally taken root.

As they prepared to escort the guard back to Blackwood...

Aidan noticed something caught beneath the observation platform.

A strip of dark cloth.

Clean.

Deliberately placed.

Folded inside it...

A single piece of charcoal.

Nothing else.

No message.

No mark.

No explanation.

Only the same kind of charcoal used to draw the mysterious map.

He stared at it for a long moment.

Someone had been here.

Close enough to leave it.

Close enough to watch them arrive.

Close enough...

To know they would find it.

As they turned back toward Blackwood, the forest remained silent.

Yet Aidan couldn't shake the feeling that somewhere beyond the trees...

Someone was still watching.

Not waiting for him to make a mistake.

Waiting to see...

What he would do next.
