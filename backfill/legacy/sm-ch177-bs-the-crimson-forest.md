## Chapter One Hundred Seventy-Seven — The Crimson Forest

Morning never truly reached the forest.

Although the sun had risen beyond the mountains, only scattered rays pierced the immense crimson canopy overhead.

Everything beneath the trees felt timeless.

The air carried the scent of cedar, damp earth, and ancient rain.

No birds sang.

No insects buzzed.

It wasn't lifeless.

It was listening.

The company resumed their journey along the forgotten road.

Every few hundred yards another crimson milestone emerged from beneath moss and tangled roots.

Most carried the same inscription.

**Leave the road wiser than you found it.**

Elias ran his fingers across the weathered stone.

Whoever built this place...

They expected people to come back.

Rowan nodded.

Exactly.

A road meant for only one journey is a path.

A road walked by generations...

Becomes history.

As they continued deeper into the forest, Aidan noticed something strange.

The trees slowly leaned toward the road.

Not enough to notice at first.

But after several miles...

It became impossible to ignore.

They weren't growing randomly.

They formed a corridor.

Almost...

As though the forest itself remembered where travelers belonged.

## Chapter One Hundred Seventy-Eight — The Child in the Woods

By midday the company heard laughter.

Children's laughter.

Everyone stopped.

Kael frowned.

Did you hear that?

Before anyone answered...

A little girl stepped onto the road.

She couldn't have been older than eight.

Her dark curls framed a face streaked with dirt and tears.

She clutched a worn cloth doll tightly against her chest.

Please...

Can you help me?

Aidan immediately knelt.

Of course.

What's wrong?

I can't find my grandfather.

We were gathering herbs...

Then I got lost.

Rowan's expression tightened.

He quietly scanned the forest.

Something wasn't right.

The girl looked terrified.

Would you help me find him?

Without hesitation, Aidan nodded.

We'll help.

The little girl smiled through her tears.

Thank you.

She turned and hurried deeper into the trees.

Elias started after her.

Aidan followed.

The others came close behind.

Only Rowan lingered.

His eyes narrowed toward the untouched road.

He whispered almost too quietly to hear.

There are no villages in this forest.

## Chapter One Hundred Seventy-Nine — The Forest Remembers

The little girl led them through dense undergrowth for nearly twenty minutes.

The farther they walked...

The quieter the forest became.

Finally...

She stopped beside an enormous crimson oak.

She pointed toward its roots.

He's down there.

Aidan looked.

No one.

Only tangled roots disappearing into the earth.

He turned back.

The little girl was gone.

Completely gone.

Not running.

Not hiding.

Gone.

Malik immediately stepped forward.

Where—

His voice stopped.

The path behind them had disappeared.

Every direction looked identical.

Cassian slowly drew his sword.

We've been led off the Pilgrim Road.

Rowan knelt beside one of the roots.

Not by the child.

By the forest.

The enormous tree suddenly creaked.

Its branches swayed despite the complete absence of wind.

Then...

Ancient words appeared across the bark.

Not carved.

Growing.

**Those who protect strangers...**

**Will never truly be lost.**

The roots slowly shifted.

A new path opened before them.

Leading directly back toward the Pilgrim Road.

Elias laughed softly.

The forest wasn't trying to trick us.

It was testing us.

Rowan smiled.

Every Foundation teaches differently.

The Second Foundation asked whether you could trust each other.

The forest asks...

Whether you'll protect someone you've never met.

Aidan looked once more toward the place where the little girl had stood.

He smiled.

Whether she had been real...

Or merely the forest's memory...

The answer would have remained the same.

He would have helped.

No one discussed hidden enemies.

They shared stories.

Especially Rowan.

Lyra quietly showed everyone the newest sketch in her book.

Beautiful.

You even captured the way it looked at him.

Lyra nodded toward Aidan.

Because it wasn't looking at the stag.

It was looking at him.

The conversation slowly turned toward childhood.

Don't.

Grab three books...

## Chapter One Hundred Eighty-One — The Crimson Wolf

Just before dawn...

Aidan awoke.

Not from danger.

From a sound.

Leaves rustling.

He quietly stepped away from camp.

The forest greeted him with pale silver mist.

Standing at the edge of the trees...

Was a massive wolf.

Its fur was midnight black.

Its eyes shimmered crimson and gold.

The same colors as Aidan's own.

Neither moved.

Neither growled.

The wolf simply watched him.

Then...

It turned.

Taking only a few slow steps before looking back.

Waiting.

Aidan followed.

Not because anyone told him to.

Because something deep inside his soul recognized the invitation.

The wolf led him through the silent forest until they reached a secluded clearing.

At its center stood an ancient stone.

Upon it rested three symbols.

A circle of runes.

A crimson crescent.

A wolf's head.

The wolf approached the stone.

Then gently pressed its forehead against the third symbol.

A low howl echoed through the clearing.

Not threatening.

Mournful.

Ancient.

Aidan felt the sound resonate within his chest.

Not in the Living Legacy.

Not in his mind.

In his very blood.

The wolf looked at him one final time.

Then faded into the morning mist.

Only its paw prints remained.

Aidan stepped toward the ancient stone.

For the first time...

He placed his hand upon the symbol of the wolf.

The stone remained cold.

Nothing awakened.

Nothing changed.

Yet deep beneath the silence...

Something had recognized him.

Not the Legacy.

Not the Foundation.

Something older.

Something that had been waiting since before history learned to write its own name.

And far away, hidden beyond every known map...

Another ancient seal quietly formed its first hairline crack.
