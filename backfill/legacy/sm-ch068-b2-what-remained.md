# Chapter Sixty-Eight: What Remained

Blackwood Academy no longer waited for dawn.

The wounded arrived before it.

The dead arrived after.

The pattern had become so familiar that no one looked surprised when another wagon rolled through the gates beneath a sky still filled with stars.

Helena walked toward it.

She already knew what she would find.

Bandages.

Blood.

Silence.

...

Aidan stood nearby helping unload the survivors.

His left arm remained secured against his side beneath a leather brace.

Every movement tugged against the torn muscles across his ribs.

He never reached with that arm anymore.

His body had quietly learned its limits.

His heart had not.

A frightened boy stumbled while climbing from the wagon.

Before anyone else moved...

Aidan caught him with his good arm.

"Easy."

Only one word.

The boy nodded.

"Thank you."

Aidan simply guided him toward the infirmary.

He never mentioned the fresh blood now soaking through his own bandages.

...

That afternoon Rowan assembled the remaining patrol captains.

The map had changed again.

Three roads were crossed out.

Two villages had been evacuated permanently.

One watchtower would not be rebuilt.

"We're shrinking our line," Rowan said.

No one objected.

Every argument had already been lost weeks earlier.

Aidan studied the map.

Not searching for another solution.

Accepting the one before him.

"We can't hold everything."

His voice was calm.

Quiet.

Matter-of-fact.

The room fell silent.

Cassian looked at him.

Months ago...

Aidan would have searched for another way.

Now...

He simply measured what could still be saved.

...

The meeting ended.

Everyone left.

Cassian remained behind.

"Aidan."

He stopped.

"When did you stop arguing with Rowan?"

Aidan considered the question.

"I don't know."

"You always challenged him."

"I was wrong."

"No."

Cassian stepped closer.

"You believed impossible things."

Aidan looked toward the academy courtyard.

Children carried water between buildings.

Students repaired shattered stone.

Life continued because someone chose to keep it moving.

"I still do."

Cassian frowned.

"Then why don't you fight anymore?"

Aidan answered without turning.

"I do."

His hand rested lightly against the map.

"I just stopped fighting reality."

The words stayed with Cassian long after Aidan walked away.

...

That night...

Lyra found Aidan beneath the old oak.

He wasn't watching the forest.

He wasn't listening.

He simply sat with his back against the ancient trunk.

Eyes closed.

Breathing slowly.

"You fell asleep here."

He opened one eye.

"For a minute."

"It was almost an hour."

"I didn't mean to."

She sat beside him.

Neither spoke.

The silence wasn't uncomfortable.

It was simply all either of them had left.

Finally Lyra leaned her head gently against his shoulder.

"You don't have to carry everything."

He didn't answer immediately.

When he finally spoke...

His voice was almost too quiet to hear.

"I know."

She waited.

"But if I put it down..."

He looked toward the lights of Blackwood.

"...someone else picks it up."

He closed his eyes again.

"I'd rather it stay with me."

Lyra reached for his hand.

He squeezed hers once.

Then they sat beneath the old oak until dawn found them both still awake.

# Chapter Sixty-Nine: The Last Good Choice

The call came before sunrise.

Not from a messenger.

From the academy wards themselves.

Three outer barriers failed within the same minute.

Not destroyed.

Abandoned.

The patrol assigned to them had fallen back.

Rowan read the report only once.

"They're driving people toward East Hollow."

Elias spread another map across the table.

"If the refugees reach the gorge before we do..."

"They'll be trapped," Malik finished.

No one wasted another word.

They moved.

...

East Hollow lay between steep cliffs and a fast-moving river.

By the time Blackwood arrived...

Hundreds of civilians crowded the narrow road.

Parents carried infants.

Older children pulled younger siblings by the hand.

Some looked back constantly.

Others no longer looked back at all.

Panic spread faster than orders.

Aidan stepped into the center of the road.

His voice barely rose above the noise.

"Move."

People listened.

Not because he shouted.

Because they recognized him.

The line began moving again.

Slowly.

Order replaced panic.

For a little while.

...

A crack echoed through the gorge.

The cliff above them shifted.

Loose stone rained onto the road.

Not enough to crush everyone.

Enough to block the only escape.

The front of the column stopped.

The rear kept pushing.

People stumbled.

Children cried.

The pressure threatened to become more dangerous than the falling rock.

Aidan climbed onto the broken stone.

His injured shoulder nearly gave out pulling himself upward.

He ignored it.

Both hands rose.

"Hold."

Crimson light spread across the fractured cliff.

It answered.

Weakly.

The stone slowed.

Not stopped.

Only slowed.

His Law had become thinner.

Strained.

His breathing grew ragged.

Blood ran from his nose again.

His knees trembled.

Cassian reached the base of the slope.

"You can't keep this up."

"I know."

The words came through clenched teeth.

"Keep them moving."

Cassian looked toward the civilians.

Then back at Aidan.

For one long moment...

Neither man spoke.

Then Cassian nodded once.

He turned away immediately.

"Move!"

His voice thundered through the gorge.

"Don't stop!"

Families ran.

Children crossed.

The elderly were carried.

Lyra and Malik pulled people through the narrowing gap.

Elias reinforced the path as best he could.

One by one...

The road emptied.

Aidan remained where he was.

Every heartbeat cost more than the last.

His vision darkened around the edges.

The Law flickered.

He steadied it.

Not because he believed he would walk away unharmed.

Because there were still people behind him.

The last little boy reached safety.

He looked back.

Their eyes met.

Aidan managed the smallest nod.

Go.

The boy ran.

Only then...

Only when he knew every civilian had crossed...

Did Aidan let the Law go.

The mountainside came down with a roar.

Stone swallowed the road behind the fleeing survivors.

Dust climbed into the morning sky.

When it settled...

The escape route was gone.

The people had lived.

The passage had not.

Cassian stood on the far side of the collapse, staring through the drifting dust toward where Aidan had been only moments before.

He could no longer see him.

Only broken stone.

And the terrible certainty that every time Aidan chose another life over his own...

The distance between the man he had always known...

And the fate waiting for him...

Grew smaller.
