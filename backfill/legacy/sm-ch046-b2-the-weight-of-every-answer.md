# Chapter Forty-Six: The Weight of Every Answer

Morning never truly arrived.

Heavy clouds buried the sun beneath a ceiling of gray, leaving Blackwood Academy wrapped in permanent twilight.

The old bell never rang.

It didn't need to.

Everyone was already awake.

No one slept deeply anymore.

...

Professor Helena made another round through the infirmary before dawn.

She stopped beside Aidan's bed.

Empty.

Again.

She wasn't surprised.

She simply sighed before folding the fresh bandages she had brought.

"You'll come here eventually," she whispered to no one.

...

She found him exactly where she expected.

The eastern wall.

Hands resting on cold stone.

Eyes fixed on the endless forest.

"You've reopened your shoulder."

"I know."

"You tore your side again."

"I know."

"You still haven't finished healing from the bridge."

"I know."

Helena stepped beside him.

"When was the last time you woke up without something hurting?"

Aidan searched his memory.

Nothing came.

"I don't remember."

That answer hurt Helena more than any wound she had stitched.

...

Below them...

Students changed patrol shifts with practiced precision.

Nobody complained.

New first-years learned barrier rotations before breakfast.

Older students silently reinforced damaged walls.

The academy had changed.

It had survived.

But it no longer felt young.

War had stolen that.

...

Aidan's attention drifted.

A wheel squeaked somewhere beyond the southern gate.

A horse breathed heavily.

Someone coughed.

Farther still...

Metal struck stone.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

His head turned immediately.

"South."

Rowan appeared beside him almost instantly.

"What is it?"

"I don't know."

He hated saying those words.

"But something's there."

Without another question...

Rowan sent scouts.

...

They returned an hour later.

"There was nobody."

Malik looked confused.

"Then what did you hear?"

The lead scout hesitated.

"We did find this."

He handed Rowan a broken iron chisel lying beside fresh rock fragments.

Someone had been cutting stone.

Recently.

But whoever had done it had disappeared before the scouts arrived.

No footprints.

No camp.

Nothing.

Only another unanswered question.

Aidan quietly looked toward the southern hills.

He had heard someone.

He was certain.

Yet certainty no longer guaranteed answers.

...

That afternoon...

The academy received another messenger.

Not from a village.

From one of Blackwood's own patrols.

The message contained only six words.

**We found something beneath the cliffs.**

Nothing more.

No explanation.

No location beyond a rough map.

Rowan immediately assembled the inner circle.

"We move together."

No one suggested otherwise.

...

The cliffs overlooked an ancient valley where fog clung stubbornly to the ground.

Aidan slowed almost immediately.

His hearing stretched through the mist.

Water.

Wind.

Loose stone.

Then...

Breathing.

Several.

Far below.

He raised one hand.

Everyone stopped.

Cassian whispered,

"How many?"

Aidan listened.

The breaths faded.

Then returned.

No rhythm.

No certainty.

"I..."

He frowned.

"I can't tell."

For the first time...

His senses failed him.

Not because they were weak.

Because whatever waited below understood how to remain hidden.

The realization sent a chill through him.

They descended carefully.

Every step tested before weight followed.

The patrol had been right.

Someone had found something.

An enormous section of cliff had collapsed weeks earlier.

Behind it...

A passage.

Old.

Far older than Blackwood.

No markings identified it.

No records mentioned it.

Only darkness stretched inward.

Aidan knelt beside the entrance.

Dust.

Broken stone.

Then...

A faint smell.

Not blood.

Not decay.

Something ancient.

Something he couldn't place.

His stomach tightened.

The sensation vanished before he understood it.

"Anything?" Rowan asked.

Aidan slowly stood.

"Only questions."

# Chapter Forty-Seven: The Door That Shouldn't Exist

The passage descended in complete silence.

Not the ordinary silence of abandoned places.

Something deeper.

As though sound itself hesitated.

Even Malik's limp echoed less than it should have.

Lyra quietly moved closer to Aidan.

"You feel it too."

It wasn't a question.

He nodded once.

The tunnel twisted naturally through the mountain until it opened into a circular chamber unlike anything Blackwood had uncovered before.

The walls were smooth.

Too smooth.

No tool marks.

No cracks.

No fallen stone.

Only age.

At the center stood a single stone doorway.

No door.

Only an empty frame carved directly into the rock.

Nothing existed beyond it.

Just solid mountain.

Cassian slowly circled the structure.

"What is this place?"

No one answered.

Because no one knew.

Aidan stepped closer.

Not carelessly.

Drawn by something he couldn't explain.

His hearing caught nothing.

His smell found nothing unusual.

Even his instincts remained strangely quiet.

That frightened him most.

He reached toward the stone.

Before his fingers touched it—

Pain exploded through his chest.

The second heartbeat struck so hard it stole his breath.

THUMP.

He staggered backward.

Lyra caught him before he fell.

"Aidan!"

"I'm alright."

He wasn't.

His heartbeat slowly steadied.

The second one lingered.

Then faded.

Helena knelt beside the stone frame.

"No dust."

Rowan frowned.

"What?"

"The floor."

She pointed.

Everywhere else the chamber held centuries of settled dust.

Except directly before the doorway.

As though...

Someone had stood there.

Recently.

No footprints remained.

No evidence.

Only absence.

Aidan looked at the empty frame again.

The feeling returned.

Not recognition.

Expectation.

As though the doorway itself had been waiting.

For what...

He had no idea.

He took one step backward.

"We leave."

Cassian looked surprised.

"Without searching farther?"

Aidan never looked away from the empty frame.

"Yes."

No one argued.

They trusted him.

The group retreated through the passage.

No one noticed that after the last lantern disappeared around the bend...

A single pebble quietly rolled across the chamber floor.

Then another.

The empty doorway remained exactly as it had before.

Silent.

Waiting.

And somewhere outside...

Before Blackwood's defenders had even reached the academy again...

Another emergency bell began ringing.
