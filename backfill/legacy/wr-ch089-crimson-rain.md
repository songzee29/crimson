The first rooftops of New Orleans appeared beneath a curtain of rain.

Aidan stopped atop the rusted frame of an abandoned warehouse overlooking the sleeping city.

His crimson eyes slowly swept across the streets.

The city breathed.

Not metaphorically.

Literally.

Thousands upon thousands of heartbeats echoed through the darkness.

Each neighborhood carried its own rhythm.

Laughter spilled from distant taverns.

Music drifted through narrow streets.

A crying infant.

A weary dockworker.

An elderly woman humming to herself behind a shuttered window.

Every living pulse reached him at once.

His knees bent instinctively.

The burning inside his throat became agony.

Aidan closed his eyes.

The notifications disappeared.

The hunger remained.

Far below…

A fisherman sliced his palm while repairing a net.

One crimson drop struck old wooden planks.

Aidan’s head snapped toward the docks.

The scent reached him instantly.

Warm.

Fresh.

Human.

His body launched before conscious thought formed.

Crimson Dash carried him across rooftops faster than the rain itself.

Brick shattered beneath each landing.

His cloak disappeared into the storm.

…

The fisherman looked up.

Only movement.

Only red eyes.

Fear exploded through his heartbeat.

He dropped the knife and ran.

Aidan followed.

Not because of anger.

Not because of hatred.

Because every instinct screamed that the blood belonged inside him.

The chase crossed abandoned warehouses and rain-soaked alleys.

The fisherman slipped.

His shoulder struck stone.

Fresh blood spread across broken skin.

The scent struck Aidan harder than the first cut.

He landed only a few steps away.

Fangs descended.

The fisherman backed against the wall.

“Please…”

The plea carried no meaning.

Only the heartbeat did.

Aidan stepped forward—

A church bell rang across the city.

Once.

Then again.

His head turned.

Beneath the sound…

Something else.

Far older.

Far deeper.

A slow vibration pulsed beneath the streets themselves.

The same silent pull he had felt miles away.

The Ancient Gates.

The hunger tore him in two.

Blood.

Or…

The call below the city.

For one impossible moment the predator stood perfectly still.

Then he turned away.

Not because mercy had returned.

Because another instinct had become stronger.

He vanished into the rain.

The fisherman collapsed where he stood, unable to understand why he was still alive.

Behind him, whispers had already begun.

“There was a monster…”

“It had red eyes…”

“It disappeared…”

The story spread with the storm.
