# Chapter Sixty-Six: Fractures

The road from the broken bridge felt longer than it had that morning.

No one spoke.

The wagons rolled behind them carrying the wounded.

Empty blankets covered the places where others should have been.

The rain had washed the blood from the road.

Not from memory.

...

Blackwood Academy opened its gates before the column arrived.

Students rushed forward with stretchers.

Healers divided the injured into rows.

Professor Helena moved from patient to patient without pausing.

She reached Aidan last.

He was still standing.

Barely.

His right hand trembled so badly he could no longer hide it by gripping his staff.

"Sit."

He didn't answer.

"Aidan."

"I can still—"

His knees buckled.

Cassian caught him before he struck the ground.

For a heartbeat neither man moved.

"I've got you," Cassian said quietly.

Aidan nodded once.

Nothing more.

...

Helena unwrapped the bandages in silence.

The bruising across his ribs had darkened nearly black.

Fresh blood seeped from the wound along his side.

His shoulder had swollen again after holding the bridge.

She rested her hands on the edge of the bed.

"You reached your limit."

Aidan stared at the ceiling.

"It wasn't enough."

"No."

Her voice carried no judgment.

"It wasn't."

The honesty hurt more than comfort would have.

...

That evening Rowan gathered only the inner circle.

No students.

No instructors.

Just the people who had carried the weight with Aidan for years.

"The attacks are accelerating," Elias said, spreading the newest reports across the table.

"They're forcing us to choose between places again," Malik added.

Cassian remained quiet.

He watched Aidan instead.

Aidan hadn't looked up once.

His eyes remained fixed on the map.

Not studying it.

Simply staring.

Finally Rowan spoke.

"We cannot answer every call."

Silence.

No one argued.

Because everyone knew he was right.

Aidan closed his eyes.

For years he had believed effort could overcome impossibility.

Now...

Even effort had limits.

...

Later that night he returned to the old oak.

The academy behind him remained awake.

The infirmary lights never went out anymore.

His breathing remained uneven.

Every deep breath reminded him of the bridge.

Every shallow one reminded him of the people he couldn't save.

The second heartbeat echoed beneath his own.

Steady.

Patient.

Waiting.

He rested one hand against the bark.

"I tried."

The wind carried the words away.

No answer came.

Only silence.

# Chapter Sixty-Seven: The Narrowing Road

Morning began with another report.

Not an attack.

A withdrawal.

One of Blackwood's outlying watch posts had been abandoned during the night.

The defenders had fallen back rather than be surrounded.

It was the first time Rowan had ordered ground given up willingly.

The academy no longer had enough people to hold every position.

That truth settled heavily over everyone.

...

Aidan volunteered for the relief column.

Rowan looked at him for a long moment.

"You can barely lift your left arm."

"I can still walk."

"You couldn't keep the bridge standing."

Aidan lowered his eyes.

"I know."

The words remained between them.

Not accusation.

Fact.

Rowan finally nodded.

"Then walk."

...

The abandoned watch post overlooked a narrow mountain pass.

Barricades had been left behind.

Supply crates destroyed so they could not be used by whoever came after.

Everything had been orderly.

Intentional.

No panic.

Only retreat.

Cassian stood beside Aidan overlooking the valley.

"We're giving up ground."

"We're keeping people."

Cassian nodded.

"It still feels like losing."

"It is."

The answer came quietly.

Neither tried to soften it.

...

As the column prepared to return, Aidan noticed something on the weathered stone beside the old signal tower.

Not a new symbol.

Not a clue.

Just fresh scratches where someone had deliberately erased something that had once been there.

He looked at it for a long moment.

Then turned away.

There were no answers to gather.

Only another road to walk.

Another group to protect.

Another day to survive.

Behind him, Cassian watched in silence.

For the first time, he saw not only Aidan's wounds...

But the way he carried them.

Not with defiance.

Not with hope.

Simply because there were still people behind him who needed someone to keep walking.

And that realization frightened Cassian more than the battles ever had.
