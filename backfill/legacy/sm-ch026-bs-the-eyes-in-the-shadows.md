## Chapter Twenty-Six — The Eyes in the Shadows

Aidan remained frozen on the sidewalk.

The black mist had vanished.

Students continued walking around him, laughing and talking as if nothing had happened.

No one reacted.

No one screamed.

No one asked where the hooded man had gone.

It was as though they had never seen him.

Only Aidan remembered.

The notification slowly faded from his vision.

Unknown Observer Detected.

Threat Assessment Unavailable.

His pulse quickened.

The Legacy had recognized the stranger.

That meant the stranger was real.

Aidan scanned every rooftop, every alley, every passing face.

Nothing.

The city looked ordinary again.

Yet something had changed.

He no longer felt alone.

Someone else had entered the game.

Someone who knew exactly who he was.

---

That evening...

Far above the city...

A lone figure stood atop an abandoned church bell tower.

The hooded man removed one black glove.

Ancient crimson markings covered his hand.

Not identical to Aidan's.

Older.

Different.

He looked toward the Darkheart estate resting beyond the city skyline.

So...

The bloodline survived.

A faint smile crossed his face.

The Founder truly planned for everything.

Without another word, he disappeared into the gathering darkness.

## Chapter Twenty-Seven — The Dream of the Founder

That night sleep came quickly.

Too quickly.

Aidan opened his eyes inside a familiar place.

The endless crimson void.

Floating symbols drifted around him like stars.

The vacant crimson throne waited in the distance.

This time...

Someone sat upon it.

A man.

Tall.

Powerfully built.

His face remained hidden behind blinding crimson light.

Only his silhouette could be seen.

He rested both hands upon the armrests.

Calm.

Patient.

Waiting.

Aidan slowly approached.

Are you...

The Founder?

The man didn't answer.

Instead, he stood.

The pressure released by that single movement surpassed anything Aidan had ever felt.

Not violent.

Absolute.

Every crimson symbol throughout the void immediately stopped moving.

Everything obeyed.

The mysterious man descended the throne's steps one at a time.

When he reached Aidan, he lifted one hand.

Not to attack.

To examine.

His fingertips stopped inches from Aidan's forehead.

The distance never closed.

Then he spoke.

The first lesson...

Has begun.

His voice carried neither pride nor disappointment.

Only certainty.

Aidan tried asking another question.

Who are you?

Why me?

What is the Legacy?

The Founder slowly lowered his hand.

Questions...

Must become earned answers.

Knowledge given too early...

Becomes a burden.

Knowledge earned...

Becomes wisdom.

The crimson light surrounding his face brightened.

One final sentence echoed across the endless void.

Do not mistake inheritance...

For destiny.

The dream shattered.

## Chapter Twenty-Eight — A New Morning

Aidan woke before sunrise.

His heart was racing.

The dream felt different from every memory the Legacy had shown him.

This wasn't history.

Someone had spoken directly to him.

He quietly dressed before anyone else in the estate had awakened.

Instead of returning to the hidden room...

He went outside.

The morning air felt cool against his skin.

He carried the practice staff toward an open field behind the estate.

One breath.

One movement.

Again.

Again.

Again.

Hours passed.

Each strike became smoother.

His feet moved more naturally.

His breathing steadied.

Without realizing it...

He had stopped thinking.

His body simply flowed.

Clap.

Someone applauded.

Aidan immediately turned.

The Guardian stood several yards away.

Exactly as he had appeared inside the Sanctuary.

Armor.

Scar.

Broken sword now repaired.

Aidan's eyes widened.

You're here?

The Guardian smiled.

No.

You're there.

The world around them rippled.

The open field dissolved into the Valley of Beginnings.

The Guardian walked toward him.

Time flows differently between our worlds.

Whenever your body trains with sincerity...

Your mind may continue training here.

He pointed the wooden staff toward Aidan.

Again.

This time...

Don't fight me.

Understand me.

Because Aidan had begun seeing more.

The shift of his weight.

The tightening of his grip.

The rhythm of his breathing.

Every action announced itself before the strike arrived.

Block.

Step.

Turn.

Breathe.

The practice staff met the Guardian's weapon over and over again.

Minutes passed.

Then hours.

Finally...

The Guardian stopped.

Good.

You're no longer watching my weapon.

You're watching me.

He stepped closer.

Most warriors lose because they chase fists...

Instead of understanding people.

Remember this.

Every battle begins long before the first strike.

The lesson made perfect sense.

Now...

Let's see if you can apply it somewhere that truly matters.
