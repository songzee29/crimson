## Chapter Three — The First Trial

Aidan's eyes snapped open.

He was lying on the cold stone floor of the hidden chamber, his chest rising and falling in desperate breaths. Sweat soaked through his clothes despite the freezing air. Every muscle in his body trembled as though he had run for days without stopping.

The pedestal before him stood empty.

The crimson book was gone.

He searched every corner of the room.

Nothing.

No pages.

No ancient artifact.

Only silence.

His heartbeat slowly steadied until another pulse echoed inside his mind.

Not from his heart.

From somewhere deeper.

A crimson symbol briefly appeared across the back of his right hand before fading beneath his skin.

The mark vanished so quickly he almost believed he imagined it.

Almost.

As he pushed himself to his feet, the entire room shuddered.

Dust drifted from the ceiling.

Somewhere beneath the estate, stone groaned against stone.

The hidden chamber was changing.

Bookshelves slowly slid away from the walls without anyone touching them, revealing passageways that had remained hidden for centuries. Ancient mechanisms awakened with thunderous rumbles, filling the chamber with the sound of gears grinding against forgotten locks.

The room had never been a destination.

It had been an entrance.

Aidan stared into the darkness beyond the newly revealed corridor.

Cold air poured outward carrying the scent of old stone and something older than civilization itself.

His instincts begged him to leave.

Instead, curiosity pulled him forward.

Each step carried him deeper beneath the Darkheart estate.

The walls became smoother.

Stranger.

Ancient crimson runes covered every surface, pulsing faintly whenever he walked past them as though recognizing his presence.

Some glowed brighter than others.

One symbol suddenly flared.

The corridor instantly filled with brilliant crimson light.

Then another.

No magic.
