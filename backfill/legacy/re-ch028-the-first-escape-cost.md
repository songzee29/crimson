Aidan turned sharply.

“There has to be another way out.”

“There is,” Lyra said.

“And?”

Her hesitation told him everything before she spoke.

“But it costs something.”

A faint glow appeared in Aidan’s vision.

> SYSTEM NOTICE  
> EMERGENCY EXIT POSSIBILITY DETECTED  
> COST REQUIRED: MEMORY ANCHOR

Aidan blinked.

“What is that?” he asked out loud.

Lyra heard him.

“The System’s giving you an exit?” she asked.

“Yes.”

Her expression darkened instantly.

“Don’t accept it.”

“Why?”

“Because memory anchors aren’t just memories,” she said quickly. “They’re things that keep you *you*.”

The footsteps grew closer.

Aidan’s breathing tightened.

“What does it want?”

Lyra shook her head.

“I don’t know exactly. But I know what it feels like after.”

Another step echoed.

Closer.

Aidan looked at the system text again.

> COST: MEMORY ANCHOR

Something in his chest tightened.

Not fear.

Recognition.

Like something inside him knew what that meant.

A memory surfaced.

Blurred.

A child’s voice.

A hand reaching out.

A word he couldn’t fully hear.

Lyra grabbed his arm.

“Aidan, listen to me. If you lose that, you don’t get it back.”

The corridor behind them filled with shadowed silhouettes.

The hunters were no longer alone.

More had arrived.

Aidan looked at the System prompt.

Then at Lyra.

Then at the approaching figures.

And for the first time since this began—

he hesitated not because he was afraid of dying.

But because he didn’t know what dying would cost him anymore.

The System waited.

The Veil waited.

Even the corridor itself seemed to hold its breath.

And then—

Aidan made a choice.
