Chapter One Hundred Four

Uncle Rez

Zee took Aidan to Vesper before sunrise.

No royal procession.

No Darkheart banners.

No announcement that a prince had crossed the border.

Only Zee.

Rez.

And a sleeping infant wrapped in a blue-black blanket.

Vesper had no idea who had entered its kingdom.

That was the point.

Rez walked beside Zee through a private passage beneath the palace.

Neither spoke until the doors closed behind them.

Then Rez looked at the child.

Aidan slept against Zee’s chest.

Tiny.

Quiet.

Three impossible foundations buried beneath seals.

Witch.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Silent.

His mother’s inheritance remained deeper still.

Ocean.

Abyss.

Siren.

Dormant.

Untouched.

Rez exhaled.

“You are leaving him with me.”

Zee looked down at his son.

The words hurt more than expected.

“Yes.”

Rez stared at him.

“You trust me that much?”

Zee looked up.

“I am leaving him with my brother.”

That ended the question.

Rez stepped closer.

Zee carefully placed Aidan into his arms.

Rez had held wounded soldiers.

Children rescued from sieges.

Dying friends.

Weapons.

Crowns.

Nothing prepared him for how small Aidan felt.

Aidan shifted.

Opened one eye.

Then grabbed Rez’s shirt.

Rez froze.

Zee almost smiled.

“He likes you.”

“He has known me for four seconds.”

“Still.”

Rez looked down.

Then back at Zee.

“What does Vesper know?”

“Nothing true.”

“Good.”

“Publicly, he is a protected royal child under House Vesper.”

“Parents?”

“Unavailable.”

“Name?”

Zee hesitated.

“Aidan.”

Rez looked at him.

“You are letting him keep that?”

“Yes.”

The world had forgotten Aidan Darkheart.

It had not taken Aidan from himself.

Rez nodded.

“Aidan.”

The child yawned.

Rez’s expression changed immediately.

Dangerously soft.

Zee noticed.

“Do not spoil him.”

Rez looked offended.

“I would never.”

Zee stared.

Rez looked back down at Aidan.

“Completely unrelated, where does Vesper keep the expensive toys?”

“Rez.”

“What?”

“He cannot even sit up.”

“That sounds like jealousy.”

⸻

There was one rule Rez did not joke about.

Water.

Zee stood beside the nursery window while Rez listened.

“No oceans.”

“Fine.”

“No lakes.”

Rez frowned.

“Fine.”

“No large rivers.”

That got his attention.

“Why?”

Zee looked toward Aidan.

“I do not fully know.”

Rez waited.

“Nymeris’s blood is in him.”

“Ocean.”

“Abyss.”

“Siren.”

Rez looked toward the sleeping child.

“You did not bind it.”

“No.”

“Could you?”

“I did not understand it well enough.”

That answer bothered Rez.

Zee continued.

“Large natural water reacted to him before birth.”

“So keep him away.”

“Yes.”

“For how long?”

Zee looked through the window toward Vesper.

“As long as we can.”

Rez nodded.

No argument.

Aidan’s Witch remained sealed.

Vampire sealed.

Wolf sealed.

And whatever waited in the deeper maternal blood would remain sleeping too.

If they were careful.

⸻

Vesper accepted the child because Rez told it to.

Not as his brother.

Not as a secret prince.

A royal ward.

Protected under his personal authority.

Most people assumed Aidan had come from a destroyed noble household.

Some believed he was the child of a dead Vesper officer.

Others invented more elaborate stories.

Rez corrected none of them.

Rumors were useful when none contained the truth.

The palace staff learned three things quickly.

Aidan was protected.

Aidan was loved.

And Prince Rez had absolutely no discipline where the child was concerned.

⸻

At six months, Aidan refused to sleep unless someone carried him.

Rez blamed Zee.

Zee was not present.

That did not stop him.

“This is your father’s fault.”

Aidan stared at him.

Rez froze.

Then looked around the empty room.

“No.”

The child had no idea what the word meant.

Good.

Rez shifted him higher against his shoulder.

“You do not know who I am talking about.”

Aidan grabbed his hair.

Rez winced.

“You are violent.”

Aidan smiled.

Rez smiled back despite himself.

“Definitely his.”

Then he caught himself again.

Silence.

The secret had to live even when no one was listening.

Rez kissed Aidan’s forehead.

“Sleep.”

Aidan did.

Eventually.

On Rez.

Where he remained for three hours because Rez refused to move and risk waking him.

⸻

At two, Aidan followed Rez everywhere.

Council chamber.

Library.

Training yard.

Kitchen.

Once, the throne room.

Rez was speaking with three Vesper officials when a tiny figure entered dragging a blanket behind him.

Everyone stopped.

Aidan walked directly to Rez.

Lifted both arms.

Rez stared down.

“No.”

Aidan continued holding his arms up.

“You have legs.”

Aidan frowned.

One official hid a smile.

Rez pointed.

“Do not encourage him.”

Aidan made a small offended sound.

Rez lasted four seconds.

Then picked him up.

The officials looked away.

Rez settled Aidan against his side.

“This means nothing.”

Aidan stole a piece of fruit from Rez’s plate.

“You are a criminal.”

Aidan ate it.

Rez continued the meeting while carrying him.

⸻

At three, Aidan learned the name that mattered most.

Rez returned from Vesper’s western border after four days away.

Aidan saw him from the top of the palace steps.

His entire face changed.

“Uncle Rez!”

Rez stopped.

The world seemed to stop with him.

Aidan ran.

Poorly.

Fast enough to terrify three servants.

Rez dropped whatever he was carrying and caught him before he reached the last step.

Aidan wrapped both arms around his neck.

“You left.”

“I work.”

“You left me.”

“For four days.”

“Long.”

Rez looked toward the servants.

“Who taught him guilt?”

Nobody answered.

Aidan pulled back.

“Did you bring me something?”

Rez stared.

“There it is.”

Aidan smiled.

Rez sighed dramatically.

Then reached into his coat.

A tiny carved blue dragon.

Aidan gasped.

Rez looked pleased with himself.

“You are spoiled.”

Aidan hugged him again.

“You spoil me.”

Rez froze.

One servant laughed.

Rez pointed at her.

“Dismissed.”

She knew he did not mean it.

Everyone did.

⸻

At four, Aidan discovered rivers existed.

This became a problem immediately.

Vesper’s smaller palace streams never bothered him.

Fountains did nothing.

Baths were ordinary.

Rain only made him angry when it ruined outside play.

Then one summer patrol brought him within sight of the Great Vesper River.

Aidan stopped walking.

Rez felt his hand go still inside his.

The river was nearly half a mile away.

Too far for an ordinary child to hear clearly.

Aidan turned toward it anyway.

His eyes changed.

Not color.

Focus.

Something beneath him recognized something beneath the water.

The river surface moved.

One long ripple traveled against the current.

Rez went cold.

“Aidan.”

The child took one step toward it.

Rez picked him up immediately.

“No.”

Aidan struggled.

“I want to see.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because I said so.”

“That is not a reason.”

Rez stared.

Four years old.

Already arguing like Zee.

Wonderful.

Aidan pointed toward the river.

“It knows me.”

Rez’s blood went cold.

“What?”

Aidan looked confused by his own words.

Then the river moved again.

Rez turned away.

Fast.

Aidan protested the entire walk home.

“You are mean!”

“Yes.”

“I hate you!”

“No, you don’t.”

“I do!”

“Fine.”

Rez carried him farther from the river.

Aidan eventually buried his face against Rez’s shoulder.

“I still want to see it.”

“I know.”

Rez’s voice softened.

“Someday.”

Not yet.

Maybe not for years.

The Ocean.

The Abyss.

The Siren blood remained dormant.

But not dead.

Rez understood the warning now.

⸻

Zee visited when he could.

Always secretly.

Never as Father.

Sometimes Aidan slept through the entire visit.

Those were easier.

Zee could sit beside the bed and look.

Growth.

Breathing.

Safe.

Sometimes Aidan was awake.

Those visits hurt differently.

At five, Aidan found Zee standing in a Vesper library.

He looked up.

“Who are you?”

Zee almost forgot how to answer.

“Zee.”

Aidan considered him.

“Uncle Rez’s friend?”

Zee swallowed.

“Yes.”

Aidan looked at Zee’s horns.

“Are those real?”

“Yes.”

“Can I touch them?”

Zee laughed before he could stop himself.

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because you will pull them.”

“I won’t.”

Lie.

Zee could hear it.

He smiled.

“Absolutely not.”

Aidan stared another moment.

Then returned to the book he was carrying.

Zee watched him walk away.

His son knew his face now.

Maybe.

But not what it meant.

That was the price.

Alive.

Safe.

Growing.

Zee accepted it again.

⸻

At six, Aidan fell asleep during one of Rez’s training lessons.

Not his own training.

Rez’s.

Aidan had insisted on watching.

He lasted twenty minutes.

Rez finished sword drills with a sleeping child stretched across a bench nearby.

Afterward, he picked Aidan up.

Aidan barely woke.

“Uncle Rez?”

“Yes.”

“Did you win?”

“There was nobody fighting me.”

“You won.”

Rez smiled.

“Obviously.”

Aidan settled back against his shoulder.

Rez carried him toward the palace.

Beyond the training yard, hidden beneath a suppression veil only he knew was there, Zee watched.

He had arrived too late for the lesson.

Stayed anyway.

Rez felt him.

Did not turn.

No need.

Aidan murmured something into Rez’s shoulder.

Rez laughed quietly.

Then kept walking.

Zee watched his son disappear into Vesper Palace.

No assassins chasing him.

No kingdom declaring his birth an abomination.

No father trying to kill him.

No prophecy deciding his childhood.

Just a boy falling asleep during training while his uncle carried him home.

It hurt.

It worked.

Both could be true.

⸻

A year later, Aidan came racing through the Vesper courtyard.

“UNCLE REZ!”

Rez turned.

Aidan hit him at full speed.

Rez caught him automatically.

“You are getting too big for this.”

“No, I’m not.”

“Yes, you are.”

“Carry me.”

“No.”

Aidan wrapped his arms around Rez’s neck.

Rez sighed.

Then lifted him anyway.

“You are unbelievably spoiled.”

“You did it.”

“I regret everything.”

Aidan laughed.

Rez carried him toward the palace.

Zee watched from the shadow of a distant tower.

Unseen.

His son remembered Rez.

Rez’s face.

Rez’s voice.

Rez’s arms around him.

The person who protected him.

The person who taught him.

The person who spoiled him beyond reason.

Uncle Rez.

Real childhood.

Real family.

Those memories belonged to Aidan.

The concealment would never take them.

Zee looked at his son one last time before leaving Vesper.

Aidan was laughing.

Safe.

Loved.

Growing.

He did not remember his father.

Not truly.

But he remembered Uncle Rez.

And for now—

Zee could live with being forgotten.

Because his son finally had what Zee Darkheart had fought the entire world to give him.

A childhood before anyone could decide what he was.
