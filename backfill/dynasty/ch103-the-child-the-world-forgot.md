Chapter One Hundred Three

The Child the World Forgot

Aidan Darkheart was born before dawn.

The moment he opened his eyes, the ocean stopped moving.

Every current around Nhalaris froze.

Not slowed.

Stopped.

For three heartbeats.

Then the Abyss answered.

A pressure wave traveled outward from the underwater kingdom.

Darkheart wards activated hundreds of miles away.

Witches felt Crimson power move beneath their magic.

Vampires heard a second heartbeat where none should exist.

Wolves went silent.

Beneath the ocean, Siren voices vanished for five full seconds.

And far beyond the kingdoms of men, something divine turned its attention toward the world.

Somewhere else—

a bearer of Dark Divinity opened his eyes.

⸻

Zee heard none of that first.

He heard Aidan cry.

Small.

Angry.

Alive.

Nymeris held him against her chest.

Exhausted.

Sweating.

Furious at everyone who came too close.

Queen disappeared.

Mother remained.

Aidan’s skin was warm.

His heartbeat was impossible.

One rhythm.

Four inheritances.

Three great supernatural foundations compressed beneath infancy.

Witch.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Complete.

Not awakened.

Not like Zee.

Zee had entered the world as all three.

Fully active.

Aidan had entered carrying all three like sealed storms beneath his blood.

And beyond them—

Nymeris.

Ocean.

Abyss.

Siren.

Separate.

Hereditary.

Not another Authority.

Something older in another direction.

Zee approached slowly.

Nymeris looked at him.

“Take him.”

Zee froze.

For a man who had fought Gods, the child terrified him more than anything ever had.

Not because of what Aidan might do.

Because of what the world would do to him.

Nymeris held him out.

Zee took his son.

Aidan immediately grabbed one of Zee’s fingers.

Tiny hand.

Impossible strength hidden beneath it.

Zee stopped breathing.

Ner stood several feet away.

He had never seen Zee look like that.

No King.

No Blood Hunter.

No Crimson Demonic Dragon.

Just father.

Aidan opened his eyes.

Dark.

For now.

Zee listened to the heartbeat.

Then whispered:

“Hello.”

Aidan tightened his grip.

Zee smiled.

Then his Vampire senses reached deeper.

And the smile disappeared.

⸻

Three foundations.

All complete.

Witch.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Not awake.

Waiting.

And too deep.

Far too deep.

Zee had never felt anything like it.

His own birth had been explosive because everything came alive at once.

Aidan’s was worse in another way.

Everything was already there.

Finished beneath the surface.

Like doors waiting for the child to become strong enough to open them.

Zee’s Crimson blood recognized the structures instantly.

His son carried the same roots.

But denser.

Broader.

Changed by Nymeris’s royal blood.

The total potential made something cold move through Zee.

Aidan could surpass him.

The thought produced no jealousy.

No anger.

Only fear.

For Aidan.

Zee looked toward the ceiling.

He could feel attention gathering.

Magic searching.

Divine awareness probing.

Bloodline sensing beginning across distances.

The birth had announced something.

Not a name.

Not yet.

But enough.

Nymeris saw Zee’s expression.

“What?”

Zee looked at Aidan.

Then at her.

“We have to hide him.”

Nymeris’s face became still.

“No.”

Immediate.

Zee expected it.

“The world felt him.”

“No.”

“Nymeris—”

“No.”

Aidan began crying again.

Nymeris took him back.

Her voice remained low.

“He was born minutes ago.”

“That is exactly the problem.”

“He has a name.”

“I know.”

“He has a kingdom.”

“I know.”

“Two.”

“I know.”

“He is my son.”

Zee’s eyes hardened.

“And mine.”

“Then do not speak about erasing him.”

“I said hide.”

Nymeris stared.

“What do you think hiding means when the world has already begun looking?”

Silence.

Zee knew.

She knew he knew.

He answered anyway.

“Records.”

Her expression changed.

“Zee.”

“Bloodline tracking.”

“No.”

“Divine observation.”

“No.”

“Birth declarations.”

“Stop.”

“Royal recognition.”

“STOP.”

Water exploded across the chamber.

The walls shook.

Aidan cried harder.

Nymeris instantly pulled the pressure back.

Mother first.

Even furious.

Zee stepped closer.

“The world knows something was born.”

“Then let them know.”

“They will come.”

“Let them.”

“You do not understand.”

Nymeris’s eyes turned cold.

“Do not say that to me.”

“I lived it.”

“And I have ruled Nhalaris through wars you have never seen.”

“This is different.”

“Because it happened to you?”

“Because I know the sequence.”

Zee’s voice sharpened.

“Birth.”

“Fear.”

“Classification.”

“Hunting.”

“People deciding what the child means before the child can speak.”

Nymeris stood despite the healers trying to stop her.

Aidan against her chest.

“I will defend him.”

“With what?”

Nymeris’s eyes flashed.

“The ocean.”

“Against Gods?”

“Yes.”

“Godslayers?”

“Yes.”

“Every kingdom that spent years trying to kill me?”

“Yes.”

“They failed to kill me because I cannot die.”

Nymeris went silent.

Zee stepped closer.

“He can.”

That landed.

For the first time, fear entered her expression.

Not surrender.

Fear.

Zee continued.

“I was killed before I understood what death was.”

Nymeris stared at him.

“My father did it.”

“I know.”

“I spent childhood being hunted because adults were afraid of what I might become.”

“I know.”

“I will not give him that childhood.”

Nymeris’s jaw tightened.

“You told me you wanted your child to choose who he becomes.”

“Yes.”

“Then let him.”

“He has to survive long enough to choose.”

There.

The center.

Neither moved.

Aidan cried between them.

⸻

Nymeris looked down at him.

Then back at Zee.

“You are letting your childhood decide his.”

“I am using my childhood to prevent his.”

“You do not know what will happen.”

“I know what already started.”

“You are taking his name before he can speak it.”

“I am protecting it.”

“From whom?”

“Everyone.”

“That includes me.”

“No.”

“Yes.”

Nymeris’s voice broke for the first time.

“If the world forgets Prince Aidan Thalryn exists, Nhalaris loses him.”

“You do not.”

“My kingdom does.”

“For now.”

“You are deciding that alone.”

“Yes.”

The answer enraged her more than anything else.

Water rose around the room.

Abyssal pressure thickened.

Ner stepped forward.

“Nymeris.”

She looked at him.

“Do not.”

Ner stopped.

Not conscience.

Not mediator claiming authority.

Just present.

Nymeris looked back at Zee.

“Your father tried to erase you.”

Zee’s expression changed instantly.

“That is not what I am doing.”

“Then tell me what you call taking his name from the world before he can speak it.”

Zee went still.

The words cut deeper than Cassel’s blade.

Nymeris continued.

“Victor decided fear gave him the right to decide whether you belonged in the world.”

“No.”

“And now you decide fear gives you the right to decide whether Aidan may be known by it.”

“Victor wanted me gone.”

“And you?”

Zee looked at Aidan.

“I want the world gone from him.”

Silence.

There.

The difference.

Not clean.

Not painless.

But real.

Nymeris hated it anyway.

“As his mother, I refuse.”

Zee looked at her.

“As his father, I cannot.”

Nymeris became Queen.

Completely.

“If you erase my son without my consent, Nhalaris will answer.”

Ner’s expression changed.

Zee said nothing.

Nymeris stepped closer.

“If Darkheart takes Prince Aidan from his own name, the sea gates open.”

Zee understood.

War.

“I mean it.”

“I know.”

“I will bring Nhalaris to your shores.”

“I know.”

“Do not test me.”

Zee looked at his son.

Then at her.

“I am not.”

⸻

The first warning reached Darkheart less than an hour later.

Coastal currents changed.

Nhalaris sea gates mobilized.

Siren guard formations moved.

Oceanic wards shifted into war configuration.

Rez arrived through Vesper transit before sunrise.

His face was furious.

“Tell me I misunderstood the message.”

Zee said nothing.

Rez looked at Nymeris.

Then Aidan.

Then Zee.

“Oh.”

Zareth arrived seconds later.

Human form.

The Dragon Link opened immediately.

She is not bluffing.

Zee answered mentally.

I know.

You are still doing it.

Yes.

Zareth’s expression darkened.

No argument.

Just understanding.

Ner stood near Aidan.

Bloodsong barely touching the room.

Keeping track of the infant’s heartbeat.

Not deciding anything for Zee.

Witness.

Family.

⸻

Zee built the first structure before noon.

Not a machine.

Not a game.

Not a foreign intelligence.

Architecture.

Crimson magic arranged around blood.

A framework.

A regulatory structure capable of holding power without creating it.

Zee worked from everything he knew.

Witch seals.

Vampire blood architecture.

Wolf instinct.

Crimson adaptation.

Dragon durability.

Years of understanding how supernatural natures reacted under pressure.

He gave the structure one purpose.

Access.

Not ownership.

Not creation.

Access.

The child already had everything.

The structure would decide nothing about who Aidan was.

It would only prevent all of him from waking at once.

Zee looked at the three immense foundations beneath Aidan’s blood.

Witch.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Separate.

Complete.

He bound each individually.

Not destroyed.

Not weakened into nothing.

Locked.

The architecture formed around them.

Crimson.

Layered.

Responsive.

A safeguard.

The beginning of what would one day be known as:

The Crimson System.

Not giver.

Gatekeeper.

Regulator.

⸻

Then Zee created something else.

Smaller.

Quieter.

Not active power.

Memory.

Truth.

Blueprints.

Why the seals existed.

What Aidan carried.

What Zee had done.

What Nymeris had fought him over.

Instructions without commands.

History without force.

The Crimson Legacy.

Archive.

Nothing more.

It would never grant Aidan power.

It would only preserve truth in case the world forgot too successfully.

In case Zee himself was no longer there when Aidan finally asked why.

⸻

The binding began.

Aidan screamed.

Nymeris moved instantly.

“Stop.”

Zee did not.

The first Witch lock resisted.

Not consciously.

Aidan was an infant.

But the power itself pushed back.

Crimson magic shook the room.

Zee increased precision.

Not force.

Structure.

The Witch foundation compressed.

Locked.

Aidan cried harder.

Nymeris struck Zee with a wall of water.

He did not retaliate.

The water hit him.

Moved him three feet.

He held the seal.

“Stop this!”

“I cannot.”

“You can.”

“I won’t.”

Nymeris sent Abyssal pressure through the room.

Zee’s scales spread automatically.

Crimson-black across his arms and throat.

No wings.

No tail.

Human shape maintained.

He grounded himself.

Held the second structure.

Vampire.

Aidan’s blood reacted violently.

Every nearby Vampire dropped to one knee.

Ner grabbed the edge of a table.

Bloodsong screaming with the infant’s pulse.

Zee’s own fangs descended.

He followed the blood structure.

Wrapped it.

Bound it.

Locked.

Two.

Aidan’s cry changed.

Smaller.

Exhausted.

Nymeris’s face broke.

Not Queen now.

Mother.

She moved toward Zee again.

He raised one hand.

“Stay.”

Witch force stopped the water.

Not her.

Only the attack.

“I will not hurt you.”

“Then stop hurting him!”

“I am trying to save him!”

“He does not understand that!”

“I know!”

That shout cracked something in Zee.

Because she was right.

Aidan did not understand.

Only pain.

Zee had become the adult causing it.

For protection.

That did not make the infant know the difference.

Victor’s shadow passed through the room again.

Zee almost stopped.

Then Aidan’s Wolf foundation surged.

The walls groaned.

Distant Wolves howled for the first time since birth.

Divine attention pressed closer.

Zee looked upward.

Time gone.

He continued.

Wolf instinct against Wolf instinct.

Not domination.

Recognition.

Pack.

Blood.

Father.

Zee placed one bleeding hand against Aidan’s chest.

“Mine.”

Not possession.

Family.

The Wolf foundation answered.

Zee wrapped the third seal around it.

Locked.

Three.

Witch.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Complete.

Bound.

Waiting.

The System settled around them.

Aidan stopped screaming.

Then breathed.

Once.

Twice.

Stable.

Nymeris reached him immediately.

She held her son.

Crying now.

Too angry to hide it.

Zee stood several feet away.

Blood running from his nose.

Hands shaking.

The binding had taken almost everything he could precisely control without hurting Aidan.

And through it all—

Nymeris’s inheritance remained.

Ocean.

Abyss.

Siren.

Untouched.

Separate.

Not an Authority.

His mother’s blood.

Zee would not erase that from him.

Could not.

Would not try.

⸻

Then came the part Nymeris feared most.

Zee looked at the world beyond the room.

The birth records.

Royal registries.

Bloodline archives.

Magical signatures.

Divine observation trails.

Political reports already beginning to form.

He constructed the concealment around Aidan’s existence.

Not reality.

Knowledge.

A veil across recognition.

The birth announcement disappeared.

Public magical records folded into emptiness.

Bloodline detection returned sealed silence.

Royal registries lost the connecting line between Zee, Nymeris, and the infant.

Written references blurred into unrelated entries.

Divine attention reached—

then slid away.

Not absolute.

Nothing was absolute.

But enough.

Enough to buy time.

Nymeris realized what he was doing.

“No.”

Zee closed his eyes.

“Zee.”

He continued.

“Do not.”

His hands shook harder.

“Please.”

That nearly stopped him.

Not the threat.

Not war.

Please.

Zee opened his eyes.

Nymeris held Aidan against her chest.

Furious.

Devastated.

Queen of Nhalaris begging him not to erase their son from history.

Zee’s voice broke.

“I am sorry.”

“I do not want your apology.”

“I know.”

“Then stop.”

Zee looked at Aidan.

Tiny.

Sleeping now.

Unaware.

Alive.

The world already hunting for what had been born.

Zee finished the concealment.

And suddenly—

quiet.

No divine pressure.

No magical searching.

No distant supernatural attention.

Nothing.

The world stopped looking.

⸻

The concealment moved through every mind that had touched the truth.

Nymeris blinked.

The fury vanished first.

Then the fear.

Then the memory of the argument.

She looked down at the infant in her arms.

Her expression softened with confusion.

“Whose child is this?”

Zee went still.

The question struck harder than any weapon.

Nymeris looked around the room.

The mobilized sea gates no longer made sense to her.

The war configuration dissolved as the reason for it disappeared.

Nhalaris forgot its prince.

The kingdom forgot why it had prepared for war.

The healers forgot what they had witnessed.

The records forgot what they had recorded.

Darkheart forgot that Zee had ever had this son.

Ner stared at the empty space where his certainty had been.

His Bloodsong no longer recognized Aidan’s name.

He looked at the infant with polite confusion.

“Why is there a child here?”

Zareth’s Link went silent.

He looked toward Zee.

Then toward Aidan.

No recognition remained.

No memory of the birth.

No memory of the seals.

No memory of the argument.

Only an unexplained unease that vanished when he tried to grasp it.

Nymeris handed the child toward the nearest healer.

“Take him to the nursery.”

Zee moved before anyone else could.

“No.”

The word came out too sharply.

Nymeris frowned.

“Why?”

Zee had no answer she could remember.

Not one she could give her.

The concealment had taken the reason from her.

From Ner.

From Zareth.

From Nhalaris.

From Darkheart.

From everyone except him.

And Rez.

Rez stood near the door.

He had not moved.

His face was pale.

His eyes were fixed on Zee.

Unlike the others, he remembered.

Every word.

Every threat.

Every seal.

Every name.

Aidan.

Rez looked at Nymeris.

She did not know him.

Not as mother.

Not as Queen defending her son.

She looked at Ner.

He did not know Aidan.

Zareth did not know Aidan.

The world had forgotten.

Rez turned back to Zee.

“You did it.”

Zee’s throat tightened.

“Yes.”

Rez looked at the infant.

Then at Zee.

“Only us.”

Zee nodded.

The words barely existed in his mouth.

“Only us.”

Nymeris watched them with growing suspicion.

“Only you remember what?”

Rez did not answer.

Zee could not.

The truth had become something held between two men in a room full of people who had once belonged to it.

Zee took Aidan from the healer.

The infant stirred.

Then wrapped his hand around Zee’s finger again.

Same as before.

No understanding of kingdoms.

No understanding of war.

No understanding of Systems.

Seals.

Legacy.

Erasure.

Only warmth.

Heartbeat.

Father.

The Crimson System rested silently around three locked foundations.

Witch.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Complete.

Bound.

Waiting.

The Crimson Legacy preserved the truth separately.

It did not grant power.

It did not control Aidan.

It only remembered what the world no longer could.

Ocean, Abyss, and Siren blood remained dormant.

Separate.

Untouched.

Unbound.

Zee held Aidan closer.

Nymeris looked at him without recognition.

Ner looked away.

Zareth’s Link remained empty.

Nhalaris had forgotten its prince.

Darkheart had forgotten Zee had this son.

His mother had forgotten.

His family had forgotten.

Only Rez looked at Zee and understood exactly what had been lost.

Zee touched his forehead to Aidan’s.

Then whispered:

“You can hate me when you are old enough to choose to.”

Rez closed his eyes.

He understood the promise.

Not forgiveness.

Not approval.

A witness.

The only witness left besides Zee.

Zee held his son.

Alive.

Hidden.

Wanted.

Chosen.

Aidan Darkheart existed.

Son of Zee Darkheart.

Son of Nymeris Thalryn.

Prince by blood of Darkheart.

Prince by blood of Nhalaris.

Carrier of Witch.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Ocean.

Abyss.

Siren.

The world knew none of it now.

His mother knew none of it.

His family knew none of it.

Only Zee and Rez remembered who he truly was.

For now, the world had forgotten Aidan Darkheart.

His mother had forgotten.

His family had forgotten.

Only his father and Rez remembered.

And Zee Darkheart had finally given his son the one thing nobody had ever given him at birth.

Time.
