Chapter Seventy

The First Name

Orin Bale lived in a city that worshiped discretion.

No banners.

No guards in uniform.

No royal colors.

Just money.

Old money.

Quiet money.

The kind that paid other people to become violent.

Zee preferred obvious predators.

They were easier.

He and Ner entered the city before dawn.

No army.

No escort.

Rez had returned to Vesper.

His kingdom had been targeted.

That came first.

Zareth remained in Drakonis.

His kingdom still had wounds of its own.

The core four did not need to stand in the same room to remain brothers.

Zee had learned that.

Mostly.

Ner walked beside him beneath a dark cloak.

“You are smiling.”

“No.”

“You are.”

“I like cities where everyone thinks secrets make them powerful.”

Ner sighed.

“Of course you do.”

Zee’s crimson-and-gold eyes remained human-pupiled.

Calm.

Thinking.

He had not come to kill Orin Bale.

Not yet.

He had come to prove the name.

That distinction mattered.

For now.

⸻

Orin owned four warehouses, two bathhouses, three blood banks, and one respectable charitable hospital.

The hospital was the problem.

Zee smelled it before they entered.

Blood.

Too much.

Not fresh violence.

Stored.

Categorized.

Separated.

Human.

Witch.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Demon.

Something amphibious.

Something Zee did not recognize.

Ner stopped beside him.

“You smell it too?”

“Yes.”

“The basement.”

“Yes.”

A nurse approached.

Human.

Tired.

No fear.

No guilt.

“Can I help you?”

Zee smiled politely.

“We are looking for Lord Bale.”

“He does not visit patients.”

“I did not ask whether he did.”

The nurse blinked.

Ner stepped in before Zee frightened her unnecessarily.

“We were told he maintains a private office here.”

“Oh.”

She pointed.

“Third floor.”

“Thank you.”

They walked away.

Ner looked at Zee.

“You were about to compel her.”

“Yes.”

“She answered.”

“Yes.”

“So why?”

Zee considered.

“Habit.”

Ner gave him a look.

That bothered Zee more than criticism.

Not because Ner was judging.

Because Zee realized he had almost used domination simply because it was easier.

He filed that away.

No guilt spiral.

Just correction.

⸻

Orin’s office had six locks.

Zee opened none.

He entered through the window.

Ner came through the door after dismantling the wards.

They arrived at the same time.

Orin Bale looked up from breakfast.

Older Human.

Maybe fifty.

Silver hair.

Steady heartbeat.

No immediate fear.

That changed when Zee sat in the chair opposite him.

“King Darkheart.”

“Orin.”

Ner closed the door.

Orin looked toward him.

Then back to Zee.

“I assume this concerns Greyhaven.”

Zee’s expression did not move.

Confirmation one.

“You know about it.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because I funded part of it.”

Ner’s Gold flickered.

Orin lifted one hand.

“Before either of you decides that sentence is enough to kill me, ask what I funded.”

Zee smiled faintly.

Smart.

“Go on.”

“Research into defensive measures against supernatural coercion.”

“Compulsion?”

“Yes.”

“Blood manipulation?”

“Yes.”

“Dragon-link interference?”

Orin’s face tightened.

“No.”

Truth.

Zee smelled no fear spike around the denial.

Interesting.

Ner stepped closer.

“What about my blood?”

Orin looked at him.

“That was not my project.”

“Were you aware it existed?”

“Yes.”

“Did you stop it?”

“No.”

There.

Guilt.

Different kind.

Ner’s face hardened.

“Why?”

“Because the Covenant is not a military order.”

Zee leaned back.

“What is it?”

“A network.”

“Five principal patrons.”

“Independent cells.”

“Separate agendas.”

“Shared intelligence.”

Rez would have liked that answer.

No single mastermind.

No perfect chain.

Messier.

Real.

Orin continued.

“I paid for anti-compulsion development after three cities lost councils to Vampire domination.”

Zee’s eyes sharpened.

“Mine?”

“No.”

Truth again.

“And when Greyhaven began planning attacks?”

“I objected.”

“Not enough.”

“No.”

Orin did not defend himself.

That was unusual.

Zee looked around the office.

No hidden weapon.

One panic switch.

Orin had not touched it.

“Why stay?”

“Because leaving the Covenant means losing access to information about what the others are planning.”

Ner laughed without humor.

“And how many people did that information save?”

Orin answered:

“Three hundred and eleven.”

Specific.

Zee looked at him.

“Prove it.”

Orin opened a drawer slowly.

Placed ledgers on the desk.

Names.

Dates.

Anonymous warnings.

Cancelled raids.

Evacuated settlements.

One entry involved Vesper.

Rez had received an unexplained warning two years earlier about a Vampire nest moving through the eastern ridge.

Zee remembered.

Orin watched him read.

“I am not innocent.”

“Good.”

Zee closed the ledger.

“I hate when people waste time pretending.”

Orin’s mouth twitched.

“I funded anti-compulsion weapons.”

“Some were tested on prisoners.”

Ner said.

Orin’s face darkened.

“I learned that after.”

“And stayed.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because Mara Vess wanted to turn the Covenant into something worse.”

There.

Second name.

Zee’s attention sharpened.

“What worse?”

“Preemptive extermination.”

Silence.

Ner glanced toward Zee.

Orin continued.

“Not stopping threats.”

“Eliminating bloodlines judged likely to produce threats.”

Zee went very still.

Infants.

Possibility as guilt.

Victor.

Different century.

Same disease.

Orin saw the change.

“That is why I stayed.”

“To stop her?”

“To slow her.”

“Did you?”

“Sometimes.”

“Not enough.”

“No.”

Again.

Truth.

Zee stood.

Orin did not.

“Am I dead?”

Zee looked at him.

Six years ago he might have killed him for complicity.

Three years ago he might have fed first and asked later.

Now?

Darker did not mean simpler.

“No.”

Orin exhaled.

Zee continued.

“But your money ends today.”

Orin looked up.

“And if I refuse?”

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

Slits.

Predatory pressure filled the room.

“Do you want to?”

Orin’s heartbeat stumbled.

“No.”

Good.

Zee leaned slightly closer.

“Give me everything you have on Mara Vess.”

Orin hesitated.

Then opened another drawer.

No compulsion necessary.

That pleased Zee more than forcing him would have.

Barely.

⸻

They left with three ledgers and one address.

Ner walked beside Zee in silence for two streets.

Then:

“You spared him.”

“Yes.”

“You wanted to kill him.”

“Yes.”

“Why didn’t you?”

Zee looked ahead.

“Because he is guilty of cowardice and complicity.”

“Not what Mara is planning.”

Ner nodded.

No praise.

No moral lesson.

Just understanding.

Zee looked at him.

“You expected me to kill him.”

“I expected you to consider it.”

“I did.”

Ner smiled faintly.

“I know.”

Zee’s hand brushed his.

Ner caught it.

Held.

For half a block.

Then released when they reached the city gate.

Romance did not stop the hunt.

It traveled with it.

Outside the walls, one of Zee’s Darkheart scouts waited with a sealed message.

“From Vesper.”

Zee opened it.

Rez’s handwriting.

Short.

Aqueduct attack stopped. Lucien alive. Twelve captured. Three escaped. One name overlaps your list: Mara Vess.

Zee smiled.

This time Ner did not ask why.

The first name had not deserved death.

The second one was becoming much harder to defend.

And somewhere south, Mara Vess was preparing to eliminate threats before they ever acted.

Zee knew exactly how dangerous that philosophy could become.

Because part of him was beginning to understand it.
