Chapter Twenty-Six

The Blood His Mother Hid

Zee did not speak immediately.

That was unusual.

Aurelia had prepared herself for questions.

Anger.

Accusations.

Perhaps the dramatic declaration that everyone lied to him.

Instead, Zee stared at his mother as though rearranging ten years of memories inside his head.

Zareth lay beside him.

The Dragon Link carried confusion from both directions.

Finally Zee looked toward the dragon.

“Then why does he make it stronger?”

“I do not know completely.”

Zee looked back at Aurelia.

“But you know where it came from.”

“Yes.”

“Who?”

Aurelia held his gaze.

“Me.”

Silence.

Zee laughed.

Not because it was funny.

Because he thought she had misunderstood him.

“You are a witch.”

“Yes.”

He waited.

Aurelia did not continue.

Zee’s smile disappeared.

“Mother.”

Aurelia lifted one hand.

For the first time in Zee’s life, she stopped hiding.

Magic moved through her.

Not the witchcraft Zee had known since childhood.

Something older answered beneath it.

Her pupils narrowed.

Heat entered the room.

Then scales appeared.

They began along Aurelia’s wrist.

Dark.

Deep red-brown beneath the candlelight.

Not Zareth’s crimson.

Not Zee’s Crimson scales.

Different.

A lineage entirely its own.

The scales moved halfway along her forearm before stopping.

Zee stared.

Zareth rose instantly.

Teal-and-gold Mana ignited along his spine.

Not aggression.

Shock.

The Dragon Link exploded with one overwhelming concept.

Dragon.

Zee stood.

“You are…”

Aurelia finished it.

“Dragon and Witch.”

Zee looked at her scales.

Then his own.

Then back again.

“No.”

Aurelia’s expression tightened.

“No?”

“You would have told me.”

The words hurt.

Zee was ten.

There was no elegant accusation.

Only a child realizing his mother had hidden part of herself every day of his life.

Aurelia lowered her hand.

The scales remained.

“I wanted to.”

“When?”

“When you were older.”

“I am older.”

“You are ten.”

“You let me think Zareth was doing this to me.”

“I never told you that.”

“You did not tell me anything!”

Crimson Mana flashed through the room.

His developing Dragon energy moved with it.

The scales along Zee’s neck spread toward his jaw.

Aurelia did not flinch.

Zareth moved closer.

The Dragon Link carried a warning.

Angry.

Zee glared at him.

“I know.”

Zareth sent another feeling.

Mother. Hurt.

Zee’s anger faltered.

Only slightly.

Aurelia saw.

“You have every right to be angry.”

“I hate when adults say that.”

“Why?”

“Because then they keep doing the thing anyway.”

Aurelia almost smiled.

This was not the moment.

“You are right.”

That surprised him.

Zee crossed his arms.

“Tell me everything.”

“No.”

His eyes widened.

Aurelia continued before he exploded.

“I will tell you what belongs to you.”

“What does that mean?”

“It means some of my history is mine to decide when to share.”

Zee stared.

“That is unfair.”

“Yes.”

He clearly had not expected agreement again.

Aurelia continued.

“But your blood belongs to you.”

She touched the scales on his forearm.

“You inherited this from me.”

“Was it there when I was born?”

“Yes.”

“Then why did no one see it?”

“Because it was dormant.”

Zee frowned.

“My Witch, Vampire, and Wolf were not.”

“No.”

“You knew those?”

“Very quickly.”

Zee touched one horn.

“And this?”

“Your horn buds appeared years later.”

“You were not surprised.”

Aurelia looked away.

Zee caught it.

“You knew.”

“I suspected.”

“How?”

“Because I had them once.”

Zee stared at her head.

“You have horns?”

“Not in this form.”

“Can you turn into a dragon?”

Aurelia went silent.

Zee leaned forward.

“Mother.”

“Yes.”

His mouth opened.

Closed.

Opened again.

Zareth’s Dragon Link filled with absolute fascination.

Zee looked toward him.

“You did not know either?”

Zareth answered with a violent burst of offended confusion.

No.

Zee nodded.

“Good.”

Aurelia raised an eyebrow.

“Good?”

“At least everyone was lied to.”

“I did not lie.”

“You hid an entire dragon.”

Aurelia almost laughed despite herself.

“It is difficult to hide an entire dragon.”

“You did.”

Fair.

Zee sat again.

“When did Zareth start waking it?”

Aurelia became more careful.

“I do not know that he did.”

“You said—”

“I said your Dragon blood came from me.”

She looked toward Zareth.

“But from the moment he appeared beside you, your dormant blood reacted to him.”

Zareth’s teal-and-gold Mana shimmered.

Zee’s Crimson Mana answered.

Two different powers.

Never becoming the same.

Aurelia watched.

“His presence may be a catalyst.”

Zee frowned.

“What is that?”

“Something that helps a change begin without being the thing that created it.”

Zee thought.

“So he did not give it to me.”

“No.”

“He just… wakes it up.”

“Perhaps.”

“Perhaps?”

“I promised you truth.”

Aurelia’s voice softened.

“Not guesses disguised as truth.”

Zee remembered all the times she had forced witches, kings, and scholars to say I do not know.

That mattered more now.

“Does the Dragon Queen know?”

“No.”

“Lucien?”

“No.”

“Rez?”

“No.”

“Ner?”

“No.”

“Vale?”

Aurelia hesitated.

Zee pointed.

“He knows.”

“He suspects.”

“That means yes.”

“It does not.”

“Caldrin?”

“Caldrin suspects everything.”

Zee nodded.

“That is true.”

The tension loosened.

Only slightly.

Zee touched the juvenile horn above his temple.

“Am I becoming a dragon?”

Aurelia looked at him.

“You already carry Dragon nature.”

“That was not my question.”

“No.”

Her answer came carefully.

“You are not becoming only anything.”

Zee frowned.

“You were born Witch, Vampire, and Wolf completely active.”

“That is why you are the First Natural Tribrid.”

“Dragon was inherited but asleep.”

“And now?”

“Awakening.”

“How far?”

“I do not know.”

“Will I grow wings?”

“Possibly.”

“Fire?”

“Possibly.”

“Can I fly?”

“Not today.”

Zee looked deeply offended.

Zareth sent laughter through the Link.

Zee shoved his snout.

“Shut up.”

Zareth shoved back.

Aurelia smiled.

Then Zee became serious again.

“Why did you hide being a Dragon?”

The question changed the room.

Aurelia’s smile disappeared.

Her scales slowly receded beneath her skin.

“Because there are things from before you were born that still know my blood.”

Zee watched her.

“Enemies?”

“Yes.”

“Dragon enemies?”

“Some.”

“Witches?”

“Some.”

“Victor?”

Aurelia went still.

Zee saw it.

“Father knows.”

Aurelia did not answer quickly enough.

Zee stood.

“He knows?”

“He knows part.”

“How much?”

“Enough that it became another thing between us.”

Zee’s expression hardened.

“Did he hate you too?”

Aurelia looked toward the candle.

“No.”

That answer carried more pain than yes would have.

“Victor once loved what he did not understand about me.”

“Then fear changed him.”

Zee thought of the father he barely remembered.

A face in fragments.

A hand he once trusted.

A voice that sometimes appeared in nightmares without words.

“Like me.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“Yes.”

Zee turned away.

For several moments he watched rain strike the dark window.

Then:

“I do not want to be like him.”

Aurelia’s expression softened.

“Then keep choosing differently.”

The same answer she had given him years ago.

People become what they keep choosing.

Zee remembered.

His anger quieted.

Not gone.

But his.

Controlled.

He turned back.

“Show me.”

Aurelia frowned.

“What?”

“Your dragon.”

“No.”

Zee’s face collapsed.

“Why?”

“Because this room is too small.”

His eyes widened.

Zareth rose immediately.

Excitement flooded the Link.

Zee grinned.

“When?”

“Not tonight.”

“Tomorrow?”

“No.”

“Next week?”

“Zee.”

“You said I deserve truth.”

“You deserve sleep.”

“That is not truth.”

“It is unfortunately true.”

Zee groaned.

Aurelia stood.

She kissed his forehead beside one horn.

“I love you.”

He remained annoyed.

“I know.”

She reached the door.

“Mother?”

Aurelia stopped.

Zee looked down at his scales.

“Are mine ugly?”

Aurelia’s face changed instantly.

“No.”

“They are darker than Zareth’s.”

“They should be.”

“Why?”

“Because they are yours.”

Zee looked toward Zareth.

The dragon sent a clear thought-fragment.

Good scales. Bad horns.

Zee stared.

“My horns are better than yours.”

Zareth growled.

Aurelia left before the argument became physical.

For the first time in years, she closed the door smiling.

The next morning began with training.

Rez attacked Zee before he finished complaining about breakfast.

A Blue-White blade of Mana swept toward his legs.

Zee jumped.

“You cheated!”

Rez followed with another strike.

“You were warned.”

“You said training!”

“This is training.”

“You attacked me while I was eating!”

“A battlefield does not respect breakfast.”

Ner sat on the wall eating an apple.

“That is the stupidest thing you have ever said.”

Rez pointed at him without looking.

“I am trying to teach.”

“You sound like Lucien.”

Rez missed a step.

Zee immediately hit him with a training staff.

Rez stared.

Zee smiled.

“Battlefield.”

Ner nearly fell from the wall laughing.

Zareth lay nearby, entirely unhelpful.

The Dragon Link carried approval.

Rez recovered.

“Again.”

This time Zee took position properly.

His body had learned seven years of survival.

Feet light.

Shoulders loose.

Wolf instincts reading movement.

Vampire senses tracking heartbeat.

Witch nature ready beneath his Crimson Mana.

Dragon energy new and restless underneath.

Rez attacked.

Zee blocked.

Blue-White Mana struck Crimson.

The impact pushed Zee backward.

He dug in.

Scales spread along his forearms.

Rez saw.

He changed angle.

Zee anticipated.

Their practice blades hit.

Again.

Again.

Rez was older.

Better trained.

More disciplined.

Zee was faster than he had any right to be.

Unpredictable.

And increasingly difficult to surprise.

Ner watched carefully.

Then Pure Golden Mana suddenly flickered around his hand.

He looked toward the palace.

Zee noticed.

“What?”

Ner jumped from the wall.

“The bronze door.”

Rez stopped.

“What about it?”

Ner’s face tightened.

“It opened.”

Everyone went silent.

Lucien had forbidden anyone from touching it.

Ner looked at his hand.

“I did not do anything.”

A bell rang beneath the palace.

Once.

Deep.

Ancient.

Then again.

Zareth stood.

The Dragon Link carried immediate warning.

Below.

A third bell rang.

The courtyard stones trembled.

Rez lowered his practice weapon.

“That cannot be good.”

Caldrin appeared on the upper balcony.

“No.”

He looked down at them.

“It cannot.”

Zee looked toward the palace entrance.

Aurelia emerged seconds later.

She met his eyes.

For once, she did not tell him to stay behind.

“Get your weapons.”

Zee blinked.

“Really?”

Aurelia pointed.

“Before I change my mind.”

Zee ran.

Ner followed.

Rez smiled.

Zareth spread his wings.

The bronze door beneath Vesper had remained sealed through kingdoms, wars, witches, demons, and generations of rulers.

Ner had refused to open it.

Yet something on the other side had apparently decided it no longer needed permission.

And when Zee Darkheart descended beneath House Vesper again, he would do so differently than before.

Not sneaking behind adults.

Not being carried away from danger.

Not merely surviving whatever found him.

This time, his mother knew he was going.

His brother stood beside him.

His best friend carried Golden Mana.

His dragon moved overhead.

And beneath Zee’s skin, a fourth inheritance had finally been named.

The hunted prince was still a child.

But for the first time in his life, the adults around him were beginning to understand something the world had learned much earlier.

Keeping Zee away from danger was becoming impossible.

Teaching him how to walk into it might be the only protection they had left.
