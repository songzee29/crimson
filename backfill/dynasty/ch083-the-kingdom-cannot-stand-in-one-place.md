Chapter Eighty-Three

The Kingdom Cannot Stand in One Place

The thing beneath Darkheart was not one monster.

It was a nest.

Ner found the first tunnel beneath the old foundations before dawn.

The stone walls were covered in claw marks.

Not Wolf.

Not Demon.

Older.

“Someone released them from the lower vaults,” Ner said.

Zee crouched beside the marks.

“Who?”

“I do not know.”

“Can you hear them?”

Ner closed his eyes.

Bloodsong spread through the stone.

His expression changed.

“Not hear.”

“What, then?”

“Feel.”

The floor moved.

A crack opened beneath them.

Three pale creatures climbed through.

They had no eyes.

Their mouths split vertically across their faces.

Kael raised one hand.

“Burn.”

Blue fire struck the first creature.

It screamed.

The sound hit the walls and returned as a dozen echoes.

More movement answered from below.

Kael’s face tightened.

“That was not the only one.”

Zee drew his sword.

“Ner, seal the lower levels.”

“I can try.”

“Do it.”

Ner stepped forward.

Bloodsong erupted.

The creatures froze.

For one second, their bodies obeyed the rhythm of his power.

Then the largest one moved.

It struck Ner across the chest.

He flew into the wall.

The impact cracked stone.

Zee turned.

“Ner!”

The creature lunged again.

Zee intercepted it.

Vampire darkness wrapped around its limbs.

The thing tore through the shadow.

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“Interesting.”

Kael struck it from the side.

“Less studying.”

The creature slammed him into the floor.

Amara shifted and attacked its throat.

Her claws cut deep.

The creature did not bleed.

It screamed.

Tessa drove a silver spike through its skull.

The body collapsed.

Below them, the nest began to rise.

Ner pushed himself upright.

Blood ran from his mouth.

“I need distance.”

Zee looked at him.

“Can you hold them?”

Ner’s eyes turned black.

“I can hold some.”

“Then hold them.”

Ner stepped into the tunnel.

Bloodsong expanded.

The air became heavy.

Every heartbeat in the lower levels fell into his rhythm.

The creatures stopped climbing.

Ner lifted both hands.

“Stay.”

The word carried through the stone.

The nest obeyed.

For now.

Zee looked toward the eastern wall.

A horn sounded.

Then another.

The enemy army had resumed its attack.

At the same moment, a messenger stumbled into the corridor.

“Majesty!”

Zee turned.

“Speak.”

“Vesper is under assault.”

Rez went still.

“How many?”

“Unknown. The southern gates are burning.”

Zee looked toward the tunnel.

Ner stood alone before the nest.

Then toward the eastern wall.

Darkheart’s army was already stretched thin.

Then toward Rez.

Vesper was his home.

Zee could not be in all three places.

He hated the truth.

That did not make it less true.

“Rez,” Zee said.

“I am going.”

“You may not be able to hold it.”

Rez’s blue-white mana gathered around his hands.

“Then I will make them regret reaching it.”

Zee looked at Ner.

“Can you survive without me?”

Ner smiled faintly.

“I survived you.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It is the only one you are getting.”

Zee turned to Kael, Tessa, and Amara.

“Take the lower levels.”

Kael looked toward the tunnel.

“And you?”

“Eastern wall.”

Tessa shook her head.

“You should go to Vesper.”

“Darkheart is already under siege.”

“Rez is alone.”

Rez looked at her.

“I am not alone.”

He pointed toward the corridor.

“Neither are you.”

That ended the argument.

Zee hated that too.

He touched Rez’s shoulder.

“Do not be reckless.”

Rez gave him a tired smile.

“You say that as if it has ever worked.”

Then he vanished in a burst of blue-white light.

Zee ran toward the eastern wall.

⸻

The second attack was larger.

Orath had brought siege towers.

The northern Wolves had brought fire.

Veyr’s mercenaries had brought no banners at all.

They climbed the walls in silence.

Zee reached the battlements as the first tower struck.

The impact threw defenders from their feet.

Merek shouted orders.

The wall held.

Then the tower gates opened.

Soldiers poured across the bridge.

Zee’s Crimson Demonic Dragon nature surged.

Crimson-black scales spread across his arms.

His horns sharpened.

Heat rolled from his body.

Dragon force filled his muscles.

He remained completely humanoid.

He stepped onto the battlement.

Then jumped.

The distance should have killed an ordinary man.

Zee struck the siege tower hard enough to split the upper platform.

The tower shuddered beneath him.

He tore through its supports with his claws and dropped through the collapsing frame.

He landed in the wreckage and rose already moving.

A second tower advanced.

Then a third.

Zee leaped from the broken tower to the next, using the shattered beams as footholds.

The second tower fired iron bolts.

One tore through Zee’s shoulder.

His body twisted from the impact.

He crashed into the wall below the battlement.

The defenders shouted.

Zee struck the stone hard enough to crack it.

Then he stood again.

The wound smoked.

The bolt had been treated with Dragonbane.

A second bolt struck his side.

Zee caught it before it could pass through him.

He snapped the shaft and pulled the head free.

The poison slowed his healing, but it did not stop him.

The enemy commander raised a sword.

“Now!”

The mercenaries surged.

Kael arrived on the battlement, blood on his face.

“Bind!”

Chains caught half the attackers.

The other half reached the wall.

Tessa fought beside him, using a broken spear as a crutch.

Amara moved through the chaos, dragging wounded defenders away from the edge.

They were winning sections.

Losing others.

No one controlled the whole battle.

Zee climbed the broken stone with his claws.

A Wolf soldier leaped onto his back.

Zee caught him and threw him over the wall.

Another struck his ribs.

A third drove silver into his thigh.

Zee roared.

The sound shook the battlements.

The attackers hesitated.

Merek used the moment.

“Push!”

Darkheart’s defenders drove the mercenaries back.

The wall held.

But the eastern gate did not.

A section of the gate collapsed inward.

Soldiers flooded through.

Zee landed in the courtyard.

He was too late.

Again.

The enemy had entered Darkheart.

He looked toward the keep.

Families were being moved through the inner streets.

Children.

Elders.

Wounded.

The kingdom was no longer a battlefield.

It was a maze of people trying to survive.

Zee ran toward the breach.

Then the Dragon Link struck his mind.

Zareth.

Pain.

Fire.

Drakonis.

We are attacked.

Zee stopped.

“By whom?”

Demon riders. Three warbands. They came through the northern pass.

Zee closed his eyes.

Darkheart.

Vesper.

Drakonis.

Three fires.

Three choices.

Zareth’s voice came again through the Link.

Do not come.

Zee’s jaw tightened.

You are asking me not to come?

I am telling you Drakonis is mine to defend.

A roar thundered through the Link.

Then the sound of stone breaking.

Protect your kingdom.

The connection narrowed.

Not gone.

Busy.

Zee opened his eyes.

The enemy had reached the inner courtyard.

He raised both hands.

“Darkness.”

The courtyard vanished beneath a wave of black.

Soldiers stumbled blind.

Darkheart defenders moved through the shadow by memory.

Kael’s chains struck.

Tessa pulled wounded citizens behind cover.

Amara found the enemy commander by scent.

She tackled him from behind.

Zee reached the breach.

The gate could not be repaired.

Not tonight.

So he changed the order.

“Collapse the outer road.”

Merek stared.

“That will cut off the eastern farms.”

“I know.”

“It will trap anyone still there.”

Zee looked toward the burning village.

The farms were already lost.

“Move everyone we can.”

The road collapsed beneath a controlled spell.

Stone buried the approach.

The enemy army was forced back from the inner wall.

Darkheart survived the night.

But the eastern territory was gone.

⸻

Vesper did not survive untouched.

Rez reached the southern gates before the attackers breached the city.

He found no army waiting.

Only citizens.

Witches with kitchen knives.

Old Vampires who could barely stand.

Children carrying buckets of water toward the fires.

Rez raised both hands.

Blue-white mana spread across the gate.

“Rise.”

The fallen stones lifted.

The gate sealed.

For a moment, the attackers stopped.

Then the first spell struck.

Rez deflected it.

The second shattered against his shield.

The third came from beneath the street.

A hidden Witch circle ignited under his feet.

Rez looked down.

Too late.

The spell detonated.

His shield broke.

The blast threw him across the square.

He struck the fountain hard enough to crack the stone.

The attackers entered.

Rez forced himself upright.

Blood ran from his ear.

His mana flickered.

He lifted one hand.

“Freeze.”

The street became ice.

Twenty soldiers fell.

The citizens surged forward.

Rez fought beside them.

Not as a prince.

As Vesper’s Witch.

He sealed doors.

Raised barriers.

Burned siege ropes.

He moved from street to street while the city fought around him.

Then the hidden circle activated again.

A second blast struck his chest.

Rez screamed.

Blue-white mana exploded outward.

The attackers were thrown back.

Rez remained standing.

Barely.

A Vampire commander approached through the smoke.

“You should have stayed beneath Darkheart’s shadow.”

Rez wiped blood from his mouth.

“I built Vesper before Zee ever found me.”

The commander smiled.

“Then die defending it.”

He moved faster than a human eye could follow.

Rez raised a shield.

The commander broke through.

A blade entered beneath Rez’s ribs.

The world went silent.

Rez looked down.

The blade was black.

Witch-killing iron.

The commander twisted it.

Rez’s mana collapsed.

He fell against the fountain.

The commander leaned close.

“Tell your king Vesper is finished.”

Rez gripped the blade.

Blue-white light gathered in his eyes.

“No.”

He pulled himself forward.

The blade went deeper.

Rez placed one hand against the commander’s chest.

“Burn.”

The spell ignited inside the Vampire.

He screamed.

Rez collapsed with him.

The city defenders drove the remaining attackers back before dawn.

But Rez did not rise.

⸻

A burst of blue-white mana flashed above Darkheart’s southern watchtower.

Vesper.

Distress.

A messenger appeared seconds later, half-supported by two Darkheart guards.

Blood covered his coat.

“King Zee.”

Zee turned.

The messenger struggled for breath.

“Vesper held.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“Rez?”

The messenger hesitated.

That was enough to frighten him.

“Prince Rez was struck with Witch-killing iron.”

Silence.

“Is he alive?”

“Barely.”

Zee moved.

Kael caught his arm.

“Where?”

“Vesper.”

“What about here?”

“Merek has command.”

Merek looked at Zee.

For one second, neither spoke.

Then Merek drew his sword.

“Go.”

Zee looked toward the wounded defenders.

Toward Tessa, whose shoulder was bound.

Toward Amara, limping from a cut across her thigh.

Toward Kael, still holding the eastern wall with one hand.

Darkheart was damaged.

Not helpless.

That distinction mattered.

Zee turned toward the southern road.

He did not transform.

He did not fly.

He ran.

Vampire speed tore him through the storm, across broken roads and burning fields.

Behind him, the kingdom continued fighting without him.

Below the northern mountains, the Dragon Link carried the distant roar of Drakonis.

Beneath Darkheart, Ner held back the nest alone.

In Vesper, Rez lay dying beside a broken fountain.

The world had finally reached the people Zee loved.

Not all at once.

From every direction.

And this time, he was already too late.
