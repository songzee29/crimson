Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Nine

The Boy Behind the Power

The Wolf became easier.

Not easy.

Never immediately.

But easier.

Years moved.

Zayrlen learned how to recognize the first pressure behind his ribs.

How hearing changed before the shift.

How scent sharpened.

How his heartbeat settled into the Wolf rhythm.

He learned not to panic when bones changed.

Learned how to let the transformation happen instead of fighting it.

Sometimes.

Other times he still ended up halfway through a hallway because he got angry and suddenly had four paws.

Once, during an argument with Victor, he transformed by accident.

Victor stared at the Crimson-and-Gold Wolf.

The Wolf growled.

Victor looked toward Zee.

“Is this your doing?”

Zee looked innocent.

“I am sitting down.”

Zayrlen barked.

Victor sighed.

“Wonderful.”

⸻

By the time Zayrlen was old enough to understand what the world thought of his father, Darkheart had become normal to him.

That was the strange part.

People whispered outside the kingdom.

The First Natural Abysscin.

The First Natural Tribrid.

Crimson Sovereign.

Crimson Demonic Dragon.

The King who killed Gods.

The man who destroyed Pyrathis.

The monster who created races.

The Blood Hunter.

Zayrlen heard the titles.

He understood some.

Others sounded ridiculous.

Because the terrifying creature kingdoms warned their children about was the same man who still inspected every bruise Zayrlen came home with.

⸻

One afternoon, Zayrlen returned from the training yard with a split lip.

He was older now.

Not little.

Still a child.

Old enough to be embarrassed.

Zee saw the blood.

Stopped mid-conversation with Rez.

“What happened?”

Zayrlen kept walking.

“Nothing.”

Zee appeared in front of him.

Vampire speed.

Zayrlen sighed.

“Dad.”

“Who?”

“Nobody.”

“Your lip disagrees.”

“I was sparring.”

“With?”

“Amara.”

Zee looked toward the courtyard.

Amara raised one hand from fifty feet away.

“He started it.”

Zayrlen looked offended.

“You said I could try.”

“I did.”

“You hit me.”

“That is what sparring is.”

Zee looked back at his son.

“Open.”

“No.”

“Zayrlen.”

“I am fine.”

Zee narrowed his eyes.

Zayrlen narrowed his back.

Rez watched with interest.

Then Zayrlen opened his mouth.

Zee checked the injury.

Small.

Already healing.

He nodded.

“Fine.”

Zayrlen wiped his mouth.

“You know I heal.”

“I know.”

“You still check.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Zee stared like the answer was obvious.

“Because you are mine.”

Zayrlen rolled his eyes.

But smiled while walking away.

Rez looked toward Zee.

“Teddy bear.”

Zee turned.

“Leave.”

⸻

Zayrlen’s Witch Mana grew with him.

Crimson-and-Gold.

Bright.

Dense.

Temperamental when he was younger.

More disciplined as he matured.

He did not cast like Zee.

That became clear early.

Zee favored precise one-word laws.

Zayrlen liked shaping.

He built.

Barriers.

Threads.

Spears.

Moving structures of Crimson-and-Gold Mana that changed shape during use.

His magic felt physical.

Almost architectural.

Ner recognized the precision underneath it.

Zee recognized the instinct to turn magic into something useful immediately.

But the expression belonged to Zayrlen alone.

Once Elias watched him form three floating Mana rings around a training target.

The rings tightened from different angles.

Elias nodded.

“Interesting.”

Zayrlen smiled.

Zee said:

“Do not compliment him too much.”

Zayrlen turned.

“You compliment me all the time.”

“That is different.”

“How?”

“I am your father.”

“That did not answer the question.”

Victor, sitting nearby, said:

“He does that.”

Zee looked toward him.

Victor smiled faintly.

Years had changed some things.

Not everything.

⸻

Victor had stayed.

Annoyingly.

Repeatedly.

He disappeared for weeks sometimes.

Returned without invitation.

Argued with Zee.

Argued with Ner.

Lost arguments to Rez.

Was threatened by Zareth often enough that it became routine.

But he stayed.

That mattered.

Not enough to erase anything.

Enough that Zayrlen grew up knowing him.

At first:

Victor.

Then occasionally:

Grandfather.

Never forced.

The first time Zayrlen said it, Victor went completely still.

Zee noticed.

Said nothing.

Ner noticed too.

Also said nothing.

Victor recovered badly.

“Do not call me that if you are about to ask for something.”

Zayrlen smiled.

“I was.”

Victor sighed.

“What?”

Zee looked over immediately.

“No.”

Victor frowned.

“He has not even asked.”

“I know.”

Zayrlen laughed.

⸻

Zayrlen’s personality sharpened with age.

He was less brooding than Zee.

More openly expressive.

More willing to laugh.

More curious.

He liked maps.

Not royal borders.

Terrain.

Rivers.

Mountain passes.

Old roads.

He liked understanding how places connected.

He hated sitting through court ceremony.

Loved field reports.

Liked sparring with Amara.

Liked tracking with Rhyven.

Loved arguing magical structure with Elias.

Kael taught him how to cheat at cards.

Ner found out.

Kael denied everything.

Rez made it worse by teaching Zayrlen how to cheat better.

Zee discovered all of them.

No one was sorry.

⸻

His temper came hot.

Fast.

Not like Zee’s coldest rage.

Zayrlen’s anger arrived visibly.

Crimson-and-Gold Mana sparking.

Wolf eyes flashing.

Heat rising through the room.

Sometimes scales appeared along his neck or forearms.

He hated being lied to.

Hated seeing people bullied.

Hated being treated like a Prince before being treated like a person.

Once a visiting noble bowed too deeply and called him:

“Future weapon of Darkheart.”

Zayrlen stared.

“I am a person.”

The noble laughed awkwardly.

Zee heard.

The entire room changed temperature.

The noble stopped laughing.

Zayrlen looked toward his father.

“Dad.”

Zee’s expression said murder.

Zayrlen shook his head.

“No.”

Zee waited.

Then looked back at the noble.

“Apologize.”

The man did.

Immediately.

Zayrlen accepted.

Zee did not forget.

⸻

His fears were quieter.

Zayrlen did not fear battle.

He feared losing control.

The first Wolf transformation had left something behind.

Not trauma.

Respect.

He knew his body contained things that could become larger than his intentions.

As he grew stronger, that mattered more.

Sometimes Dragon pressure escaped without warning.

Rooms felt smaller around him.

Candles bent.

Stone warmed.

People noticed.

Zayrlen noticed them noticing.

He did not like that.

One night he asked Zee:

“Did people look at you like that?”

Zee knew exactly what he meant.

“Yes.”

“When you were little?”

“Yes.”

“Did you hate it?”

Zee looked toward him.

“Yes.”

Zayrlen sat beside him on the balcony wall.

Older now.

Legs hanging over the edge.

“Does it stop?”

“No.”

Zayrlen frowned.

Zee continued.

“You stop needing it to.”

That answer sat with him.

⸻

The Dragon remained the unanswered part.

Scales.

Heat.

Strength.

Pressure.

Durability.

Instinct.

All present.

No form.

No shift.

No wings.

No Dragon body.

Zareth sometimes watched him when the pressure rose.

Never pushed.

One afternoon, Zayrlen caught him.

“What?”

Zareth smiled.

“Nothing.”

“You are staring.”

“I learned from Victor.”

Victor, across the courtyard, looked offended.

Zayrlen frowned.

“Is something wrong with me?”

Zareth’s expression changed immediately.

“No.”

“Then what?”

Zareth crouched.

“You have Dragon blood.”

“I know.”

“It is getting louder.”

Zayrlen looked toward Zee.

Zee had felt it too.

His Crimson Demonic Dragon nature responded differently now whenever Zayrlen entered a room angry or excited.

Recognition.

Pressure.

Something approaching.

But Zee gave no prediction.

No schedule.

No promise.

“When?” Zayrlen asked.

Zee answered:

“I do not know.”

“Will it hurt?”

Zee paused.

“I do not know.”

Zayrlen studied him.

“You hate saying that.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Zee stared.

Zayrlen smiled and ran.

Ner laughed from nearby.

“He is yours.”

“He is also yours.”

“Unfortunately.”

⸻

Zayrlen’s Wolf form changed as he grew.

Larger.

Faster.

Still Crimson and Gold.

Still unmistakably Wolf.

No Dragon anatomy.

No Phoenix traits.

He became skilled enough to shift during movement.

Not perfect.

Sometimes one paw hit the floor wrong.

Sometimes he overestimated how large he was.

Once he shifted indoors and destroyed a table.

Victor looked at the pieces.

“Your father did worse.”

Zayrlen looked toward Zee.

“How much worse?”

Zee pointed at Victor.

“Do not encourage him.”

Victor smiled.

⸻

Ner trained Zayrlen differently.

Not combat first.

Control.

Breathing.

Mana flow.

Listening.

Knowing when anger belonged to him and when instinct was amplifying it.

Zayrlen inherited none of Ner’s exact Bloodsong.

None of his Pure Gold Mana.

But Ner understood what it meant to contain multiple supernatural natures without letting one erase the person.

That lesson mattered.

“You are not your Wolf,” Ner told him.

Zayrlen nodded.

“You are not your Witchcraft.”

Another nod.

“You are not your Dragon.”

Zayrlen looked down.

Ner tapped his chest.

“They are you.”

Zayrlen looked back up.

That distinction stayed.

Years later, it would save him.

⸻

Darkheart changed around him too.

Children became friends.

Friends became young fighters, scholars, healers, scouts.

Zayrlen learned which guards would let him sneak through kitchens.

Which Witches hated paperwork.

Which Wolves cheated at races.

Which Vampires pretended not to smile.

He knew Darkheart not as a kingdom he might inherit.

As home.

That mattered more.

⸻

One evening, Zee returned from a border mission covered in blood that was not his.

Zayrlen met him in the hall.

Older now.

Old enough to know what the blood meant.

He looked at Zee.

“Bad?”

Zee removed one glove.

“Handled.”

Zayrlen nodded.

Then looked at the blood again.

“You kill a lot of people.”

“Yes.”

No lie.

“Are they always bad?”

Zee paused.

Important question.

“Not everyone I have killed deserved it.”

Zayrlen looked surprised.

Zee continued.

“I learned that too late.”

The old Rell scar sat beneath that answer.

Zayrlen listened.

“Now?”

“Now I make sure.”

Zayrlen nodded slowly.

Then asked:

“Would you kill someone for me?”

Zee looked at him.

“Immediately.”

Zayrlen stared.

“That was too fast.”

Ner, entering behind them, laughed.

Zee ignored him.

“You asked.”

“I meant if they hurt me.”

“Yes.”

“What if I started it?”

Zee stopped.

Different question.

“If you started it, then we find out why.”

“And if I was wrong?”

“You answer for being wrong.”

Zayrlen smiled faintly.

“There.”

“What?”

“That sounded like Dad.”

Zee frowned.

“What did the first answer sound like?”

“Monster.”

Zee considered.

“Also Dad.”

Zayrlen laughed.

⸻

Later that same year, Zayrlen stood alone in the upper courtyard at sunset.

Crimson-and-Gold Mana moved around his hands.

Wolf senses open.

Small Dragon scales along one shoulder.

Nothing dramatic.

Yet.

Zee watched from the arch.

The Crimson Demonic Dragon inside him stirred.

Harder than before.

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

Zayrlen turned.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You are doing the Zareth thing.”

“I am not.”

“You are staring.”

Zee walked over.

Placed one hand on Zayrlen’s shoulder.

The scales were warm.

Too warm.

Not dangerous.

Different.

Something deep inside the boy pressed back against Zee’s Dragon nature.

Ancient blood recognizing itself.

Zayrlen frowned.

“Dad?”

Zee removed his hand.

“It is getting stronger.”

“The Dragon?”

“Yes.”

Zayrlen looked toward the castle.

Then back at Zee.

“Do you know when?”

“No.”

“Still hate saying it?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Zee grabbed the back of his shirt before he could run.

Zayrlen laughed.

“Dad!”

“You are disrespectful.”

“You raised me.”

“That is not a defense.”

“It is a strong one.”

Zee pulled him close anyway.

Zayrlen complained.

Did not actually try very hard to escape.

Older.

Stronger.

More independent.

Still his son.

Still the same child Zee once carried out of a rain-soaked garden wrapped in a cloak after becoming a Wolf for the first time.

The Dragon remained beneath the surface.

Growing.

Waiting.

And somewhere ahead—

without ritual, warning, or permission—

it would finally decide it had waited long enough.
