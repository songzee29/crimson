Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Eight

The First Form

Time finally moved.

Not in one clean jump.

Not in birthdays lined neatly across a page.

It moved in smaller things.

Zayrlen learning how to sit without falling sideways.

Then learning how to stand.

Then discovering that standing was not nearly as interesting as running.

Too early.

Too fast.

Usually directly toward something breakable.

⸻

His first steps happened in the southern hall.

Ner stood three feet away.

Zee stood four.

Zayrlen looked between them.

Tiny.

Unsteady.

Determined.

Ner held out both hands.

“Come here.”

Zayrlen took one step.

Wobbled.

Stopped.

Zee smiled.

“Good.”

Zayrlen looked toward him instead.

Ner immediately frowned.

“No.”

Zee looked innocent.

“I did nothing.”

“You exist.”

“That sounds personal.”

Zayrlen took another step.

Then another.

Straight toward Zee.

Ner stared.

“He was supposed to come to me.”

Zee crouched.

“He made a choice.”

“He is barely walking.”

“Still counts.”

Zayrlen stumbled the last step.

Zee caught him instantly.

Lifted him.

Zayrlen laughed.

That was the end of walking practice.

At least according to Zee.

Ner folded his arms.

“You are supposed to put him back down.”

“He walked.”

“Yes.”

“That sounds exhausting.”

“He needs practice.”

Zee looked at Zayrlen.

Zayrlen grabbed one of his horns.

Zee sighed.

“No.”

The child pulled.

Ner smiled.

“Looks like he disagrees.”

⸻

By the time Zayrlen learned to speak in full little sentences, Darkheart had accepted one important fact.

The Prince had opinions.

Many.

Most of them inconvenient.

He liked climbing.

He liked sitting on Zee’s shoulders.

He liked following Rhyven around because Rhyven could become a Wolf.

He liked Zareth because Zareth was huge when transformed.

He liked Rez because Rez let him touch almost anything in Vesper that was not actively on fire.

He liked Elias because Elias answered questions seriously.

He liked Kael because Kael cheated at games.

He liked Amara because she did not.

He liked Tessa until she brought medicine.

Then he considered her an enemy of the state.

Victor—

was complicated.

Zayrlen had grown up knowing him as the older man who appeared often enough to become familiar but not trusted enough to ever be left alone with him.

Victor never complained about that in front of the child.

Mostly.

Zee still watched.

Always.

Years had not erased what Victor did.

They had only made murder less practical.

⸻

Zayrlen’s Witch Mana appeared naturally.

Not during a lesson.

During breakfast.

He wanted fruit.

The bowl was across the table.

Ner said:

“Ask.”

Zayrlen reached.

Nothing happened.

He frowned harder.

Crimson-and-Gold Mana sparked around his fingers.

The bowl launched across the table.

Zee caught it before it hit Victor in the face.

Silence.

Zayrlen stared.

Victor looked toward Zee.

Zee looked at the bowl.

Then at his son.

“Did you do that?”

Zayrlen smiled.

“Yes.”

Ner closed his eyes.

“That is not encouragement.”

Zee set the bowl down.

“I asked a question.”

“You sounded proud.”

“I am allowed to be proud.”

“He almost hit Victor.”

Victor said:

“I have survived worse.”

Zee looked at him.

“Unfortunately.”

Victor sighed.

Zayrlen laughed.

⸻

The magic became more frequent after that.

Crimson-and-Gold Witch Mana.

His own.

Not Ner’s exact Pure Gold.

Not Zee’s Crimson Witch expression.

Distinct.

Sometimes it lifted toys.

Sometimes it shut doors.

Sometimes it cracked glasses when his temper got ahead of him.

Once it turned every candle in the nursery Crimson-and-Gold because Zayrlen wanted “pretty fire.”

Zee stared at the room.

Ner stared at Zayrlen.

Zayrlen looked delighted.

Victor, standing at the doorway, said:

“He gets that from you.”

Zee turned.

“Leave.”

“I meant the flair.”

“Leave farther.”

⸻

Wolf instincts came differently.

Quietly at first.

Zayrlen could hear footsteps before ordinary children should.

He recognized family by scent.

He sometimes growled when annoyed.

Not consciously.

The first time he did it at Victor, Zee nearly smiled himself sick.

Victor noticed.

“You are enjoying this.”

“No.”

“You are.”

Zayrlen growled again.

Zee picked him up.

“My baby has excellent instincts.”

Victor stared.

Ner laughed from across the room.

⸻

Dragon traits appeared in flashes.

Heat when upset.

Temporary scales along his shoulders.

A strange weight behind his presence.

Strength wildly disproportionate to his size.

Once, at maybe four years old, Zayrlen pulled a heavy training chest across the floor because it was “in the way.”

Kael watched.

Then looked at Zee.

“He should not be able to do that.”

Zee looked at the chest.

Then at Zayrlen.

“Apparently he disagrees.”

The Crimson Demonic Dragon inside Zee sometimes reacted to his son.

Not alarm.

Recognition.

Blood.

Something old.

Something growing.

Zareth noticed too.

But never pushed.

Never called it awakening.

Never predicted when anything would happen.

It simply existed.

Waiting.

⸻

Zayrlen remained a child.

That mattered more than the power.

He scraped his knee running through the western courtyard and came directly to Zee like he had been mortally wounded.

Zee abandoned a council discussion instantly.

Rez watched him go.

Victor looked confused.

“It is a scraped knee.”

Zee turned while lifting Zayrlen.

“Correct.”

“He is not dying.”

“I know.”

“You left a council.”

“My son is bleeding.”

“One drop.”

Zee looked down.

There were technically three.

Victor gave up.

Ner arrived with a cloth.

“He is fine.”

Zee was already inspecting the knee.

“I know.”

Zayrlen sniffed.

“It hurts.”

Zee’s entire face softened.

“I know, baby.”

Victor looked away.

That word still did something uncomfortable to him.

Years later, it still would.

⸻

The first transformation happened in the rain.

Zayrlen was still young.

Old enough to run fast.

Young enough that he still slept better if one of his fathers stayed until he drifted off.

He had been playing in the lower gardens with Rhyven.

Not training.

Playing.

Rhyven had shifted into his Phoenix-Wolf form because Zayrlen begged.

The child chased him between the hedges.

Laughing.

Wet.

Mud on his clothes.

Zee watched from beneath a stone arch with Ner beside him.

Victor remained farther back.

Exactly where he had learned to remain.

Rhyven darted left.

Zayrlen followed.

Then slipped.

Not badly.

But the sudden movement triggered something.

Wolf instinct.

Adrenaline.

Excitement.

Frustration.

All at once.

Zayrlen hit the wet ground.

Crimson-and-Gold Mana burst outward.

Rhyven stopped.

Zee straightened.

Ner whispered:

“Zee.”

“I see it.”

The child pushed himself up.

Then froze.

His heartbeat changed.

Wolf pressure rolled across the garden.

Zayrlen looked at his hands.

“Dad?”

Fur appeared first.

Crimson.

Deep.

Then Gold streaked through it.

His body shifted rapidly.

Bones moving.

Spine changing.

Hands becoming paws.

Clothing tearing loose beneath the transformation.

Zayrlen cried out.

Zee moved one step forward.

Then stopped.

Not because he was afraid.

Because forcing contact during an uncontrolled first transformation could make panic worse.

Ner grabbed his arm.

“Let him finish.”

Zee looked like he hated that sentence.

Still—

he stayed.

For three seconds.

Then the transformation completed.

⸻

A Wolf stood in the rain.

Four-legged.

Crimson and Gold.

Not black.

No horns.

No wings.

No Dragon tail.

No scales.

No Phoenix fire.

Wolf.

True.

Beautiful.

The coat was deep Crimson with Gold running through it in natural streaks along the shoulders, chest, flanks, and legs.

Crimson-Gold eyes stared out from a face still unmistakably Zayrlen.

Young.

Powerful.

Confused.

He looked down at his paws.

Then immediately fell over.

Rhyven made a noise suspiciously close to laughter.

Zee looked at him.

Rhyven stopped.

Zayrlen tried standing.

Managed.

Took three steps.

Then every new Wolf sense hit him at once.

Rain.

Mud.

Grass.

Heartbeats.

Blood.

Magic.

People.

Castle stone.

Rhyven.

Ner.

Victor.

Guards.

Everything.

His ears flattened.

He backed up.

Whined.

Zee’s expression changed instantly.

“That is enough.”

He stepped into the rain.

Ner did not stop him.

⸻

Zayrlen saw him.

Or smelled him.

Probably both.

The Wolf froze.

Zee crouched.

No King.

No Abysscin.

No monster.

Just father.

“Hey.”

Zayrlen whined again.

“You are okay.”

The Wolf took one uncertain step.

Then another.

Then suddenly ran.

Straight at Zee.

Fast.

Far too fast for his size.

Zee caught him anyway.

The young Wolf crashed into his chest.

Zee landed on one knee in the mud.

Zayrlen shoved his head under Zee’s chin.

Zee wrapped both arms around the Wolf.

“There you are.”

The Wolf trembled.

Ner came closer.

Not touching yet.

“Can he change back?”

Zee looked at the Wolf.

“No idea.”

Victor almost said something.

Zee looked over.

Victor wisely did not.

⸻

Zayrlen tried.

Nothing.

He made a frustrated sound.

Tried harder.

Crimson-and-Gold Mana sparked.

Nothing changed.

Zee immediately realized the mistake.

“Stop.”

The Wolf looked at him.

“Do not force it.”

Zayrlen whined.

“I know.”

Zee stroked the fur between his ears.

“You do not have to fix anything right now.”

The Wolf settled slightly.

“Just breathe.”

Ner crouched beside them.

“You are still you.”

Zayrlen looked toward him.

Ner smiled.

“Very fluffy you.”

The Wolf sneezed.

Rhyven lost the battle and laughed.

Zayrlen turned and barked at him.

Zee smiled.

“Good.”

Ner stared.

“Do not encourage him.”

“He defended himself.”

“He barked.”

“Still.”

⸻

They stayed in the garden.

No ritual.

No command.

No forced shift.

Eventually Zayrlen stopped panicking.

He sniffed Zee.

Then Ner.

Then walked in a small circle around them.

Then discovered his tail.

That became a problem.

He spun after it.

Missed.

Tried again.

Zee watched with absolute seriousness.

Victor stared at him.

“You are smiling.”

“No.”

“You are.”

Zee ignored him.

Zayrlen caught his tail.

Fell over.

Ner laughed.

Zee picked him up again.

The Wolf was too large to carry comfortably.

Zee did it anyway.

“Do not start.”

Ner raised both hands.

“I said nothing.”

“You were going to.”

“Yes.”

⸻

By sunset, Zayrlen’s body finally relaxed.

The Wolf form released.

Crimson-and-Gold fur folded away.

Bones shifted back.

A small Human child ended up curled against Zee’s chest, exhausted and naked beneath the remains of a cloak.

Zee wrapped him immediately.

Checked his hands.

Arms.

Face.

Breathing.

Heartbeat.

Eyes.

Ner watched.

“He is fine.”

“I know.”

“Then why are you checking him like he fell from a tower?”

“He transformed into a Wolf.”

“Yes.”

“My baby transformed into a Wolf.”

Zayrlen opened one tired eye.

“Dad.”

Zee looked down instantly.

“What?”

“Did I do good?”

That finished him.

Zee’s expression softened completely.

“Yes.”

No hesitation.

“You did perfect.”

Ner smiled.

Victor looked away again.

Rhyven grinned.

Zayrlen smiled weakly.

Then fell asleep against Zee.

Zee carried him all the way back to the keep.

Of course he did.

His son had found his first true form.

Crimson and Gold.

Four-legged.

Wolf.

And Zee Darkheart—

First Natural Abysscin.

First Natural Tribrid.

King.

Monster.

Was unbearably proud of his baby.

⸻
