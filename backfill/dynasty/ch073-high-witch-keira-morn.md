Chapter Seventy-Three

High Witch Keira Morn

Keira Morn attacked Drakonis because she thought Zee would not arrive in time.

She was correct.

Zee did not.

Zareth handled her first.

By the time Zee and Ner reached the mountain kingdom, half of Keira’s strike force was already dead.

The other half wished they were.

Zareth had trapped them inside a volcanic pass.

Not with magic.

With geography.

One collapsed ridge behind.

Dragonfire ahead.

No escape.

Rez would have approved.

Zee landed at the lower path—

without wings.

He climbed.

Fast.

When he reached the pass, Zareth stood over a shattered siege engine.

Blood on his teeth.

Teal-and-Gold fire leaking between them.

The Link hit Zee immediately.

Anger.

Pride.

One image.

Keira.

Above.

Zee looked.

A woman in dark violet robes stood on a narrow rock shelf surrounded by twelve witches.

High Witch.

Powerful.

Calm.

She looked down.

“King Darkheart.”

Zee smiled.

“Everyone keeps making this easy.”

Keira’s expression tightened.

“I did not come for you.”

“I know.”

That surprised her.

Zee looked toward Zareth.

“You came for him.”

“Yes.”

Zareth growled.

Keira continued.

“Drakonis shields you.”

“No.”

“Vesper shields you.”

“No.”

“Your lover shields you.”

Ner’s face hardened.

Zee barely reacted.

Keira smiled.

“You think you stand alone?”

Zee looked around.

Zareth.

Ner.

Drakonis soldiers.

His people elsewhere.

“No.”

Then back at her.

“I think they stand with me.”

Difference.

Keira raised both hands.

The mountain lit.

Thousands of runes burned beneath the stone.

Ner’s Pure Gold surged immediately.

“That is not an attack.”

Zee looked at him.

“What?”

“It is a cage.”

The entire pass sealed.

Not around Zee.

Around Zareth.

Dragon suppression.

Designed for true Dragon physiology.

Zareth roared.

His wings locked.

Teal-and-Gold Mana collapsed inward.

He dropped to one knee.

Zee’s eyes changed.

Slits.

Keira smiled.

“There.”

“You wanted to see whether the bond weakened when the Dragon did.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because every structure has a load-bearing point.”

Zee’s face went cold.

“You think Zareth is mine.”

“I think he is your weakness.”

The Link filled with pure offense.

Even suppressed, Zareth wanted to kill her personally.

Zee understood.

Good.

“Then you miscalculated.”

Keira laughed.

“How?”

Zee looked at Zareth.

“Because this is his kingdom.”

Then stepped back.

Keira frowned.

Zee shouted:

“Zareth!”

The Crown Prince lifted his head.

The Link carried one thing.

Break it.

Not rescue.

Not instruction.

Trust.

Ner smiled.

Then went to work.

⸻

Pure Gold spread across the mountain runes.

Ner did not overpower them.

He studied.

“Seven anchors.”

Keira heard.

“Wrong.”

Ner frowned.

Then smiled.

“Eight.”

Keira’s face changed.

Good.

“One hidden in the lava channel.”

Zee moved.

Silent Witchcraft shattered two attacking spells without looking away from the pass.

Keira’s witches descended.

Zee took them.

Not all.

Drakonis soldiers joined.

The battle became ugly fast.

One Witch threw chain-lightning.

Zee raised his palm.

The arc stopped.

He twisted his wrist.

It bent around him and struck empty rock.

Another charged with a blade.

Zee stepped inside.

Pressure point.

Elbow.

Knife gone.

Unconscious.

Third tried compulsion through enchanted blood.

Zee laughed.

Actually laughed.

The foreign domination hit his mind.

His Vampire nature pushed back.

Crimson Adaptation reinforced resistance.

His predatory presence surged.

“Mine.”

The attacker froze.

Zee’s command crushed through hers.

She dropped to her knees.

Not dead.

Broken control.

Keira saw.

Fear entered her scent.

Good.

Zee smiled less.

That was how he became scarier.

Ner shouted:

“Anchor eight!”

Zee looked toward the lava channel.

Too far.

Zareth could not move.

Keira’s witches blocked the path.

Zee’s Dragon nature answered.

Not full form.

Never.

Crimson Demonic Dragon heat surged through his body.

Dark scales spread across his neck and arms.

He ran straight through a wall of fire.

Not immune.

Burned.

Healing started.

He reached the channel.

One hand plunged into molten-hot stone around the sigil.

Pain tore through him.

He closed his fist.

“Break.”

The anchor shattered.

Ner hit the other seven at once.

Pure Gold.

Precision.

The cage failed.

Zareth stood.

The mountain shook.

Keira finally understood.

“Oh.”

Zee smiled.

“Your problem.”

Zareth launched.

⸻

The Crown Prince did not need Zee to defeat Keira.

That mattered.

Zareth hit the rock shelf like a falling fortress.

Witches scattered.

Keira threw Dragon-binding chains.

Zareth tore through them.

One wrapped his foreleg.

He used it to pull her toward him.

Smart.

Keira cut the chain.

Too late.

Zareth’s tail struck the ledge.

Stone exploded.

She fell.

Zee caught her.

Not kindness.

He wanted answers.

Keira looked up at him from one hand around her throat.

“You saved me.”

“No.”

Zareth landed behind him.

The Link carried murderous patience.

Zee looked toward Keira.

“Council.”

She smiled through pain.

“You already killed two.”

“Three if you count Greyhaven’s leadership.”

“You think that ends anything?”

“No.”

Zee tightened his grip.

“Names.”

“You have them.”

“Father Caldus.”

Her heartbeat changed.

Fear.

There.

Zee noticed.

Interesting.

“You are afraid of him.”

Keira said nothing.

Ner approached.

Bloodsong humming faintly beneath his voice.

“Why?”

Keira stared at Zee.

“Because Caldus believes Mara was too merciful.”

Silence.

That was impressive.

Zee almost smiled.

“What does he want?”

“To remove the bloodlines entirely.”

“Which ones?”

Keira laughed weakly.

“All unstable convergence lines.”

Ner’s face hardened.

“Meaning?”

“Mixed blood.”

“Adaptive blood.”

“Unknown hybrids.”

“Dragon-Witch lines.”

“Bloodsong.”

“Tribrid.”

Zareth snarled.

Zee’s face became empty.

Not children specifically.

Entire lineages.

That was larger.

Still not one world alliance.

One man’s ideology.

Keira continued.

“He thinks the world survived because species remained separate.”

Zee looked at Ner.

Then Zareth.

Then thought of Aurelia.

Veyrath.

Mixed families at his fortress.

People born between categories.

“Where is he?”

Keira smiled.

“You will never find him.”

Zee smelled lie.

Not total.

She knew something.

“Where?”

Resistance.

Strong.

Zee compelled.

“Tell me.”

Keira’s eyes remained shut.

Blood wards tightened.

Zee pushed.

Voice.

Predatory pressure.

Blood dominance.

“Tell me.”

Her body shook.

Still resisted.

Powerful.

Good.

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

He stepped closer.

His fangs descended.

“Then I will taste the answer.”

Keira’s eyes opened.

Fear.

Not because feeding extracted memories.

It did not.

But blood analysis might reveal travel residue, recent feeding, magical environments, foreign substances.

Zee had become very good at reading what bodies carried.

Keira knew.

“Wait.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“Useful.”

She gasped:

“Salt cathedral.”

Ner frowned.

“Where?”

“Western salt flats.”

“Which kingdom?”

“None.”

“Caldus lives there?”

“No.”

“Then what?”

“Messages.”

Zee released some pressure.

A lead.

Enough.

Zareth’s Link pushed a question.

Kill?

Zee looked at Keira.

She attacked Drakonis.

Built a suppression cage around Zareth.

Participated in planned bloodline extermination.

No remorse.

Still dangerous.

Zee could kill her.

Wanted to.

Then Zareth lowered his head beside him.

His fight.

His kingdom.

His choice.

Zee released Keira.

She hit the ground.

Stared.

Zareth’s claw came down.

One strike.

Final.

Keira Morn died beneath the Crown Prince of Drakonis.

Zee did not object.

The kill had never belonged to him.

Good.

⸻

The surviving witches surrendered.

Zareth spared seven.

Executed three who had killed hatchery guards during the attack.

The rest went into Drakonis custody.

His judgment.

His kingdom.

Zee watched without interfering.

Later, on the highest ridge, Zee stood beside Zareth.

The Link carried quieter things now.

Pain from suppression.

Anger.

Satisfaction.

Then amusement at the fact Zee had nearly burned his hand off reaching the lava anchor.

Zee looked at him.

“You are welcome.”

Zareth sent an image of Zee screaming internally.

Rude.

Ner laughed behind them.

Zee turned.

“You heard nothing.”

“I know your face.”

“Everyone needs new material.”

Ner walked closer and kissed Zee once.

Quick.

Warm.

Then looked toward the sunset.

“One name left.”

Zee pulled the Covenant list from his coat.

Father Caldus.

The last principal name.

Not necessarily the last enemy.

Never that simple.

But the last of the Five still actively moving.

Zee folded the paper.

His pupils became human again.

Calm.

Thinking.

He had killed Mara before another strike.

Dren before another experiment.

Zareth had killed Keira after Drakonis was attacked.

Each line different.

Each choice moving.

Now Caldus intended something broader than one kingdom.

Bloodlines.

Entire categories of people.

Zee looked west.

“Salt cathedral.”

Ner nodded.

“When?”

Zee thought.

For once, not immediately.

“After we know what it is.”

Ner smiled faintly.

“Growth.”

Zee looked at him.

“No.”

Then his eyes shifted into slits.

“Preparation.”

Far below, Zareth roared across Drakonis.

The sound carried through mountain valleys.

Not fear.

Warning.

And somewhere beyond the western flats, a man called Father Caldus was preparing to erase bloodlines he believed should never have existed.

He had not attacked Zee directly yet.

That was becoming less relevant.

Because King Zee Darkheart had finally reached the point where evidence of intended extermination was enough.

He was no longer waiting for the first body.
