Chapter Sixty-Five

Before They Could Try Again

The blood-call came from a ruined monastery three valleys east of Darkheart.

No active kingdom claimed it.

No road led there.

That was probably why whoever chose it believed they were safe.

They were wrong.

Ner tracked the resonance.

Zee tracked Ner.

Not because Ner was helpless.

Because blood was being pulled from him in tiny amounts every few minutes.

Not enough to kill.

Enough to map.

Test.

Measure.

Someone wanted to understand what the Witch Bloodsong had become.

Ner hated that more than pain.

“They are sampling me.”

Rez looked over.

“From this distance?”

“Yes.”

“How?”

Ner’s jaw tightened.

“I do not know.”

Zee’s Wolf scent found no trail yet.

Vampire blood sense found Ner everywhere.

Too much.

Every drop taken from him left a faint path through the air like a pressure line.

Zee’s Crimson adaptations noticed the foreign influence woven around it.

Not magic he knew.

Not standard curse.

Not compulsion.

Something blood-based.

Ner stopped.

“There.”

The monastery sat below them.

Broken walls.

Collapsed tower.

No visible guards.

Rez studied the ridge.

“Too quiet.”

Zee already knew.

“Six outside.”

Ner looked over.

“You can hear them?”

“No.”

Wolf scent.

Ash.

Leather.

Old blood.

One hidden beneath stone.

Two downwind.

Three on the roof.

Zee’s assassin training filled the rest.

“Maybe more inside.”

Rez drew his sword.

“We go together.”

Zee looked at him.

“Yes.”

That surprised him.

Rez smiled.

“You are learning.”

“Do not ruin it.”

Ner flexed his fingers.

Pure Gold formed.

Then his voice dropped.

“I want whoever is doing this alive.”

Zee looked at him.

Ner’s eyes were hard.

“For now.”

Good.

His choice.

Not Zee’s.

⸻

The first guard disappeared without sound.

Zee took him from behind.

Pressure point.

Unconscious.

Second guard turned.

Ner whispered one note.

The man’s blood pressure dropped.

His knees hit stone.

Rez caught him before his head struck.

“Useful.”

Ner looked annoyed.

“I know.”

Third guard attacked from above.

Rez moved first.

Blue-White Mana froze the ledge.

The assassin slipped.

Zee caught him by the throat.

Not to save him.

To keep him quiet.

One squeeze.

Unconscious.

No deaths yet.

That mattered because Ner wanted answers.

The roof guards lasted less than a minute.

Then the monastery woke.

Wards lit.

Red.

Not Gold.

Blood magic.

Zee felt them immediately.

His Witch nature reacted.

One hand lifted.

“Break.”

The nearest ward cracked.

Not all.

Good.

Resistance meant structure.

Ner stepped closer.

“Wait.”

Zee stopped.

Ner studied the remaining lines.

“These are not trying to keep us out.”

Rez frowned.

“Then what?”

“Keep something in.”

Silence.

Zee smelled fear.

Inside.

Many heartbeats.

Not fighters.

Captives.

His face changed.

No longer an investigation.

Now personal morality had entered.

Ner heard it too.

“They have people.”

Rez drew his sword fully.

“Then fast.”

They entered.

⸻

The monastery basement held nineteen prisoners.

Human.

Witch.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Two mixed-bloods Zee did not know.

All restrained.

All alive.

Thin tubes carried blood from their arms into glass channels along the walls.

Ner stopped breathing.

Zee did not.

He became cold.

A woman in white robes stood at the center.

Not afraid.

That was interesting.

She held one hand over a bowl filled with dark red liquid.

Ner’s blood.

Zee knew immediately.

The woman smiled.

“There he is.”

Ner’s voice sharpened.

“My blood.”

“Yes.”

“Give it back.”

The woman laughed.

“No.”

Zee looked at her.

No obvious weapon.

Witch.

Experienced.

Four guards.

Two hidden behind the columns.

One exit below.

Rez caught Zee’s glance.

Understood.

He moved left.

Ner moved right.

The woman watched Zee.

“You are quieter than expected.”

Zee said nothing.

“You killed a God.”

Still nothing.

“You created him.”

She pointed toward Ner.

Ner’s face hardened.

“I am standing here.”

The woman ignored him.

Mistake.

Ner spoke one word.

“Look.”

Her blood answered before her head did.

She turned toward him.

Pure Gold flashed.

Bloodsong resonance struck the bowl.

Every drop of stolen blood rose.

Not into Ner.

Away from her.

The woman’s smile vanished.

Zee moved.

Vampire speed.

Knife at her throat.

No theatrics.

“Now you can talk.”

Her guards attacked.

Rez intercepted two.

Blue-White Mana redirected the third into a pillar.

Ner’s Gold sealed the fourth inside a narrow boundary.

The hidden guards emerged.

Zee did not look.

His Wolf instincts already knew.

One clawed hand swept backward.

Caught the first across the chest.

The second tried a poisoned needle.

Zee caught his wrist without turning.

Nanda Parbat.

“Wrong hand.”

Snap.

The guard screamed.

Zee looked at the woman.

“Who are you?”

She smiled despite the knife.

“Scholar.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“Bad answer.”

She swallowed.

“Maera Sol.”

“Why Ner?”

“Because he should not exist.”

Ner laughed once.

“No.”

Maera looked toward him.

“You died.”

“Yes.”

“And returned with Witch nature intact.”

“Yes.”

“Vampire traits.”

“Yes.”

“Voice resonance.”

Ner’s face went cold.

“Yes.”

“You are proof that—”

Zee pressed the blade closer.

“Careful.”

Maera looked toward him.

Then understood.

He was not protecting Ner from information.

He was warning her not to speak about Ner as an object.

Different.

She adjusted.

“You are proof that Zee’s blood can alter supernatural inheritance.”

Zee went still.

That was new.

Ner looked at him.

Rez did too.

Maera continued.

“We wanted to know whether it can happen again.”

Zee’s face emptied.

Behind her, nineteen captives bled into channels.

“Using them.”

“Yes.”

No hesitation.

No coercion.

No remorse.

Zee’s darkness made the decision before she finished breathing.

But Ner wanted answers.

So Zee waited.

Barely.

Ner stepped closer.

“What did you learn?”

Maera smiled.

“That your blood is impossible.”

“Try harder.”

“Your Witch nature remains independent.”

“Yes.”

“The Vampire blood did not suppress it.”

“Yes.”

“And there is a third resonance inside you.”

Ner’s eyes narrowed.

“What third?”

Maera opened her mouth.

Bloodsong hit first.

Ner’s voice changed.

Not loud.

Layered.

“Tell me.”

Maera stiffened.

Her blood answered.

Not compulsion exactly.

Siren-Vampire influence carried through circulation.

She fought it.

Strong mind.

Blood ward.

Ner pushed harder.

Pure Gold formed around the effect, containing it to one target.

Precise.

Controlled.

“Tell me.”

Maera’s eyes filled with rage.

“Siren.”

Silence.

Ner froze.

Zee’s pupils shifted human for one heartbeat.

“What?”

Maera gasped as the Bloodsong released.

“Dormant bloodline.”

“Old.”

“Very old.”

“Your mother’s line carried traces.”

Ner stared.

His face gave away nothing.

Zee saw the impact anyway.

Siren.

A name finally.

Not full explanation.

Enough.

Ner looked toward the prisoners.

“What did you do to them?”

“Testing compatibility.”

Rez’s expression hardened.

“How many died?”

Maera did not answer.

Zee smelled the lie before she spoke.

“None.”

Zee smiled.

Small.

Terrible.

“Wrong.”

Maera looked at him.

He could smell death on her clothing.

Old blood.

Many sources.

“Seventeen?”

Her face changed.

There.

Zee leaned closer.

“More.”

Ner whispered:

“Zee.”

Not stop.

He looked at Ner.

Ner’s eyes were Golden and dark-red.

His expression was hard.

“I am done asking.”

That was permission only for himself.

Not Zee.

Still, Zee understood.

Maera did too.

She panicked.

Finally.

“Wait.”

Zee’s smile disappeared.

“No.”

He killed her quickly.

Knife across the throat.

No feeding.

Not every death needed appetite.

Maera collapsed.

The guards froze.

Zee looked at them.

“Anyone here by force?”

Two immediately raised their hands.

Truth.

He smelled it.

The rest did not.

Rez moved.

“Take the two.”

Ner’s Gold sealed the others.

Zee looked toward the blood channels.

Then at the captives.

His hand lifted.

Every glass line shattered at once.

Silent Witchcraft.

Ner moved among the prisoners.

Bloodsong guided him to the weakest.

Rez organized release.

Zee stood in the center.

Not helping yet.

Thinking.

The old Zee would have stopped here.

Rescue.

Capture.

Return home.

Let law decide the rest.

The twenty-one-year-old King thought farther.

“Who funded this?”

One surviving guard looked away.

Zee heard his heartbeat jump.

There.

He walked over.

“Name.”

The guard resisted.

Zee’s predatory presence filled the room.

Vampire.

Not Witch.

“Name.”

No eye contact.

The guard clenched his teeth.

Zee’s will pressed through voice and blood recognition.

Resistance cracked.

“House Veylan.”

Rez looked over.

“Where?”

“South.”

“Distance?”

“Two days.”

Ner turned.

“Zee.”

He knew that face.

Not because Zee was angry.

Because he was planning.

“What?”

“You are not going tonight.”

Zee looked toward nineteen bleeding prisoners.

Then the blood channels.

Then Maera.

“No.”

Ner relaxed slightly.

Zee continued.

“I am going tomorrow.”

Rez sighed.

“There it is.”

Ner’s jaw tightened.

“What are you planning?”

Zee answered honestly.

“To make sure they cannot do this again.”

That could mean several things.

Everyone heard the darker one.

Rez approached.

“We know one facility.”

“Funded by one House.”

“According to one guard.”

“Yes.”

Zee looked at him.

“I will verify.”

That mattered.

No reckless massacre.

No immediate assumption.

But also no waiting for House Veylan to strike twice.

The philosophy was changing.

Ner saw it.

“You are going after them before they come again.”

Zee’s eyes shifted into slits.

“Yes.”

No apology.

No guilt.

No pretending he was merely responding.

He looked toward the rescued captives.

“They already came.”

That distinction satisfied him.

For now.

By dawn, all nineteen prisoners were alive.

The two coerced guards had been separated for questioning.

House Veylan’s name had been confirmed through ledgers, payments, and blood-sealed correspondence.

No guessing.

No rumor.

Evidence.

Rez read the final document.

Then looked at Zee.

“Well.”

Zee was cleaning one knife.

“What?”

“They funded it.”

“Yes.”

“Authorized the deaths.”

“Yes.”

“Ordered continued experimentation on Ner.”

Zee’s hand stopped.

Human pupils vanished.

Slits.

Rez noticed.

“So.”

Zee sheathed the knife.

“So now I go south.”

Ner stepped beside him.

“I am coming.”

Zee looked over.

Not to protect him.

Not to stop him.

His choice.

“Good.”

Rez sighed.

“I suppose I am coming too.”

Zee smiled.

“Of course.”

Far away, Zareth remained in Drakonis handling his own kingdom’s war.

The Link carried distance, heat, and fierce attention.

Not every problem required all four physically present.

The bond remained anyway.

Zee walked toward the monastery exit.

Once, enemies attacked his people and Zee waited until blood was spilled before hunting them.

Now someone had reached for Ner once.

Zee had found the hand.

Tomorrow he intended to find the arm attached to it.

And for the first time, the difference between protection and elimination had begun to feel very small.
