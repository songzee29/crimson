Chapter Sixty-Three

The King Is the Wolf

The northern attack lasted twenty-three minutes.

The people who ordered it expected hours.

That was their first mistake.

Their second was assuming King Zee Darkheart would command from behind walls.

The invading force crossed the northern forest before dawn.

Two hundred mercenaries.

Thirty witches.

A dozen Vampires.

Wolf trackers.

Three siege constructs.

No unified supernatural empire.

Just a neighboring lord who saw Victor dead, Darkheart succession changed, and rumors of Zee’s transformation as a narrow opportunity before the House stabilized.

Reasonable.

Wrong.

Zee listened to the report once.

Then looked at Lord Merek.

“How many entered the forest?”

“Two hundred forty-seven confirmed.”

“Civilians?”

“None.”

“Darkheart villages?”

“Evacuated.”

“Good.”

Rez entered the war chamber.

“They are expecting a defensive line here.”

He pointed to the river road.

Zee looked.

Then shook his head.

“No line.”

Merek frowned.

“Majesty?”

“They came through my forest.”

Rez understood first.

“Oh.”

Ner looked between them.

“What?”

Rez smiled faintly.

“He is going hunting.”

Zee’s crimson-and-gold eyes shifted.

Slits.

“Yes.”

⸻

The transformation hurt less than it had when he was younger.

Not because Wolf Zee had become weaker.

Because Zee had finally stopped treating transformation as secondary.

Bones shifted.

Spine changed.

Hands became paws.

Clothing fell around a body becoming something else entirely.

When the transformation finished, a massive four-legged wolf stood where the King had been.

No horns.

No scales.

No Dragon traits.

Wolf.

Complete.

Near-black fur carried subtle deep-crimson undertones beneath moonlight.

His crimson-and-gold eyes remained unmistakable.

Slit pupils now.

Predatory.

Rez crouched.

“Still strange.”

Wolf Zee stared.

Ner smiled.

“You say that every time.”

Rez looked offended.

“Because he usually has hands.”

Zee showed teeth.

Rez stood.

“Yes, yes. Terrifying.”

Zareth’s amusement rolled through the Link from above.

Zee ignored all of them.

Then ran.

⸻

The forest belonged to Zee before it belonged to Darkheart law.

Wolf instinct knew that.

Territory was not parchment.

It was scent.

Movement.

Trees.

Wind.

Who crossed.

Who bled.

Who feared.

Zee ran north without Vampire speed.

Did not need it.

Wolf endurance carried him mile after mile.

His paws barely sounded.

He found the invaders by smell first.

Oil.

Steel.

Sweat.

Vampire blood.

Spell ash.

Fear disguised beneath discipline.

He circled once.

Counted.

Not numerically.

Predator map.

Three groups.

One main column.

Two flanks.

Witches protected center.

Vampires scouting ahead.

Wolf trackers spreading wide.

They expected Darkheart patrols.

Not the King.

Zee followed one scout.

The Wolf mercenary stopped suddenly.

He smelled something.

Turned.

Nothing.

Zee had already moved downwind.

Countertracking.

Nanda Parbat inside Wolf form.

The mercenary continued.

Zee circled ahead.

Waited behind a fallen tree.

The scout passed.

Zee struck.

One bite.

Throat.

Silent.

No feeding.

Not every kill required blood.

He dragged the body beneath brush.

Continued.

Second scout disappeared.

Then third.

By the time the invading commander realized his perimeter had holes, Zee had already entered the column.

Panic began from the outside.

Not the center.

Good.

Fear spread inward.

One mercenary whispered:

“Something is following us.”

Another answered:

“Wolves?”

Zee moved through black trees.

One man saw crimson-and-gold eyes.

Only for a second.

Then nothing.

“The Blood Hunter.”

Wrong name.

Old name.

Still useful.

A Vampire commander hissed:

“He is not here.”

Zee attacked from behind.

The commander never finished the sentence.

Wolf jaws crushed the back of his knee.

He screamed.

Zee released.

Ran before weapons turned.

Did not kill him.

Not yet.

Fear needed a witness.

The commander shouted:

“FORM!”

Too late.

The forest erupted.

Darkheart Wolves came from the western slope.

Not helpless subjects.

Not waiting for Zee.

His people had learned.

They attacked the flank independently.

Darkheart witches hit from elevated ground.

Ner’s Pure Gold flashed far behind the enemy and sealed retreat paths without trapping civilians because there were none.

Rez had positioned all of it before Zee transformed.

King and future king thinking differently.

Zareth remained above, watching for siege constructs trying to break through forest cover.

No one rode him.

No one needed to.

The invasion collapsed into chaos.

That was when Zee transformed back.

Not in front of everyone.

Behind an overturned siege cart.

Human shape returned.

He pulled a black coat from a hidden pack placed earlier.

Nanda Parbat planning.

Then walked into the chaos.

One mercenary charged.

Zee barely moved his hand.

The man flew sideways through a tree.

Witch.

Another cast.

Zee flicked two fingers.

The spell reversed direction.

Hit the caster.

“Sleep.”

Three soldiers dropped.

Vampire Zee surfaced next.

He heard every heartbeat.

One false surrender.

Two genuine.

One man reaching for a poisoned knife behind his back.

Zee looked at him.

“Don’t.”

The man froze.

Not because he chose to.

Compulsion reached through voice and predatory presence.

No eye contact.

The mercenary struggled.

Zee felt resistance.

Pushed.

The hand opened.

Knife dropped.

The man stared.

Zee smiled faintly.

“Better.”

The invading commander finally found him.

Human.

Armored.

No supernatural blood.

Interesting.

He pushed through retreating fighters.

“Darkheart!”

Zee turned.

The commander raised a sword.

“You call this a kingdom?”

Zee looked around.

His Wolves holding territory.

Witches controlling lines.

Ner’s Gold cutting escape from reinforcement routes.

Rez directing prisoners away from active fighting.

Zareth tearing a siege construct apart with his claws rather than burning the forest.

“Yes.”

The commander attacked.

Zee could have killed him five different ways before the blade descended.

He chose assassin technique.

Step inside.

Wrist.

Elbow.

Shoulder.

Sword gone.

Knee buckled.

The commander hit dirt.

Zee placed one boot on his chest.

“Who hired you?”

The man spat.

Zee heard fear anyway.

“No one.”

Lie.

Zee smelled the stress shift.

“Again.”

The commander laughed.

“You think you scare me?”

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

Predatory presence rolled outward.

The commander stopped laughing.

Zee leaned slightly closer.

“No.”

A small smile.

“I know.”

The man’s heartbeat accelerated.

Zee listened.

Enjoyed it.

“Who?”

The commander resisted.

Zee could compel him.

Could torture him.

Could feed even though Human blood would offer only nourishment.

Instead Zee stepped back.

“Rez.”

Rez approached through smoke.

“What?”

Zee pointed.

“Alive.”

Rez looked surprised.

“Useful?”

“Yes.”

The commander smiled weakly.

Relief.

Zee saw.

Then added:

“Everyone above him too.”

The relief died.

Rez smiled.

There it was.

The difference.

At fifteen, Zee stopped attacks.

At twenty-one, he wanted the person who sent them.

⸻

The battle ended with ninety-three surrendering.

Forty-one escaped south.

The rest died or were captured fighting.

Zee did not chase the forty-one.

Not yet.

He wanted them running.

Stories traveled better on living feet.

By noon, one had already reached the nearest border settlement.

By evening, three kingdoms had heard the same version.

A near-black Wolf with crimson-and-gold eyes had hunted the invading scouts personally.

The Wolf disappeared.

King Zee appeared in the middle of the battle.

Some claimed they were two different beings.

Others knew better.

The story spread.

So did another.

The King did not always send Wolves after his enemies.

Sometimes the King was the Wolf.

Zee approved of that one.

⸻

The second attack came before midnight.

Not soldiers.

Poison.

A kitchen servant collapsed after opening a sealed wine case meant for the royal table.

Zee smelled it from two rooms away.

Metal.

Sweet root.

Demon residue.

Old poison.

Nanda Parbat recognition hit immediately.

He entered the dining hall.

“Do not touch anything.”

Ner stopped with one hand above the table.

Rez looked toward the servant.

“Alive?”

“Yes.”

Zee crossed to him.

Vampire blood analysis.

Human.

Poison entering fast.

Foreign demonic residue mixed in.

Zee had encountered enough similar compounds through years of feeding that his body recognized the pattern.

Not copied antidote.

Adapted tolerance.

Understanding.

He bit his own finger.

Let one drop of Crimson blood fall beside the poisoned wine.

The two reacted.

Black vapor rose.

Zee watched.

“Binding toxin.”

Ner frowned.

“For what?”

“Suppression first.”

Zee looked toward the servant.

“Then paralysis.”

Rez’s face darkened.

“Made for you.”

“Probably.”

Ner looked toward the sealed cases.

“How many?”

“Six.”

Zee barely lifted a hand.

The lids tore open.

Silent magic.

No speech.

Each bottle rose.

He rotated one finger.

Five shattered against the far wall.

The sixth stayed.

Evidence.

Rez looked at him.

“You kept one.”

“I am learning.”

“Terrifying.”

The servant convulsed.

Ner dropped beside him.

Pure Golden Mana stabilized the blood flow.

Then Ner hummed.

Low.

Bloodsong.

The poison trembled inside the servant’s veins.

Ner’s face tightened.

“I can feel where it is.”

Zee crouched.

“Can you move it?”

“Maybe.”

“Do not guess with him.”

Ner nodded.

Good.

Independent judgment.

Zee looked toward his own wrist.

Different choice now.

He opened the skin.

Not for turning.

Not desperation.

Blood.

Controlled.

“One drop.”

Ner looked up.

“You sure?”

“No.”

Good answer.

Zee placed a single drop against the servant’s tongue.

His Crimson blood met the toxin.

Not transforming the man.

Too little.

Instead the blood reacted violently to the poison because Zee’s biology had already adapted against similar suppression compounds.

The toxin began breaking apart.

Ner guided it.

Gold and Bloodsong contained the residue.

The servant breathed easier.

Rez looked between them.

“That was new.”

Zee’s expression sharpened.

“Yes.”

Not turning.

Not hybrid creation.

Not yet.

But his blood could apparently carry adaptation outward in trace amounts under certain conditions.

Dangerous.

Very dangerous.

Zee filed that away.

No experimentation tonight.

The servant opened his eyes.

“Majesty…”

Zee looked at him.

“Who delivered the wine?”

The man swallowed.

“Southern trader.”

“Name?”

He gave it.

Rez memorized.

Ner looked toward Zee.

“You are smiling again.”

Zee stood.

The first attack had come from the north.

Poison from the south.

Different people.

Different reasons.

Good.

That meant no convenient single mastermind.

It meant the world was testing him independently.

Zee looked toward the surviving bottle.

Then toward the darkness outside.

“They still think attacking me is the dangerous part.”

Rez sighed.

“What part are they missing?”

Zee’s crimson-and-gold eyes shifted into slits.

His voice stayed calm.

“What happens when I stop waiting.”

Ner watched him.

Not approval.

Not fear.

Recognition.

Zareth’s roar rolled across the night from the eastern towers.

The Link hit Zee with sudden urgency.

Drakonis.

Smoke.

Blood.

Attack.

Zee turned immediately.

There it was.

North border.

Poison.

Now Zareth.

Back to back.

Exactly as the world had chosen.

Zee grabbed his coat.

Rez said:

“You just finished a war.”

Zee looked at him.

“No.”

He smiled faintly.

“I finished an invitation.”

Then he headed east.

The world had begun sharpening the monster again.

This time, King Zee Darkheart knew exactly what it was doing.

And he was beginning to wonder how long he should keep letting it swing first.
