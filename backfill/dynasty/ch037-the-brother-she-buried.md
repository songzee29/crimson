Chapter Thirty-Seven

The Brother She Buried

They did not return to Vesper.

Theron refused.

Not because of Lucien.

Not because of House Vesper.

Because Khell was moving.

Every hour mattered.

They established temporary shelter inside an abandoned stone monastery two miles from Morcant’s estate.

Zareth guarded the courtyard.

Vale secured the roads.

Caldrin questioned Morcant.

Mara checked old trade maps.

Ner helped the rescued captives recover without using more Golden Mana than necessary.

Rez trained.

Of course he did.

Zee found him in the courtyard shortly after midnight.

Rez was practicing the same sword sequence over and over.

Shorter stance.

Lower center.

Adjusted reach.

Again.

Again.

Again.

Zee leaned against a column.

“You are tired.”

Rez did not stop.

“So are you.”

“I am not swinging a sword.”

“You should be.”

Zee frowned.

Rez completed the sequence.

Turned.

Started again.

His ten-year-old body moved more naturally now.

Not perfectly.

But better.

“You are getting fast.”

“I was already fast.”

“You fell off a chair yesterday.”

Rez stopped.

“That was one time.”

“Ner says twice.”

“Ner is a liar.”

Zee smiled.

Then looked toward the monastery door.

Theron slept inside.

Or tried to.

Aurelia had not left his side for hours.

Rez noticed Zee’s expression.

“You okay?”

“Yes.”

Rez stared.

Zee sighed.

“I do not know.”

“Better.”

Zee walked into the courtyard.

“I have an uncle.”

“Yes.”

“My mother has a brother.”

“Yes.”

“She thought he was dead.”

“Yes.”

“He thought she was dead.”

“Yes.”

Zee looked toward him.

“That is horrible.”

Rez lowered the sword.

“Yes.”

No joke.

No correction.

Just truth.

Zee sat on the courtyard wall.

“Can you imagine?”

Rez looked at him.

“Losing my brother?”

Zee nodded.

Rez’s expression changed.

He sat beside him.

“I prefer not to.”

Zee remembered the night his power changed Rez.

I can’t lose my best friend.

I wish I could stay with him.

He looked at Rez’s younger face.

“I still feel bad.”

Rez groaned.

“We are not doing this every night.”

“I changed seven years.”

“You changed my body.”

“Same thing.”

“No.”

Rez tapped Zee’s forehead.

“The years are here.”

Then tapped his own chest.

“And here.”

“You did not take them.”

Zee looked at him.

Rez continued.

“I remember being seven.”

“I remember watching you grow.”

“I remember every stupid thing you did.”

“There are many.”

“Exactly.”

Zee smiled faintly.

Rez nudged him.

“My body is ten.”

“I hate parts of that.”

“I also enjoy parts.”

Zee blinked.

“You do?”

Rez looked offended by his own admission.

“I heal from training faster.”

“That might be you being ten.”

“I know.”

“I can climb palace walls again without everyone screaming about royal dignity.”

“Lucien still screams.”

“That is different.”

Zee laughed.

Rez smiled.

Then:

“And I get more time.”

Zee stopped.

Rez looked toward his younger hands.

“I do not know what this means long-term.”

“No one does.”

“But if this body keeps aging normally…”

He looked at Zee.

“I have years ahead I had already passed once.”

Zee had not thought about it that way.

Rez continued.

“I am angry you changed me without meaning to.”

Zee flinched.

“I know.”

“I am also still here.”

Both could be true.

Zee leaned his shoulder against Rez’s.

“Older brother.”

Rez smiled.

“Still.”

“You are ten.”

“I will stab you.”

“Historically.”

Rez shoved him off the wall.

Zee landed laughing.

The sound reached the monastery.

Aurelia heard it.

So did Theron.

Her brother lay on a narrow bed inside what had once been a prayer room.

He smiled faintly.

“You have a strange child.”

Aurelia looked toward the courtyard.

“You have no idea.”

Theron studied her.

“I would like one.”

Aurelia looked back.

“One what?”

“An idea.”

She sighed.

“You need rest.”

“I spent six years in a cage.”

“I have rested enough.”

“You were tortured.”

“Yes.”

“Starved.”

“Yes.”

“Used to identify your own blood for traffickers.”

His expression darkened.

“Yes.”

Aurelia sat beside him.

“Then stop pretending you are fine.”

Theron smiled.

“You sound like Mother.”

Aurelia’s face changed.

The room went quiet.

Theron looked away.

“Sorry.”

“No.”

She touched his hand.

“Say her name if you want.”

“I cannot tonight.”

Aurelia nodded.

Some wounds could be survived only in pieces.

Theron looked toward the doorway.

“Tell me about Zee.”

Aurelia almost laughed.

“That could take several years.”

“I appear to have time.”

“Do not joke like that.”

“Why?”

“Because I buried you in my head once.”

Theron’s smile disappeared.

Aurelia continued.

“I will not do it again.”

He squeezed her hand.

“Then do not.”

Silence.

Finally Aurelia began.

“He was born during Blood Rain.”

Theron stared.

“I heard stories.”

“They were not enough.”

“Witch?”

“Yes.”

“Vampire?”

“Yes.”

“Wolf?”

“Yes.”

Theron slowly sat straighter.

“All naturally?”

“Yes.”

“From birth?”

“Yes.”

“And Dragon?”

“Dormant.”

Theron stared toward the courtyard.

“Four blood natures.”

“Yes.”

“Three awake at birth.”

“Yes.”

“Then the rumors—”

“The world calls him the First Natural Tribrid.”

Theron nodded slowly.

“That remains accurate.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“You understand?”

“I understand blood classification.”

“Not him.”

“No one understands him.”

That sounded correct enough.

Theron continued.

“His Dragon nature came from you.”

“Yes.”

“The Drakonis prince?”

“Catalyst.”

“Not source.”

“Correct.”

“Link?”

“Unknown.”

Theron leaned back.

“Good.”

Aurelia frowned.

“Good?”

“You said unknown.”

She smiled faintly.

“Zee appreciates that too.”

Then Theron’s expression darkened.

“Victor.”

Aurelia went still.

“What about him?”

“He is Zee’s father.”

“Yes.”

“How did he take the birth?”

Aurelia looked away.

Theron knew immediately.

“Aurelia.”

“He was afraid.”

“Of Zee?”

“Yes.”

“Of you?”

“Eventually.”

Theron’s jaw tightened.

“What did he do?”

She did not answer.

Theron read enough in her silence.

His Dragon nature surfaced.

Dark red scales appeared along his wrist.

“Aurelia.”

“He hurt Zee.”

“How?”

“Do not.”

“Aurelia—”

“He killed him.”

The room died into silence.

Theron stared.

“What?”

Aurelia’s voice became flat.

“Once.”

“My son returned.”

“And death never reached him again.”

Theron’s face went through disbelief, rage, confusion.

Aurelia stopped him before questions multiplied.

“No.”

“You do not need to understand tonight.”

“Neither do I.”

“Victor does not.”

“Zee does not fully.”

Theron stood too quickly.

His legs nearly failed.

Aurelia caught him.

“I am going to kill Victor.”

“No.”

Theron stared.

“He murdered your child.”

“And failed to keep him dead.”

“That is not a defense.”

“I did not say it was.”

Theron breathed hard.

Aurelia guided him back onto the bed.

“Victor’s story is not tonight’s enemy.”

Theron looked toward her.

“Khell is.”

“Yes.”

“Four Veyrath captives.”

“Yes.”

“Then Victor gets to continue breathing until later.”

Aurelia gave him a look.

Theron smiled weakly.

“I am compromising.”

“You have not changed.”

“No.”

Then his eyes drifted toward the courtyard again.

“Zee knows?”

“Not every detail.”

“But enough?”

“He knows Victor fears him.”

Theron shook his head.

“Children should not know that kind of father.”

Aurelia looked toward the moonlit doorway.

“Zee knows many things children should not.”

That truth hurt both of them.

⸻

Lady Morcant began talking before dawn.

Not because Caldrin tortured her.

He did not need to.

He placed Khell’s broken transfer contract on the table and told her that Damaris would assume she betrayed him if she remained silent.

Morcant lasted twenty minutes.

Khell had moved the remaining four Veyrath captives southeast.

Not toward another estate.

Toward a mobile auction.

A private sale conducted aboard three river vessels that traveled between neutral territories.

No permanent address.

No stable jurisdiction.

Buyers came to the boats.

The boats came to no one.

Mara knew them.

“The Red Passage.”

Vale frowned.

“Vampire route?”

“Originally.”

“Now?”

“Anyone with enough money.”

Aurelia stood over the map.

“Where?”

Morcant remained bound across the room.

“Lower Marrow River.”

“How long?”

“Day and a half.”

Zee stood beside Aurelia.

Rez.

Ner.

Theron, despite every order that he remain in bed.

Zareth watched through a broken monastery window.

Theron pointed toward the river.

“Khell will expect pursuit.”

Caldrin nodded.

“Yes.”

“So he will divide cargo.”

Mara looked at him.

“Not if the auction requires all four.”

“Why would it?”

Morcant smiled bitterly.

“Because one of them is worth more than the others.”

Aurelia turned.

“Who?”

Morcant looked at Theron.

“You should know.”

Theron’s face hardened.

“I do not.”

“She smells like you.”

Silence.

Theron slowly went pale.

Aurelia noticed.

“Who?”

He looked toward her.

“No.”

“Theron.”

“No.”

Zee watched his uncle.

Fear.

Real fear.

Aurelia stepped closer.

“Who?”

Theron whispered:

“Seraphine.”

Aurelia froze.

Rez looked toward Zee.

Ner stopped breathing for a heartbeat.

Zee asked quietly, “Who is Seraphine?”

Neither sibling answered immediately.

Then Aurelia said:

“Our sister.”

The world changed again.

Zee stared.

“You have a sister?”

Theron looked sick.

“She was eight when we separated.”

Aurelia’s eyes filled.

“I thought she died with the eastern nest.”

“So did I.”

Morcant watched them.

“She did not.”

Aurelia turned toward her so sharply that Witch Mana flashed around the room.

“You saw her?”

“Yes.”

“When?”

“Six years ago.”

“Alive?”

“Yes.”

“After that?”

“I bought her.”

Theron surged forward.

Vale caught him.

Aurelia did not move.

That was worse.

Morcant continued quickly.

“Khell refused to complete the sale.”

“Why?”

“Theron.”

Everyone looked toward him.

Morcant smiled without humor.

“He discovered they shared blood.”

“He realized she could identify Veyrath lines even more accurately.”

Theron looked physically ill.

Khell had kept siblings as tools without allowing them to know.

Aurelia stepped toward Morcant.

“Where is she now?”

“The Red Passage.”

“Certain?”

Morcant hesitated.

Aurelia’s eyes turned lethal.

“Yes.”

One word.

Enough.

Aurelia turned toward the map.

“We leave now.”

Theron stood.

“So do I.”

“No.”

His head snapped toward her.

“Aurelia.”

“You can barely stand.”

“My sister is alive.”

“She is my sister too.”

“Then you understand.”

“Yes.”

Aurelia faced him.

“Which is why I will not rescue Seraphine only to watch you die trying to reach her.”

Theron’s expression hardened.

Zee watched.

Then stepped between them.

Both adults looked down.

“I have an idea.”

Aurelia closed her eyes.

“That sentence has ruined many days.”

Zee ignored her.

“Theron comes.”

She opened her mouth.

“But not into the fight.”

Theron looked offended.

Zee continued.

“He knows Khell.”

“He can identify people.”

“He knows Seraphine’s scent.”

Theron nodded.

“But he stays with Vale until we know where she is.”

Vale frowned.

“I am apparently everyone’s keeper.”

Rez raised one hand.

“Welcome.”

Zee continued.

“Zareth scouts the boats from above.”

The Dragon Link sent approval.

“Ner finds magical cages.”

Ner nodded.

“Rez controls the deck.”

Rez smiled.

Aurelia looked toward him.

“What do you do?”

Zee grinned.

“Whatever goes wrong.”

Caldrin laughed.

“That may be the most accurate assignment.”

Aurelia stared at her son.

Ten.

Still ten.

Still young enough that she wanted to carry him away from every road.

Old enough that he had begun thinking in people rather than powers.

Roles.

Strengths.

Limits.

Not throwing himself at everything first.

Progress.

Dangerous progress.

She looked toward Theron.

Then the river.

Then Zee.

“Fine.”

His eyes widened.

“Really?”

“Do not make me regret it.”

Rez laughed.

“She says that to everyone now.”

Aurelia pointed toward him.

“You are still grounded.”

Rez stared.

“I am Crown Prince.”

“Physically ten.”

Rez looked betrayed.

Ner covered his mouth.

Theron watched all of them.

For the first time since leaving Morcant’s cage, something like life returned to his expression.

“You raised a strange family.”

Aurelia looked toward Zee.

Rez.

Ner.

Zareth.

Then her brother.

“Apparently.”

They reached the lower Marrow River by night.

Three ships moved beneath a moonless sky.

Black hulls.

No flags.

Red lanterns burning along their decks.

The Red Passage.

Zareth circled high above.

The Dragon Link brought Zee images.

Guards.

Chains.

Cages below deck.

Many heartbeats.

One felt different to Zareth only because Zee had given him Theron’s scent from a piece of cloth.

Related.

Veyrath.

Zee stood on the ridge overlooking the river.

Aurelia beside him.

Theron behind.

Mara pointed toward the center vessel.

“That is Khell’s.”

Rez adjusted his smaller sword.

Ner removed his gloves.

Pure Golden Mana flickered.

Vale drew steel.

Caldrin smiled.

Zee’s horns warmed.

Not instability.

Not loss of control.

Anticipation.

He breathed.

Focused.

Listened.

The heat remained his.

Aurelia noticed.

“You good?”

Zee looked at her.

“Truth?”

“Yes.”

“I am scared.”

Aurelia’s expression softened.

“Good.”

He frowned.

“Rez says that too.”

“Because fear is useful when it tells the truth.”

Zee looked toward the ships.

Someone his mother had mourned for years might be inside.

Seraphine Veyrath.

His aunt.

Another person Zee had never known existed.

Another piece of Aurelia’s life returning from the dead without actually returning from death at all.

No resurrection.

No rewritten history.

Only survival finally discovered.

Zee smiled faintly.

“Then I am very useful.”

Aurelia laughed once.

The center ship rang its auction bell.

Buyers began boarding.

Theron’s face hardened.

“They are selling her tonight.”

Zee looked toward Zareth overhead.

The Dragon Link tightened.

Ready.

Then toward Rez.

Ner.

Aurelia.

Vale.

Caldrin.

The hunted prince had spent ten years learning what it felt like when strangers came to take people from him.

Tonight, for the first time, Zee Darkheart would board a slave ship for someone he had never met simply because she belonged to someone he loved.

He drew his blade.

“Let’s go get my aunt.”
