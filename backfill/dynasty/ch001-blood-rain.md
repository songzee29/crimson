Chapter One

Blood Rain

Queen Aurelia Darkheart screamed as midnight approached.

The sound traveled through the birthing chamber, passed beneath iron-bound doors, and rolled through Castle Darkheart until guards stationed in distant towers heard the pain of their queen. Servants paused in torchlit corridors. Priests lowered their heads over unfinished prayers. Soldiers who had faced cavalry charges without flinching exchanged uneasy looks as the ancient fortress seemed to tighten around them.

Castle Darkheart had witnessed executions, coronations, betrayals, and wars older than the living memory of its kingdom. Its walls had endured siege fire and witchcraft. Its black towers had watched generations of rulers rise beneath the silver moon and descend into burial vaults beneath the throne.

Yet on this night, the castle felt afraid.

The wind had stopped hours earlier.

Black banners bearing the crowned serpent of House Darkheart hung motionless from the battlements. The surrounding forests had fallen silent. Even the wolves that prowled the northern hills had ceased their howling, leaving the darkness beyond the walls unnaturally empty.

Inside the royal chamber, the kingdom’s finest physicians and witches surrounded Aurelia’s bed.

Moisture covered the queen’s brow. Her dark hair clung to her face, and her fingers tightened around the sheets as another contraction tore through her body. She had endured a labor lasting nearly an entire day, but the child showed no sign of weakness.

If anything, the pressure within the chamber continued to increase.

King Victor Darkheart stood beside her.

He had removed his crown but not his sword. The weapon remained fastened at his side, an old habit born from years of border wars and palace betrayals. His black ceremonial armor had been replaced by a simple dark tunic, yet he looked no less imposing. He held Aurelia’s hand with both of his, his expression controlled even as fear tightened behind his eyes.

“Look at me,” he said.

Aurelia forced her eyes open.

Victor leaned closer. “You are not dying tonight.”

Despite her pain, she managed a weak laugh. “Was that an order?”

“Yes.”

Another contraction seized her before she could answer.

Aurelia cried out and crushed Victor’s fingers in her grip. He did not pull away.

The royal witches raised their hands.

Soft violet mana gathered around their palms. Threads of blue healing magic moved over Aurelia’s body, easing torn muscles and strengthening her heartbeat. The eldest among them murmured an old birthing prayer while the physicians prepared linen, silver instruments, and bowls of heated water.

Then the witch’s voice faltered.

The violet light around her hands flickered.

She frowned and reached deeper.

Nothing answered.

Her magic remained within her. She could feel it behind her ribs, woven through her blood and spirit as it had been since childhood.

Yet she could not grasp it.

It was as though she stood at the bottom of a dark sea, reaching toward a distant surface while an impossible weight pressed down from above.

The healing threads around Aurelia vanished.

A younger witch gasped.

“My spell—”

Her blue mana collapsed before it formed.

One after another, the witches lost control of their magic. Pale green wards dissolved from the walls. Protective symbols dimmed. The sacred flames in the bronze braziers narrowed until only thin points of light remained.

Victor released Aurelia’s hand and turned.

“What happened?”

The eldest witch tried again. Sweat formed across her brow as she strained for the power she knew was still there.

“I can feel my magic, Your Majesty.”

“Then use it.”

“I cannot reach it.”

Another witch stared at her trembling hands. “Something is drowning it.”

The chamber grew colder.

Aurelia gritted her teeth as the pressure inside her changed.

The child moved.

Not with the weak, desperate struggle of an infant fighting to enter the world.

With force.

A pulse rolled through the chamber.

Every candle went out.

Victor drew his sword before darkness had fully settled.

Steel rang through the room.

“Light.”

No spell answered.

A physician hurried toward the brazier with flint in hand, but before he reached it, crimson illumination seeped through the chamber.

It came from Aurelia.

No.

From within her.

A deep red glow passed beneath the skin of her stomach, pulsing in time with a heartbeat that did not belong to the queen.

Once.

Twice.

The third beat shook the castle.

Stone dust drifted from the ceiling.

Outside the chamber, guards stumbled as the floor moved beneath their boots. Horses screamed within the royal stables. Bells suspended in the western tower began ringing without hands or wind.

Aurelia stared down at the crimson light beneath her skin.

“Victor.”

He returned to her side, sword still in hand.

“I am here.”

“What is happening to our child?”

Victor had no answer.

Beyond Castle Darkheart, the first drop fell.

It struck the helmet of a sentry standing upon the northern wall.

The young soldier wiped the warm liquid from his cheek and looked at his fingers.

Red covered them.

He lifted his gaze toward the sky.

A second drop landed on the stone beside him.

Then another.

Within moments, blood began falling from the heavens.

It struck battlements and rooftops in heavy drops. It washed over the stone faces of ancient gargoyles. It soaked banners, armor, fields, and roads until the kingdom beneath Castle Darkheart appeared drowned beneath a crimson storm.

The sentry backed away from the wall.

“Sound the alarm.”

No one moved.

“Sound it!”

The warning horn cried across the fortress.

In the villages below, people rushed from their homes only to recoil beneath the bleeding sky. Mothers pulled children into doorways. Farmers abandoned livestock. Priests fell to their knees in flooded roads while blood streamed across their faces.

The storm did not remain over House Darkheart.

It spread.

Across the western kingdoms, blood struck palace windows and temple roofs. It ran down the marble towers of rival courts. It stained rivers and turned snowfields red. It fell upon battle camps where soldiers from enemy nations stopped killing one another long enough to stare upward.

No ruler understood it.

No priest could name it.

The ancient world watched the heavens bleed.

In the northern forests, the wolves went mad.

The first alpha collapsed mid-howl.

His bones shifted beneath his skin. His claws tore trenches through the frozen earth as his eyes darkened with a terror he could not comprehend. The other wolves backed away from him, whining, until the same madness seized them.

Pack law shattered.

Mates attacked one another.

Young wolves fled into the trees.

Alphas tore at their own kin as if every instinct within them had become tangled and violent. The madness passed from forest to forest, crossing borders no human army could traverse in a single night.

Some wolves ran toward Castle Darkheart.

Others fled from it.

None knew why.

Far beneath the eastern mountains, ancient vampires awakened.

Stone lids slid from coffins that had not opened for centuries. Dust-covered eyes stared into darkness. Deep within the blood of every vampire, an unfamiliar heartbeat sounded.

One beat.

Then another.

It was not their own.

It did not belong to prey.

It did not resemble the pulse of any witch, wolf, mortal, or vampire they had ever hunted.

A young vampire fell from his knees and clutched his chest.

“What is that?”

The elder beside him listened.

He had lived long enough to remember civilizations whose names no longer existed, yet no memory answered him.

The heartbeat passed through their blood one final time.

Then disappeared.

Across the world, vampires remained awake.

Listening.

Afraid.

Within Castle Darkheart, Aurelia screamed.

The Crimson Mana surrounding her burst outward.

Physicians were thrown against the walls. Witches collapsed to their knees. Victor drove his sword into the floor and used it to remain standing as the chamber filled with red light.

The pressure became unbearable.

The witches’ magic had not vanished.

It remained buried beneath the overwhelming presence entering the world.

Aurelia’s final cry ended abruptly.

Then came silence.

The infant was born.

The midwife caught him with shaking hands and nearly dropped him when his eyes opened.

They were crimson.

Not bloodshot.

Not reflecting the light.

Crimson from edge to edge.

Small fangs rested behind his lips. His tiny fingers opened and closed while Crimson Mana leaked naturally from his body in slow, living ribbons. Beneath his newborn skin, his heartbeat carried a predatory rhythm no human child should possess. A low sound rumbled in his chest—not quite a growl, but enough to make the nearest wolf-blooded guard outside the chamber recoil.

The child did not cry.

He looked at the world.

The Blood Rain stopped in midair.

For one suspended moment, every crimson drop hung between the sky and earth.

The wolves froze.

The vampires felt the impossible heartbeat settle.

The pressure inside the chamber eased.

The midwife placed the newborn against Aurelia’s chest.

Aurelia stared at him through tears.

“My son.”

The infant turned toward her voice.

Her hand trembled as she touched his cheek.

“Zee.”

The name left her softly.

“Zee Darkheart.”

Crimson Mana curled around Aurelia’s wrist but did not harm her. It felt warm against her skin, almost curious, before returning to the child.

Victor released his sword.

He stared at his son.

The fangs.

The crimson eyes.

The strange mana.

The low, instinctive sound within the child’s breathing.

Every sign should have driven him away.

Instead, Victor stepped forward.

Zee turned his head toward him.

The king’s expression changed.

For the first time that night, he was not looking at an omen.

He was looking at his child.

His heir.

Victor reached down and offered one finger.

Zee’s tiny hand closed around it.

The strength in the newborn’s grip surprised him.

A faint smile touched Victor’s face.

Then the air split.

Far beyond House Darkheart, inside a mountain kingdom built around rivers of molten stone, a crimson dragon egg cracked open.

Dragon attendants gathered around it.

The egg belonged to the royal clutch, guarded since the first sign of life appeared beneath its shell. Teal-and-gold fire burned within the ceremonial braziers while the Dragon King and Queen watched from the obsidian platform above.

The shell broke.

A newborn crimson hatchling pushed free.

His scales gleamed dark red beneath the cavern light. Small wings unfolded against his wet body. Teal-and-gold mana moved over him in intertwined currents, distinct colors flowing together without becoming one.

The Dragon Queen stepped forward.

The hatchling lifted his head.

Then vanished.

There was no spell.

No portal.

No burst of flame.

One moment, he stood among the fragments of his shell.

The next, the royal nest was empty.

The Dragon Queen’s roar shook the mountain.

At Castle Darkheart, the crimson hatchling appeared upon Aurelia’s bed.

The royal guards drew their weapons.

Witches raised hands, their magic still difficult to reach beneath the lingering pressure.

The hatchling ignored them.

His unsteady legs carried him across the blanket toward Zee.

Victor moved between the creature and his family.

“Stay back.”

The hatchling stopped.

Teal-and-gold mana shimmered faintly around his scales.

He did not attack.

He looked past Victor toward the newborn prince.

Zee stirred.

A simple feeling passed between the two infants.

Recognition.

Not knowledge.

Not memory.

Only the instinctive certainty that neither was alone.

The hatchling continued forward.

Victor tightened his grip on his sword but did not strike.

The tiny dragon reached Zee, circled once, and curled against the child’s side. Zee’s hand settled against the hatchling’s neck.

The tension inside both newborns eased.

Safe.

Aurelia looked from one child to the other.

“What is he?”

The eldest witch had recovered enough to stand.

She studied the hatchling’s crimson scales and strange mixed mana.

“I do not know.”

“Where did he come from?”

“I do not know that either.”

No one inside Castle Darkheart recognized him.

No one knew that a kingdom beyond the mountains had lost its newborn crown prince at the exact moment Zee entered the world.

The suspended blood resumed falling.

The pressure around Zee weakened until the royal witches could finally touch their power again. Violet light returned to the eldest witch’s hands. Another restored the ward around the chamber. A third began healing Aurelia’s injuries.

The younger witch stared at her own palms.

“It was never gone.”

The eldest looked toward Zee.

“No.”

“Then why could we not reach it?”

The elder had lived for more than a century.

She had no answer.

Before she could offer even a theory, the chamber doors opened.

A scout entered drenched in blood.

He dropped to one knee, chest heaving from the force of his run.

“My King.”

Victor turned.

“Report.”

“Messages have begun arriving from every border. The blood fell beyond our lands. The wolves have attacked settlements throughout the north. Foreign courts are demanding answers.”

“We have none.”

“They do not believe that.”

Victor’s face hardened.

The scout looked toward Zee.

Rumor had already outrun the truth.

Servants had seen the crimson light.

Guards had heard the newborn’s strange heartbeat.

Physicians had witnessed fangs in the mouth of a royal child born to an ancient Witch House. Beyond the walls, wolf-blooded soldiers claimed they felt something awaken inside them when Zee took his first breath.

The scout lowered his eyes.

“Several kingdoms are calling the child unnatural.”

Aurelia tightened her hold around Zee.

Victor stepped between the scout and the bed.

“He is the heir of House Darkheart.”

“My King, they are already sending riders.”

“For what purpose?”

“To learn what was born here.”

Victor looked toward the blood-streaked windows.

Across the ancient world, rulers were gathering witnesses. Witches argued over the moment their magic became unreachable. Vampires searched their memories for the source of the heartbeat. Hunters entered forests filled with maddened wolves and found entire packs moving toward the same distant kingdom.

No one understood Zee.

That did not prevent them from fearing him.

By dawn, the first royal messenger would leave a neighboring capital carrying a demand for the newborn’s death.

By sunrise, eleven more would follow.

The impossible had been born.

And the world had already begun to move against him.
