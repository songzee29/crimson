Chapter One Hundred

The Year the Sea Came Ashore

Zee turned twenty-five while standing knee-deep in seawater that should not have existed.

Darkheart was forty miles inland.

That made the situation irritating.

Nymeris found it worse.

She stood in the flooded market of Southhold with water curling around her boots.

“This is not mine.”

Zee looked at the water.

“It came from your direction.”

“The ocean is not one enormous bucket I personally manage.”

“That sounds irresponsible.”

Nymeris stared at him.

Ner laughed behind them.

“You two have known each other too long.”

It had been more than a year since Zee killed Kharos.

Long enough that Nymeris no longer arrived at Darkheart as a foreign Queen.

She still arrived with guards.

Still carried Nhalaris authority.

Still argued with Zee before most meals.

But Southhold knew her.

Darkheart children recognized her.

Nhalaris had begun recognizing Zee too.

Not all of them liked him.

That was fine.

Zee liked very few councils himself.

⸻

The flood came from beneath the earth.

Tessa discovered the first body.

Not drowned.

Drained.

A Vampire woman lay beside an underground spring with every drop of blood gone.

No bite marks.

No wounds.

Kael crouched beside her.

“What does that?”

Ner touched the water.

His Bloodsong recoiled.

“Something beneath us.”

Nymeris knelt immediately.

Her expression changed.

“What?”

Zee asked.

She looked toward the flooded street.

“Abyssal current.”

“You said it was not yours.”

“It is not.”

“Convenient.”

Nymeris ignored him.

“There are currents beneath the world older than Nhalaris.”

That got Zee’s attention.

“How old?”

“Old enough that my people built around some instead of disturbing them.”

Amara arrived from the northern road.

“Tracks.”

“What kind?”

She shook her head.

“Nothing I know.”

Good.

Zee smiled.

Nymeris noticed.

“Why are you happy?”

“New monster.”

“You have terrible instincts.”

“Usually effective.”

⸻

The thing emerged after sunset.

It came through the flooded well.

Long.

Pale.

Almost transparent.

No eyes.

Too many joints.

Its mouth opened across its chest.

Three Darkheart soldiers attacked first.

One died before Zee reached them.

The creature touched him.

His blood left his body through unbroken skin.

Tessa shouted:

“Do not let it touch you!”

Zee was already moving.

The creature struck.

He caught one limb.

Instant mistake.

Blood pulled toward his skin.

Vampire instinct screamed.

Crimson biology resisted.

His veins darkened.

He tore free.

“Annoying.”

Nymeris raised both hands.

Water slammed the creature against stone.

It twisted inside the pressure.

Not swimming.

Using water like flesh.

Nymeris’s eyes narrowed.

“It belongs to the Deep Below.”

Zee looked at her.

“That sounds dramatic.”

“It is trying to drink your kingdom.”

“Fair.”

The creature lunged.

Zee disappeared beneath it.

Vampire speed.

Claws opened its underside.

No blood.

Only black fluid.

Amara attacked from the flank in Wolf form.

Kael bound two limbs with Witchcraft.

Ner hit the creature with Bloodsong.

Nothing.

He frowned.

“No heartbeat.”

Tessa shouted from behind a shield line:

“Then stop trying to talk to it!”

Ner glared.

“I was not talking.”

“You were singing.”

“Different.”

The creature broke Kael’s chains.

Nymeris’s water caught it again.

Zee understood.

“Hold the upper half.”

Nymeris looked at him.

“Why?”

“Because I want the lower half.”

“That sentence was horrifying.”

“Hold it.”

She did.

Zee moved.

Silent Witchcraft compressed the stone beneath the creature.

Wolf instinct tracked every joint.

Dragon force entered his arms.

He caught two limbs.

Pulled.

The creature screamed.

Not from a mouth.

From the water.

Nymeris flinched.

“There.”

Zee saw it.

A black organ beneath the translucent body.

Not heart.

Anchor.

He drove his hand through the creature.

Closed his fist.

“Break.”

The anchor ruptured.

The thing collapsed.

Water hit the street all at once.

Zee stood dripping.

Nymeris stared at him.

“You put your hand inside it.”

“Yes.”

“Without knowing what it was.”

“Yes.”

“You are twenty-five now.”

Zee frowned.

“So?”

“I had hoped age would improve you.”

“It has.”

Ner walked past them.

“It has not.”

⸻

The flooding stopped before dawn.

But the damage remained.

Seven dead.

Thirty-one injured.

Two streets destroyed.

A section of Southhold evacuated.

Nymeris stayed.

Not because diplomacy required it.

Because Darkheart needed water engineers.

Nhalaris sent them.

Zee did not ask twice.

That mattered.

Months earlier, he would have tried solving everything himself.

Now he accepted help without pretending it made him smaller.

Nymeris noticed that too.

She said nothing.

Also progress.

⸻

The attack was not the only reason she stayed.

Three days later, Nhalaris received news.

A surface kingdom had begun dumping cursed iron into a coastal trench to poison an underwater trade route.

Nymeris read the report in Zee’s council chamber.

Her face went still.

Zee recognized the expression.

Queen.

Not woman.

Not friend.

Ruler.

“How many affected?”

“Two settlements.”

“Dead?”

“Twenty-three.”

Zee stood.

Nymeris looked at him.

“This is Nhalaris.”

“Yes.”

“You do not need to come.”

“I know.”

That answer stopped her.

Zee reached for his coat.

Ner smiled faintly.

Nymeris frowned.

“Why?”

Zee looked toward her.

“You stayed.”

Simple.

She understood.

⸻

They went together.

Not Darkheart saving Nhalaris.

Not Nhalaris needing rescue.

Two kingdoms responding because one had been harmed.

Nymeris led.

Zee followed her law beneath the coast.

Ner coordinated resonance through the poisoned current.

Zareth guarded the surface approach from the air.

Kael stayed in Darkheart.

Tessa stayed with Southhold.

Amara remained on the Safe Roads.

Nobody abandoned their own responsibilities simply because Zee moved.

The world had finally become too large for that.

Good.

The coastal kingdom responsible for the poisoning expected an underwater retaliation.

It did not expect Zee.

Its commander looked from Nymeris to him.

“This is not Darkheart territory.”

Zee smiled.

“I know.”

“Then why are you here?”

Nymeris answered first.

“Because you killed my people.”

Zee answered second.

“And she is mine.”

Silence.

Nymeris turned slowly.

Zee realized how that sounded.

He corrected immediately.

“Family.”

Nymeris kept staring.

Ner covered his mouth.

The enemy commander looked confused.

Zee sighed.

“Bad phrasing.”

Nymeris finally looked back toward the soldiers.

“We discuss that later.”

“Preferably never.”

⸻

The cursed-iron stores burned before sunrise.

The poison routes were destroyed.

The civilians working the docks were evacuated before the military warehouses collapsed.

The commander who ordered the dumping tried escaping by ship.

Nymeris sank the ship.

Zee caught the commander before he drowned.

The man looked relieved.

Then Zee handed him to Nymeris.

Relief disappeared.

Good.

Her kingdom.

Her judgment.

Zee did not take it from her.

That mattered more than killing the man himself.

⸻

When they returned to Darkheart, Nymeris found Zee on the western balcony.

“You called me family.”

Zee looked out over the city.

“Yes.”

“You corrected yourself very quickly.”

“It sounded possessive.”

“It did.”

“I hate ownership.”

“I noticed.”

Silence.

Nymeris stepped beside him.

“So?”

“So what?”

“Was it true?”

Zee looked at her.

She was not asking as Queen.

That made the answer easier.

“Yes.”

Nymeris looked away toward Darkheart.

Then nodded.

“Good.”

No speech.

No ceremony.

No promise.

Family did not always arrive through blood.

Sometimes it arrived through flooded streets, broken currents, arguments, and someone showing up because you stayed when their people needed you.

For the first time, Nymeris Thalryn was no longer merely the Queen beneath the water.

She was one of Zee’s people.

Not subject.

Not lover.

Not subordinate.

Family.

And Zee was becoming the same to her.
