Chapter Eleven

The Kingdom That Said Yes

House Vesper did not close its gates.

That alone made it different.

Every kingdom Aurelia had passed near since leaving Castle Darkheart had doubled its patrols. Some barred roads. Others searched wagons. Villages displayed bounty notices bearing Zee’s face, each drawing worse than the last.

House Vesper did the opposite.

Its border fires remained lit.

Its bridges remained open.

Travelers still crossed beneath the silver banners of the kingdom without being dragged from horses and questioned about a newborn child.

That did not mean Vesper was peaceful.

The outer walls were enormous.

Soldiers watched every road.

Witches stood beside archers.

War beasts slept beneath covered stone pens near the gates.

This was not softness.

It was confidence.

Caldrin noticed first.

“They know we are coming.”

Vale looked toward the distant towers.

“Yes.”

Aurelia adjusted Zee beneath her cloak.

“Then why have they not sent soldiers?”

The royal witch answered quietly.

“Perhaps they do not intend to stop us.”

Caldrin frowned.

“That would be new.”

The crimson hatchling lifted his head.

The Dragon Link carried curiosity.

Not fear.

That mattered.

They approached the first Vesper checkpoint.

Six soldiers waited.

No weapons drawn.

The officer at the center stepped forward.

“Queen Aurelia Darkheart?”

Vale’s hand moved toward his sword.

The officer noticed.

“You may keep it.”

Vale did.

Aurelia looked at the man.

“Who asks?”

“Captain Soren Vesper.”

“And what do you want?”

“To escort you.”

“Where?”

“To the capital.”

Caldrin laughed quietly.

“That sounds very much like arrest.”

Captain Soren looked at him.

“If this were an arrest, you would already be surrounded.”

Caldrin glanced toward the ridges.

Then smiled.

“We are surrounded.”

“Yes.”

Aurelia’s eyes narrowed.

“How many?”

“Enough.”

“Why have they not attacked?”

Soren looked toward the bundle in her arms.

“Because our king ordered them not to.”

The hatchling moved beneath the cloak.

Soren saw the crimson snout.

He did not reach for a weapon.

He did not kneel.

He simply stared for one second longer than polite.

Then looked back to Aurelia.

“You will be safe inside the capital.”

Aurelia’s expression became cold.

“I no longer believe anyone who says that.”

Soren nodded.

“That is reasonable.”

The answer surprised her.

He continued.

“Then do not believe me.”

“Believe the gates are open.”

“Believe no bounty hunter has crossed them alive.”

“Believe King Lucien has refused every demand to surrender you.”

“Beyond that, decide for yourself.”

Vale studied him.

“Why?”

Soren looked toward the road.

“That answer belongs to the king.”

Aurelia hesitated.

Then she looked down at Zee.

Three days of running.

Blood.

Monsters.

Mercenaries.

Gravehounds.

Her body could not keep this pace much longer.

Zee needed shelter.

Even temporary shelter.

She nodded.

“Take us to Lucien.”

⸻

The Vesper capital rose from a valley of white stone and dark rivers.

It was nothing like Castle Darkheart.

Darkheart had been built upward, black towers clawing at the sky.

Vesper spread outward.

Terraced walls followed the land instead of dominating it. Silver banners hung from towers shaped like open hands. Bridges connected districts divided by narrow rivers. Marketplaces remained crowded despite rumors of war.

People saw Aurelia’s escort.

They saw the guards.

They saw the queen beneath her hood.

Some recognized her.

Whispers began.

Then someone noticed the dragon hatchling.

A child pointed.

Aurelia tensed.

No one attacked.

No one ran.

People stared.

That was all.

For Zee, it was the first crowd that did not immediately become danger.

He watched from Aurelia’s arms.

Crimson eyes curious.

The hatchling looked too.

The Dragon Link carried something unfamiliar.

People.

Noise.

Smell.

Food.

Zee responded with hunger.

The hatchling chirped.

Aurelia almost laughed.

“You two have survived armies and monsters, and this is what interests you.”

Food.

The feeling returned.

Caldrin heard the chirp.

“I agree with the dragon.”

“No one asked you.”

“I am wounded.”

“You have mentioned.”

“Because no one appreciates me.”

Vale looked toward him.

“I appreciate silence.”

Caldrin sighed.

The tension eased.

Only slightly.

Then the palace gates opened.

King Lucien Vesper waited inside.

He wore no armor.

No crown either.

Only a dark blue coat embroidered with silver thread.

Beside him stood a boy.

Perhaps seven years old.

Dark-haired.

Sharp-eyed.

Blue-white mana moved faintly around his fingers before disappearing.

Prince Rez Vesper.

Aurelia stopped.

Lucien descended the palace steps alone.

No guards followed.

“Aurelia.”

She did not bow.

“Lucien.”

His eyes moved toward Zee.

Then the hatchling.

He took in both.

No fear entered his face.

Interest did.

Concern.

But not fear.

That alone nearly made Aurelia distrust him more.

“You know what happened?” she asked.

“Some of it.”

“How much?”

“Enough to know most reports are nonsense.”

Caldrin muttered, “Finally.”

Lucien continued.

“I know the sky bled.”

“I know wolves lost control.”

“I know vampires felt something in their blood.”

“I know witches briefly struggled to reach their own power.”

“I know your son has visible vampire traits, wolf blood, witch nature, and mana no one recognizes.”

Aurelia’s grip tightened.

Lucien saw it.

“I did not say I understand him.”

She relaxed slightly.

“Good.”

“I do not.”

He looked toward Zee.

“And I do not need to understand a child before deciding not to murder him.”

Silence.

Aurelia stared at him.

Behind Lucien, Rez watched Zee carefully.

Not with fear.

With fascination.

Lucien stepped aside.

“You may enter.”

Aurelia did not move.

“Why?”

“Because twelve kingdoms marched against a newborn.”

“That is not an answer.”

“No.”

Lucien looked toward the silver banners above the palace.

“Because House Vesper does not decide innocence by majority vote.”

Aurelia studied him.

“And if Zee becomes dangerous?”

Lucien answered without hesitation.

“Then we deal with what he does.”

“Not what frightened men imagine he might do.”

The words hit Aurelia harder than she expected.

For a moment, she could not speak.

Then Rez moved.

He stepped from behind his father.

Lucien looked down at him.

“Rez.”

“I know.”

The boy stopped several feet from Aurelia.

His attention remained on Zee.

The hatchling raised his head.

Teal-and-gold mana flickered.

Rez’s blue-white mana reacted instinctively.

Aurelia tensed.

Lucien lifted one hand.

“Rez.”

The mana vanished.

Rez looked annoyed.

“I was not attacking.”

“I know.”

The boy looked toward Aurelia.

“Can he hear me?”

Aurelia frowned.

“He is a newborn.”

Rez nodded.

“That was a stupid question.”

Caldrin coughed to hide a laugh.

Rez ignored him.

He looked at the hatchling.

“And that one?”

“He cannot speak either.”

Rez considered.

“Good.”

Aurelia raised an eyebrow.

“Why?”

“People talk too much.”

Lucien closed his eyes briefly.

“My son.”

“What?”

“Try hospitality.”

Rez looked back at Zee.

Then held out one finger.

He did not touch him.

He simply waited.

Zee stared.

The crimson hatchling watched.

The Dragon Link carried uncertainty.

New.

Zee moved.

His tiny hand reached out.

Aurelia almost pulled him back.

Then stopped.

Zee’s fingers closed around Rez’s.

Blue-white mana flickered.

Crimson Mana answered.

The two powers touched.

Neither consumed the other.

Neither changed.

They simply existed together for one brief moment.

Rez’s eyes widened.

Zee blinked.

The hatchling chirped.

Something quiet passed through the courtyard.

No prophecy.

No revelation.

No ancient certainty.

Only the beginning of a relationship no one yet understood.

Rez smiled.

“He is warm.”

Aurelia laughed.

A real laugh.

The first in days.

Then a horn sounded from the outer walls.

Everyone turned.

Captain Soren appeared at the courtyard entrance.

“My King.”

Lucien’s expression changed.

“What?”

“Riders at the eastern gate.”

“How many?”

“Three.”

“Banner?”

“None.”

Caldrin sighed.

“Assassins.”

Soren shook his head.

“Worse.”

Vale looked toward him.

“What is worse than assassins?”

Soren’s face hardened.

“They are carrying royal seals.”

Lucien looked toward Aurelia.

“Which kingdoms?”

“Twelve.”

The courtyard went silent.

The coalition had found her.

Lucien’s jaw tightened.

“What do they demand?”

Soren answered.

“Queen Aurelia and the Tribrid child.”

Rez still held Zee’s hand.

Lucien looked at his son.

Then at Aurelia.

Then toward the eastern walls.

“Tell them no.”

Soren nodded.

“They also say refusal will be considered an act of war.”

Lucien smiled without humor.

“Then they finally understand where we stand.”

He turned toward the palace.

“Raise the shields.”

The Vesper bells began ringing.

Soldiers moved.

Witches took their positions.

Blue-white ward fires ignited along the upper walls.

Aurelia stared at Lucien.

“You would risk your kingdom for him?”

Lucien looked back.

“No.”

Her expression changed.

Then he continued.

“I would risk my kingdom for the right to decide what kind of kingdom it is.”

Outside the city, twelve royal riders waited for an answer.

Inside, Zee Darkheart entered the first kingdom that had chosen not to fear him before knowing him.

And before the sun set, House Vesper would discover exactly what accepting the First Tribrid cost.
