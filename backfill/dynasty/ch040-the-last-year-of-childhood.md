Chapter Forty

The Last Year of Childhood

House Vesper did not celebrate the return of House Veyrath.

Not publicly.

Lucien refused.

Too many people had escaped the Red Passage.

Too many buyers had seen Aurelia.

Too many brokers had heard the Veyrath name spoken aloud.

Trying to pretend nothing had happened would not protect anyone.

But announcing seven rescued survivors to every crown in the known world would be worse.

So Vesper did what it had always done best.

It made room.

Quietly.

Seven members of Veyrath blood had entered the rescue.

Seven returned free.

No cages.

No contracts.

No owners.

Theron walked through Vesper’s gates beside Aurelia instead of behind chains.

Seraphine came two steps later, glaring at every tower as though personally deciding whether she approved of the kingdom.

The others followed beneath Vesper protection.

Some wanted names restored.

Others did not.

One refused to sleep indoors.

Another refused to allow anyone to close a door behind him.

One of the rescued women spent the first night sitting in a garden until sunrise because grass beneath bare feet was apparently something captivity had made precious.

No one tried to hurry them.

Freedom did not make people whole by sunset.

It only gave them the right to decide what came next.

Zee understood that better than he expected.

He stood on the eastern balcony three days after their return and watched Theron argue with a healer below.

“You are supposed to use the cane,” the healer said.

“I am using it.”

“You are carrying it.”

“That counts.”

“It absolutely does not.”

Theron looked toward Aurelia for support.

She was sitting beneath an old cedar with Seraphine.

She did not help.

“Use the cane.”

Theron looked betrayed.

“You used to like me.”

“I used to be eight.”

“Children have better judgment.”

Seraphine snorted.

“You spent half our childhood throwing him out of trees.”

Theron pointed toward her.

“She remembers.”

Aurelia smiled.

Zee watched that smile longer than he meant to.

Ner leaned against the balcony beside him.

“You are doing it again.”

Zee looked over.

“What?”

“Watching your mother to make sure she is still happy.”

Zee frowned.

“I am not.”

Ner raised one eyebrow.

At ten, Nereus Azrion had somehow mastered the exact look Aurelia used when Zee lied badly.

Zee hated that.

“She lost them.”

Ner looked toward the garden.

“She found them.”

“That is not the same.”

“No.”

Zee looked at him.

Ner understood.

Finding people who had survived did not erase the years they were gone.

It did not erase cages.

Did not erase House Veyrath breaking apart.

Did not erase Victor.

Did not erase anything.

It simply meant grief had been wrong about one thing.

Some of them had lived.

Zee rested his arms against the stone railing.

“I thought getting them back would fix her.”

Ner was quiet.

Then:

“That was stupid.”

Zee turned sharply.

Ner continued before he could argue.

“Not because you wanted it.”

“Because people are not broken doors.”

Zee frowned.

“You have been spending too much time with my mother.”

“She likes me.”

“She likes everyone more than me.”

“That is because you are difficult.”

Zee shoved him.

Ner shoved back.

Neither moved much.

Behind them came Rez’s voice.

“If you both fall off that balcony, I am not explaining it.”

Zee looked back.

Rez crossed the room carrying two wooden practice blades.

His current body had stopped looking strange to Zee weeks ago.

That surprised him more than the magic itself had.

Rez was simply Rez.

Ten.

Fast.

Annoying.

His sword balance had settled.

His stride belonged to him again.

He tossed one blade toward Zee.

Zee caught it.

“No.”

“Yes.”

“I am busy.”

“You are staring at your mother from a balcony.”

“That is busy.”

Rez pointed the second blade toward the courtyard.

“Training.”

Ner smiled.

“I will watch.”

Rez looked at him.

“You are training too.”

Ner’s smile vanished.

“That seems unnecessary.”

“You told me yesterday my left side was slow.”

“It is.”

“Prove yours is better.”

Ner sighed.

“This friendship is exhausting.”

Zee looked toward the courtyard below.

Aurelia noticed the three boys.

“Do not break anything.”

Rez shouted down, “That depends on Zee.”

Zee looked offended.

“You changed age because of him,” Seraphine called from beside Aurelia. “I have known the boy three days and already believe that.”

Zee stared.

Aurelia had told her.

Of course she had.

He looked toward Rez.

“You told her?”

Rez shrugged.

“She is family.”

Seraphine cupped her hands around her mouth.

“And it is funny.”

Zee’s crimson eyes narrowed.

Seraphine smiled sweetly.

Zee liked her.

That was becoming a problem.

Damaris Khell did not receive a dramatic execution.

That disappointed Caldrin.

He admitted it freely.

The decision belonged to the seven people his trade had harmed directly.

Aurelia refused to take that choice from them.

Khell was tried under Vesper law for trafficking, illegal imprisonment, blood trade, magical coercion, and the purchase and sale of living supernatural captives within routes protected by Vesper treaties.

Morcant was charged separately.

Neither became a mystery.

Neither revealed a hidden throne behind the world.

Khell had buyers.

Morcant had money.

They had both profited from people other kingdoms preferred not to notice.

That was enough evil without making it grander.

Khell was sentenced to permanent confinement beneath Vesper wards after the surviving captives rejected immediate execution.

Not mercy.

Control.

They wanted names.

Ledgers.

Routes.

Buyers.

People still missing.

Khell knew too much to die quickly.

Morcant’s estate was stripped of every captive holding, collection ledger, restraint design, and blood contract Vesper could identify.

Several kingdoms protested the seizure.

Lucien sent each protest back with copies of purchase records.

Most became suddenly quiet.

Mara was not pardoned.

That mattered too.

She had helped destroy Khell’s ownership structure.

She had helped free people.

She had also once carried people into cages.

Both truths remained.

Lucien offered her no clean absolution.

Instead, she agreed to testify, identify routes, locate former buyers, and help Vesper dismantle trafficking paths she had once used.

Theron hated her.

Seraphine hated her differently.

One of the other rescued Veyrath survivors refused to be in the same room with her.

Mara accepted all three responses.

Zee asked her once whether she expected forgiveness.

She shook her head.

“No.”

“Then why stay?”

“Because leaving would be easier.”

Zee thought about that.

Then nodded.

Sometimes doing better did not mean being rewarded for it.

Sometimes it simply meant refusing to keep being worse.

⸻

The first rumor reached Vesper nine days later.

House Veyrath lives.

The second changed it.

Aurelia Darkheart is Veyrath.

The third became nonsense.

Aurelia had raised a hidden army of Dragon-Witches beneath Vesper.

The fourth claimed Zee had hatched from a Dragon egg.

The fifth insisted Zareth was actually Zee’s brother.

Zee nearly choked when Rez read that one aloud.

“No.”

Ner took the parchment.

“It gets worse.”

“How?”

“It says Queen Aurelia laid both eggs.”

Zee stared.

Zareth’s amusement exploded through the Dragon Link.

“No.”

Rez was already laughing.

Seraphine took the rumor sheet.

She read it once.

Then looked toward Aurelia.

“Mother would have enjoyed this.”

Aurelia covered her face.

Theron laughed until the healer threatened him.

Rumors were spreading.

That part could not be stopped.

House Veyrath was no longer safely dead in the imagination of the world.

Some survivors wanted that.

Theron did.

He refused to live under another name.

Seraphine did not.

Not yet.

She wanted the right to exist before she wanted recognition.

The other survivors divided between them.

Aurelia did not force unity.

House Veyrath had survived destruction.

It did not need to survive by becoming another cage.

Some stayed in Vesper.

Some planned eventually to leave.

Some wanted to search for more blood.

Others wanted nothing to do with Veyrath politics ever again.

Family grew.

It did not become simple.

Zee liked that.

He was learning simple answers were usually suspicious.

⸻

Near the end of autumn, Aurelia found Zee alone in the old training yard.

He was sitting on the ground with one knee raised, staring at his hands.

No Mana.

No scales spreading.

No horns burning.

Just hands.

Aurelia sat beside him.

“You are quiet.”

“I can do that.”

“Rarely.”

Zee looked toward her.

She smiled.

He looked away again.

“What are you thinking?”

“Rez.”

Aurelia waited.

Zee continued.

“I keep thinking about what I did.”

She did not tell him to stop.

That helped.

“I do not want to do it again.”

“Then do not.”

He frowned.

“You say that like I know how.”

“No.”

Aurelia looked toward the empty yard.

“I say it because not understanding a power does not mean you should poke it until someone suffers.”

Zee’s mouth twitched.

“That sounded like Caldrin.”

“Then I am deeply ashamed.”

He laughed.

Then became serious.

“What if it happens anyway?”

“Then you take responsibility for what happens.”

He looked at her.

“Not blame for everything.”

“Responsibility for what is yours.”

She touched one of his hands.

“Those are different.”

Zee thought of Khell.

Mara.

Morcant.

The hunters.

All the people whose choices had once felt like something Zee should somehow have prevented simply by existing differently.

He had spent years believing danger around him belonged to him.

Now he knew better.

Not completely.

Enough to start.

Aurelia continued.

“Fear is not control.”

“I know.”

“No.”

She looked at him.

“You are beginning to.”

Zee frowned.

“Same thing.”

“It is not.”

He sighed dramatically.

Aurelia smiled.

“You tried suppressing every Dragon reaction after Rez changed.”

Zee looked down.

“They got worse.”

“Yes.”

“Then Rez told me to listen.”

“And?”

“I hate when Rez is right.”

Aurelia laughed.

Zee smiled.

Then touched one developing horn.

“Sometimes the heat means anger.”

“Sometimes danger.”

“Sometimes Zareth.”

“Sometimes you.”

“Sometimes nothing I understand yet.”

Aurelia nodded.

“And now?”

“Now I notice before I explode.”

“Usually.”

Zee glared.

“Mother.”

“Usually is progress.”

He leaned against her.

At ten, he still did that.

Still fit beneath her arm.

Still allowed himself to.

Aurelia wrapped him close.

Zee listened to her heartbeat.

Not because his vampire nature forced him.

Because it was hers.

Steady.

Alive.

One day the world would take things from him no power could restore.

He did not know that future yet.

But he had already learned enough not to mistake survival for control over everything.

Some losses could not be undone.

Some people could only be protected while they were here.

That knowledge did not make him calm.

It made him present.

“Mother?”

“Yes?”

“I am glad they lived.”

Aurelia closed her eyes.

“So am I.”

“It does not fix what happened.”

“No.”

“But it matters.”

“Yes.”

Zee nodded.

That was enough.

Winter came early.

By the first snowfall, Rez had stopped training as though his body were something borrowed.

It was simply his.

He ran the western wall beside Zee and Ner every morning.

He complained when they cheated.

They cheated anyway.

Ner’s Golden Mana had begun developing precision rather than merely power. He could form thin barriers instead of massive spheres, thread Mana through damaged wards, and strike hard enough to remind everyone that rare did not mean delicate.

Zareth spent increasing stretches in Drakonis.

That had been difficult at first.

For Zee.

Not Zareth.

The Dragon Crown Prince had obligations.

Family.

Training.

Politics.

A kingdom that expected to know the young prince who had spent much of his earliest life beside a Darkheart child.

Zee hated the first three-week absence.

He slept badly.

Checked the Dragon Link constantly.

Pretended he did not.

When Zareth returned, he brought no apology.

Only a stolen Drakonis ceremonial banner tied around one horn.

Zee laughed for ten minutes.

The Link had changed too.

Distance no longer made it disappear entirely.

It stretched.

Thinned.

Carried impressions rather than clarity.

But it endured.

Zareth had his own life.

Zee was learning that love did not require keeping someone within reach every second.

He disliked the lesson.

Still learned it.

⸻

On the final night of the year, the core four climbed onto the western roof.

It had become tradition without anyone deciding it was.

Zee sat near the edge.

Ner beside him.

Rez sprawled behind them.

Zareth occupied half the roof.

Snow gathered along his crimson scales and melted whenever Teal-and-Gold Mana warmed beneath them.

Below, Vesper celebrated quietly.

Not Zee.

Not Veyrath.

Not victory.

The year ending.

That felt better.

Rez threw a small piece of ice at Ner.

Ner caught it with Golden Mana.

Then sent it back twice as fast.

It struck Rez in the forehead.

Rez sat up.

“That was magic.”

“Yes.”

“We said no magic.”

“You said no magic.”

“That is what we means.”

Ner looked toward Zee.

“Does it?”

“No.”

Rez stared at both.

“You are terrible people.”

Zareth sent through the Link:

Agree.

Zee looked at him.

“You are included.”

The Dragon Prince immediately withdrew agreement.

Zee laughed.

Then silence settled naturally.

No danger.

No alarm.

No approaching hunter.

Just the four of them.

Zee looked over Vesper.

He remembered being carried into these walls as an infant while kingdoms wanted his blood.

Remembered cages.

Assassins.

Frost.

Dreams.

The Vault.

His mother becoming a Dragon beneath the moon.

Sevran stepping from her past.

Rez changing because Zee lost control.

The Red Passage.

Theron.

Seraphine.

He had spent childhood running because adults believed the safest direction was always away.

Sometimes they were right.

Sometimes running had kept him alive.

But Zee was not the child Aurelia once carried between kingdoms anymore.

He had chased Khell.

Walked into Morcant’s estate.

Helped free people he had never met.

Learned to stop before rage chose for him.

Learned that fear could warn instead of rule.

Learned that family made him vulnerable.

Learned that family also made him harder to isolate.

Ner nudged his shoulder.

“What?”

Zee realized he had been staring.

“Nothing.”

“You look weird.”

“That is rude.”

“You are smiling at the city.”

Rez sat up.

“He does that when he is thinking too much.”

Zee turned.

“You both know too much about me.”

Rez smiled.

“That seems like your problem.”

Zareth lowered his head beside them.

The Link carried warmth.

Home.

Not a word.

Not exactly.

But close.

Zee looked at the three of them.

Then toward the rooms below where Aurelia sat with Theron and Seraphine.

House Veyrath breathed again.

Vesper still stood.

Drakonis still claimed its prince.

Victor still lived in Castle Darkheart.

The world still hunted what it feared.

Nothing was solved forever.

Zee was glad.

He did not want his life finished at ten.

Snow fell harder.

He reached one hand toward it.

A flake landed against a Crimson scale and vanished.

For the first ten years of Zee Darkheart’s life, the world had tried to decide what kind of creature he would become before he was old enough to decide anything for himself.

Witch.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Sleeping Dragon.

Prince.

Monster.

Abomination.

Weapon.

Miracle.

Threat.

Zee looked at his hand.

Then closed it.

The world could keep its names.

Childhood ended without a crown.

Without a prophecy.

Without another enemy appearing at the gate.

It ended with a boy sitting beside the people who had survived it with him.

A boy who knew more about himself than before.

A boy whose enemies knew more about him too.

A boy no longer small enough to carry away every time danger came.

Zee Darkheart had survived childhood.

And childhood had not survived him unchanged.
