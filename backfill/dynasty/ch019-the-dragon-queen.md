Chapter Nineteen

The Dragon Queen

The dragons arrived at sunset.

Not an army.

A royal procession.

That distinction did little to comfort anyone standing beneath them.

Twelve dragons descended from the clouds in widening circles around the valley. Some carried emerald scales. Others were silver, bronze, obsidian, or deep blue. Their mana painted the air in different colors as they landed across the ridges.

No two felt the same.

At their center flew the largest.

Her scales were dark crimson threaded with black along the spine, and two enormous horns swept backward from her skull like a crown grown from bone. Heat rolled from her body despite the mountain cold.

Zareth stood beside Zee.

He did not hide.

He stared.

The Dragon Link filled suddenly with something he had never shown Zee before.

Warmth.

Not Aurelia.

Different.

Fire.

A heartbeat heard through a shell.

A scent from before sight.

Mother.

Zee looked toward the descending dragon.

Aurelia understood nothing of the image.

But she understood Zareth.

His body had gone completely still.

The Dragon Queen landed.

The valley shook.

For several seconds, she remained in her true form.

Her eyes fixed upon Zareth.

Then they closed.

When she opened them again, something raw had replaced royal power.

Grief ending.

She shifted.

Crimson scales became dark skin. Wings folded into nothing. The massive dragon became a tall woman dressed in black and crimson armor, her long hair streaked faintly red beneath the setting sun.

No crown rested upon her head.

She did not need one.

Vaelor immediately knelt.

“My Queen.”

Every dragon along the ridges lowered its head.

Aurelia did not.

Neither did Zee.

Zareth stared.

The Dragon Queen walked forward.

No guard accompanied her.

No weapon was drawn.

She stopped several steps from the hatchling.

“Zareth.”

The sound of his name came differently from her.

Not title.

Not command.

Love.

Zareth made a small noise.

Then moved.

For the first time since his birth, he left Zee willingly.

He ran.

His movements were clumsy from excitement, his half-grown wings spreading uselessly as he crossed the snow.

The Dragon Queen dropped to her knees.

Zareth crashed into her.

She held him.

The valley disappeared for her.

Her hands moved over his wings.

His horns.

His face.

Checking.

Counting.

Confirming.

Alive.

Whole.

Real.

“You impossible little beast.”

Her voice broke.

Zareth buried his face against her.

The Dragon Link flooded Zee with warmth so powerful it confused him.

Mother.

Home.

Love.

Zee watched.

Aurelia looked down at him.

Somewhere between the two children, Zee understood enough to be calm.

Zareth was safe.

Then the Dragon Queen stood.

Still holding her son.

Zareth immediately looked toward Zee.

The distance was only several feet.

Too much.

Unease entered the Link.

Zee shifted in Aurelia’s arms.

The queen noticed.

“So that is him.”

Aurelia’s expression hardened.

“That is Zee.”

The Dragon Queen studied the infant.

Not with reverence.

Not hatred.

Assessment.

Her eyes moved over his crimson gaze, visible fangs, and the soft Crimson Mana gathering beneath his skin.

“The child who stole my son.”

Zareth growled.

The queen looked down.

The sound surprised her.

Aurelia stepped forward.

“My son stole nothing.”

“He vanished from a sealed royal chamber at the exact moment yours entered the world.”

“And Zee was a newborn.”

“So was Zareth.”

“Exactly.”

The queen stared.

Aurelia continued.

“If you came here expecting me to explain what happened, you traveled for nothing.”

“You do not know?”

“No.”

“Your witches?”

“No.”

“Your king?”

Aurelia’s face changed.

“No.”

The answer carried enough history that the Dragon Queen did not press.

She looked at Zee again.

“Then why does my son react as if separation from yours is injury?”

“We do not know.”

“Why can they sense one another?”

“We do not know.”

“Why did Zareth cross kingdoms to appear beside him?”

“We do not know.”

The Dragon Queen’s eyes narrowed.

“You say that easily.”

“I learned it painfully.”

Silence.

Something passed between the two queens.

Different kingdoms.

Different blood.

The same exhaustion with people turning uncertainty into violence.

The Dragon Queen looked down at Zareth.

“My son returns home tonight.”

Zareth immediately twisted in her arms.

The Dragon Link tightened.

No.

Zee reacted.

Aurelia felt his body tense.

“Do not.”

The queen looked toward her.

“He is Crown Prince of Drakonis.”

“He is also terrified.”

“He will calm.”

“Perhaps.”

Aurelia stepped closer.

“But what if he does not?”

The Dragon Queen’s face hardened.

“He belongs with his family.”

Aurelia looked at Zareth.

“He found family here too.”

Vaelor shifted behind his queen.

That sounded dangerously close to challenge.

The Dragon Queen heard it differently.

She looked at Zee.

“He has known my son for months.”

“From his first breath.”

“And you allowed this bond to continue?”

Aurelia almost laughed.

“How exactly would you suggest I stop two newborns from feeling something none of us understand?”

The Dragon Queen looked toward Vaelor.

He wisely remained silent.

Zareth struggled again.

She placed him on the ground.

He immediately ran back to Zee.

The little dragon climbed halfway into Aurelia’s lap and pressed his body against Zee’s.

Relief flooded the Link.

Together.

The Dragon Queen watched.

No one spoke.

Then she approached.

Aurelia did not retreat.

The queen crouched until her eyes were level with Zee’s.

Zee stared at her.

Crimson.

Calm.

Curious.

Her draconic eyes narrowed.

“You are very small for something that has frightened half the world.”

Zee grabbed one of the dark braids hanging beside her face.

Caldrin made a choking sound behind his hand.

The Dragon Queen froze.

Zee pulled.

Aurelia closed her eyes.

“Zee.”

The queen stared at the tiny fist wrapped around her hair.

Then Zareth chirped.

Something almost like amusement entered the Dragon Link.

Zee smiled.

The Dragon Queen slowly removed her braid from his hand.

“He has no manners.”

“He is three months old.”

“That is not an excuse in Drakonis.”

Aurelia raised an eyebrow.

The queen almost smiled.

Almost.

Then her expression became serious again.

“There is a larger problem.”

“There always is.”

“My court knows Zareth lives.”

Aurelia glanced toward the dragons surrounding the valley.

“I assumed.”

“Not all of them are pleased with where we found him.”

Vale stepped closer.

“What does that mean?”

The Dragon Queen stood.

“It means some believe your child took him.”

Aurelia’s jaw tightened.

“Based on what?”

“Exactly what everyone else uses.”

The queen looked toward Zee.

“Fear.”

She continued.

“Some of my lords consider this bond an insult to the Crown.”

“Others believe Zareth should be removed from Zee immediately.”

“One faction has already demanded the Tribrid be brought to Drakonis for judgment.”

Aurelia’s expression became murderous.

“He is an infant.”

“Yes.”

“So was my son when twelve kingdoms marched to kill him.”

“I know.”

“Do you?”

The Dragon Queen’s gaze sharpened.

“I have spent months believing my child dead or stolen.”

The valley went silent.

“I know exactly what fear can make a parent imagine.”

That stopped Aurelia.

The queen looked toward Zareth.

“And I will not let frightened dragons turn imagination into fact.”

Something eased inside Aurelia.

Not trust.

Respect, perhaps.

Small.

Fragile.

Real.

“What will you tell them?”

“The truth.”

The queen’s mouth hardened.

“That Prince Zareth lives.”

“That he appears healthy.”

“That his connection to Zee Darkheart is real.”

“That we do not understand it.”

“And that no dragon acts against the Darkheart child without my authority.”

Vaelor looked toward her.

“My Queen.”

She turned.

“Do you object?”

“No.”

He hesitated.

“But some will.”

“I am counting on it.”

Caldrin muttered, “I like her.”

Vale looked at him.

“You like anyone likely to start a civil war.”

“It makes people interesting.”

The Dragon Queen heard.

“Who is that?”

Aurelia sighed.

“A problem.”

“Excellent.”

Caldrin looked offended.

⸻

Night settled over the valley beneath dragon wings.

For the first time, the campfires burned openly.

No hunter would approach unnoticed now.

That did not make Aurelia relax.

She sat beside the fire while Zee slept beneath blankets, Zareth curled around him.

The Dragon Queen sat opposite her.

Neither had guards close enough to hear.

For a long while, they said nothing.

Then the queen spoke.

“Victor Darkheart killed him.”

Aurelia’s eyes lifted.

The fire cracked between them.

“Who told you?”

“Rumors.”

“Then you do not know.”

“No.”

The queen studied her.

“But your face does.”

Aurelia looked toward Zee.

She did not describe it.

She would not.

“He hurt our son.”

The queen understood the boundary.

“And Zee survived.”

Aurelia’s voice became cold.

“Zee lives.”

Nothing more.

The Dragon Queen nodded.

“Different dragons believe different versions.”

“Let them.”

“One claims Zareth restored him.”

Aurelia almost laughed.

“He was barely large enough to stand.”

“Another claims you performed forbidden witchcraft.”

“I was too busy screaming.”

The queen’s eyes softened.

“Another believes Victor never truly succeeded.”

Aurelia looked toward the flames.

“I wish that were true.”

Silence.

The Dragon Queen did not ask for details.

That mattered.

Instead, she said, “My people will keep wondering.”

“They may wonder forever.”

“You have accepted that?”

“No.”

Aurelia touched Zee’s blanket.

“I simply stopped believing everyone deserves an answer.”

The Dragon Queen considered that.

Then nodded.

“Good.”

Far above them, dragons circled.

One roar rolled across the mountains.

Another answered.

The queen looked upward.

“News will travel by morning.”

“What news?”

“That Drakonis refused the Twelve Crowns.”

Aurelia looked sharply at her.

“You are taking a side?”

“No.”

The queen’s expression hardened.

“I am rejecting theirs.”

The distinction sounded familiar.

Lucien.

Sovereignty.

Judgment based on action.

Not fear.

The Dragon Queen continued.

“If the Twelve Crowns believe they may cross our skies hunting a child traveling beside my heir, they will learn otherwise.”

Aurelia looked toward Zareth.

“You would protect Zee?”

“For Zareth.”

There was no softness in the answer.

Aurelia respected it more because of that.

“And if Zareth someday leaves him?”

“Then my obligation changes.”

“Fair.”

The queen looked toward Zee.

“But perhaps by then I will have another reason.”

Aurelia smiled faintly.

“Perhaps.”

⸻

By morning, the compromise had been decided.

Zareth would not be forcibly separated from Zee.

Not yet.

The Dragon Queen refused to risk damaging her son for the sake of satisfying frightened nobles.

He would remain with Zee while both were infants.

Drakonis would send representatives regularly.

Zareth would learn his kingdom.

His blood.

His family.

And when he became old enough to choose with more than instinct, the question would be asked again.

No spell bound the agreement.

No contract.

Two mothers simply gave their word.

It was enough.

For now.

Before leaving, the Dragon Queen approached Zareth.

She pressed her forehead against his.

He chirped.

Then she looked toward Zee.

“Do not get my son killed.”

Aurelia’s expression sharpened.

The queen raised one hand.

“I know.”

Zee could not die.

But Zareth could.

So could Aurelia.

Rez.

Lucien.

Vale.

Caldrin.

Everyone around him.

The danger surrounding Zee had never been only Zee.

Aurelia understood that better than anyone.

The Dragon Queen shifted.

Her human form erupted into crimson scales and enormous wings.

The other dragons followed.

Vaelor changed last.

Before taking flight, he looked toward Zareth.

“Your Highness.”

Zareth bit his tail.

Vaelor roared.

Caldrin laughed so hard he nearly fell.

Then the dragons rose.

The sky disappeared beneath wings.

Zareth chased them several steps before stopping.

The Dragon Link carried conflicting feelings.

Home.

Mother.

Sad.

Then he looked back at Zee.

Together.

He returned.

⸻

The news reached the Twelve Crowns four days later.

Not through spies.

Through dragons.

A royal proclamation was delivered to every major court:

PRINCE ZARETH DRAKONIS LIVES.

HE REMAINS UNDER THE PROTECTION OF THE DRAGON CROWN.

ANY ATTACK THAT ENDANGERS THE PRINCE WILL BE ANSWERED BY DRAKONIS.

Zee’s name never appeared.

It did not need to.

Every ruler understood.

Killing the Tribrid had become more complicated.

Not impossible in their minds.

Complicated.

Some kings withdrew their hunters.

Others increased the bounty.

Several vampire courts became more interested.

Wolf kingdoms argued over whether dragons had chosen a side.

Witch factions accused Drakonis of protecting an abomination.

House Vesper gained breathing room as attention shifted north.

And inside Castle Darkheart, King Victor received the proclamation in silence.

He read it twice.

Then looked toward the empty nursery that had once belonged to his son.

The world was gathering around Zee.

Vesper.

Dragons.

Aurelia.

Every new bond made Victor more afraid.

Because the prophecy had never said Zee would conquer alone.

Victor folded the parchment.

“An abomination,” he whispered.

The word felt easier than son.

So he used it again.

“The world will regret protecting that abomination.”

Yet he gave no order to kill Zee.

He had learned that lesson once.

Once was enough.

⸻

Spring came slowly to the crownless valley.

By the time the last heavy snow began melting, Zee could pull himself upright by gripping Zareth’s wing.

Zareth hated this.

Zee found that hilarious.

The first time Zee managed three unsteady steps without falling, Aurelia cried.

The fourth step ended with him landing face-first against the dragon.

Zareth hissed.

Zee grabbed his horn.

They rolled into the grass together.

The Dragon Link filled with outrage.

Then laughter.

Something inside Aurelia loosened.

The world still hunted him.

The black-robed observers still watched from places no one had found.

Kings still argued over his death.

Vampires still listened.

Wolves still followed instincts they did not understand.

Dragon nobles now debated whether his existence threatened their crown prince.

None of that disappeared.

But Zee was growing anyway.

That was the victory.

Not safety.

Growth.

Every month the world failed to stop him became another month in which Zee learned its sounds, its scents, its dangers, and its love.

And somewhere far beyond the valley, hunters began changing their contracts.

The oldest notices asked for proof of Zee Darkheart’s death.

The newest ones asked different questions.

Where does he sleep?

Who does he love?

Who does he follow?

Who can be taken from him?

The hunt was beginning to understand something far more dangerous than immortality.

If the world could not end Zee Darkheart…

it could still teach him what loss felt like.
