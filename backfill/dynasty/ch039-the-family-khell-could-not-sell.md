Chapter Thirty-Nine

The Family Khell Could Not Sell

The Red Passage stopped being an auction within thirty seconds.

It became evacuation.

Buyers fled toward the lifeboats.

Guards abandoned posts.

Someone screamed that Drakonis had declared war.

Someone else screamed that Zee Darkheart was attacking.

Neither was true.

No one waited for corrections.

Zareth tore open the upper deck with careful violence.

Careful for a Dragon still meant half the roof disappeared into the river.

The Dragon Link carried intense concentration.

No fire. Breaking only. Good?

Zee looked upward.

“Good!”

Aurelia stared at him.

“You are encouraging him.”

“He followed instructions!”

The entire vessel tilted.

Aurelia grabbed Zee before he slid into a cage.

“Barely!”

Rez and Caldrin fought the remaining guards near the corridor.

Rez’s smaller body moved differently now.

Five days earlier he would have hated that.

Tonight he used it.

A guard swung high, expecting a teenager’s reach.

Rez went under him.

Blue-White Mana flashed around the man’s ankle.

He fell.

Rez stepped onto the weapon hand.

“You really should stop looking at me like I am ten.”

The guard stared.

Rez paused.

“Physically.”

He kicked the sword away.

Caldrin smiled.

“Needs work.”

“I am busy!”

Ner stood before the cage runes.

Golden Mana spread across the floor.

Not touching the locks.

Reading the connections.

“Zee!”

“What?”

“They go into the hull.”

Zee looked.

Ner pointed.

“The cages are tied to the ship structure.”

Aurelia understood.

“If the ship breaks apart—”

“The restraints tighten.”

Zee looked toward the ceiling.

“Zareth!”

The Dragon stopped instantly.

Wood creaked.

The ship settled.

Zee sent:

Stop breaking.

Zareth answered with deep confusion.

You said break.

“I changed my mind.”

The Link carried outrage.

Rez shouted from the corridor.

“This is why battle plans exist!”

Zee ignored him.

Ner’s Golden Mana moved deeper.

“There is one central seal.”

“Where?”

“Below us.”

Where Khell had escaped.

Of course.

Aurelia looked toward the four captives.

Then Zee.

“No.”

Zee stared.

“I did not say anything.”

“You are going after him.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

“We need the seal.”

“Caldrin goes.”

Caldrin looked toward the corridor.

“Gladly.”

Zee shook his head.

“Ner can find it.”

Ner looked at Aurelia.

“I can.”

Aurelia’s expression hardened.

Ner continued.

“And Zee can sense Khell.”

Zee nodded.

“Heartbeat.”

Rez walked back into the chamber.

“I go too.”

Aurelia looked at his bloodied sleeve.

“You are injured.”

“Scratch.”

“Your body is ten.”

“My memory is still seventeen.”

“Your blood is currently coming out of you.”

Rez looked down.

“Minor complication.”

Seraphine gripped the bars.

“Aurelia.”

Everyone turned.

“Go.”

Aurelia stared.

“You just found me.”

“And I spent years waiting for someone to choose rescue over revenge.”

Seraphine looked toward the floor.

“If that seal controls these cages, go.”

Aurelia closed her eyes.

Then opened them.

“Vale stays here.”

He nodded.

“Zareth stays above and keeps the ship stable.”

The Dragon Link delivered a feeling of intense dissatisfaction.

“Tell him.”

Zee did.

Zareth answered:

Boring.

Zee almost smiled.

Aurelia looked at Zee, Ner, Rez, and Caldrin.

“Together.”

No argument.

They descended.

⸻

Khell’s lower chamber was beneath the waterline.

The air smelled of river mud, ink, blood, and magic.

Ner’s Golden Mana led them through two narrow corridors.

Zee listened.

Khell’s heartbeat ahead.

Fast.

Another heartbeat.

Then another.

“Two guards.”

Rez drew his sword.

His smaller hand fit the new hilt properly now.

“Finally.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“That enthusiasm worries me.”

“It should.”

They turned the corner.

The first guard attacked.

Rez took him.

Blue-White Mana shifted the man’s footing.

Caldrin disarmed the second.

Aurelia hit both with binding magic.

Ten seconds.

Done.

Zee looked impressed.

Rez smiled.

“Experience.”

Ner added, “And short legs.”

Rez glared.

Khell stood inside the final chamber.

Behind him was a metal pillar covered in red runes.

The central seal.

He held a knife.

Not against himself.

Against a boy.

One of the missing Veyrath captives.

Zee froze.

The boy looked perhaps fourteen.

Thin.

Dark red scales covered one cheek.

Khell held the blade beneath his jaw.

“Stop.”

Aurelia did.

Ner’s Golden Mana dimmed.

Rez lowered his sword slightly.

Caldrin did not.

Khell looked toward Aurelia.

“You destroyed years of work.”

“You purchased people.”

“I preserved bloodlines everyone else wanted extinct.”

Aurelia’s face became cold.

“In cages.”

“Alive.”

The captive boy whispered, “Do not listen.”

Khell tightened the knife.

Blood appeared.

Zee heard the boy’s heartbeat accelerate.

His horns heated.

Rez saw.

“Control.”

Zee breathed.

Khell’s gaze moved toward him.

“You are the one, then.”

Zee frowned.

“The one what?”

“The rumors.”

“Natural Witch.”

“Vampire.”

“Wolf.”

His eyes moved toward Zee’s horns.

“And something else.”

Zee did not answer.

Khell smiled.

“I have spent my life buying rare blood.”

“You are the first thing I have ever seen whose price I cannot calculate.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“I am not for sale.”

“Everything is.”

Ner’s Golden Mana flashed.

“No.”

Khell looked toward him.

Then laughed softly.

“Two impossibilities in the same room.”

Rez stepped sideways.

Khell moved the knife.

“Do not.”

Rez stopped.

Aurelia’s voice remained calm.

“What do you want?”

“A way out.”

“No.”

“Then the boy dies.”

Zee’s face changed.

Khell looked at him.

“There.”

“That is the part of you people should fear.”

Zee stared.

“What?”

“You care.”

Khell smiled.

“That can always be priced.”

Zee’s Mana surged.

Aurelia looked sharply at him.

“Zee.”

He stopped himself.

Barely.

Khell saw.

Smiled wider.

He had found leverage.

Or thought he had.

The captive boy looked at Zee.

Then at Ner.

Then at Rez.

His eyes shifted toward the metal pillar.

Small.

Quick.

Ner saw.

The boy was telling them something.

Not with magic.

With his eyes.

Pillar.

Lower rune.

Ner followed.

One red symbol pulsed out of sequence.

He understood.

The boy had been watching Khell use the seal.

Ner slowly lowered his hands.

Khell relaxed slightly.

Mistake.

Rez moved first.

Not toward Khell.

Toward the ceiling.

Blue-White Mana struck a pipe above him.

It burst.

River water exploded downward.

Khell flinched.

The blade moved one inch away from the boy’s throat.

Enough.

Zee crossed the distance.

Vampire speed.

One hand caught Khell’s wrist.

The other grabbed the boy’s coat.

Zee pulled them apart.

Khell slashed.

The knife cut across Zee’s forearm.

Blood.

Pain.

Crimson scales spread around the wound.

Zee did not stop.

He threw Khell into the wall.

Caldrin pinned him before he could rise.

Aurelia caught the Veyrath boy.

Ner ran toward the pillar.

“Lower rune!”

The rescued boy shouted, “Twist it!”

Ner grabbed the rune with Golden Mana.

It resisted.

He frowned.

“This is not a lock.”

Aurelia looked over.

“What?”

“It is a contract.”

Khell laughed from the floor.

“Exactly.”

Blood contract.

Not Zee’s blood.

Khell’s.

Ner’s expression hardened.

“Then we need him.”

Caldrin smiled.

“Excellent.”

Khell stopped laughing.

Rez walked over.

“Open it.”

“No.”

Caldrin adjusted the knife against his shoulder.

Khell smiled through pain.

“You will not kill me while those cages depend on me.”

Aurelia looked toward the Veyrath boy.

“Does he need to be alive?”

The boy nodded.

“Yes.”

Khell smiled.

Aurelia continued.

“Conscious?”

The boy hesitated.

“I do not know.”

Khell’s smile vanished.

Caldrin looked delighted.

“Worth testing?”

Zee stepped forward.

“No.”

Everyone looked at him.

He stared at Khell.

“We are not guessing with people upstairs.”

Aurelia’s expression softened slightly.

Good.

Zee had learned.

Khell looked toward him.

“Then you lose.”

Zee shook his head.

“No.”

He looked at Ner.

“You said contract.”

“Yes.”

“Contracts have rules.”

Ner’s eyes sharpened.

“Yes.”

“Can you see them?”

“Some.”

“Find the part that says what Khell has to do.”

Ner approached the pillar.

Pure Golden Mana spread slowly over the runes.

Lines appeared.

Layers.

Conditions.

Ownership.

Payment.

Transfer.

Release.

Ner’s face twisted with disgust.

“He built people into trade terms.”

Aurelia’s jaw tightened.

“Can you break it?”

“Maybe.”

Zee immediately said, “Do not guess.”

Ner looked at him.

Then smiled faintly.

“Learning.”

“I hate you.”

“No, you do not.”

Ner kept reading.

Rez watched the corridor.

Caldrin watched Khell.

Aurelia held the rescued boy.

Zee listened to every heartbeat.

Then Ner stopped.

“There.”

A single line glowed Gold.

“What?” Rez asked.

Ner smiled.

“Khell does not have to voluntarily release them.”

Khell’s face changed.

Ner looked toward him.

“The contract says release occurs if ownership transfers.”

Silence.

Caldrin began laughing.

Khell struggled.

“No.”

Ner continued.

“If Khell no longer owns the cages…”

Aurelia understood.

“…the restraints open.”

Zee looked at Khell.

“Then sell them.”

Khell stared.

“To whom?”

Zee smiled.

“Me.”

Aurelia’s head snapped toward him.

“No.”

“Mother.”

“We are not purchasing our family.”

“I know.”

He looked at Ner.

“Can a trade be one coin?”

Ner examined the contract.

“Yes.”

Khell laughed bitterly.

“You think I will agree?”

Rez stepped forward.

“You already said everything has a price.”

Khell’s face hardened.

Caldrin pulled a copper coin from his pocket.

“Inflation has been cruel.”

Zee took it.

He held the coin toward Khell.

“One copper.”

“No.”

Zee looked toward Ner.

“Does he need to say yes?”

Ner studied the runes.

“No.”

Khell froze.

Ner smiled.

“He authorized standing transfers through registered brokers.”

Everyone slowly looked at Mara’s absence.

Then remembered.

Mara.

Former registered broker.

Still on the upper deck.

Rez grinned.

“Oh, that is beautiful.”

Aurelia almost smiled.

Zee ran.

⸻

Mara stared at the copper coin.

“You want me to what?”

“Transfer the cages.”

“To Zee?”

“Yes.”

“From Khell?”

“Yes.”

“Without Khell signing?”

Ner pointed toward the contract.

“You are still registered.”

Mara stared at the Golden copy of the relevant condition.

Then began laughing.

Not politely.

Not quietly.

Years of fear broke inside it.

“Khell wrote that clause.”

“Why?” Rez asked.

“So brokers could move stock when he was not present.”

Aurelia’s face hardened at the word.

Mara corrected herself.

“People.”

Then looked toward Zee.

“One copper?”

Zee held it out.

Mara took the coin.

“No.”

Zee frowned.

“What?”

She turned toward the nearest river window.

Then threw it into the water.

Zee stared.

Mara looked at him.

“No one buys them.”

Ner smiled.

“Then transfer ownership to no one.”

Mara placed her hand against the contract seal.

The red runes ignited.

Khell screamed below deck.

Every cage upstairs opened.

Iron doors released simultaneously.

Restraints fell.

Seraphine collapsed into Aurelia’s arms.

The other Veyrath survivors emerged.

No owners.

No buyers.

No cages.

Free.

Mara looked at the dead contract light fading beneath her hand.

Her voice broke.

“That is better.”

Zee nodded.

“Yes.”

Much better.

⸻

Khell was dragged onto the deck in chains.

The irony was not subtle.

Rez enjoyed it.

Ner enjoyed it more.

Caldrin enjoyed it most.

Zareth landed beside the ship, wings beating hard enough to keep himself above the river rather than placing his full weight on the damaged deck.

Seraphine saw him.

Then looked at Zee.

Then Aurelia.

“You have a Drakonis prince following your child.”

Zee frowned.

“He does not follow me.”

Zareth sent:

Lie.

Zee looked toward him.

“Not helping.”

Seraphine smiled weakly.

For the first time in years, perhaps.

Theron arrived aboard a smaller skiff with Vale.

The moment he saw Seraphine, everything stopped.

Seraphine stared.

Theron stared back.

“No.”

He climbed onto the deck before Vale could stop him.

Nearly fell.

Recovered.

“Seraphine.”

Her face crumpled.

“Theron?”

He reached her.

She hit him.

Hard.

Theron stumbled.

Zee stared.

Rez nodded.

“Sibling reunion.”

Seraphine hit him again.

“You left me!”

“I thought you died!”

“I was eight!”

“I know!”

“You were supposed to find me!”

“I tried!”

Then she threw both arms around him.

Theron held her.

Aurelia joined them.

Three siblings.

All believed dead by at least one of the others.

All alive.

No magic brought them back.

No power rewrote their losses.

They had survived separately.

That made the reunion hurt differently.

It was not undoing history.

It was discovering history had continued somewhere else.

Zee stood beside Rez and Ner.

Zareth hovered nearby.

Ner wiped one eye.

Rez noticed.

“Crying?”

“No.”

“You are.”

“River wind.”

“We are barely moving.”

Ner looked at him.

“I will throw you overboard.”

Zee smiled.

Then Aurelia looked toward him.

She extended one hand.

Zee walked over.

She pulled him into the embrace.

Theron laughed.

Seraphine looked down at Zee’s horns.

Then his face.

“So this is your son.”

Aurelia nodded.

Seraphine touched one Crimson scale near Zee’s temple.

He allowed it.

“He feels like Veyrath.”

“Partly.”

“And something else.”

“Several something elses.”

Zee frowned.

Everyone laughed.

He looked offended.

That made them laugh harder.

For one brief moment, the Red Passage stopped being a place where people had been priced.

It became the place where a family found one another again.

Then Zee looked toward Khell.

The broker sat bound against the mast.

Alive.

Furious.

Defeated.

Not the end of the world.

Not the mastermind behind every hunt.

Just one man who had made a fortune from suffering.

Aurelia followed Zee’s gaze.

“What do we do with him?” Zee asked.

Aurelia looked toward the freed captives.

“Not my decision alone.”

Theron nodded.

Seraphine’s expression became cold.

“Good.”

Khell looked toward Zee.

“You think this changes anything?”

Zee met his eyes.

“It changed them.”

He pointed toward the freed Veyrath.

“That is enough.”

Khell said nothing.

Zee turned away.

The answer surprised Aurelia.

Years ago, Zee would have needed the final word.

Needed violence to feel complete.

Now he understood something else.

Sometimes defeating an enemy mattered less than what you carried away from them.

Tonight, they carried people.

Names.

Family.

Proof that House Veyrath was not as dead as Aurelia had believed.

And a new problem.

Because seven rescued Veyrath survivors could no longer disappear quietly.

By sunrise, buyers would talk.

The River Market would talk.

The Ashen Talons would hear.

Drakonis might hear.

Victor might hear.

The world would learn that Aurelia Veyrath had stepped out of hiding.

Zee stood beside his mother as the three Red Passage ships drifted toward Vesper-controlled waters.

He looked at Theron.

Seraphine.

The other survivors.

Then Rez.

Ner.

Zareth.

His family had become larger in a single week.

That should have made him feel safer.

Instead, Zee understood exactly what it meant.

More people to love.

More people the world could threaten.

But also—

more people who could stand beside him when it tried.

And this time, the world had not hunted Zee Darkheart.

Zee had hunted it back.

He had found what it took.

And he had brought them home.
