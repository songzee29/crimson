Chapter Seventy-One

The Woman Who Chose First

Mara Vess lived in a monastery carved into red cliffs.

Not hidden.

Protected.

There was a difference.

Everyone nearby knew she was there.

No one approached.

Zee understood why.

Bodies hung from the cliff road.

Not fresh.

Not decorative either.

Warnings.

Each corpse wore a wooden plaque naming the crime.

FERAL VAMPIRE.

CHILD-KILLER.

BLOOD TRAFFICKER.

DEMON CULTIST.

Ner read the fourth.

“She kills monsters.”

Zee looked upward.

“So do I.”

“That is probably the point.”

The road continued.

Then the plaques changed.

UNSTABLE WITCH BLOODLINE.

Zee stopped.

Next.

WOLF CHILD — PROPHECY RISK.

His face went cold.

Next.

MIXED BLOOD — FUTURE THREAT.

Ner’s Golden Mana flared.

“There.”

Difference.

Not crimes committed.

Possibility.

Zee looked toward the monastery doors.

“Verified.”

Ner did not disagree.

⸻

Mara was waiting.

Mid-forties.

Witch.

White hair.

Black robes.

No army in the chamber.

Only six armed followers.

She smiled when Zee entered.

“King Darkheart.”

Zee said nothing.

Mara looked toward Ner.

“The anomaly.”

Ner’s voice sharpened.

“Nereus.”

Mara ignored the correction.

That irritated Zee more than it should have.

She gestured toward the table.

“Sit.”

Zee remained standing.

“No.”

Mara smiled wider.

“You know why I wanted your records.”

“To build weapons.”

“To understand what comes next.”

“There is always a next.”

“Yes.”

She leaned forward.

“And you are proof.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“Of?”

“That power compounds.”

“Blood adapts.”

“Species change.”

“Hybrids emerge.”

“Dragons mutate.”

“Gods die.”

Ner’s face hardened.

Mara continued.

“The world is becoming less controllable.”

Zee almost laughed.

Victor would have loved her.

Mara looked at him.

“So I choose first.”

There.

Her philosophy.

Simple.

Clean.

Horrible.

“Before what?” Zee asked.

“Before danger matures.”

Ner looked toward the hanging road outside.

“Children.”

“Potential catastrophes.”

“They were children.”

“Both can be true.”

Zee’s pupils became slits.

Mara noticed.

Good.

“You understand me.”

“No.”

“I think you do.”

Mara stood.

“You have begun killing threats before they strike.”

Silence.

Ner looked toward Zee.

Not accusing.

Watching.

Mara smiled.

“You came here before I attacked Darkheart again.”

“I came because you already burned my villages.”

“And because you found future plans.”

“Yes.”

“Exactly.”

Zee’s expression stayed calm.

Mara stepped closer.

“The only difference between us is scale.”

“No.”

“What difference?”

Zee looked toward the bodies outside.

“Evidence.”

Mara laughed.

“You think evidence will save you from becoming practical?”

Zee said nothing.

That bothered him because part of the answer was no.

Mara continued.

“You killed your father.”

“Yes.”

“You killed a God.”

“Yes.”

“You drain supernatural enemies to evolve.”

“Yes.”

Ner’s eyes shifted toward him.

Mara smiled.

“You are becoming exactly what intelligent people should fear.”

Zee took one step closer.

“Then fear me.”

Her smile vanished.

“But do not use me to justify dead children.”

Silence.

Good.

⸻

Mara attacked first.

Appropriate.

The floor lit with black sigils.

Ner moved.

Pure Gold snapped around Zee before the spell completed.

The black structure hit the boundary and split.

Mara’s followers attacked from both sides.

Zee barely moved.

One hand.

“Down.”

Crimson Witchcraft smashed three to the floor.

The other three resisted.

Good.

Ner took one.

Gold locked the weapon arm.

Bloodsong struck circulation.

“Still.”

The follower froze.

Zee took the second through Vampire speed.

One instant standing.

Next unconscious.

Pressure point beneath the jaw.

The third was a Wolf.

He lunged.

Zee smiled.

Then shifted.

Four-legged Wolf form hit the stone.

Near-black fur.

Crimson-and-gold eyes.

No horns.

No scales.

Just Wolf.

The enemy hesitated.

Zee did not.

They collided.

Claws.

Teeth.

Weight.

No Vampire speed.

No Witchcraft.

Wolf Zee drove him across the floor.

The enemy tried turning away.

Zee caught the shoulder.

Pinned him.

Could kill.

Did not.

Not yet.

He shifted back.

Human-shaped.

Mara stared.

“Every nature complete.”

Zee straightened.

“Three always were.”

She looked toward the darker scales along his neck.

“And the fourth?”

Zee smiled faintly.

“None of your business.”

Mara cast.

This time directly at Ner.

Wrong choice.

Zee moved without thought.

Raised one palm.

The spell stopped.

He closed his fist.

It shattered.

Then his other hand flicked.

Mara flew backward through her own table.

Wood exploded.

Zee continued walking.

No hurry.

Mara rose coughing.

“You protect him because he proves your blood can make more.”

Zee’s face changed.

“No.”

She laughed.

“You love him.”

“Yes.”

No hesitation.

Ner went still.

Not because it was new.

Because Zee said it here, covered in blood, while walking toward someone he intended to kill.

Both true.

Both Zee.

Mara attacked again.

Zee disappeared.

Reappeared behind her.

Knife beneath ribs.

Not fatal.

Yet.

Assassin placement.

She gasped.

Zee whispered:

“Who else did you order killed?”

Mara smiled through pain.

“Hundreds.”

Truth.

His Vampire blood sense caught no deception.

Zee pushed the knife deeper.

“How many children?”

“Enough.”

That was the answer.

No remorse.

No coercion.

No uncertainty.

Zee could kill her now.

He wanted to.

Ner said:

“Wait.”

Zee stopped.

Not because Ner commanded.

Because he trusted the reason would matter.

Ner approached.

“Where are the lists?”

Mara’s expression shifted.

Lists.

Good.

Zee smiled faintly.

Ner noticed.

“You forgot the evidence.”

“I was distracted.”

“By murder.”

“Yes.”

Mara laughed.

“You two are absurd.”

Ner looked at her.

“Where?”

She resisted.

Ner’s voice changed.

Bloodsong.

“Tell me.”

Mara had wards.

Strong.

Her blood fought the resonance.

Ner pushed with Golden precision around the command, isolating the effect from everyone else.

“Tell me.”

Blood vessels stood along Mara’s neck.

She finally gasped:

“Lower crypt.”

Ner released her.

Zee looked toward him.

“Done?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Mara understood.

Fear entered her.

Finally.

“Wait.”

Zee looked at her.

“You just spent ten minutes explaining why waiting is weakness.”

Mara’s face twisted.

“I can help you.”

“Probably.”

“Then—”

“I do not want your help.”

Zee pulled the knife free.

Mara stumbled.

Relief.

Wrong.

He caught her by the throat.

Vampire strength lifted her.

She struggled.

“Mercy.”

Zee stared.

At nineteen, he had begun asking why.

At twenty-one, he still did.

“Give me a reason.”

“I know every threat forming against you.”

“Names?”

“I can tell you.”

“Lists?”

“Yes.”

“Where?”

“Crypt.”

Already had it.

No new value.

Mara realized.

Fear deepened.

Zee enjoyed it.

He did not pretend otherwise.

“You are going to kill me.”

“Yes.”

“You are proving me right.”

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“No.”

His fangs descended.

“You killed people for what they might become.”

He pulled her closer.

“I am killing you for what you already chose.”

Then he fed.

⸻

Mara’s Witch blood hit hard.

Old.

Potent.

Layered with defensive rituals and traces of dozens of supernatural substances she had consumed over years trying to harden herself.

Zee drank all of it.

Not her spells.

Not her knowledge.

Her blood entered Zee and met Crimson biology.

Poison resistance stirred.

Suppression resistance.

Blood analysis.

The foreign mixtures began separating almost instantly.

His body was becoming frighteningly good at deciding what belonged.

Mara weakened.

Her hands stopped moving.

Her heartbeat slowed.

Zee did not stop until it ended.

He dropped the body.

No guilt.

No apology.

Ner watched him wipe blood from his mouth.

“You enjoyed that.”

“Yes.”

“Because she was afraid?”

“Yes.”

“Because of the blood?”

“Yes.”

“Because you killed her?”

Zee thought.

Then:

“Yes.”

Ner nodded.

No approval.

No condemnation.

He simply knew Zee.

That mattered.

⸻

The lower crypt held forty-seven names.

Some marked dead.

Some pending.

Some under observation.

Not all guilty.

That was the worst part.

A twelve-year-old Witch with unstable fire magic.

A Vampire child born from an old predatory bloodline.

A Wolf girl whose father had massacred a village.

A mixed-blood infant Mara believed could become dangerous.

Potential.

Nothing more.

Ner stood over the list.

“She would have killed them.”

“Yes.”

Zee’s face was unreadable.

Then he burned the list.

Not the evidence ledger beside it.

Only the kill orders.

Ner looked over.

“Why keep one?”

“Proof.”

Growth.

Darkness had not made him stupid.

Outside, Mara’s surviving followers surrendered after learning she was dead.

Zee spared four who had never participated in child killings.

Two confessed to carrying out executions.

They died.

Quickly.

No spectacle.

The monastery belonged to no one by sunset.

Zee did not take it.

Did not want it.

He sent the evidence to three kingdoms whose citizens had been murdered.

Let them tear apart the rest of Mara’s network.

He had other problems.

One of the ledgers held another Council name.

Not Orin.

Not Mara.

Dren Solvar.

Beside it:

PRIMARY INTEREST: KING ZEE DARKHEART — BLOOD ACQUISITION ALIVE IF POSSIBLE.

Ner read it.

Then looked toward Zee.

Zee smiled.

Slow.

Predatory.

“Alive?”

Ner sighed.

“There it is.”

Zee folded the page.

Mara had wanted to kill threats before they matured.

Dren Solvar wanted Zee alive.

For blood.

For study.

For something worse.

The difference was almost refreshing.

Zee tucked the page into his coat.

“Next.”

No rest.

No reset.

The world kept coming.

And King Zee Darkheart was becoming very comfortable meeting it halfway.
