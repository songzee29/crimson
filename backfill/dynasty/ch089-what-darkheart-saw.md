Chapter Eighty-Nine

What Darkheart Saw

Nobody celebrated the quarry victory.

Darkheart had rescued thirty-nine citizens.

Destroyed a Rell strike force.

Killed the general responsible.

By every military measure, Zee had won.

The kingdom felt different when he returned.

People moved aside.

Too quickly.

Soldiers bowed.

Too deeply.

A child stopped talking when Zee passed.

He noticed everything.

Of course he did.

That was the curse of senses sharpened over twenty-four years.

Fear smelled different when it belonged to enemies.

He enjoyed that.

Fear from his own people smelled wrong.

Worse—

part of him still enjoyed it too.

That was the problem.

⸻

Ner entered Zee’s chambers after midnight.

Zee stood at the window.

He had washed.

The blood scent remained in his memory.

“Say it,” Zee said.

Ner closed the door.

“You lost control.”

“Yes.”

“You killed people who surrendered.”

“Yes.”

“You nearly killed a boy who had not even reached the battle.”

“Yes.”

Ner studied him.

Zee did not turn.

“I know what I did.”

“Then why are you standing here alone?”

Zee finally looked at him.

“Because I wanted more.”

Ner went still.

Zee continued.

“When Zareth stopped me, I was not tired.”

His voice remained calm.

“I was not finished.”

No excuse.

No grief performance.

Truth.

“I liked them running.”

Ner’s face tightened.

“I liked begging.”

Silence.

“I liked knowing they could not stop me.”

Ner walked closer.

“And now?”

Zee looked toward the city.

“I still like it.”

That was darker than regret.

Ner understood.

“So what changes?”

Zee looked at him.

“Rules.”

“For you?”

“Yes.”

That surprised Ner.

Zee continued.

“When I am feeding during battle, someone watches.”

“Who?”

“You.”

“When I am not there?”

“Kael.”

Ner frowned.

“He is not your keeper.”

“No.”

“Neither am I.”

“I know.”

Zee turned fully.

“You are witnesses.”

Different.

“I do not need someone deciding my morality.”

His eyes hardened.

“I need someone capable of telling me when I stop deciding at all.”

Ner considered.

Then nodded.

That he could do.

Not conscience.

Not ownership.

Recognition.

Zee touched Ner’s hand.

“I almost became Mara.”

Ner shook his head.

“No.”

Zee looked at him sharply.

Ner continued.

“Mara killed people for what they might become.”

A pause.

“You killed people because you were furious.”

“That is supposed to make me feel better?”

“No.”

Good.

Ner squeezed his hand.

“It is supposed to be accurate.”

Zee almost smiled.

Did not.

⸻

Kael came the next morning.

Alone.

He did not bow.

Zee noticed.

“Sit.”

“I would rather stand.”

“Fine.”

Kael looked at him.

“You scared me.”

There it was.

Zee nodded.

“I know.”

“I have seen you kill.”

“Yes.”

“I have seen you feed.”

“Yes.”

“I have watched you enjoy both.”

Zee waited.

Kael’s jaw tightened.

“Yesterday was different.”

“Yes.”

“You did not care who had stopped fighting.”

“No.”

Silence.

Kael looked toward the door.

“I need to know something.”

“What?”

“If you lose control around us…”

He stopped.

Zee understood.

“Will I kill you?”

Kael looked back.

“Yes.”

Zee answered immediately.

“If you are innocent?”

“Yes.”

That honesty hurt.

“Yesterday, for a little while, I do not know.”

Kael’s face went pale.

Zee continued before he could speak.

“That is why yesterday does not happen again without consequence.”

“What consequence?”

“If I cannot regain control, you leave.”

Kael frowned.

“Leave?”

“Get civilians away from me.”

“And you?”

“Zareth.”

Kael understood.

The one being physically powerful enough to engage Zee without instantly dying.

Not necessarily defeat him.

Engage.

The Link also gave Zareth the best chance of reaching him before violence became mindless.

Kael looked toward the window.

“I am staying in Darkheart.”

Zee said nothing.

“I did not say because I trust you completely.”

“Good.”

That surprised Kael.

Zee’s expression remained serious.

“Do not.”

Kael stared.

“I want people who choose me.”

Not worship.

Not blind loyalty.

“If yesterday changes your choice, then change it.”

Kael looked at him for several seconds.

Then:

“I am still here.”

Good.

His choice.

Not Zee’s.

⸻

The quarry changed military doctrine.

No celebration.

No public lie.

Darkheart acknowledged what happened.

Not every detail.

Enough.

Seventy-four Rell soldiers died.

Twenty-one had surrendered before death.

That number spread.

Foreign kingdoms seized it immediately.

THE CRIMSON KING SLAUGHTERS PRISONERS.

True.

Not the whole truth.

Still true.

Zee refused to deny it.

Rez arrived from Vesper furious.

Not at the rumors.

At Zee.

“You gave every enemy court exactly what they wanted.”

“I know.”

“Do you?”

“Yes.”

Rez threw three diplomatic letters onto the table.

“Vesper is being asked whether we support prisoner slaughter.”

Zee looked at them.

“What did you say?”

“No.”

Zee nodded.

Rez blinked.

“That’s it?”

“That is your answer.”

“You are not angry?”

“Why would I be?”

Rez stepped closer.

“Because I publicly condemned what you did.”

“You should.”

Silence.

Rez’s anger changed shape.

Zee continued.

“Vesper is not Darkheart.”

“No.”

“You are not my subject.”

“No.”

“You do not owe me lies.”

Rez exhaled.

“Good.”

Then hit Zee.

Hard.

Across the jaw.

Everyone in the room froze.

Zee slowly looked back at him.

Rez flexed his hand.

“That was personal.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“Feel better?”

“A little.”

“Good.”

Ner laughed from the table.

The darkness loosened for half a second.

Then Zareth arrived.

Human form.

No smile.

He looked at Zee.

“I felt you disappear.”

Zee’s expression changed.

The Link between them opened.

Zareth sent the memory back.

Not what Zee remembered.

What Zareth felt.

Rage so complete language vanished.

Blood hunger.

Heat.

Joy.

Then emptiness where Zee’s deliberate thought should have been.

Zee went still.

“That bad?”

Zareth answered aloud.

“Yes.”

Then privately:

I could not find you inside it for several seconds.

That scared Zee.

More than the bodies.

Because control had always been part of his identity.

He killed because he chose.

Fed because he chose.

Hunted because he chose.

Yesterday, choice vanished.

Zareth folded his arms.

“If it happens again, I hit you.”

Rez smiled.

“I support this.”

Zee looked at Zareth.

“You think that will work?”

“No.”

Zareth smiled without humor.

“But I will enjoy trying.”

That finally made Zee laugh.

Dark.

Small.

Real.

⸻

Rell expected retaliation.

It came.

Just not the way anyone expected.

Zee did not massacre towns.

He did not burn farms.

He did not kill civilians.

He destroyed Rell’s slave records.

Every one Darkheart could reach.

Kael led raids against labor prisons.

Tessa emptied three medical camps holding injured escapees.

Amara hunted transport routes.

Rez froze Rell accounts inside Vesper.

Ner identified blood-family separations and reunited children with parents.

Zareth destroyed two military bridges after civilians crossed.

Darkheart took people.

Thousands.

Anyone who chose to leave.

Rell lost workers.

Soldiers.

Taxpayers.

Families.

Whole districts emptied.

Not through conquest.

Through escape.

King Rell demanded Zee return them.

Zee’s answer was public.

“No.”

One word.

Enough.

⸻

By spring, more than two thousand former Rell subjects lived under Darkheart protection.

Southhold expanded again.

Another town began forming near the eastern river.

Darkheart needed more soldiers.

More healers.

More roads.

More food.

More everything.

The kingdom was becoming harder to kill.

And Zee was becoming harder too.

Not safer.

Harder.

He stood above Southhold one evening while children played beneath Elara Voss’s name.

The Wolf girl from the quarry was among them.

Scar at her throat.

Alive.

That mattered.

Twenty-one surrendering soldiers were dead because Zee lost control.

That mattered too.

He would not erase one truth with the other.

Ner joined him.

“What are you thinking?”

Zee looked toward the growing kingdom.

“That I cannot promise I will never lose control again.”

Ner said nothing.

Zee’s pupils slowly narrowed.

“But next time…”

His voice darkened.

“I want my enemies to be the only ones who have reason to fear it.”

Below them, Darkheart continued building.

The King protecting it had crossed another line.

This time he knew exactly where the line was.

And knowing did not make him less dangerous.

It made the next crossing a choice.
