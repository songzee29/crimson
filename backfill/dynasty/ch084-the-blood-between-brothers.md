Chapter Eighty-Four

The Blood Between Brothers

Zee reached Vesper before sunrise.

The southern gate was broken.

Smoke covered the streets.

Bodies lined the square.

Rez lay beside the shattered fountain.

Lucien knelt over him.

Blue-white Mana flickered weakly beneath Rez’s skin, then vanished.

The black blade was still inside him.

Zee stopped.

He could smell what it was doing.

Witch-killing iron.

Not merely blocking magic.

Eating it.

Rez opened his eyes.

“About time.”

Zee dropped beside him.

“You look terrible.”

“You always say that.”

“Because you keep proving me right.”

Rez tried laughing.

Blood came instead.

Lucien looked at Zee.

“Healers cannot remove the blade. His Mana collapses every time they touch it.”

Zee examined the wound.

Foreign Witch suppression.

Vampire residue from the attacker.

Iron poisoning.

Rez’s Blue-White nature was fighting everything at once.

Losing.

Zee knew that smell.

Death.

Again.

His mind went immediately to Kael.

Tessa.

Amara.

Ner.

His blood.

Rez saw the answer in his face.

“No.”

Zee looked at him.

“You do not even know what I was going to say.”

“Yes, I do.”

Lucien’s expression sharpened.

“What?”

Rez looked toward his father.

“Zee’s blood.”

Silence.

Lucien understood.

Kael had become Witch-Vampire.

Rez could become the same.

Maybe.

Or die anyway.

Zee leaned closer.

“I will not do it without you choosing.”

Rez closed his eyes.

“How long?”

Lucien answered quietly.

“Minutes.”

Rez breathed.

Then looked at Zee.

Twenty-three years of friendship sat between them.

Childhood.

Wars.

Bad decisions.

A kingdom that took Zee in before anyone else wanted him.

Aurelia.

Ner.

Zareth.

Everything.

Rez whispered:

“If I wake up calling you sire, I am killing you.”

Zee almost laughed.

“You could try.”

Rez smiled weakly.

“Do it.”

Zee opened his wrist.

Lucien did not interfere.

Zee pressed the wound to Rez’s mouth.

“Drink.”

Rez swallowed.

Once.

Twice.

Blue-White Mana erupted.

The fountain froze solid.

Lucien was thrown backward.

Zee held Rez down as Witch-killing iron began burning inside him.

Rez screamed.

His Mana fought.

Then Zee’s blood reached it.

Not replacing.

Not consuming.

Preserving.

Adding.

The same impossible bridge that saved Kael formed again.

Vampire blood entered beside Witch.

Rez’s heartbeat stopped.

Zee froze.

“No.”

One second.

Two.

Then—

THUMP.

Rez gasped.

Blue-white light exploded from his eyes.

Dark-red veins flashed beneath them.

The black blade shifted as his body began pushing it out.

Zee grabbed the handle.

“Ready?”

Rez growled:

“No.”

Zee pulled anyway.

Rez screamed loud enough to wake half of Vesper.

The blade hit stone.

His wound began closing.

Fast.

Lucien stared.

“Rez?”

Rez opened his eyes.

Blue-White Mana remained.

Pure.

Unchanged.

Then fangs descended.

Rez touched them.

“Oh, I hate this.”

Zee laughed.

Actually laughed.

Rez looked offended.

“I almost died.”

“You complain very quickly for a corpse.”

“I was never a corpse.”

“Your heart stopped.”

“For how long?”

“Two seconds.”

Rez stared.

“That barely counts.”

Zee grabbed the back of his neck and pulled him into an embrace.

Hard.

Rez went silent.

Then held him back.

No joke.

Not for a moment.

Zee had nearly lost his brother.

Again, his blood had refused a burial.

Rez’s hunger arrived before noon.

He hated that more than the fangs.

“I can hear everyone.”

Zee leaned against the wall.

“Yes.”

“I can smell blood.”

“Yes.”

“I know Lucien cut himself shaving three rooms away.”

Lucien looked offended.

“You were not supposed to know that.”

Rez stared at Zee.

“How do you live like this?”

Zee smiled.

“Badly at first.”

Kael arrived from Darkheart by evening.

The moment he saw Rez, he stopped.

Then grinned.

“Oh, this is excellent.”

Rez pointed at him.

“Leave.”

“No.”

“You are enjoying this.”

“Immensely.”

Kael stepped closer.

Blue-White Mana.

Vampire heartbeat.

Fangs.

Stable.

Another Witch-Vampire.

But not the first.

Rez remained Rez.

Crown Prince of Vesper.

Blue-White Witch.

Now Vampire too.

No ownership.

No blood command pulling him toward Zee.

Nothing changed his loyalty except survival itself.

Kael folded his arms.

“Welcome.”

Rez glared.

“To what?”

“The constant hunger, inconvenient hearing, accidental furniture destruction, and people asking invasive questions.”

Rez looked at Zee.

“You did this to me.”

“You consented.”

“I was dying.”

“You were still annoying.”

Kael laughed.

Good.

Rez was alive.

That was enough.

Then Zee felt Zareth through the Dragon Link.

Pain.

Smoke.

Exhaustion.

And words.

Drakonis held.

Beneath them came something worse.

Bodies.

Many.

Zee’s expression changed.

How many?

A pause.

Thirty-eight.

Zee closed his eyes.

Zareth continued.

Do not come. The battle is over.

Zee felt the torn flesh beneath Zareth’s scales despite the calm words.

You are hurt.

I heal.

Thirty-eight do not.

Silence crossed the Link.

Then Zareth answered.

I know.

No comfort could change it.

Zee opened his eyes.

Another sensation reached him.

Not Zareth.

A Darkheart messenger entering Vesper.

Blood on his armor.

“Majesty.”

Zee turned.

The man bowed.

“The nest beneath Darkheart broke through the second seal.”

Ner.

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“Is he alive?”

“Yes.”

Relief.

Then:

“But we lost the lower guard.”

“How many?”

The messenger swallowed.

“Fourteen.”

Rez closed his eyes.

Kael’s smile disappeared.

Zee stood perfectly still.

Vesper survived.

Rez survived.

Drakonis survived.

Darkheart survived.

Fifty-two people did not.

Victory.

Again.

Zee was beginning to hate that word.
