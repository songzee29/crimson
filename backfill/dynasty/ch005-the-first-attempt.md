Chapter Five

The First Attempt

The siege began again before sunrise.

This time, the kingdoms did not test the walls.

They tried to break them.

Three siege engines released at once, hurling stones large enough to crush houses. They struck the eastern battlements with enough force to split ancient masonry and throw soldiers from the walls. Horns answered from every direction. Arrows darkened the pale morning sky. Witches on both sides raised barriers, curses, and counterspells while infantry advanced beneath layered shields.

Castle Darkheart answered with violence of its own.

The Witch Kingdom had been built for war.

Runes buried deep within the fortress stone awakened under the hands of Darkheart battle-witches. Entire sections of wall shimmered beneath defensive magic. Enemy ladders caught fire before touching stone. The ground beneath one advancing battalion cracked open and swallowed horses, soldiers, and siege shields alike.

None of it stopped the next wave.

There were too many kingdoms.

Too many soldiers.

Too much fear.

King Victor stood upon the eastern battlement as arrows struck the barrier before him and burst into splinters.

“Left tower!”

A commander shouted the warning.

Victor turned just as an enemy stone smashed through the upper section of the tower.

Black stone exploded outward.

Men screamed.

A witch vanished beneath falling masonry.

Victor raised one hand and unleashed a violent burst of dark violet force. The largest pieces stopped in midair long enough for several soldiers to escape before crashing into the courtyard below.

“Get the wounded inside!” he ordered.

The High Marshal rushed toward him.

“We cannot hold every wall at this rate.”

“We do not need every wall.”

“My King?”

Victor pointed toward the eastern approach.

“They are attacking where they believe we are strongest.”

The marshal followed his gaze.

Understanding came quickly.

“A distraction.”

“Exactly.”

Victor looked south.

“Send riders to the lower tunnels. If even one foreign soldier enters beneath this castle, collapse the passage with them inside.”

“Yes, Your Majesty.”

The marshal hurried away.

Victor remained on the wall.

Below him, thousands of men were dying.

For Zee.

Because of Zee.

The thought came before Victor could stop it.

He hated himself for thinking it.

Then another soldier beside him screamed as an arrow pierced his throat.

Victor caught the man before he fell.

Blood poured over the king’s hands.

The soldier looked at him desperately.

“My King…”

Victor pressed one hand against the wound and tried to slow the bleeding.

The man died before the nearest healer reached them.

Victor stared at the blood covering his palms.

The prophecy returned.

Every blade raised against him shall sharpen what it seeks to destroy.

Victor looked toward the tower where his son slept.

Something inside him hardened.

Not completely.

Not yet.

But enough.

—

Queen Aurelia had not slept.

She sat beside the nursery window with Zee wrapped tightly against her chest.

The crimson hatchling remained pressed against them both.

Each impact against the walls made the tiny dragon flinch.

Each time he flinched, Zee stirred.

Their link carried only the simplest things between them.

Fear.

Unease.

Warmth.

Near.

Safe.

The hatchling pushed his snout against Zee’s shoulder.

Zee’s fingers closed weakly around one of his small horns.

The dragon calmed.

Aurelia watched them in silence.

“They are trying to kill you,” she whispered.

Zee did not understand the words.

His crimson eyes opened briefly.

Aurelia stroked his cheek.

“And your father is deciding whether to help them.”

The admission hurt more once spoken aloud.

The eldest royal witch stood near the door.

She had volunteered to remain with Aurelia.

Not under Victor’s command.

Under her own.

“My Queen…”

Aurelia looked at her.

“If he orders you to take Zee from me, what will you do?”

The witch did not answer immediately.

That frightened Aurelia.

Finally, the woman said, “I serve House Darkheart.”

“So do I.”

“Yes.”

“And?”

The witch looked toward the child.

“I do not believe fear is enough reason to murder a newborn.”

Aurelia’s shoulders loosened slightly.

“Then you will help me.”

“If it comes to that.”

“It already has.”

The witch lowered her eyes.

Outside, another impact shook the castle.

Dust drifted from the ceiling.

The dragon hatchling growled.

Then every flame in the nursery bent toward Zee.

The witch looked up sharply.

Crimson Mana thickened around the child.

Not violently.

Not consciously.

It simply gathered.

Zee’s breathing changed.

His heartbeat deepened.

The witch took one step backward.

Aurelia noticed.

“What?”

“I do not know.”

“Do not begin guessing.”

“I wasn’t.”

The woman studied Zee carefully.

“Something about him is reacting.”

“To the siege?”

“Perhaps.”

“To fear?”

“Perhaps.”

“To the dragon?”

“Perhaps.”

Aurelia’s jaw tightened.

“You truly know nothing.”

The witch looked at her.

“No.”

Then, after a moment:

“And I am beginning to think that is the only honest answer anyone in this kingdom has.”

—

By midday, the first outer village burned.

Victor watched smoke rise from beyond the walls.

It belonged to farmers who had refused evacuation.

The enemy had taken the settlement without resistance.

Then burned it anyway.

The High Marshal approached.

“They are sending another envoy.”

Victor did not turn.

“Kill him.”

The marshal froze.

Victor closed his eyes.

“No.”

He exhaled.

“Bring him inside.”

The correction came quickly.

But the marshal had heard the first answer.

So had Victor.

The envoy entered the lower audience chamber less than an hour later.

This one was younger.

He wore no crown colors.

Only plain gray cloth and a silver chain marking him as a neutral herald.

Victor laughed when he saw it.

“There are no neutrals left.”

The envoy bowed.

“Then consider me useful instead.”

Victor remained standing.

“What do they want now?”

“The same thing.”

“My son.”

“Yes.”

“Alive?”

The envoy hesitated.

Victor’s expression darkened.

“Answer.”

“Not necessarily.”

Silence filled the chamber.

The envoy continued carefully.

“Some rulers would accept proof of death.”

“And the others?”

“Some want the body.”

Victor’s face changed.

The envoy saw the danger too late.

“Leave.”

“My King—”

“Leave.”

“I was sent to explain—”

Victor crossed the distance between them in three strides and seized the man by the throat.

The guards moved instinctively.

No one interfered.

Victor lifted the envoy from the floor.

“If one foreign hand touches my son’s corpse…”

The words caught in his throat.

His son’s corpse.

He nearly released the man.

Then fear returned.

“If anyone attempts to claim his body as a trophy, relic, weapon, specimen, sacrifice, or prize…”

Victor tightened his grip.

“I will tear apart every kingdom represented outside my walls.”

The envoy clawed weakly at Victor’s hand.

Victor dropped him.

The man collapsed, coughing.

“Tell them no.”

The envoy looked up.

Victor’s voice lowered.

“And tell them something else.”

The room seemed colder.

“Zee Darkheart belongs to House Darkheart.”

The envoy stared at him.

Victor heard the words after saying them.

Belongs.

Not lives.

Not is protected by.

Belongs.

The envoy slowly stood.

“Then you intend to decide his fate yourself.”

Victor did not answer.

The man bowed.

“I think that is what they feared.”

He left.

Victor remained alone.

For a long time.

—

That evening, the castle changed around Zee.

The guards outside the nursery doubled again.

No one entered without being searched.

Windows were sealed.

Servants were replaced by soldiers personally selected by Victor.

Aurelia noticed every change.

So did the royal witch beside her.

“This is no longer protection,” Aurelia said.

The witch remained silent.

Aurelia stood.

“Pack what Zee needs.”

“My Queen?”

“Clothes. Blankets. Food.”

“For where?”

Aurelia looked toward the sealed window.

“Anywhere my husband cannot reach him.”

The witch’s face tightened.

“You are speaking of fleeing Castle Darkheart during a siege.”

“I am speaking of keeping my son alive.”

“The roads are surrounded.”

“Then we do not use roads.”

The witch looked toward the nursery door.

“There are old passages.”

Aurelia nodded.

“Exactly.”

The crimson hatchling lifted his head.

A simple feeling passed through the Link.

Move.

Zee stirred.

Aurelia looked down.

“Even he knows.”

The witch began gathering supplies.

Neither woman heard the footsteps approaching.

The door opened.

Victor entered.

Alone.

Aurelia stopped.

The witch did not.

She continued folding linen into a travel satchel.

Victor noticed.

“Where are you going?”

Aurelia answered without hesitation.

“With my son.”

“Where?”

“Away from you.”

Victor’s expression tightened.

“Aurelia.”

“You prepared an execution chamber.”

He went still.

The witch stopped packing.

Silence swallowed the room.

Victor looked at her.

“How do you know?”

“Do you think stone servants do not speak?”

“I gave no order to execute him.”

“You prepared for it.”

“That is not the same.”

“It is when the child is two days old.”

Victor stepped closer.

Aurelia moved between him and Zee.

The hatchling rose.

Teal-and-gold mana flickered around his crimson scales.

Victor saw it.

“Move.”

Aurelia did not.

“You are frightening him.”

“He is a newborn.”

“He knows fear.”

Victor looked toward Zee.

The infant stared back with crimson eyes.

No accusation.

No understanding.

Just recognition.

Father.

That simple truth almost undid everything.

Victor’s face softened.

For one moment.

Aurelia saw it.

“Victor…”

His eyes remained on Zee.

“He held my finger.”

Aurelia said nothing.

“He trusted me.”

“He still does.”

Victor swallowed.

“That is the problem.”

“No.”

Aurelia’s voice hardened.

“That is the only thing left worth saving.”

Victor looked at her.

“You heard what the prophecy says.”

“I heard what you told me it says.”

“Every attack may make him harder to kill.”

“You do not know that.”

“He was born under Blood Rain.”

“That is not an answer.”

“Wolves lost control.”

“Not an answer.”

“Vampires felt him in their blood.”

“Still not an answer.”

“The witches—”

“Still.”

“Not.”

“An answer.”

Each word struck harder than the last.

Victor’s voice rose.

“What would satisfy you?”

“Knowing who our son becomes by allowing him to become someone.”

The room fell silent.

Even the hatchling stopped growling.

Aurelia stepped closer.

“You are condemning him for surviving a future that has not happened.”

Victor’s breathing slowed.

“And if I am right?”

“And if you are wrong?”

He had no answer.

Another siege stone struck the castle.

The entire nursery shook.

A crack appeared across one wall.

The hatchling cried out.

Zee startled.

Crimson Mana erupted from him.

Not as an attack.

As instinct.

A wave of red force passed through the nursery.

The falling stones stopped before reaching the cradle.

For one impossible moment, fragments of ceiling hung in the air.

Victor stared.

Aurelia stared.

The witch stared.

Then the stones dropped harmlessly around the room.

Zee began crying.

A true newborn cry.

Small.

Frightened.

Human enough to make Aurelia’s heart break.

She gathered him into her arms immediately.

The hatchling pressed close.

Victor did not move.

His eyes remained on the fallen stone.

The prophecy returned.

Every blade raised against him shall sharpen what it seeks to destroy.

The castle had been struck.

Zee had responded.

Victor saw confirmation.

Aurelia saw a terrified child.

That difference destroyed the last fragile bridge between them.

Victor whispered, “It has started.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“No.”

He backed away.

“You saw it.”

“I saw my son survive falling stone.”

“He stopped it.”

“He was afraid.”

“Exactly.”

Victor’s fear crystallized.

Every danger would teach Zee.

Every attack would reveal more.

Every survival would become something else.

He no longer saw possibility.

He saw escalation.

Aurelia saw the change happen in his face.

“Victor.”

He turned toward the door.

“Lock the nursery.”

The guards outside stiffened.

Aurelia’s expression went cold.

“You will not imprison me.”

“This is not about you.”

“It is now.”

Victor looked toward the royal witch.

“You remain.”

The witch hesitated.

A dangerous mistake.

Victor noticed.

“Is there a problem?”

“No, Your Majesty.”

“Good.”

He stepped into the corridor.

Aurelia called after him.

“What are you going to do?”

Victor stopped.

For several breaths, he faced away from her.

Then he answered.

“What a king must.”

The doors closed.

Locks turned from the outside.

Aurelia rushed forward.

“Victor!”

No answer came.

Only retreating footsteps.

The crimson hatchling pressed against Zee.

Danger.

The feeling crossed the Link more strongly than ever before.

Zee cried harder.

—

Victor descended beneath the western tower.

Alone.

No guards.

No council.

No witnesses.

The execution chamber had been restored.

The stone table stood clean beneath iron chains.

A brazier burned in each corner.

Weapons once used against traitors and enemy kings rested along the wall.

Axes.

Blades.

Hooks.

Iron spikes.

Victor ignored them.

He walked toward the center of the chamber.

There, on a smaller table, sat something newly brought from the royal armory.

A dagger.

Nothing mystical.

Nothing ancient.

Nothing tied to prophecy.

Simple steel.

Victor had chosen it for exactly that reason.

No magic.

No ritual.

No spectacle.

If Zee was only a strange child born beneath terrible omens…

The blade would prove it.

If the prophecy was true…

Victor did not allow himself to finish the thought.

He picked up the dagger.

His hand trembled.

“No foreign king.”

His voice echoed through the chamber.

“No frightened mob.”

“No vampire.”

“No witch.”

“No wolf.”

He stared at the blade.

“If this must be done…”

His breathing became uneven.

“I decide.”

For a moment, he almost dropped it.

Then he remembered the soldier dying in his arms.

The villages burning.

The twelve armies.

The falling stone freezing above the cradle.

Fear won.

Not completely.

Enough.

Victor slipped the dagger beneath his cloak.

Above him, Aurelia pounded against the locked nursery doors.

The royal witch whispered hurriedly over an old hidden passage beneath the floor.

The crimson hatchling stood over Zee, tiny wings spread as wide as his newborn body allowed.

And beneath Castle Darkheart, King Victor Darkheart began walking toward the first murder attempt of Zee’s life.

Not as a foreign invader.

Not as a monster.

As his father.
