Chapter Fifty-Three

The Men Who Built a Sun

Silver rain fell upward.

Zee hated it immediately.

Not because it was dangerous.

Because every drop that struck his skin rolled toward the sky instead of the ground.

His hair slowly became wetter from underneath.

“That is disgusting.”

Rez looked over while trying to keep his footing on black grass that leaned away from him.

“Of all the things here, that bothers you?”

“Yes.”

Ner tilted his head.

“It is actually fascinating.”

“You would say that.”

A droplet struck Ner’s chin and slid upward along his cheek.

He smiled.

Zee stared.

Then realized he was staring.

Then became irritated with himself because even impossible rain could apparently become a Ner problem.

Rez saw.

He said nothing.

That was worse.

Zareth moved overhead.

His crimson body crossed a violet sky where two mountain ranges hung at different angles, one below and one suspended sideways in the distance.

Through the Link came tactical impressions.

Antlered creature ahead.

Large herd farther east.

Fire-light beyond ridge.

Movement.

Human-sized.

Zee looked toward Rez.

“There are people.”

Rez’s expression sharpened.

“How many?”

Zareth sent scattered points.

Twelve.

Maybe more below ground.

Ner frowned.

“Human?”

Zee waited for the Link.

No certainty.

“Two legs.”

Rez sighed.

“Useful.”

They followed the antlered creature across a landscape that made direction meaningless.

The black grass ended at a cliff.

Except the cliff extended horizontally into open air.

The creature walked onto it.

Zee stopped.

“No.”

Ner looked toward him.

“You are scared of heights?”

“No.”

“You have Zareth as a best friend.”

“I am scared of walking on walls that think they are floors.”

Rez stepped beside him.

“Reasonable.”

The antlered creature continued.

Its six legs adhered to stone as if gravity agreed with it.

Zareth banked nearby.

The Link delivered an image of the same path from above.

Except from Zareth’s angle, the cliff was level ground.

Zee frowned.

“This place is rude.”

Ner smiled.

“Move.”

He stepped onto the vertical stone.

Nothing happened.

His body rotated without rotating.

One heartbeat Zee watched Ner standing sideways.

The next Zee’s mind accepted the surface as ground.

Ner looked back.

“Come on.”

Zee hated that Ner looked good while doing impossible things.

He stepped.

The world shifted.

Rez came next.

No comment.

His face suggested comment would happen later.

They crossed.

At the far end, the smell reached Zee.

Smoke.

Metal.

Blood.

Not much blood.

Oil.

Alchemy.

Witchcraft.

And something artificial burning too hot.

Zee stopped.

“I know that smell.”

Rez looked toward him.

“What?”

“Not the whole thing.”

He inhaled.

“Spell fuel.”

Ner’s expression sharpened.

“Human-made?”

“Witch-made.”

Zareth landed nearby.

The antlered guide finally stopped.

Ahead, black grass opened into a crater.

Someone had built a camp inside it.

Not tents.

Structures.

Metal supports drilled into pale stone.

Wooden platforms.

Ropes.

Crates.

Three portable ward pylons.

A central framework surrounded something bright.

Too bright.

White-green light burned inside an iron cage.

It resembled a tiny sun.

Zee’s Dragon nature recoiled.

Hard.

Crimson scales raced across his neck.

His shoulder ridges tightened.

Zareth snarled so violently the ground vibrated.

The Link hit Zee with pain.

Wrong-fire.

Dragon instinct hated it.

Ner stared.

“What is that?”

Rez looked toward the workers below.

“Whatever it is, they built it.”

The antlered creature lowered its head.

The black grass around its feet faded gray.

Zee understood.

“That is what damaged the route.”

Ner crouched.

Golden Mana formed along his fingertips.

He studied the distant framework without touching it.

His face changed.

“No.”

Rez looked toward him.

“What?”

“That is not producing energy.”

“What is it doing?”

Ner looked at the world around them.

“Taking structure.”

Zee frowned.

“Explain.”

“I am trying.”

Ner narrowed his eyes.

“Magic usually has an anchor.”

“Caster.”

“Object.”

“Place.”

“Blood.”

“Something.”

He pointed toward the white-green light.

“That thing is anchoring itself by forcing the surrounding space to become simpler.”

Rez looked toward the impossible mountain overhead.

“Which means?”

“It is making this place obey ordinary geometry.”

Silence.

Zee looked at the crater again.

Around the artificial light, the deep wild looked almost normal.

Ground stayed down.

Rain fell downward.

Trees grew upright.

Distances held.

A circle of kingdom logic carved into a place that did not share it.

Rez’s face hardened.

“They are not studying the region.”

“No,” Ner said.

“They are changing it.”

The antlered creature moved its head toward the eastern horizon.

Zee followed.

Far away, herds of pale animals crowded against folded terrain.

Their migration route severed by the artificial stable zone.

Zee’s darkness stirred.

This time not because he enjoyed violence.

Because someone had entered a place they did not understand and decided understanding was unnecessary if they could force it to behave.

He thought of Victor.

Different scale.

Same temptation.

Control what frightens you.

Call that safety.

Rez watched the camp.

“We need to know why.”

Zee looked at him.

“You already know enough?”

“No.”

Good.

Rez continued.

“Maybe they are preventing something worse.”

Ner nodded reluctantly.

“Possible.”

Zee did not like it.

That did not make it false.

Zareth’s Link pushed a memory.

Drakonis border engineers stabilizing volcanic ground before hatchling migrations.

Sometimes changing dangerous land saved lives.

Important distinction.

Zee exhaled.

“Fine.”

Rez smiled.

“You are learning patience.”

“Do not ruin it.”

⸻

They entered the camp openly.

That was Rez’s decision.

Zee hated it.

Sneaking would imply guilt if the camp was legitimate.

Attacking would be worse.

So four fifteen-year-olds and one enormous Dragon walked down the crater slope toward a group of researchers who reacted exactly as badly as expected.

Weapons came out.

Wards ignited.

Someone shouted:

“Dragon!”

Someone else shouted:

“Darkheart!”

Zee sighed.

“Of course they know me.”

Rez lifted empty hands.

“We are not attacking.”

A woman near the central framework stepped forward.

Forties.

Witch.

Dark green Mana.

Heavy coat.

Silver goggles.

Not a soldier.

Her first reaction to Zee was not fear.

It was annoyance.

“What are you doing here?”

Zee blinked.

That was new.

Rez answered.

“Investigating disappearances tied to this region.”

“You should not be here.”

Ner looked toward the artificial sun.

“That makes at least six people who should not be here.”

The woman’s eyes narrowed.

“Who are you?”

“Nereus Azrion.”

Her expression changed at the name.

Not greed.

Recognition.

Professional.

“Golden Mana.”

Ner’s face hardened.

“My name was first.”

The woman paused.

Then:

“Fair.”

She looked toward Rez.

“Prince Vesper.”

“Yes.”

Then Zareth.

She swallowed.

“Crown Prince Drakonis.”

Zareth stared.

Finally Zee.

“Zee Darkheart.”

Zee smiled.

“There.”

“What?”

“You used my name.”

The woman looked confused.

Rez covered his face briefly.

“My name is Doctor Maeven Oris.”

Zee frowned.

“Doctor?”

“Scholar.”

“That sounds safer.”

“It is not.”

Ner smiled faintly.

Rez asked, “What did you build?”

Maeven looked toward the caged light.

“A stabilization core.”

“Purpose?”

“To create a survivable research perimeter.”

Ner stared at her.

“You are rewriting local spatial behavior.”

Maeven’s expression sharpened.

“You understand it?”

“No.”

That surprised her.

Ner continued.

“I understand what it is doing.”

“Not why it works.”

Maeven looked at him differently now.

Respect.

Maybe.

“Neither do we.”

Zee laughed once.

Everyone looked at him.

“You built it anyway?”

Maeven’s face hardened.

“We lost eleven researchers before the first stable perimeter.”

Rez’s expression changed.

“Dead?”

“Missing.”

Important.

Maeven continued.

“We came here after finding evidence of rare spatial minerals beyond the western range.”

Zee looked toward the pale supports.

“Mining?”

“Research.”

Ner glanced at the crates.

Some were marked with ore weights.

Maeven saw.

“Research funds itself poorly.”

Ner’s mouth tightened.

There.

Less noble.

More complicated.

Rez asked, “Did you know you were blocking migration?”

Maeven frowned.

“What migration?”

Zee looked toward the antlered creature.

It had remained beyond the camp boundary.

Maeven saw it.

Her face went pale.

“That thing has been following us for weeks.”

“Did it attack?”

“No.”

“Did you attack it?”

Maeven hesitated.

Zee’s expression cooled.

“How many?”

“Two.”

Zareth growled.

Maeven looked toward the Dragon.

“They approached the perimeter.”

Ner stared.

“So?”

“We did not know what they were.”

Zee almost laughed at the familiarity of that sentence.

Rez spoke first.

“And what happened after?”

“The terrain became more hostile.”

Zee looked toward him.

Rez looked back.

There.

Pattern.

Maeven’s face changed.

“You think it is retaliating.”

Zee shook his head.

“I think you keep calling everything retaliation because you assume it is thinking about you.”

Maeven stared.

That landed.

Zee continued.

“Maybe you built your core across where they live.”

Ner stepped closer to the framework.

“Turn it off.”

Maeven immediately said, “No.”

“Why?”

“Because if we shut it down too quickly, every stabilized boundary may collapse at once.”

Ner stopped.

That mattered.

“How many?”

“Seven field anchors.”

“How far?”

“Four miles.”

Rez cursed quietly.

They had found one camp.

The damage was larger.

Zee looked toward the antlered creature.

The pressure in his bones intensified.

Images.

Herds trapped.

Folds compressed.

One section tearing.

Not anger.

Emergency.

Ner looked at Zee.

“It is showing you something.”

“Yes.”

“What?”

Zee described it.

Maeven went pale.

“That cannot happen.”

Ner rounded on her.

“You just said you do not understand how the region works.”

Maeven stared.

Then shut her mouth.

Good.

Rez stepped toward the camp map.

“Show us the seven anchors.”

Maeven hesitated.

Rez’s voice sharpened.

“Now.”

Future king.

No shouting needed.

Maeven unfolded a field chart.

Seven stabilization points formed a rough ring.

Ner stared.

Then his face changed.

“Oh, no.”

Zee hated that phrase from him.

“What?”

Ner traced the circle.

“These are not independent.”

Maeven nodded.

“They reinforce each other.”

“No.”

Ner looked at her.

“They are tightening.”

Maeven frowned.

“What?”

“Your anchors are not holding seven areas stable.”

Pure Golden Mana flickered over the map.

The geometry became visible.

Lines pulling inward.

“They are compressing everything unstable between them.”

Rez immediately understood.

“A trap.”

“For the land itself.”

The ground shuddered.

Hard.

Zareth spread his wings.

The artificial sun brightened.

Every impossible mountain in the distance shifted at once.

The antlered creature outside the perimeter collapsed onto its front legs.

Zee felt the pressure hit his Dragon nature like a hammer.

His horns burned.

Crimson lines exploded across his vision.

Folds everywhere.

Too many.

All being dragged toward the stabilization ring.

Ner grabbed his shoulder.

“What do you see?”

Zee could barely answer.

“Everything is folding inward.”

Rez looked toward Maeven.

“How long?”

She stared at her instruments.

“I do not know.”

“Guess.”

“Minutes.”

Zee smiled without humor.

“There goes diplomacy.”

Rez pointed toward the central core.

“No one destroys anything yet.”

Zee looked offended.

“I know.”

Ner was already studying the map.

“We have to release the anchors in sequence.”

Maeven stared.

“That will collapse the perimeter.”

“Yes.”

“And kill us.”

“Maybe.”

Maeven looked horrified.

Ner continued.

“If we leave them active, you may collapse whatever this place uses instead of geography.”

“That could be worse.”

Rez took command.

“Zareth.”

The Dragon Prince looked toward him.

“Aerial anchors.”

Zareth launched.

“Ner, sequence.”

“Working.”

“Maeven, evacuation.”

She hesitated.

Rez shouted for the first time.

“Move!”

She moved.

“Zee.”

Zee looked toward him.

Rez pointed toward the Crimson lines only Zee could see.

“You tell us where reality is safe enough to stand.”

Zee smiled.

“That is a terrible job.”

“You are uniquely qualified.”

“Rude.”

“Accurate.”

The camp erupted.

Researchers grabbed equipment.

Others abandoned it.

Crates were left.

Good.

Life before ore.

Zee moved through the crater seeing folds no one else could.

“Not left!”

A team changed direction.

The ground where they would have stepped opened into silver ocean.

“Right!”

Ner shouted from the central frame.

“Anchor six first!”

Zareth’s Link flashed.

Found.

Teal-and-Gold Mana struck not the anchor itself but the support braces around it.

Careful.

Independent judgment.

The first anchor tilted.

Ner released its magical connection remotely.

The world screamed.

There was no other word.

A vibration passed through Zee’s teeth.

The black grass outside the perimeter turned white.

Maeven shouted, “That made it worse!”

Ner ignored her.

“Anchor two!”

Zee saw the folds.

“No!”

Everyone stopped.

Ner looked at him.

Zee pointed to the map.

“If two releases now, that whole side overlaps.”

Ner stared.

Then recalculated.

“You are right.”

That scared Zee.

Ner rarely said it that quickly.

“Four.”

Rez relayed.

Zareth turned midair.

The Dragon Link carried understanding.

Anchor four fell.

A section of artificial normalcy vanished.

Gravity changed.

Three researchers lifted from the ground.

Zee jumped.

Caught one.

Rez grabbed another.

Ner threw a Golden plane beneath the third and rotated it until the woman dropped safely.

Maeven stared.

“This is impossible.”

Zee shouted, “Stop saying that!”

Another tremor.

The artificial sun cracked.

White-green fire leaked through the cage.

Zareth screamed through the Link.

Pain.

Zee’s Dragon nature answered.

The fire hurt Dragons.

Not ordinary flame.

Something about forced stability cut against what Dragon magic sensed in the deep wild.

Zee’s scales spread.

He looked at the core.

Darkness stirred.

Destroy it.

Easy.

No.

Not yet.

Rez saw him.

“Zee!”

“I know!”

“Good!”

Ner shouted:

“Three and seven together!”

Zee checked the folds.

“Yes!”

Zareth hit seven.

Zee ran toward three.

Maeven shouted, “What are you doing?”

“Helping!”

“There is a release lever!”

“Where?”

“Inside the frame!”

Zee smiled.

“Of course.”

He entered.

White-green fire struck his arm.

Pain tore through him.

His Crimson scales blackened.

Did not die.

Did not stop.

His body adapted enough to keep moving.

Not immunity.

Resistance growing second by second.

The fire bit differently each time.

His Crimson Mana changed around it.

Less like a shield.

More like a refusal to let the forced stability define his blood.

Unique.

His.

Zee reached the lever.

Pulled.

Anchor three released.

At the same instant, Zareth tore seven free.

The artificial sun imploded.

Not exploded.

Light collapsed inward.

The crater disappeared.

For one heartbeat Zee stood nowhere.

No ground.

No sky.

No sound.

Then Ner’s hand caught his.

Golden Mana.

Boundary.

Rez grabbed Ner.

Zareth’s Link hit Zee like a chain of instinct.

Together.

Four points.

Not enough to control the deep wild.

Enough not to lose one another.

The world returned.

Black grass.

Silver rain upward.

No camp.

No crater.

No artificial sun.

Researchers scattered across the field.

Alive.

Maeven lay twenty yards away staring at the sky.

All seven anchors were gone.

The deep wild settled.

Slowly.

The strange mountains returned to impossible positions.

The migrating herd began moving.

The antlered creature rose.

Zee lay on his back breathing hard.

Ner’s hand was still locked around his.

Neither released.

Rez sat nearby covered in black grass.

Zareth landed with enough force to shake silver rain from the sky.

The Link flooded Zee.

Alive.

Angry.

Relieved.

Zee laughed.

Ner looked at him.

“What?”

“We fixed it.”

Rez groaned.

“We survived fixing it.”

“Same thing.”

“No.”

The antlered creature approached.

Every researcher tensed.

Zee lifted one hand.

“Do not.”

They listened.

Good.

The creature stopped near Maeven.

She stared up at it.

No weapon.

No spell.

For the first time, perhaps, no assumption.

The creature lowered its antlers.

A fold opened between them.

Maeven saw another landscape.

A migration.

Broken paths reconnecting.

Then the view closed.

Maeven whispered:

“We were in the way.”

Zee sat up.

“Yes.”

She looked toward him.

Not angry.

Ashamed.

“We thought the instability was the danger.”

Ner finally released Zee’s hand.

“Sometimes the instability is just the rule.”

Maeven nodded.

That lesson hurt her.

Good.

Lessons should.

Rez stood.

“What happens to your research?”

Maeven looked toward the destroyed equipment.

“Over.”

One of her researchers protested.

“Doctor—”

“No.”

She looked toward the migrating herd.

“We entered without understanding enough to know what damage meant.”

Then toward the surviving staff.

“We leave.”

Zee watched her.

No villain defeated.

No execution.

No glorious victory.

Just a woman realizing curiosity had become entitlement somewhere along the way.

Zee understood that danger.

He felt it himself.

He wanted to know everything about the deep wild.

Wanted to touch the folds.

Test the Crimson perception.

See what else existed beyond kingdom maps.

But wanting knowledge did not make everything his to open.

That mattered.

The antlered creature turned.

Walked toward a fold.

Then stopped.

Looked back.

Not at the researchers.

At the four.

Zee felt one final concept.

Not invitation.

Not friendship.

Recognition.

Different.

Allowed.

For now.

Then it vanished.

No door.

No spectacle.

One step here.

Next step elsewhere.

Zareth watched.

The Link carried wonder.

Ner stared at the empty space.

Rez exhaled.

“Well.”

Zee smiled.

“What?”

Rez looked around at silver rain, impossible mountains, and a herd walking through the side of a hill.

“We need better maps.”

Ner laughed.

Zee did too.

The known world had become smaller again.

Not because they had conquered the deep wild.

Because they had discovered that something could exist beyond kingdoms without asking to become one.

And somewhere beyond those folding horizons, the world continued.

Older.

Stranger.

Alive.

Waiting for no prince.

Not even Zee Darkheart.
