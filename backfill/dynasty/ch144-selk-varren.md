Chapter One Hundred Forty-Four

Selk Varren

Selk Varren had spent years preparing to survive Zee Darkheart.

That was why his house had no windows.

Why the outer walls contained silver.

Why Wolfbane grew beneath the southern foundation.

Why every corridor could flood with scent-destroying smoke.

Why Dragonbane had been hammered into the support beams.

Why suppression stones sat beneath every floor.

Why six separate escape routes existed beneath the estate.

Selk had prepared for Witch.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Dragon.

He had updated those preparations every year.

And when the message arrived that Merek Voss had disappeared inside Darkheart—

Selk did not panic.

He smiled.

“So.”

The older man folded the message.

“He finally came.”

Selk Varren was Human.

Entirely.

No supernatural blood.

No hidden ancestry.

No magic.

And that had never stopped him from becoming one of the most respected suppression architects money could buy.

He understood supernatural power because he had spent his life studying how it behaved.

Magic had patterns.

Predators had instincts.

Strength created habits.

Speed created confidence.

Immortality created arrogance.

Everything could be studied.

Everything could be interrupted.

Including Zee Darkheart.

Selk knew because once—

he had helped prove it.

⸻

Three days after Merek disappeared, Selk stood inside his underground command chamber.

Maps covered one wall.

Weapons another.

And across the largest table—

years of research.

ZEE DARKHEART.

Old sketches.

Witness accounts.

Battle reports.

Known Witch responses.

Vampire movement patterns.

Wolf pursuit behavior.

Dragon durability.

Later reports regarding the Crimson Sovereign.

Rumors about Phoenix wings had been added.

But rumors were not data.

Selk did not trust rumors.

One of his guards entered.

“Southern perimeter is clear.”

“Eastern?”

“Clear.”

“Air?”

“Nothing.”

Selk nodded.

“Then he is not here.”

A voice behind him answered.

“Yes, I am.”

Selk froze.

Every guard turned.

Zee sat on the edge of the research table.

One leg crossed over the other.

He was holding one of Selk’s files.

Reading it.

Selk stared.

Zee flipped a page.

“You spelled my mother’s name wrong.”

Nobody moved.

Zee looked up.

“Aurelia.”

He tapped the page.

“One L.”

Selk’s hand moved beneath the table.

Zee smiled.

“Go ahead.”

Selk pressed the trigger.

The entire room activated.

⸻

Suppression hit first.

A brutal field exploded through the chamber.

Witch Mana interference.

Spell disruption.

Casting destabilization.

Zee felt it pass through him.

Did nothing.

Selk watched carefully.

Second layer.

Silver mechanisms opened inside the walls.

Anti-Vampire frequencies filled the chamber.

Nothing.

Third.

Wolf irritants flooded the vents.

Nothing.

Fourth.

Dragonbane bolts fired from hidden compartments.

Zee moved.

Black-crimson wings erupted behind him.

One sweep.

The bolts shattered against Phoenix force.

Selk’s smile disappeared.

Zee remained sitting.

“Finished?”

Selk stared at the wings.

Black-crimson.

Scarlet burning through the feathers.

Not Dragon.

Not anything in his files.

He immediately changed tactics.

“Containment seven.”

The floor opened.

Metal restraints launched upward.

Designed for Zee’s known strength.

Zee let them catch him.

Wrists.

Ankles.

Torso.

Wings.

The restraints locked.

Selk breathed.

There.

Zee looked down.

Selk approached slowly.

“You’ve changed.”

“Yes.”

“But not enough.”

Zee looked amused.

Selk stopped ten feet away.

“I studied you before people understood what you were.”

“You studied reports.”

“I predicted you.”

“You did.”

Selk smiled again.

“You always answer confinement with force.”

Zee said nothing.

“You test the restraint. Then Witchcraft. If magic fails, Vampire speed. If movement fails, Dragon strength. If—”

Black-crimson fire appeared beneath Zee’s skin.

Selk stopped.

The restraints began glowing.

Not melting.

Glowing.

Then Zee’s Phoenix wings folded inward.

Heat intensified.

The restraints turned white.

Selk backed away.

Zee had not pulled once.

Had not tested their strength.

Had not used Witchcraft.

Had not used Vampire speed.

Had not used Wolf instinct.

Had not used Dragon force.

The metal liquefied.

Zee stood.

Selk whispered, “Phoenix.”

“Closer.”

“What are you?”

Zee stepped through the molten restraints.

“The First Natural Abysscin.”

Selk’s face tightened.

That word existed nowhere in his research.

Zee smiled.

“There’s your problem.”

⸻

Selk ran.

Not blindly.

He hit a wall mechanism.

The chamber split into six corridors.

Doors slammed between them.

False routes opened.

Gas released.

Pressure traps activated.

Zee stayed where he was.

He did not chase.

Selk moved through the western route.

Changed to northern.

Dropped two levels.

Entered the secondary tunnel.

His guards scattered deliberately.

Different directions.

Different scents.

Different footfalls.

Everything designed to complicate Zee’s Wolf tracking.

Except Zee was not using his Wolf.

He stood in the command chamber.

Closed his eyes.

And allowed Phoenix heat to expand.

Not fire.

Heat.

Controlled.

Through stone.

Through metal.

Through the architecture.

One corridor carried colder outside air.

An escape tunnel.

Zee opened his eyes.

His wings spread.

One violent beat—

and he was gone.

⸻

Selk reached the surface.

Night air struck his face.

A carriage waited.

Two horses.

Three armed guards.

He climbed aboard.

“Move.”

The carriage launched forward.

Selk looked behind them.

Nothing.

Good.

He exhaled.

Then the horses screamed.

Black-crimson fire landed across the road.

Not touching them.

A wall.

Perfectly controlled.

The driver pulled hard.

The carriage turned.

Phoenix pressure slammed into its side.

Wood exploded.

Selk rolled across the ground.

He came up with a silver weapon already in his hand.

Zee landed.

Human form.

Phoenix wings behind him.

Selk fired.

The projectile entered Zee’s shoulder.

Deep.

Selk smiled.

Zee looked down.

Blood ran.

Then stopped.

The wound closed around the projectile.

Black-crimson heat pushed the metal back out.

It dropped onto the road.

Selk’s smile vanished.

Zee looked at his healed shoulder.

Then at Selk.

“That one would’ve been annoying years ago.”

Selk backed away.

“This isn’t possible.”

“It clearly is.”

“You cannot simply become something nobody prepared for.”

Zee laughed softly.

“You helped.”

Selk’s face changed.

Zee approached.

“That night.”

Selk understood.

“No.”

“Yes.”

“We defeated you.”

“Yes.”

The answer came without hesitation.

Selk stared.

Zee did not rewrite it.

Did not diminish it.

Did not pretend otherwise.

“You did.”

Selk’s pride returned.

Just slightly.

“It was perfect.”

Zee’s smile disappeared.

Selk continued.

“Do you know what that operation was?”

“Yes.”

“One of the greatest tactical accomplishments of my life.”

Silence.

Selk’s eyes brightened with old professional pride.

“You had answers for everything. So we stopped giving you one problem at a time.”

Zee remembered stone against his face.

Selk remembered timing charts.

That difference sat between them.

“You were extraordinary,” Selk said. “Which made solving you extraordinary.”

Zee’s wings opened wider.

“You went home proud.”

“Yes.”

“I went home different.”

Selk finally heard the danger.

⸻

He raised both hands.

“I was not the commissioner.”

“I know.”

“I never met them.”

“I know.”

“I built suppression timing.”

“I know.”

“I didn’t choose the contract.”

Zee stopped.

“You chose to help.”

Selk’s jaw tightened.

“It was work.”

There it was.

Zee stared at him.

Selk did not apologize.

Perhaps that was the only respectable thing about him.

“It wasn’t personal.”

Black-crimson fire crawled over Zee’s fingers.

“For you.”

Selk swallowed.

Zee stepped closer.

“For you, I was timing.”

Another step.

“Patterns.”

Another.

“A problem.”

Selk stood his ground.

“For me, you were one of the people who helped put my face against stone.”

The fire darkened.

Scarlet burned beneath.

“So now it is personal.”

⸻

Selk attacked first.

Not with suppression.

A blade.

Human.

Simple.

Fast.

Zee moved his wing.

The blade struck black-crimson feathers.

Snapped.

Selk drove a second weapon toward Zee’s throat.

Zee caught his wrist.

No Vampire speed.

Phoenix movement.

Selk twisted.

Kicked.

Zee released him.

Selk rolled backward.

Three guards attacked from opposite sides.

Zee’s wings moved.

Phoenix force threw two into the dirt.

The third stopped.

Zee looked at him.

The man hesitated.

He had not been part of Chapter 128.

Just hired protection.

“Leave.”

The guard looked toward Selk.

Then ran.

Selk watched him go.

“You’re letting him live?”

“He didn’t jump me.”

That answer terrified Selk more than indiscriminate killing would have.

Zee knew exactly why he was here.

⸻

Selk activated his final defense.

A ring beneath the road ignited.

Dragon-resistant alloy rose around Zee.

Witch suppression layered over it.

Silver spikes locked inward.

Wolf irritants sprayed.

Selk backed away.

Four different counters.

Together.

Just like before.

For one second—

Zee disappeared inside.

Selk smiled.

Then black-crimson fire leaked through every seam.

His smile faded.

Scarlet followed.

The structure began shaking.

Selk stared.

“No.”

The entire containment system erupted outward.

Zee stepped through the fire.

Untouched.

His wings stretched behind him.

Selk finally understood.

Not that Zee had become impossible to hurt.

Not that his old work had been foolish.

Something worse.

His work had been good.

His conclusions had been good.

His preparation had been excellent.

For the wrong man.

“You knew,” Selk whispered.

Zee tilted his head.

“You let me activate everything.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Zee smiled.

“I wanted you to know it wouldn’t matter.”

Selk stared at him.

Then laughed once.

Bitterly.

“You really did learn from us.”

Zee’s smile widened.

“That part was free.”

⸻

Selk’s fear settled.

Not disappeared.

Settled.

He knew he was dead.

Professionals behaved differently once uncertainty ended.

He lowered his hands.

“Then ask.”

Zee’s expression changed.

“Ask what?”

“The question you came with.”

“I came to kill you.”

“You always have another question.”

Zee studied him.

Selk smiled faintly.

“I studied you, remember?”

Zee almost laughed.

Almost.

“Fine.”

He stepped closer.

“Who kept the research?”

Selk’s eyes flickered.

That was enough.

Zee noticed.

“Who?”

Selk looked away.

“Sera.”

Sera Quill.

The tactician.

One of the eight.

“She kept copies?”

“Copies?”

Selk laughed.

“She kept everything.”

Zee’s expression hardened.

“Why?”

“Because she never believed the job was finished.”

That mattered.

Selk continued.

“After the operation, most of us moved on. Sera didn’t.”

“How long?”

“Years.”

“Monitoring me?”

“You.”

Selk paused.

“Darkheart.”

Zee went still.

“Explain.”

“She continued collecting reports. Battles. New abilities. Changes in your behavior. Your kingdom. People close to you.”

The air became hotter.

Selk saw it.

“She wasn’t commissioned to continue. Not as far as I know.”

“Then why?”

“Because you became the only target she never understood completely.”

Selk’s mouth twisted.

“She hates incomplete work.”

Zee’s eyes burned Crimson-Gold.

“Where is she?”

“I don’t know.”

Zee’s wings shifted.

“I genuinely don’t.”

Selk continued quickly.

“But she contacted Jorren six months ago.”

Zee stopped.

Jorren Vale.

Strike leader.

The man who had been there.

“Why?”

“I don’t know.”

“Did Jorren answer?”

“Yes.”

“How do you know?”

“Because Sera asked me for updated suppression designs two weeks later.”

There.

The board changed.

Sera and Jorren were still connected.

And someone had continued studying Zee long after Chapter 128.

Selk smiled weakly.

“Useful?”

“Yes.”

“Enough to live?”

“No.”

Selk nodded.

“Thought so.”

⸻

Zee raised one hand.

Black-crimson fire formed.

Selk looked at it.

Then at him.

“One thing.”

Zee waited.

“We did beat you.”

“Yes.”

“Don’t pretend we didn’t.”

Zee’s face remained calm.

“I never have.”

Selk nodded.

For some reason, that seemed to matter to him.

Zee stepped closer.

“You won that night.”

Scarlet ignited beneath the black flame.

Selk held his gaze.

Zee’s voice dropped.

“This one is mine.”

The fire moved.

Selk Varren died without screaming for long.

Zee made certain of that.

⸻

By dawn, Selk’s estate still stood.

Most of it.

His guards lived if they had been smart enough not to force Zee’s hand.

His research did not.

Black-crimson fire consumed every page involving Darkheart.

Every timing chart.

Every suppression model.

Every study of Zee’s family.

Every copied weakness.

Except one bundle.

Sera Quill’s correspondence.

Zee took that.

Then he left.

Behind him, one sentence began moving through the supernatural world.

Selk Varren was dead.

And Zee Darkheart had come personally.
