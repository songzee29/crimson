Chapter Seventy-Nine

The Wolf Who Refused to Die

Summer came with war.

Not a grand one.

A border war.

Those were sometimes worse.

The Kingdom of Brann wanted three miles of Darkheart forest and the iron beneath it.

Nothing mystical.

Nothing prophetic.

Land.

Money.

Power.

They invaded with soldiers, Wolves, and Witch artillery.

Darkheart pushed them back.

Brann returned with silver.

Darkheart adapted.

Brann returned with fire.

Darkheart reinforced.

Then Brann learned about Kael.

The third assault carried weapons built specifically for hybrid bodies.

They did not know enough to make them effective.

They killed ordinary defenders instead.

That was worse.

⸻

Zee reached the southern ridge halfway through the battle.

Too late for the first line.

Again.

Nine Darkheart soldiers already dead.

Seventeen wounded.

Kael fought among them.

Witch sigils flashed from one hand.

Vampire speed carried him between soldiers.

He caught a silver-tipped spear through the side.

Snapped the shaft.

Kept moving.

Alive.

Harder to kill.

Exactly what Zee wanted.

Then Zee smelled someone else.

Vampire.

Darkheart.

Dying.

He found her beneath a collapsed barricade.

Her name was Tessa Vale.

Twenty-eight.

Vampire for eleven years.

Former traveling healer before turning.

Joined Darkheart after her old Vampire nest demanded she feed from unwilling humans.

She refused.

They broke both her hands.

Zee’s people found her half-dead on a road two years earlier.

Tessa stayed.

She learned battlefield medicine.

Never became a great fighter.

Did not want to.

She saved people instead.

Today she had pulled four soldiers out of Witchfire before the ridge collapsed on her.

One leg was crushed.

Half her chest burned.

Worse—

a Wolf weapon had torn through her abdomen.

Not ordinary claws.

A curse-treated blade carrying concentrated Wolf venom designed to overwhelm Vampire healing.

Tessa’s regeneration had stopped.

Zee lifted the stone from her.

She opened one eye.

“King.”

“Don’t talk.”

“That bad?”

“Yes.”

“Wonderful.”

He almost smiled.

Then she coughed.

Blood.

Vampire.

Old healing energy failing.

A Darkheart medic knelt.

“Her body is rejecting everything.”

Zee already knew.

Human blood would not save her.

Vampire blood would not.

Healing magic failed around the Wolf venom.

Zee looked toward the battlefield.

Kael.

Alive because of him.

Ner.

Alive because of him.

Two times.

Two different outcomes.

His blood did not simply make copies.

It met the person already there.

Preserved.

Added.

Changed.

Maybe.

Tessa saw the look.

“No.”

Zee looked back.

“You know what I’m thinking?”

“Everyone knows what you’re thinking now.”

“That sounds invasive.”

“Your face is terrible at secrets.”

She coughed again.

Pain twisted her.

Zee crouched lower.

“Tessa.”

“No experiments.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

“I’m offering survival.”

Her expression shifted.

“What happened to Kael?”

“Maybe.”

“Maybe?”

“I still do not know what determines it.”

“Excellent sales pitch.”

He almost laughed.

Then her heartbeat stumbled.

No time.

Zee looked directly at her.

“If I do nothing, you die.”

Tessa closed her eyes.

“I know.”

“If I give you my blood, you may still die.”

“I know.”

“You may change.”

Silence.

She opened her eyes.

“Into what?”

“I do not know.”

The battlefield thundered behind them.

Tessa looked past Zee.

At Darkheart soldiers holding the line.

At Kael moving impossibly fast between them.

At a young Wolf guard dragging a wounded Witch to safety.

Then back to Zee.

“Will I still be me?”

Zee answered carefully.

“Kael is.”

“Ner is.”

“That is all I can promise.”

Tessa breathed once.

Then:

“Do it.”

Zee opened his wrist.

⸻

She drank.

One swallow.

Nothing.

Second.

Her Vampire nature reacted violently.

Fangs extended.

Veins darkened.

The Wolf venom inside her surged.

Zee almost pulled away.

Tessa grabbed his wrist.

“More.”

He let her.

Third swallow.

Her heartbeat slammed.

Vampire regeneration restarted.

Then something else happened.

The Wolf venom did not burn out.

It changed direction.

Not poison anymore.

Catalyst.

Tessa screamed.

Bones shifted beneath skin.

Not transformation.

Not yet.

Muscles tightened.

Her scent changed.

Zee froze.

Wolf.

Inside Vampire.

Real.

Her Vampire blood remained.

No erasure.

No replacement.

A second supernatural nature was entering beside it.

Her crushed leg snapped back into place violently.

She screamed louder.

Claws erupted from her hands.

Not Vampire nails.

Wolf claws.

Her eyes opened.

Predatory.

Wild.

Still Tessa.

Then she bit Zee’s wrist harder by instinct.

He did not pull away.

“Enough.”

She growled.

Actually growled.

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“Enough.”

Vampire dominance hit.

Tessa froze.

Then—

resisted.

Not much.

But more than before.

Wolf aggression pushing against Vampire submission.

Interesting.

Zee tightened the command.

“Release.”

She did.

Immediately recoiled.

Horror filled her face.

“I bit you.”

“Yes.”

“I’m sorry.”

“Do not start that.”

“I lost control.”

“You were dying.”

“That is not a defense.”

Good.

Still herself.

Zee smiled faintly.

“No.”

Then the Brann artillery fired again.

The battlefield remembered them.

Zee stood.

“Tessa, stay here.”

She pushed herself upright.

Her crushed leg held.

Zee stared.

“So that is a no.”

Tessa looked equally shocked.

“I think my leg works.”

The medic whispered:

“That was pulverized.”

Tessa stood.

Vampire healing alone should not have repaired it that fast under Wolf venom.

But the venom was no longer venom.

The Wolf nature had changed everything.

Tessa looked toward the battle.

Then her nose twitched.

She froze.

“What?”

“I can smell them.”

“Who?”

“Brann.”

She turned toward the southern woods.

“Flanking.”

Zee heard nothing yet.

Vampire senses caught heartbeats seconds later.

She had scented them first.

Wolf.

Complete enough to matter immediately.

Zee smiled.

“Stay behind me.”

Tessa looked insulted.

“I am older than you.”

“I am King.”

“That has nothing to do with age.”

Fair.

They went together.

⸻

Brann’s flank expected wounded defenders.

Instead they found Zee Darkheart.

That was unfortunate.

They also found Tessa.

That was historic.

The first Wolf attacked her.

Tessa moved on Vampire instinct.

Too fast.

Caught his arm.

Then Wolf instinct changed the angle.

She twisted with the attack rather than against it.

Claws extended.

The enemy smelled her.

His face changed.

“Vampire.”

Tessa smiled.

“Yes.”

Then her own scent reached him.

The Wolf froze.

“No.”

Tessa looked briefly confused.

Then understood.

“Yes.”

She threw him through a tree.

Zee almost laughed.

No time.

Ten attackers.

He took four.

Silent Witchcraft slammed one into another.

Vampire speed put him behind the third.

Assassin knife ended the fourth.

Tessa fought two.

Not polished.

Not Kael.

Not Zee.

New.

Her Vampire strength hit first.

Wolf aggression followed.

She took a blade through the shoulder, snarled, healed, and kept moving.

One attacker fled.

Tessa’s head snapped toward him.

Pursuit instinct.

Zee saw it arrive.

The Wolf inside her wanted chase.

She wanted it too.

Then remembered the wounded behind them.

Choice.

She stayed.

Good.

Not controlled by nature.

Person.

The remaining Brann soldiers surrendered when Zee’s predatory presence rolled through the trees.

He looked at them.

“Leave Darkheart.”

They obeyed.

Not compulsion.

Fear.

Better.

⸻

The war ended before nightfall.

Brann withdrew after losing its third artillery line.

No conquest.

No annexation.

Darkheart kept its forest.

That should have been the story.

It was not.

The story became Tessa Vale.

A Vampire battlefield healer entered the battle dying.

She walked out with a Wolf’s claws.

Kael found her sitting beside a medical wagon.

He stared.

Tessa stared back.

“You too?”

Kael smiled.

“Apparently.”

Ner arrived later.

Gold.

Bloodsong.

Examination with permission.

He listened to Tessa’s blood.

Then looked at Zee.

“Vampire.”

“Yes.”

“Wolf.”

“Yes.”

“Both stable.”

There it was again.

Kael leaned against the wagon.

“So what do we call this one?”

Tessa glared.

“This one has a name.”

Kael immediately raised both hands.

“Fair.”

Zee smiled.

Good.

Person first.

Tessa looked toward her claws.

They retracted slowly.

“Vampire-Wolf.”

Ner nodded.

“Seems obvious.”

Tessa looked at Kael.

“You got Witch-Vampire?”

“Yes.”

“That is disappointingly practical.”

“We are founding history under pressure.”

“Could have tried harder.”

Zee laughed.

Actual laughter.

Short.

Rare.

Tessa looked toward him.

Then became serious.

“You knew this might happen?”

“No.”

“You hoped?”

Zee considered.

“I hoped you would live.”

“That is not the same.”

“No.”

She nodded.

Good.

⸻

By autumn, two original hybrids existed inside Darkheart.

Kael Marrin.

Witch-Vampire.

Tessa Vale.

Vampire-Wolf.

Neither belonged to Zee.

Neither served because blood demanded it.

Kael stayed in the guard.

Tessa returned to battlefield medicine and started learning her Wolf senses because apparently smelling infections before symptoms appeared was useful.

Neither became an army.

But the world did not care about nuance.

Rumors multiplied.

King Zee can combine species.

True, apparently.

King Zee can build supernatural bloodlines at will.

False.

King Zee is replacing ordinary soldiers.

False.

King Zee can create Tribrids.

Unknown and deeply alarming.

Darkheart is manufacturing monsters.

Wrong.

Then the first border kingdom closed trade.

A second recalled its ambassador.

A third sent spies.

A Vampire court offered Tessa a fortune to defect.

A Witch kingdom tried bribing Kael.

Both refused.

Their choice.

Zee approved.

Then refugees increased.

Because fear worked both directions.

If the old kingdoms feared hybrids—

people with dangerous blood knew exactly where to run.

Darkheart.

The gates widened.

New homes spread beyond Victor’s old walls.

Markets changed.

Patrols became mixed.

Witch.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Now two hybrids among them.

Zee stood on the western tower with Ner one evening.

Below, Kael argued with Tessa over whether Vampire hunger became worse when Wolf instinct wanted the same prey.

Tessa claimed yes.

Kael claimed she was dramatic.

They nearly started fighting.

Ner smiled.

“They are becoming friends.”

Zee watched.

“Terrible.”

“You like it.”

“Yes.”

Then his expression cooled.

Beyond Darkheart’s walls, another refugee caravan approached.

More people.

More responsibility.

More targets.

Ner looked toward him.

“Two.”

Zee knew what he meant.

Two hybrids.

“Yes.”

“What happens when the world sees enough of them?”

Zee watched children running beside the incoming wagons.

“They attack harder.”

“And then?”

His crimson-and-gold eyes shifted slowly into slits.

Zee looked over the kingdom the old world kept trying to frighten back into obedience.

Witches.

Vampires.

Wolves.

Outcasts.

Mixed families.

Kael.

Tessa.

People choosing him.

Then he answered.

“Then we survive harder.”

Far beyond the walls, rulers were beginning to ask how many new bloodlines King Zee Darkheart could create.

Inside Darkheart, Zee was asking a different question.

How many burials could he prevent?

And with every attack, the answer was becoming more dangerous.
