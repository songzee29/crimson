Chapter Sixty-Two

The Crimson Demonic Dragon

The God ran for six years.

Not every day.

Not every country.

Not in panic.

Gods did not run like frightened men.

They vanished into temples that did not admit mortals.

Crossed kingdoms through worship.

Changed names.

Changed faces.

Used priests, mercenaries, and monsters as distance.

Zee lived anyway.

He became King.

He built power.

He loved Ner.

He fought beside Rez.

He watched Zareth grow into Drakonis authority.

He buried more enemies than he bothered counting.

But he never forgot the seventh heartbeat that had escaped Aurelia’s death.

Now there was nowhere left to go.

The God stood alone on a black plateau above a dead sea.

No temple.

No followers.

No army.

Only wind.

Zee walked toward him.

The God smiled.

“So.”

Zee said nothing.

“You finally found me.”

Still nothing.

The God’s smile weakened.

Zee’s crimson eyes stayed fixed on him.

Human pupils.

For now.

No guards followed.

Rez knew where Zee had gone.

Ner knew.

Zareth knew.

None had tried to stop him.

This belonged to Zee.

The God tilted his head.

“You look different.”

Zee stopped twenty feet away.

“So do you.”

The God laughed.

“Do you remember my face?”

“No.”

That bothered him.

Zee continued.

“I remember your blood scent.”

The smile disappeared.

Good.

Zee inhaled once.

Divine blood.

Old.

Powerful.

Hidden under incense, cold air, and something metallic.

Same as six years ago.

No mistake.

The God lifted one hand.

Gold-white force slammed across the plateau.

Zee moved before it hit.

Vampire speed.

Assassin angle.

Not away.

Inside.

He crossed half the distance before the God corrected.

A second blast hit Zee in the chest.

Stone shattered beneath him.

He flew backward.

Rolled.

Stopped on one knee.

Blood touched his mouth.

The God smiled again.

“There.”

Zee stood.

“You expected more?”

“I expected grief.”

Zee wiped his lip.

“You are six years late.”

Then disappeared.

⸻

The God was fast.

Not Vampire fast.

Different.

Distance folded around his body through divine authority.

One moment near.

Next far.

Zee stopped trying to chase the body.

Wolf instinct tracked pressure through the ground.

Vampire blood sense followed the divine pulse beneath false distance.

Assassin training watched where the God wanted Zee looking.

Witchcraft touched only what mattered.

The God appeared behind him.

Zee flicked two fingers.

The divine spear changed angle by inches.

Enough.

It missed his spine.

Zee turned.

Claws tore across the God’s face.

Wolf.

Not magic.

The God staggered.

Surprise.

Zee smelled divine blood.

Real.

He smiled.

Slit pupils.

The God touched his cheek.

Looked at the gold-red blood.

“You wounded me.”

“Yes.”

“How?”

Zee attacked again.

No explanation.

The God blocked his knife.

Zee let it go.

Trap.

Left hand caught the wrist.

Right elbow hit the throat.

Dragon durability met divine force when the God countered with a palm strike.

Zee’s bones screamed.

Did not break.

Not all of them.

He grabbed the arm anyway.

Vampire strength.

Wolf leverage.

Assassin precision.

He threw the God across the plateau.

The God stopped in midair.

Zee barely raised one hand.

“Fall.”

Crimson Witchcraft struck.

Simple.

Sharp.

The God dropped hard enough to split black stone.

Zee was on him before the impact finished.

Knife to throat.

The God caught his wrist.

Divine power exploded.

Zee’s hand burned.

Scales blackened.

Regeneration began immediately.

The God kicked him away.

Zee landed badly.

One knee.

One hand.

Breathing harder now.

Good.

The God stood.

“You cannot kill me.”

Zee looked up.

“I heard that before.”

“From whom?”

“My father.”

The God’s face changed.

Zee rose.

“He was wrong too.”

⸻

The fight lasted longer than Zee wanted.

Long enough for the plateau to crack.

Long enough for Zee to bleed from six places.

Long enough for the God to learn that killing Zee was not the same as hurting him.

Divine force crushed his shoulder.

It repaired.

A blade of light opened his abdomen.

His Vampire regeneration pulled the wound closed faster after Zee fed from his own bitten tongue and forced blood back into damaged tissue.

A divine command struck his mind.

“Kneel.”

Zee’s body buckled.

One knee touched stone.

The God smiled.

Then Zee looked up.

Crimson eyes brightened.

His will pushed back.

Vampire compulsion.

Predatory dominance.

Witch resistance.

Years of hostile magic rewritten into Crimson tolerance.

“No.”

The divine command tightened.

Zee’s horns heated.

Scales spread across his shoulders.

The ridges beneath his back burned.

Something deep moved.

Not old adaptation.

Not Demon.

Not Vampire.

Dragon.

The God frowned.

“What is that?”

Zee did not know.

The pressure doubled.

His Dragon blood answered.

For years it had existed beneath everything.

Born with him.

Awakened slowly.

Horns.

Scales.

Heat.

Durability.

Instinct.

Never wings.

Never transformation.

Never Zareth.

Now it simply finished becoming itself.

No ritual.

No voice.

No destiny.

The sleeping line in Zee’s blood opened.

Completely.

His body locked.

Then heat tore through every vein.

Crimson-black scales spread farther over his neck, chest, ribs, and spine.

His horns sharpened.

Darkened.

Became more severe. More crown-like.

The structures beneath his shoulder blades hardened into powerful ridges.

Nothing opened from them.

No wings.

No tail.

No Dragon body.

Zee remained Zee.

But the blood was no longer sleeping.

Dragon.

Fully active.

The God stepped back.

Actually stepped back.

Zee noticed.

That pleased him.

“What are you?”

Zee stood.

The divine command shattered.

“I told you.”

His pupils became slits.

“Zee.”

⸻

The second half of the fight was worse for the God.

Not easy.

Never easy.

But different.

Zee’s Dragon instincts changed how he met force.

He stopped yielding ground unnecessarily.

His body heat climbed.

Divine blades that had cut deep now struggled longer against darkened Crimson scales.

Zee caught one bare-handed.

It burned through his palm.

He held it anyway.

Closed his fist.

“Break.”

Witchcraft and physical force hit together.

The divine weapon fractured.

The God stared.

Zee headbutted him.

Mature horns opened the God’s brow.

Blood ran.

Zee smelled it.

Hunger hit immediately.

Not ordinary hunger.

Curiosity.

Power.

Six years.

Mother.

The God tried to escape.

Zee expected it.

Assassin countertracking had already taught him every visible pattern in the God’s retreat.

Wolf instinct knew which direction fear preferred.

Vampire blood sense found the pulse.

Zee vanished.

Appeared in front of him.

The God stopped.

“No.”

Zee almost smiled.

“There it is.”

The God struck.

Zee took the blow across scaled ribs.

Caught his arm.

Twisted.

Bone cracked.

Divine bone.

The scream was satisfying.

Zee did not hide that.

He drove the God to the ground.

Knee against the spine.

One hand gripping hair.

The God whispered:

“You do not know what happens if you drink me.”

Zee’s fangs descended.

“No.”

That frightened the God more than certainty would have.

“Then stop.”

Zee leaned closer.

“You watched my mother die.”

“I did not strike her.”

“You came with the ones who did.”

“That is not the same.”

Zee’s voice stayed calm.

“It is enough.”

He bit.

⸻

God blood did not taste like power.

It tasted like contradiction.

Cold and burning.

Heavy and weightless.

Ancient enough that Zee’s body rejected it for one violent instant.

Then his Crimson nature took control.

He drank harder.

The God screamed.

Divine blood hit Zee’s Vampire nature first.

His heart hammered.

Then Witch.

Crimson Mana flared.

Then Wolf.

Every predatory instinct became razor-sharp.

Then Dragon.

The newly awakened natural bloodline surged.

And something old answered from deeper inside Zee.

Demon blood.

Years processed.

Years rewritten.

Never gone.

The old Demonic adaptation collided with true divine blood.

The reaction nearly tore Zee apart.

He stopped feeding for half a second.

The God tried to move.

Zee snarled.

Bit deeper.

Kept drinking.

Divine blood rejected Demon influence.

Demonic adaptation rejected divine presence.

Dragon blood reacted to both.

Three incompatible forces crashed through Zee’s body.

None belonged in control.

His Crimson biology refused all of them.

So it rewrote.

Zee screamed against the God’s throat.

Crimson-black fire moved under his scales without burning outside.

His horns lengthened slightly.

Sharpened.

The black near their bases spread farther.

His scales deepened from Crimson into dark crimson-black across broader regions of his torso and arms.

Heat rolled off him in waves.

The God weakened.

Zee did not stop.

Blood.

All of it.

Every last divine drop.

The God’s heartbeat slowed.

Then stopped.

Zee kept feeding until there was nothing left to take.

He released the corpse.

It hit stone dry.

Dead.

The seventh attacker was finished.

Aurelia’s killer had nowhere left to run.

Zee stood.

Then nearly fell.

Something inside him changed permanently.

Not a God nature.

Nothing divine settled cleanly inside him.

No domain.

No stolen authority.

No copied miracle.

The divine blood had become pressure.

The old Demon adaptation had become poison to it.

The Dragon bloodline became the battlefield.

Crimson rewrote the outcome.

The Dragon nature mutated.

Darkened.

Changed.

Not Demon.

Not Dragon alone.

Not God.

Something Zee.

His eyes burned.

When he opened them again, Crimson and Gold existed together permanently.

Not one overtaking the other.

Both.

His pupils returned human as his breathing slowed.

For one moment, Zee simply stood over the dead God.

Then the plateau shook.

Not from magic.

From witnesses.

Far across the broken ridge, surviving priests and soldiers who had hidden during the fight stared at him.

They had seen the Dragon traits fully awaken.

Seen the God fall.

Seen Zee drain him dry.

Seen the transformation afterward.

One priest whispered:

“Dragon…”

Another:

“No.”

His voice shook.

“That is not a Dragon.”

Zee looked toward them.

Every heartbeat accelerated.

Slit pupils returned.

Nobody moved.

A third man whispered the name that would escape the plateau before Zee did.

“The Crimson Demonic Dragon.”

Zee frowned.

He disliked it immediately.

Which meant it would probably survive.

⸻

Ner was the first person to touch his face when Zee returned to Darkheart.

He did not ask permission.

He simply stepped close and placed one hand against Zee’s cheek.

Zee’s pupils shifted human.

Ner noticed.

“You look terrifying.”

Zee sighed.

“Good to see you too.”

Rez walked around him once.

“More scales.”

“Yes.”

“Bigger horns.”

“Yes.”

“Eyes.”

“I noticed.”

Rez stopped in front of him.

“You killed the God.”

“Yes.”

Zareth landed beyond the courtyard.

Then froze.

The Dragon Link struck Zee harder than it had in years.

Recognition.

Shock.

Dragon.

Then—

wrong.

Not wrong as rejection.

Wrong as impossible.

Zee looked toward him.

The Link sharpened.

Zee’s Dragon blood.

Fully awake.

But changed.

Darkened by something Zareth could sense and did not recognize.

Zee walked over.

Placed one hand against Zareth’s snout.

The Dragon inhaled.

Teal-and-Gold Mana stirred.

Zee’s new Crimson Demonic Dragon force answered automatically.

Heavy.

Hot.

Predatory.

Zareth pulled back half an inch.

Zee stared.

Then Zareth pushed his forehead against Zee’s chest harder than necessary.

The Link carried fierce acceptance.

Still Zee.

Good.

That mattered.

Ner joined them.

Rez beside him.

No Aurelia.

That empty place remained.

Zee looked back toward Castle Darkheart.

King.

Tribrid.

Wolf.

Vampire.

Witch.

Dragon blood fully awake and no longer ordinary Dragon blood at all.

A title already spreading behind him.

The Crimson Demonic Dragon.

He had not chosen it.

The world had.

Zee smiled faintly.

“Terrible name.”

Rez laughed.

Ner said, “It is dramatic.”

Zareth sent approval.

Zee looked betrayed.

“Not you too.”

The moment lasted three seconds.

Then the first alarm horn sounded.

Not from Darkheart.

From the northern border.

Rez’s expression changed.

Ner turned.

Zareth lifted his head.

Zee’s smile vanished.

His pupils narrowed into slits.

The God was dead.

The world had seen what Zee became.

And apparently the world had decided the intelligent response was to attack immediately.

Zee flexed one blood-warm hand.

“Good.”

Rez looked toward him.

“That sounded concerning.”

Zee started walking.

“It was.”
