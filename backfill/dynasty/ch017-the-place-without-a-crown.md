Chapter Seventeen

The Place Without a Crown

They left Vesper territory six days later.

This time, not even the palace servants knew.

Lucien told the court Aurelia remained inside the western wing under heavy guard.

Rez was furious when he learned he would not be going.

His argument lasted long enough that Lucien threatened to lock him inside his room.

Rez responded by reminding his father that he knew three secret passages.

Lucien discovered four that afternoon.

In the end, Rez stood beside Zee’s cradle the night before departure.

He held the wooden wolf the hatchling had nearly destroyed weeks earlier.

One ear was gone.

The tail had teeth marks.

Rez placed it beside Zee.

“This time it is yours.”

The hatchling looked at it.

Rez pointed.

“No.”

The dragon looked at him.

“No.”

The hatchling took the wolf anyway.

Rez sighed.

Zee laughed.

Rez looked toward him.

“I will see you again.”

Zee reached.

Rez gave him one finger.

Crimson Mana flickered.

Blue-white answered faintly.

No danger.

Familiar.

Rez smiled.

“You better remember me.”

Aurelia watched from the doorway.

“He will.”

Rez looked at her.

“You do not know that.”

“No.”

She smiled softly.

“But I think he will.”

That was enough.

⸻

The place Aurelia chose had no kingdom.

No crown.

No walls.

No trade road.

It lay north of Vesper, beyond a mountain range so old the maps disagreed on its name.

Caldrin knew the region.

That worried everyone.

“Why do you know it?” Vale asked.

“I have had an interesting life.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It is the only one you are getting.”

The journey took eleven days.

Snow became ice.

Ice became black stone.

The forests thinned until twisted trees clung to cliffs under constant wind.

No armies followed.

No bounty hunters appeared.

No masked observers crossed their path.

The silence should have comforted Aurelia.

Instead it felt like warning.

Even the hatchling became uneasy.

The Dragon Link carried distant impressions.

High.

Cold.

Open.

Then something else.

Territory.

Not theirs.

Zee felt it too.

His wolf instincts stirred.

Vale looked toward the mountains.

“What lives here?”

Caldrin shrugged.

“Depends how unlucky we are.”

Aurelia glared.

“You brought us.”

“You chose the destination.”

“You knew the road.”

“I never claimed the destination was friendly.”

The royal witch muttered, “One day I will turn you into something small.”

Caldrin smiled.

“I have been threatened with worse.”

They reached the valley at dusk.

It was enormous.

Black mountains surrounded a frozen lake.

Steam rose from cracks in the earth.

No human settlement existed.

No castle.

No shrine.

No ruins.

Just wilderness.

Aurelia dismounted.

“This.”

Vale looked around.

“This is your plan?”

“No roads.”

“No walls.”

“No known sanctuary.”

Caldrin nodded.

“No people.”

The hatchling spread his wings.

The wind caught him.

For one terrifying second, Aurelia thought he would be thrown backward.

Instead he beat his wings.

Once.

Twice.

Then lifted.

Only a few feet.

Unsteady.

Wild.

But flying.

Zee saw him.

The Dragon Link erupted with joy.

Up.

The hatchling climbed another foot.

Then crashed into the snow.

Caldrin laughed.

Aurelia covered her mouth.

Vale shook his head.

The hatchling emerged furious.

Snow covered his face.

Zee laughed so hard he hiccuped.

The little dragon glared at him.

Then sneezed.

Teal-and-gold sparks burst from his nose.

Zee laughed harder.

For several moments, the valley felt alive.

Then the ground moved.

Everyone froze.

Not an earthquake.

Something beneath the frozen lake.

Vale drew his sword.

Caldrin stopped smiling.

The royal witch raised both hands.

Aurelia lifted Zee.

The hatchling ran back toward him.

The ice cracked.

A line spread across the lake.

Then another.

Something massive moved beneath it.

The hatchling stared.

The Dragon Link carried fear.

Big.

Zee’s heartbeat accelerated.

A shadow passed below the ice.

Long.

Too long.

The frozen surface exploded.

A creature rose from the lake.

Its body resembled a serpent covered in dark blue scales and ice-white bone plates. Four long limbs ended in claws large enough to tear horses apart. Antler-like horns curved from its skull.

Water poured from its jaws.

Its eyes opened.

Silver.

The royal witch whispered, “Frost Wyrm.”

Caldrin exhaled.

“Unlucky, then.”

The beast roared.

The valley shook.

Vale moved forward.

“Get Aurelia back.”

“There is nowhere back.”

The Frost Wyrm climbed onto the ice.

It smelled them.

Its head moved.

Vale.

Caldrin.

The witch.

Aurelia.

Then the hatchling.

The creature stopped.

The crimson dragon stared back.

Something ancient passed between them.

Not recognition.

Species.

Rival.

The hatchling spread his small wings and growled.

Caldrin looked at him.

“I admire the confidence.”

The Frost Wyrm charged.

The witch cast.

Violet force struck its skull.

The beast barely moved.

Vale and Caldrin separated.

Aurelia ran toward the rocks.

The Wyrm followed.

Not them.

Zareth.

The hatchling realized too late.

It snapped at him.

The Dragon Link flooded Zee.

Fear.

Run.

Pain coming.

Zee reacted.

Crimson Mana burst outward.

The Frost Wyrm stumbled.

Not enough.

Its claw came down.

The hatchling tried to fly.

Too slow.

The claw struck.

He disappeared into the snow.

Zee screamed.

Aurelia turned.

“No!”

The Dragon Link became pain.

Then anger.

Then something deeper.

Zee’s wolf nature surged.

His vampire senses sharpened.

His witch mana flooded outward.

All three existed already.

All three answered at once.

The air around him darkened crimson.

The Frost Wyrm turned.

Its silver eyes fixed on Zee.

The newborn stared back.

The beast roared.

Zee roared too.

Small.

But not human.

The sound carried three instincts at once.

Predator.

Magic.

Blood.

The Wyrm stopped.

Its head tilted.

Zee’s Crimson Mana pressed against it.

The beast resisted.

Unlike the Wildbloods.

Unlike the Gravehounds.

This creature was ancient enough, strong enough, and territorial enough not to simply submit.

It attacked.

The royal witch shouted.

Aurelia ran.

The Wyrm’s jaws opened.

Zee reached instinctively toward the heartbeat inside it.

Too deep.

Too strong.

He could feel it.

Could not control it.

The Wyrm struck.

Then teal-and-gold fire exploded across its eye.

The hatchling lived.

He flew badly.

One wing bent.

Blood covered his side.

But rage kept him airborne.

He attacked again.

The Wyrm snapped toward him.

Zee felt the movement through the Link.

His mana reacted.

The air around the Wyrm’s head compressed.

Its bite missed.

Vale climbed onto its side and drove his sword between bone plates.

Caldrin attacked the rear leg.

The witch cast directly into the exposed wound.

The Frost Wyrm screamed.

Its blood hit the snow.

Zee smelled it.

Cold.

Ancient.

Powerful.

His vampire nature responded violently.

Hunger.

Aurelia felt him shift.

“No.”

Zee stared at the blood.

The Dragon Link carried the hatchling’s pain.

The Wyrm struck him again.

That decided it.

Zee reached.

Crimson Mana touched the spilled blood.

The blood moved.

Not all of it.

A thin stream.

It crossed the snow.

Entered Zee’s mouth.

Aurelia froze.

The effect was immediate.

Cold tore through his body.

His skin paled.

Crimson Mana surged.

The absorbed blood carried something built for frozen oceans, deep winters, and survival beneath ice.

Zee’s body reacted.

Rejected pieces.

Kept others.

Changed them.

His breath misted.

Then crimson frost appeared across Aurelia’s sleeve where his fingers rested.

The royal witch stared.

Not white frost.

Not the Frost Wyrm’s power copied.

Crimson ice.

Thin.

Alive with Zee’s mana.

The Frost Wyrm sensed it.

For the first time…

The ancient beast looked afraid.

Zee inhaled.

Cold no longer hurt him the same way.

The adaptation settled.

Incomplete.

New.

His first true Crimson Evolution had begun.

The Frost Wyrm roared and retreated toward the lake.

No one followed.

It crashed through the ice and vanished beneath black water.

Silence returned.

The hatchling landed badly beside Zee.

He collapsed.

Aurelia knelt.

“Come here.”

The dragon crawled toward Zee instead.

The Dragon Link carried one thing.

Together.

Zee touched his scales.

Crimson frost appeared briefly beneath his fingertips.

The hatchling stared.

Then sneezed teal-and-gold sparks.

The frost shattered.

Caldrin looked at the broken red crystals.

“What do we call that?”

Aurelia looked at him.

“We do not.”

“Why not?”

“Because we do not understand it.”

He nodded.

“Fair.”

Vale stared toward the frozen lake.

“We chose this place because no one else would come here.”

Caldrin looked at the blood on the snow.

“That may still be true.”

The witch looked toward Zee.

“But now we know why.”

Aurelia stood with her son.

The valley had nearly killed them within minutes of arrival.

No armies.

No assassins.

No politics.

Only the world itself.

And Zee had survived differently again.

They built camp beneath the cliffs.

For the first time, no foreign eyes watched.

No king issued orders.

No bounty hunter tracked the road.

Only mountains surrounded them.

For several weeks, perhaps months, this place might belong to no crown.

That did not make it safe.

But it gave Zee something he had never had.

Room to grow.

⸻

Three months passed.

Winter deepened.

Zee learned to crawl before he learned fear of heights.

The hatchling learned to fly before he learned restraint.

Aurelia learned never to leave food unattended.

Vale built defenses.

The royal witch taught Aurelia how to recognize magical disturbances.

Caldrin disappeared for days at a time and returned with meat, information, and stories nobody believed.

Zee grew.

Not peacefully.

Never peacefully.

The mountains tested him constantly.

Cold storms.

Predators.

Thin air.

Unstable ground.

Once, a cliff cat entered camp and nearly dragged him from a blanket before the hatchling attacked it.

Another time, Zee crawled too close to a thermal fissure and instinctively coated his hands in Crimson Mana before touching burning stone.

Each danger changed him slightly.

His body learned.

His senses sharpened.

His Crimson Mana became easier to summon under stress.

His crimson frost appeared whenever intense cold threatened him.

Not Frost Wyrm magic.

Something new.

His.

The first evolution born from absorbed blood.

Then spring came.

And with it…

A rider.

Caldrin spotted him first.

One man.

Half-dead.

Vesper colors hidden beneath a torn cloak.

Vale met him beyond camp.

The rider collapsed from the saddle.

Aurelia rushed forward.

“Who are you?”

The man looked up.

“Vesper messenger.”

Her blood went cold.

“Lucien?”

“Alive.”

“Rez?”

“Alive.”

She exhaled.

Then saw his face.

“What happened?”

The messenger coughed blood.

“The Twelve Crowns broke the sanctions.”

Aurelia frowned.

“What does that mean?”

“They stopped isolating Vesper.”

“Why?”

The rider looked toward Zee.

Now older.

Crawling beside the crimson dragon.

“Because they are no longer afraid of House Vesper sheltering him.”

Vale’s expression hardened.

“Why not?”

The messenger swallowed.

“They are afraid of what he is becoming out here.”

Silence.

Caldrin looked toward the mountains.

“How did they find us?”

The messenger’s eyes filled with dread.

“They did not.”

Aurelia froze.

“Then how do they know?”

“Bodies.”

“What bodies?”

The rider struggled to breathe.

“Hunters.”

“Mercenaries.”

“Witches.”

“Things sent north.”

Vale’s face darkened.

“We have not seen them.”

“No.”

The messenger looked toward the valley entrance.

“Because something has been killing them before they reach you.”

Everyone went still.

Aurelia looked toward the dark mountain passes.

For months, she had believed Zee had finally escaped the hunt.

She was wrong.

The hunt had continued.

It simply had not been reaching him.

Caldrin slowly drew his sword.

“Something out there is protecting the valley.”

The royal witch looked toward Zee.

“Or protecting something inside it.”

Zee sat in the snowmelt grass.

His crimson eyes turned toward the distant mountains.

The hatchling stopped playing.

The Dragon Link tightened.

Watching.

Old.

Near.

A shadow moved across the highest ridge.

Too large to be human.

Too fast to be the Frost Wyrm.

Then it vanished.

Aurelia lifted Zee immediately.

The sanctuary without a crown had never been empty.

Something had allowed them to stay.

And now…

It knew Zee was growing.
