Chapter Fifty-Seven

Bloodsong

The attack began before sunset.

Seven killers.

No warning.

No demand.

No banner.

No speech.

The Human struck first.

A crossbow bolt entered through Vesper’s western window aimed directly at Zee’s heart.

Zee heard the string release.

Moved.

The bolt missed.

The Witch had expected that.

Her spell bent the bolt midair.

Zee’s assassin-trained eyes caught the hand movement.

Too late.

Aurelia did not hesitate.

She stepped between them.

The bolt entered beneath her ribs.

Everything stopped.

Not the battle.

Zee.

Aurelia looked down.

The metal head had passed completely through.

Black runes activated across the shaft.

Dragon-killing poison.

Witch suppression.

Something else.

Built for Zee.

Aurelia had taken it instead.

“Mother?”

The word came out small.

Ten years old again.

Five.

Newborn.

Every age at once.

Aurelia collapsed.

Zee caught her.

The room exploded around them.

The Vampire came through the shattered window.

The Wolf hit Rez from the side.

The Demon erupted from shadow.

The attacking Dragon crashed into the courtyard where Zareth met it with a roar that shook the palace.

The Witch started another spell.

The Human reloaded.

And above the broken western wall—

someone stood in air.

A God.

Not glowing.

Not speaking.

Simply present with enough supernatural pressure that every ward in Vesper screamed.

Zee saw none of it clearly.

Only Aurelia.

Blood filled her mouth.

“No.”

His hands went to the bolt.

She caught his wrist.

“Do not.”

“I can get it out.”

“No.”

“I can—”

“Zee.”

Her voice stopped him.

He knew injuries.

He knew anatomy now.

That made this worse.

He saw exactly how much damage the bolt had done.

What it had severed.

What the runes were burning through.

What removing it would change.

Nothing.

“No.”

Aurelia touched his face.

The battle thundered around them.

Rez shouted an order.

Ner’s Gold hit something hard enough to crack stone.

Zareth roared outside.

Zee heard only her heartbeat.

Weak.

Irregular.

“Do not be angry with me.”

He stared.

“What?”

Aurelia smiled through blood.

“I chose.”

“No.”

“Yes.”

Her fingers touched one crimson-black horn.

“My son.”

Zee’s eyes filled.

“You were going to take it.”

He shook his head violently.

“It was for me.”

“I know.”

“Then why—”

Aurelia pulled him closer.

“Because I got to choose too.”

His face broke.

“Mother.”

“Listen.”

Her heartbeat weakened.

“Do not turn this into something ugly just because it hurts.”

Zee could not breathe.

She smiled faintly.

“I saved you.”

“Let that mean something.”

“I need you.”

“I know.”

“No, you don’t.”

“I do.”

Aurelia’s eyes softened.

“My little impossible boy.”

Her hand slid from his face.

Zee caught it.

“No.”

Her heartbeat stopped.

Once.

No second beat.

No return.

No miracle.

No Crimson reaction.

Aurelia Veyrath Darkheart died in her son’s arms.

Permanently.

Zee stared at her.

The world resumed violently.

“ZEE!”

Rez.

He looked up.

The Human fired again.

Zee caught the bolt without looking.

His hand closed around it.

The shaft snapped.

The assassin inside him cataloged everything.

Human.

Upper gallery.

Reload time three seconds.

Vampire.

Right side.

Fastest.

Witch.

Rear arch.

Needs line of sight and left hand.

Wolf.

Engaging Rez.

Dominant right leg.

Dragon.

Courtyard.

Zareth holding.

Demon.

Shadow entry through reflected surfaces.

God.

Above.

Uncommitted.

Watching.

Then Zee smelled blood.

Golden blood.

Ner.

He turned.

Ner stood against the eastern wall with one hand pressed to his abdomen.

Pure Golden Mana flickered around him.

Weak.

A Demon-made blade had gone through his side.

Too much blood.

Too fast.

“Ner.”

Ner looked at him.

Tried to smile.

Failed.

Zee was there instantly.

He caught him before his knees hit.

“No.”

Ner’s breathing came shallow.

“Zee…”

“No.”

Gold flickered.

Died.

Returned.

Died again.

Zee pressed both hands to the wound.

Too much.

He knew anatomy now.

Again.

Knowledge becoming cruelty.

“Ner, look at me.”

Ner did.

Barely.

“I am tired.”

“No.”

Zee panicked.

Not assassin calm.

Not strategy.

Fifteen.

Terrified.

Mother dead behind him.

Best friend dying in front of him.

“Take mine.”

Ner frowned weakly.

“What?”

Zee bit open his own wrist.

Deep.

Blood flooded immediately.

Crimson.

His.

He shoved his wrist against Ner’s mouth.

“Drink.”

Ner coughed.

Zee forced the blood past his lips.

“Take it.”

Rez shouted from across the room:

“Zee!”

The Wolf hit him again.

Rez rolled.

Blue-White Mana exploded.

Zee did not look.

“LIVE.”

Ner swallowed.

Once.

Twice.

Crimson blood entered him.

Nothing happened.

His Golden Mana flickered.

Then vanished.

Ner’s eyes closed.

“No.”

Zee shook him.

“Ner.”

No response.

“Ner.”

No breathing.

Heartbeat slowing.

Then—

stopped.

Zee froze.

Aurelia behind him.

Dead.

Ner in his arms.

Dead.

Something inside Zee opened.

Not rage.

That would have been easier.

The League had given him structure.

Aurelia had given him grief.

Now both met.

Zee placed Ner down carefully.

Very carefully.

Then stood.

Twenty-eight seconds would pass before Ner’s heart returned.

Zee did not know that.

At that moment, he believed there was nothing left to save.

So he turned toward the people who had done it.

And became quiet.

Rez saw.

That frightened him more than screaming would have.

“Zee.”

No answer.

The Human fired.

Zee vanished.

Not merely Vampire speed.

Assassin movement inside supernatural speed.

He appeared behind the Human before the crossbow finished recoiling.

One hand trapped the weapon.

The other pulled the killer backward by the jaw.

The man reached for a knife.

Zee already knew.

He caught the wrist without looking.

Broke it.

The Human screamed.

Zee smiled.

There.

He enjoyed the sound.

This time he did not pretend otherwise.

His fangs sank into the man’s throat.

The Human convulsed.

Zee drank.

Not one bite.

Not enough to weaken.

Everything.

Blood flooded his mouth.

Hot.

Ordinary.

Human.

It fed him.

Strength.

Hunger.

Nothing supernatural.

The man’s heartbeat raced.

Slowed.

Stopped.

Zee dropped the empty body.

One.

The Vampire attacked before it hit the floor.

Zee turned.

Caught the first strike.

Vampire against Vampire.

Speed collided.

The enemy had centuries of predatory technique.

Zee had fifteen years of surviving plus Nanda Parbat.

The Vampire went for Zee’s heart.

Zee let the hand come close.

Trap.

Knife through the inside of the elbow.

Joint broken.

Turn.

Wall.

Zee slammed the Vampire face-first into stone.

The attacker hissed:

“You think you scare me?”

Zee’s crimson eyes looked almost black around the edges.

“No.”

He sank his fangs into the Vampire’s neck.

“Not yet.”

Then drank.

Vampire blood hit differently.

Cold fire.

Predatory history.

Not memories.

Not stolen abilities.

Something about Zee’s own Vampire nature tightened.

Analyzed.

Rejected what was foreign.

Kept what could become his.

Crimson pressure moved through his veins.

The Vampire screamed.

Then realized Zee was not stopping.

Fear.

There.

Zee liked that too.

The Vampire emptied.

Two.

The Witch cast from behind.

Zee heard the breath before the spell.

He released the corpse.

Turned.

One finger.

Tiny Witch spell.

Not a blast.

A precise disruption against the caster’s wrist.

Her hand jerked half an inch.

The spell missed.

Assassin Zee crossed the distance.

The Witch tried speaking another incantation.

Zee’s hand closed around her throat.

“Quiet.”

Vampire compulsion slammed into her.

Her eyes found his.

Then resisted.

Powerful mind.

Zee pressed.

“Quiet.”

Her jaw locked.

She stared in horror as her own mouth refused her.

Zee smiled.

His fangs descended.

The Witch tried one final spell through her free hand.

Zee stabbed the nerve beneath her shoulder.

Arm dead.

Then fed.

Magic entered with the blood.

Not as spells.

Not knowledge.

His Witch nature reacted violently.

Crimson Mana flared beneath his skin.

Adaptation.

Possibility.

The Witch’s power did not become Zee’s.

Whatever his blood wanted from it was rewritten before he understood it.

She died empty.

Three.

The Wolf came next.

Rez had already wounded him.

Blue-White Mana locked one knee.

The Wolf tore free anyway.

Fast.

Brutal.

Claws ripped across Zee’s chest.

Blood sprayed.

Zee laughed.

That made the Wolf hesitate.

Wrong choice.

Zee’s own Wolf nature surged independently.

Scent.

Balance.

Hunt.

He dropped lower.

No magic.

No Vampire speed for one moment.

Just Wolf.

He read the attacker’s shoulders.

Weight.

Breathing.

Intent.

Dodged before the claws moved.

Came behind him.

Bit.

The Wolf roared.

Zee’s fangs tore deep.

Wolf blood hit his tongue.

Hotter than Human.

Wild.

His muscles reacted.

Instinct sharpened.

Not copied.

Adapted.

Crimson heat moved through tendon and bone.

The Wolf tried tearing away.

Zee held him.

Drank him dry.

Four.

Outside, Zareth and the attacking Dragon smashed through an outer tower.

Stone rained.

The Dragon killer was larger.

Older.

Fully mature.

Zareth was losing ground.

Zee appeared in the courtyard.

The attacking Dragon saw him.

Crimson scales.

Horns.

Blood covering his mouth.

The Dragon inhaled.

Fire.

Zee moved sideways.

Flame scorched one arm.

Dragon durability held enough to keep flesh attached.

Pain.

Good.

Zee saw the opening beneath the jaw.

Assassin anatomy did not perfectly translate to Dragons.

But Zareth had taught him years of movement.

Wing tension.

Neck rotation.

Where scales overlapped.

Zee ran.

The Dragon snapped.

Zareth hit from the opposite side.

Not commanded.

Chosen.

Teal-and-Gold Mana struck the attacker’s face.

Zee climbed the exposed shoulder.

No riding.

No mounting for travel.

Combat.

He drove a blade beneath a scale seam.

The Dragon roared.

Zee ripped it wider.

Blood erupted.

Dragon blood.

His developing Dragon nature screamed for it.

Zee sank his fangs into the wound.

Impossible amount.

He drank anyway.

The Dragon thrashed.

Zareth held the neck down.

Not to help Zee feed.

To keep the attacker from crushing him.

Zee drank.

Dragon blood entered him like molten metal.

His horns burned.

Crimson scales spread almost across his entire torso.

The ridges beneath his shoulder blades swelled violently.

No wings.

Not yet.

No awakening.

But something inside the sleeping fourth nature rolled toward consciousness and stopped at the edge.

The enemy Dragon weakened.

Collapsed.

Zee kept feeding.

Until nothing remained.

Five.

The Demon attacked from Zee’s own shadow.

Nanda Parbat saved him.

Not magic.

Training.

Zee noticed the shadow move against the direction of firelight.

He spun before the Demon emerged fully.

Knife through the wrist.

Crimson Mana through the floor.

Not a giant spell.

One precise binding line.

The Demon snarled.

Black-red energy tore it apart.

Zee let go.

Wolf tracking caught the sulfur-blood scent.

Vampire speed followed.

The Demon reappeared near Aurelia’s body.

That was a mistake.

Zee’s face changed.

Not uncontrolled.

Worse.

He crossed the courtyard.

The Demon smiled.

“Your mother screamed beautifully.”

Zee stopped.

For one heartbeat.

Then smiled back.

“No.”

The Demon’s expression shifted.

“She died beautifully.”

Zee hit him.

One punch.

Dragon durability behind Vampire strength.

The Demon’s jaw broke.

Zee caught him before he fell.

“I want you awake.”

The Demon’s eyes widened.

There.

Fear.

Zee enjoyed it completely.

He drove the Demon against a broken pillar.

Broke one arm.

Then the other.

Not necessary.

Chosen.

Rez saw.

“Zee!”

Zee did not stop.

The Demon spat blood.

“You are proving us right.”

Zee leaned close.

“I stopped caring what killers think of me.”

Then fed.

Demon blood tasted wrong.

Bitter.

Burning.

Alive in a way ordinary blood was not.

Zee’s supernatural nature reacted violently.

Witch Mana recoiled.

Vampire hunger deepened.

Wolf instinct snarled.

Dragon energy heated.

Then Crimson blood imposed itself over all of it.

The Demon’s strange essence did not remain foreign.

It began being broken down into something new.

Something Zee-shaped.

Not a copied demonic power.

An adaptation still forming.

Zee drank until the Demon went still.

Six.

Silence hit the courtyard.

The God remained.

High above.

Watching.

Zee looked up.

Blood covered his face, throat, hands, clothing.

Six bodies empty around Vesper.

The God’s expression—if divine faces could be read like ordinary ones—changed.

Calculation.

Then distance opened around them.

Zee moved.

Too late.

He reached the rooftop where the God had stood an instant earlier.

Nothing.

No blood.

No trail.

No scent.

No divine essence.

Gone.

Zee stared at empty sky.

The seventh escaped.

That mattered.

He would remember.

Then—

Heartbeat.

Tiny.

Impossible.

Behind him.

Zee froze.

One beat.

Pause.

Another.

He turned.

Ner.

Rez heard Zee whisper his name.

“Ner?”

Zee was beside him instantly.

Thirty seconds.

Almost exactly.

Ner’s chest moved.

A shallow breath.

Then another.

His eyes opened.

Not normally.

For one heartbeat they were Pure Gold.

Then something dark-red flashed beneath the iris.

Fangs descended.

Small.

Sharp.

Vampire.

Zee stared.

Rez stopped nearby.

“What…”

Ner gasped.

Pure Golden Mana erupted around him.

Still there.

Unchanged.

Witch.

Alive.

Then Ner cried out.

A sound left his throat.

Not scream.

Note.

One single impossible tone.

Every drop of spilled blood in the courtyard trembled.

Zee froze.

Rez did too.

Even Zareth stopped moving.

The blood responded to Ner’s voice.

Not rising.

Not obeying exactly.

Resonating.

A subtle vibration passed through it like music moving through strings.

Ner slapped one hand over his mouth.

The effect stopped.

His eyes widened.

“What did I do?”

Zee did not know.

Nobody did.

Good.

The hidden bloodline inside Ner had awakened with death.

Something ancient.

Siren.

But no one there had the name yet.

They only saw what existed.

Golden Witch Mana.

Vampire fangs.

A heartbeat that had returned after stopping.

And a voice that blood itself seemed to hear.

Zee touched Ner’s face.

“You are alive.”

Ner looked at him.

Confused.

Terrified.

Alive.

Zee laughed once.

Then grabbed him.

Ner held him back weakly.

For one heartbeat relief nearly consumed everything.

Then Zee looked over Ner’s shoulder.

Aurelia.

Still on the floor.

Still dead.

Nothing changed.

No heartbeat.

No second chance.

No impossible return.

Ner followed his gaze.

His face broke.

“Zee…”

Zee stood slowly.

The assassin quiet returned.

But different.

Grief now lived inside it.

He walked to Aurelia.

Knelt.

Took her hand.

Cold beginning already.

Zareth approached slowly.

The Dragon Link carried pain so deep Zee almost could not separate it from his own.

Rez stood behind him.

Ner remained nearby, trembling with a body none of them understood.

Zee looked at his mother.

Her final words came back.

I chose.

Do not make this ugly.

He wanted to destroy the world anyway.

That truth remained.

But six drained bodies lay around him.

The seventh killer had escaped.

Ner was alive because something in Zee’s blood had done what Zee never knew it could do.

His mother was dead because she had chosen him.

Everything had changed in minutes.

Nanda Parbat had given him precision.

Aurelia’s death gave precision grief.

Six different bloods burned through his body, each being pulled apart and rewritten by a Crimson nature that had only begun revealing what feeding could become.

And Ner—

Ner had returned as something new.

Not only Witch.

Not only Vampire.

Something in his voice had awakened with blood.

No one had a name for it yet.

They would.

Later.

For now Zee bent over Aurelia’s hand and pressed his forehead against it.

No scream.

No resurrection.

No promise to undo the impossible.

Only one quiet sentence.

“I will not waste it.”

Behind him, Ner’s strange new heartbeat continued.

Golden Mana flickered.

A faint red resonance answered somewhere beneath it.

The first turning had happened by accident.

The first Witch Bloodsong had been born without anyone knowing the name.

The God had escaped.

Aurelia was gone forever.

And when Zee Darkheart finally raised his head, something in the fifteen-year-old boy had died with his mother even though Zee himself never could.

What remained still loved.

Still grieved.

Still cared.

But the world would never again meet the same Zee Darkheart who had walked into that room before sunset.
