Chapter Forty-One

The Boys Who Hunt Back

Five Years Later

The vampire hit the ground hard enough to split the frozen earth.

Zee landed on him a heartbeat later.

At fifteen, there was nothing small about the impact.

The vampire rolled.

Zee’s claws caught only snow.

“Left!”

Ner’s voice.

Zee moved without looking.

A silver chain cut through the space where his throat had been.

Rez came through the trees on the opposite side.

His sword flashed once.

Blue-White Mana followed.

The chain froze from end to end.

The vampire holding it swore.

Rez smiled.

“Should have let go.”

He twisted his wrist.

The frozen chain snapped.

Ner raised both hands.

Pure Golden Mana spread between the surrounding trees—not a wall, but six thin planes of light angled precisely enough to close every escape without trapping the four boys inside.

The vampire looked around.

Then upward.

Mistake.

Zareth dropped through the winter canopy.

Fifteen years had turned the Crown Prince of Drakonis into something the forest itself seemed too small to contain.

His crimson Dragon body struck the clearing with controlled force.

Snow exploded outward.

Teal-and-Gold Mana burned beneath his scales.

Wings wider than entire cottages folded along his sides.

The fleeing vampire stopped.

Zee stood.

He was taller now.

Lean rather than bulky, but built by years of running, fighting, climbing, hunting, and surviving things designed to break him.

His horns had grown with him.

Crimson-black curves swept farther back from his temples now, still not fully mature but impossible to hide beneath ordinary hair.

Permanent Crimson scales patterned more of his body than they had at ten—along the sides of his neck, shoulders, ribs, forearms, and portions of his temples.

His fangs had matured.

So had the way people reacted when they saw them.

The vampire stared.

Recognition arrived.

Not from Zee’s face.

From everything else.

“No.”

Zee smiled.

“Yes.”

The vampire backed away.

“You are Darkheart.”

Rez walked into the clearing.

At fifteen, his current body moved with absolute familiarity.

No hesitation.

No recalculation.

His sword belonged in his hand.

His balance belonged beneath his feet.

Blue-White Mana moved around him with the kind of precision that came from years of choosing how to use what he already knew differently.

The vampire looked at him.

“Vesper.”

Rez inclined his head.

Ner stepped from between the trees.

His Golden Mana dimmed around the barriers but did not disappear.

He had grown taller too, his features sharper, confidence sitting on him more naturally now.

He wore no gloves.

Had not for years unless secrecy required them.

The vampire saw the Gold.

His expression worsened.

“Azrion.”

Ner smiled.

“You know all our names.”

“Everyone knows your names.”

Zee’s smile faded slightly.

That still felt strange sometimes.

Five years ago strangers had called him the Darkheart child.

Now hunters whispered Zee Darkheart before he entered rooms.

Some stories claimed he could tear souls out through blood.

False.

Some said he had killed armies.

Very false.

One tavern story insisted he turned into a black Dragon during every full moon.

Zareth had enjoyed that one.

Zee had not.

The vampire looked upward toward Zareth.

“And Drakonis.”

The Dragon Prince lowered his head.

The evolving Link pressed a clear intention against Zee.

He smells afraid.

Zee answered with an image.

Good.

Not because fear pleased him.

Because this vampire had spent six months trafficking missing village children between two border territories.

Fear seemed appropriate.

Zee looked toward the unconscious man beneath his boot.

“Where are the others?”

The vampire spat blood.

“Go to hell.”

Ner sighed.

“We rode three days for this.”

Rez looked toward Zee.

“Want me?”

“No.”

Zee crouched.

The vampire flinched.

Interesting.

Zee did nothing magical.

He simply looked at him.

“Six children disappeared from Deren Village.”

No response.

“Two from wolf territory.”

Still nothing.

“One human boy was found in a ditch.”

The vampire’s heartbeat changed.

Zee heard it.

His Wolf instinct noticed the scent shift.

His Vampire nature counted the pulse.

His Witch Mana stayed quiet.

Dragon heat moved gently beneath his scales.

Controlled.

No overlap.

No surge.

No accident.

Zee tilted his head.

“You did not kill him.”

The vampire stared.

Rez looked toward Zee.

“You sure?”

“No.”

The vampire smiled faintly.

Zee continued.

“But that bothered you.”

The smile vanished.

“There.”

Ner folded his arms.

“You always get creepy when you do that.”

Zee glanced toward him.

“You like it.”

“I did not say that.”

“You looked impressed.”

“I was looking at the prisoner.”

Rez sighed.

“Can both of you flirt after we find the children?”

Silence.

Zee turned.

Ner turned.

Rez blinked.

“What?”

Zee’s face warmed.

That was irritating.

Crimson Dragon energy responded faintly beneath the scales along his neck.

Ner saw.

His mouth almost smiled.

Almost.

“We were not flirting,” Zee said.

Rez looked between them.

“Fine.”

Ner added, “We were not.”

Zareth sent a burst of unmistakable amusement through the Link.

Zee looked upward.

“Do not start.”

The vampire stared.

“You people are insane.”

Rez looked down at him.

“That is rude.”

Then the other captured vampire groaned.

Back to work.

Zee crouched again.

“Where?”

The vampire refused to answer.

So Zee looked toward Ner.

Ner stepped closer.

Not to unlock anything.

His Golden Mana had become far more than a key over five years.

Thin lines of light spread from his fingers and passed over the vampire’s coat.

Not through flesh.

Across objects.

Metal.

Leather.

Dust.

Then stopped at one pocket.

Ner reached inside.

The vampire lunged.

Rez’s sword touched his throat immediately.

Ner removed a black wooden token.

A symbol carved into it.

Wolf territory.

Rez recognized it.

“Old mill.”

Ner nodded.

“North road.”

The vampire’s heartbeat betrayed him.

Zee smiled.

“Thank you.”

The man swore.

Zareth sent Zee an image.

Mill.

Trees.

Smoke.

Then another.

Movement to the west.

Zee looked up sharply.

“Zareth found it.”

Rez sheathed his sword.

“Alive?”

The Link carried distant heartbeats.

Small.

Many.

Zee’s expression hardened.

“Yes.”

Ner dropped the Golden barriers.

“Then why are we still talking?”

They moved.

⸻

The old mill sat beside a frozen river two miles north.

They did not approach from one side.

They had stopped doing that years ago.

Zareth stayed high enough to track exits.

Rez circled west.

Ner went east.

Zee took the river.

No one needed to explain the plan.

They had used versions of it dozens of times.

Zee moved across the ice without breaking stride.

At fifteen his vampire speed had become less wild.

More economical.

His Wolf nature handled balance.

Witch Mana softened impact.

Crimson Dragon durability absorbed whatever neither prevented.

He reached the mill wall.

Stopped.

Listened.

Nine captives.

Three guards.

One vampire.

Two humans.

One heartbeat inside the upper office.

Different.

Wolf.

Zee frowned.

Not every person inside belonged to the same enemy.

Good to remember.

He touched the stone.

A faint vibration moved through his horns.

The Dragon nature had become stronger over five years.

Far stronger.

Still unfinished.

The ridges beneath his shoulder blades had developed too.

Visible now beneath thin clothing when his Dragon energy surged.

Sometimes they hardened enough that Aurelia would stare at them and refuse to guess what came next.

No wings.

Not yet.

The Vault’s old judgment remained lodged inside Zee’s memory.

Four.

Three awake.

Whatever counted as complete had still not happened.

Zee did not force it.

He had learned better than that.

He sent an image through the Dragon Link.

Nine.

Zareth answered.

Roof clear.

Not words.

Not quite.

Intent sharpened by fifteen years of familiarity.

Rez appeared through a crack in the western wall.

Ner’s Gold glimmered once from the east.

Ready.

Zee entered.

The first guard never saw him.

Zee caught the man’s wrist before the knife cleared leather.

Turned.

Dropped him.

Second guard came from behind.

Rez intercepted.

Blue-White Mana struck the floor.

The man slipped.

Rez’s pommel hit his jaw.

Down.

The vampire lunged toward the rear door.

Ner closed it with a Golden plane.

The vampire rebounded.

Zee caught him.

“Children.”

The vampire snarled.

“You think saving nine matters?”

Zee’s face changed.

“Yes.”

The vampire hesitated.

That answer had not been dramatic enough.

Good.

Zee tightened his grip.

“Where is the wolf upstairs?”

The vampire smiled.

“Not ours.”

There.

Zee released him just enough for Rez to bind him.

Then took the stairs.

He found a girl in the upper office.

Sixteen, maybe.

Wolf.

Bruised.

Armed with half a broken chair leg.

She swung the moment Zee entered.

He caught it.

“Stop.”

She snarled.

“Get away from me.”

“I am not with them.”

“Everyone says that.”

Fair.

Zee released the chair leg and stepped back.

She saw the horns.

The scales.

Recognition hit.

Not reassurance.

Fear.

“You.”

Zee stopped.

He knew that tone.

Not Zee.

Not a person.

A story.

“The Tribrid.”

He hated that less than monster.

Still not enough.

“My name is Zee.”

The girl stared.

Below them something crashed.

Ner shouted, “Rez!”

Rez shouted back, “That was not me!”

Zareth roared outside.

The girl flinched.

Zee sighed.

“That is Zareth.”

Her eyes widened.

“Drakonis?”

“Yes.”

Another crash.

Zee added, “Probably him.”

The Dragon Link sent offense.

The girl slowly lowered the wood.

“You came for them?”

“For everyone.”

Her expression changed.

“I am not one of the kidnapped children.”

“I know.”

“You know?”

“You smell like wolf territory.”

She stared.

“That is invasive.”

Zee blinked.

Then laughed.

“Sorry.”

That disarmed her more than any display of power could have.

“My brother is downstairs,” she said.

Zee’s smile disappeared.

“Which one?”

“Little.”

“Brown hair.”

“Scar over his chin.”

Zee listened.

Found the heartbeat.

“Alive.”

The girl almost collapsed.

She caught herself against the desk.

Zee did not touch her without permission.

Another lesson.

“They took him four days ago.”

“I followed.”

“They caught me yesterday.”

Zee nodded.

“So this was your hunt too.”

She looked at him.

He understood.

The world did not revolve around him.

Five years had made that truth instinctive.

He gestured toward the stairs.

“Come on.”

⸻

By midday the missing children were outside.

All alive.

Cold.

Hungry.

Terrified.

Alive.

Ner used Golden Mana only when one restraint refused to open normally.

Rez organized blankets and water before anyone asked.

Zareth remained at a distance because several children were more frightened of a massive Dragon than reassured by him.

He did not take offense.

Much.

The Dragon Link carried mild insult anyway.

Zee sat beside the smallest boy from wolf territory.

The boy kept staring at his horns.

Finally:

“Do they hurt?”

“Sometimes.”

“Can I touch one?”

Zee looked toward Aurelia in his memory.

Then toward the boy.

“Why?”

The child shrugged.

“I want to know if it feels like bone.”

Zee smiled.

“Okay.”

The boy touched the horn carefully.

“Hard.”

“Yes.”

“Cool.”

“Sometimes.”

The girl upstairs—his sister—stood nearby watching.

Her suspicion had not vanished.

Good.

Trust should not always be instant.

A rider appeared on the southern road.

Then another.

Local guards.

Not Vesper.

Not Zee’s.

They had their own authorities.

Their own responsibilities.

Zee stood.

The lead guard saw him.

Stopped.

The entire patrol slowed.

Recognition again.

One whispered something.

Zee heard.

“Darkheart.”

The leader dismounted carefully.

“We received reports of fighting.”

Rez approached.

“Trafficking ring.”

Ner pointed toward the captives.

“Three perpetrators alive.”

The guard looked between the boys.

Then toward Zareth.

Then back to Zee.

“You did this?”

The girl from wolf territory answered first.

“They did.”

Zee looked at her.

She shrugged.

“Truth.”

The guard’s expression became complicated.

Fear.

Relief.

Political calculation.

Zee knew that face too.

He had seen it on kings.

Hunters.

Village leaders.

People deciding whether Zee Darkheart was a threat before remembering he had just saved children.

The guard bowed.

Not deeply.

Not royal.

Respect without submission.

“Thank you.”

Zee blinked.

That still happened rarely enough to surprise him.

“You are welcome.”

The guard looked toward the prisoners.

“We take custody.”

Rez asked, “Under whose authority?”

“Border Council.”

Rez nodded.

“Send the records to House Vesper and the wolf council.”

The guard looked at him more closely.

“Prince Rez.”

“Yes.”

Recognition there too.

Different.

Political.

Established.

Rez no longer needed age explained to anyone who mattered.

He was simply fifteen-year-old Rez Vesper, Crown Prince, and apparently terrifying to bureaucrats.

The guard nodded quickly.

“Of course.”

Ner whispered to Zee.

“He likes that.”

Rez heard.

“I do.”

Zee smiled.

Then the rescued wolf girl approached.

“What happens to you now?”

Zee frowned.

“What do you mean?”

“You go home?”

“Eventually.”

“You are not from here.”

“No.”

“Then why were you hunting them?”

Zee looked toward the children.

Simple question.

Complicated answer.

Because someone had asked Vesper for help.

Because trafficking routes crossed old Veyrath maps.

Because Rez had pushed for intervention at council.

Because Ner found inconsistencies in missing-person wards.

Because Zareth spotted one transport wagon from the sky.

Because five years had taught them not every battle needed to begin at their own doorstep.

Zee shrugged.

“They were hurting people.”

The girl stared.

“That is it?”

“Is that not enough?”

She had no answer.

Zee turned away.

Across the frozen field, Ner was helping a child tie a blanket correctly.

His hair had fallen into his eyes.

He pushed it back impatiently.

Zee looked a little too long.

Ner glanced up.

Caught him.

Zee immediately looked elsewhere.

Too late.

A small smile appeared on Ner’s face.

Not mocking.

Worse.

Knowing.

Zee’s horns warmed.

Not danger.

He glared at them as though they had betrayed him personally.

Rez appeared beside him.

“You should really learn a different reaction.”

Zee did not look over.

“I do not know what you are talking about.”

“Of course.”

“Say anything and I will throw you in the river.”

Rez smiled.

“Fifteen years old and still terrible at lying.”

Zee elbowed him.

Rez elbowed back.

Zareth’s amusement crossed the Link.

Zee looked around at all three of them.

Fifteen.

Five more years of arguments.

Missions.

Training.

Scars.

Victories.

Mistakes.

No longer children adults carried from danger.

Not adults either.

Not finished.

Not close.

The world had changed around them.

Veyrath lived openly enough now that some kingdoms acknowledged its survivors.

Drakonis had learned to share its Crown Prince with a bond it still did not fully understand.

Vesper had become harder, richer, and more politically important after years of refusing to surrender the people under its protection.

Victor remained at Darkheart.

The black-robed observers remained somewhere beyond sight.

Hunters still came.

Bounties still appeared.

Stories about Zee multiplied faster than truth.

But something fundamental had reversed.

At ten, Zee had begun walking toward the world.

At fifteen, the world had started learning what that meant.

He looked toward the captured traffickers.

Then the rescued children.

Then Ner.

Rez.

Zareth.

Zee Darkheart smiled.

The boy they had once hunted was gone.

What stood in his place was still fifteen.

Still learning.

Still afraid of some things.

Still capable of making terrible decisions when angry.

Still his mother’s son.

Still Rez’s brother.

Still Ner’s best friend.

Still linked to Zareth by something neither kingdom could name.

But prey?

No.

That part of childhood was over.

And the world was beginning to understand the difference.
