Chapter Forty-Nine

The Son Who Came Back

Castle Darkheart had not changed.

Zee had.

That became obvious before the gates opened.

The towers were still black stone.

The old Witch sigils still burned along the outer walls.

House banners still hung from spear-pointed battlements.

The valley below still carried the same wind.

The same smell.

Pine.

Cold iron.

Old magic.

Memory.

Zee stood at the end of the approach road with Aurelia beside him.

Rez slightly behind.

Ner on Zee’s other side.

Zareth remained above the valley, too large to land inside the outer court without turning diplomacy into architecture damage.

The Dragon Link held steady.

Watchful.

Not frightened.

Zee felt his mother’s tension anyway.

He looked over.

Aurelia’s face remained composed.

Queen.

Dragon-Witch.

Veyrath.

Darkheart by marriage and history.

But Zee knew her heartbeat.

Faster.

He touched her hand.

Aurelia looked down.

“I am fine.”

Zee raised an eyebrow.

She sighed.

“Do not.”

“You taught me.”

“That was a mistake.”

He smiled.

Then the gates opened.

Darkheart soldiers waited inside.

Some Zee recognized.

Older.

Scarred.

Different uniforms.

Five years.

Life had continued here too.

One soldier saw Zee.

His face changed.

Recognition.

Fear.

Then something almost like awe.

Zee ignored all three.

The captain approached.

He bowed first to Aurelia.

“Your Majesty.”

Then Rez.

“Crown Prince Vesper.”

Ner received a respectful nod.

Then the captain looked toward Zee.

Longer.

“Prince Darkheart.”

Zee’s expression sharpened.

Prince.

Not abomination.

Not curse.

Interesting.

“Captain.”

The man hesitated.

“You have grown.”

Zee almost laughed.

“So have you.”

The captain blinked.

Aurelia hid a smile.

They entered.

No attack.

No assassins.

No blades.

That made Zee more alert, not less.

Darkheart servants watched from halls.

Witches paused near stairways.

Whispers followed.

Zee heard all of them.

Tribrid.

Dragon horns.

Veyrath.

Victor’s son.

Monster.

Prince.

He could have listened harder.

Instead he chose one sound.

Ner’s footsteps.

Steady beside him.

That helped.

Rez noticed the technique.

Said nothing.

They reached the throne hall.

King Victor Darkheart waited.

Five years had changed him too.

More gray near the temples.

Harder face.

Heavier crown.

Still the same eyes Zee remembered from nightmares he rarely had anymore.

Victor sat beneath the Darkheart crest.

No weapon visible.

Good.

Zee stopped at the center of the chamber.

Aurelia remained beside him.

Victor’s gaze went to her first.

“Aurelia.”

“Victor.”

No warmth.

No old love offered for public consumption.

Then Victor looked at Zee.

The hall became quiet enough that Zee heard candles.

For several seconds neither spoke.

Victor stared at the horns.

The scales.

The Crimson eyes.

The boy he had once killed.

The son who had become something death could never take again.

Fear entered Victor’s face.

Older now.

More disciplined.

Still fear.

Zee saw.

For years he had imagined this moment differently.

Sometimes he screamed.

Sometimes Victor begged.

Sometimes Zee hurt him.

Sometimes he laughed.

Reality was quieter.

Victor finally said:

“You came.”

Zee looked around.

“You asked.”

“I demanded.”

Zee’s mouth twitched.

“There he is.”

Aurelia looked toward him.

Not now.

Zee controlled the smile.

Victor’s gaze moved toward Rez.

“Vesper.”

Rez nodded.

“Darkheart.”

Then Ner.

Victor’s eyes lingered on the Pure Golden Mana signature beneath his skin.

“Azrion.”

“Nereus.”

Zee immediately looked toward Ner.

Ner did not look back.

Victor noticed.

Zee hated that.

Then Victor looked upward as a distant Dragon roar shook the valley.

His face tightened.

“Drakonis.”

Zee smiled.

“Zareth says hello.”

That was not exactly what the Link said.

The actual impression involved suspicion, teeth, and the castle roof.

Close enough.

Victor’s hands tightened against the throne.

“You bring foreign princes into a House matter.”

Rez answered before Zee.

“I am here because Vesper is named in your claim.”

Victor’s eyes moved toward him.

“You are fifteen.”

Rez’s expression did not change.

“Yes.”

Nothing else.

No argument.

No explanation.

Victor waited.

Rez did not give him more.

Good.

Victor looked toward Zee.

“You have become surrounded by people who encourage your defiance.”

Zee frowned.

“I was defiant before them.”

Ner coughed to hide a laugh.

Aurelia looked toward the ceiling.

Victor’s expression hardened.

“This is not a joke.”

“No.”

Zee’s humor disappeared.

“That is why I came.”

The throne hall changed.

Zee stepped forward.

Victor watched every movement.

Fear.

Control.

Calculation.

Zee did not enjoy it.

Not yet.

“I read your claim.”

“You are my son.”

“Yes.”

“And heir of House Darkheart.”

“Sometimes.”

Victor’s jaw tightened.

“You mock your birthright?”

“No.”

Zee looked directly at him.

“I mock the way you remember it only when you want control.”

A murmur moved through the hall.

Victor’s face darkened.

“Clear the chamber.”

Aurelia immediately said, “No.”

Victor looked toward her.

“This concerns my son.”

“And mine.”

“House Darkheart—”

“Does not erase me.”

Silence.

Victor looked toward the assembled court.

Then seemed to reconsider.

“Everyone except the principals.”

Servants left.

Minor nobles followed.

Darkheart guards remained.

So did Rez.

Ner.

Aurelia.

Zee.

Zareth above.

Victor stood from the throne.

That was the first time Zee realized his father no longer seemed enormous.

Childhood memory had made Victor taller.

Fifteen years had corrected the illusion.

Victor descended the steps.

“You intervene in foreign disputes.”

“Yes.”

“You cross borders.”

“Yes.”

“You consort with Veyrath survivors.”

Zee looked toward Aurelia.

“They are my family.”

Victor flinched at that.

Tiny.

Enough.

“You involve yourself with traffickers, covens, Northern Councils, mercenaries—”

“Usually because they are hurting people.”

“You believe that grants you authority?”

“No.”

Zee’s answer surprised him.

Victor frowned.

Zee continued.

“I choose whether I care.”

“That is worse.”

“Why?”

“Because you have power without law.”

Rez’s expression sharpened.

Zee thought about Renn.

Voss.

Khell.

People with law.

People who used it badly.

“Law does not make people good.”

“No.”

Victor stepped closer.

“But power without law makes monsters.”

There.

Monster.

Same word.

Older conversation.

Zee’s horns warmed.

Aurelia noticed.

Ner too.

No one spoke.

Zee breathed.

“What law did you use when you killed me?”

Victor froze.

The room disappeared around the question.

Aurelia went rigid.

Rez looked toward Zee.

Ner’s expression changed.

Victor’s face lost color.

No repeated act.

No new violence.

Only the one truth between them.

Victor answered quietly.

“None.”

That surprised Zee.

He had expected justification.

Prophecy.

Fear.

The kingdom.

Anything.

Victor continued.

“I was afraid.”

Zee stared.

“That is not an apology.”

“No.”

“Do you regret it?”

Victor looked at him.

For one moment, Zee saw the father from before the prophecy.

The man who had wanted to hold his newborn son.

Then fear returned.

“I regret that I did not understand what it would create.”

Zee’s face went cold.

Aurelia’s eyes hardened.

There.

Not regret for Zee’s pain.

Regret for consequence.

Victor saw the reaction.

“Do not twist my words.”

“I did not.”

Zee stepped closer.

“You regret making me harder to kill.”

Victor’s silence answered.

Something dark inside Zee smiled.

He felt it.

That same part that enjoyed Renn’s scream.

Victor deserved fear.

Zee believed that.

The temptation to step closer, show fangs, let Crimson Mana fill the room until Victor remembered exactly how powerless he had been against the thing he tried to destroy—

It was strong.

Easy.

Satisfying.

Zee’s Mana stirred.

Darkheart wards flickered.

Guards tensed.

Aurelia said nothing.

Ner said nothing.

Rez said nothing.

No one called his name.

This was his choice.

Zee let the Mana settle.

Victor noticed.

His fear changed.

Confusion.

“You could have threatened me.”

“Yes.”

“You wanted to.”

“Yes.”

Victor looked at him more carefully.

“Why didn’t you?”

Zee smiled faintly.

“Because then you would make my point for me.”

Victor frowned.

“You think restraint proves you safe?”

“No.”

Zee’s smile disappeared.

“I am not safe.”

That silenced even Aurelia for a heartbeat.

Zee continued.

“I am a Vampire.”

“A Wolf.”

“A Witch.”

“Dragon is waking inside me.”

“I have been hunted since birth.”

“I fight.”

“I hurt people.”

“Sometimes I like hurting them.”

Aurelia watched.

Proud and frightened all at once.

Zee held Victor’s gaze.

“But you do not get to take everything frightening about me and call it proof that you were right.”

Victor’s jaw tightened.

“You are fifteen.”

“Yes.”

“You do not understand what kingdoms see when they look at you.”

Zee laughed softly.

“I have spent fifteen years being told.”

“Then listen.”

“No.”

That word landed.

Zee moved closer until only several feet remained.

“You listen.”

Victor went still.

“The world can hate me.”

“Fear me.”

“Call me monster.”

“Try to kill me.”

“Apparently they enjoy trying.”

Ner’s mouth twitched.

Zee continued.

“But you do not get to claim me because my reputation became inconvenient.”

“You do not get to decide I am your heir when it gives you authority and your abomination when it gives you distance.”

Victor’s face hardened.

“I am King of this House.”

“Yes.”

“You are its prince.”

“Yes.”

“Then you answer to me.”

Zee thought.

Then smiled.

“No.”

Darkheart wards flickered again.

Victor’s eyes sharpened.

Zee continued before anyone could mistake him.

“I answer for what I do.”

“That is different.”

“You want obedience.”

“I want order.”

“You want control.”

Victor stepped forward.

“And what would you know about control?”

Zee’s scales brightened.

Ner looked toward him.

Zee heard his own heartbeat.

Then Victor’s.

Then Aurelia’s.

Then nothing else.

“One of my best friends is alive because I learned the difference between control and fear.”

Rez’s face changed slightly.

Victor did not understand the reference.

He did not need to.

Zee continued.

“I have hurt people because I lost control.”

“I have stopped because I chose to.”

“I have walked away from men I wanted to kill.”

“Have you?”

The question struck.

Victor’s face changed.

Zee saw it.

Aurelia did too.

Victor looked away first.

Zee did not smile.

That mattered.

He could have enjoyed winning.

Part of him did.

But this was not Renn.

Not Voss.

Not a trafficker beneath his hands.

This was his father.

Ugly in a different way.

Victor returned to the throne steps.

“What do you want, Zee?”

First time.

His name.

Not boy.

Not abomination.

Not heir.

Zee.

He noticed.

“I want your claim withdrawn.”

“No.”

Zee expected that.

“Then we go before the Witch Crowns.”

Aurelia turned toward him.

Victor stared.

Rez did too.

Ner remained still.

Victor laughed once.

“You think they will side with you?”

“I do not know.”

Truth.

Victor’s expression shifted.

Zee continued.

“But I will stand there.”

“And when they ask whether Vesper has prevented you from returning?”

“I will tell them no.”

“When they ask whether Aurelia turned you against your House?”

“I will tell them no.”

“When they ask whether I am your father?”

“I will tell them yes.”

Victor waited.

Zee’s crimson eyes hardened.

“And when they ask whether I belong to you…”

Silence.

“…I will tell them no.”

The words did not shake the castle.

No prophecy answered.

No Blood Rain began.

They were only words.

That made them stronger.

Victor stared at his son.

Perhaps for the first time understanding the problem in a way prophecy never explained.

Zee had grown into someone he could no longer control through fear.

Victor looked toward Aurelia.

“You allowed this.”

Aurelia’s face became cold.

“I raised him.”

Different answer.

Victor closed his eyes.

For several seconds he looked older than his crown.

Then:

“I will withdraw the guardianship claim.”

Zee blinked.

Rez’s eyebrows rose.

Aurelia did not relax.

Victor lifted one hand before anyone spoke.

“Not the succession question.”

There.

Of course.

Zee looked toward the throne.

Then Victor.

“Keep it.”

Victor frowned.

“The throne?”

Zee shrugged.

“I did not come for it.”

That landed harder than Zee expected.

Victor had built so much fear around what Zee might take.

Power.

House.

Crown.

Perhaps hearing Zee refuse to beg for any of it unsettled him more than ambition would have.

“You are heir by blood.”

“Then blood will still be there tomorrow.”

Victor stared.

Zee turned.

Conversation over.

Victor said:

“Zee.”

He stopped.

Not because the King commanded it.

Because his father used his name again.

Victor looked at the horns.

The scales.

The son he had failed.

Fear remained.

So did something else.

Something too small to call love.

Too complicated to call regret.

“I do not know what you will become.”

Zee looked back.

“Neither do I.”

Victor’s face tightened.

The prophecy had given him terror disguised as certainty.

Zee’s answer gave him something worse.

Unknown.

Zee smiled faintly.

“That is kind of the point.”

He left the throne hall.

Aurelia followed.

Rez and Ner behind her.

Above the castle, Zareth roared once.

Not victory.

Recognition.

Zee crossed the outer courtyard beneath Darkheart banners that still belonged partly to him whether Victor liked it or not.

Ner fell into step beside him.

“You did well.”

Zee looked over.

“That sounds surprised.”

“I expected at least one broken table.”

“Rez told me kings cannot punch trade.”

Rez, behind them, called:

“That is not what I said.”

Zee smiled.

Ner bumped his shoulder.

“You wanted to scare him.”

“Yes.”

“But you didn’t.”

“I think I scared him anyway.”

Ner smiled.

“Probably.”

Their hands brushed as they walked.

This time neither moved away.

Zee noticed.

Ner noticed.

Rez definitely noticed.

Zee looked over his shoulder.

Rez immediately studied the sky.

Traitor.

Aurelia waited at the gate.

Zee reached her.

For a moment neither spoke.

Then she pulled him into her arms.

Fifteen.

More dangerous.

More independent.

Still hers.

Zee held her back.

“You okay?”

“No.”

“Truth.”

Aurelia laughed quietly into his hair.

“That was the truth.”

Zee smiled.

Then she whispered:

“I am proud of you.”

His chest tightened.

“For not killing him?”

Aurelia pulled back.

“For choosing why you didn’t.”

Zee looked toward Castle Darkheart.

He had returned expecting confrontation.

He left without a throne.

Without reconciliation.

Without pretending Victor had changed enough to deserve forgiveness.

Nothing was magically repaired.

Good.

Real things rarely were.

But Victor had withdrawn his attempt to control Zee’s present life.

Zee had stood inside the castle where his first death began and left because he chose to.

No one carried him.

No one rescued him.

No one decided his answer first.

The hunted child had come home.

The hunter had left.

And somewhere between those two versions of Zee Darkheart, something else had begun taking shape.

Not a king.

Not yet.

A boy learning that sometimes the most powerful thing he could take from an enemy—

was the right to define him.
