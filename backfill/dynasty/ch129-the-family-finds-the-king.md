Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Nine

The Family Finds the King

Zee tried to stand before his body was ready.

Of course he did.

His palms pressed against blood-slick stone.

One knee came beneath him.

The other refused.

Pain ripped through his ribs before he got halfway upright.

His damaged Phoenix wing dragged behind him.

Too many feathers gone.

Bone wrong somewhere near the outer joint.

The healing had started.

Slowly.

Not enough.

His left eye remained swollen nearly shut.

Blood still covered his mouth.

One horn had cracked deeply enough that every pulse of regeneration sent pain down the side of his skull.

Dragon scales across his ribs were torn open.

Some were missing entirely.

The suppression collar lay broken beside him, but whatever had been inside it still crawled through his system.

His Witchcraft felt distant.

Vampire hearing came and went.

Wolf scent returned in broken fragments.

Phoenix fire flickered instead of burning steadily.

Dragon strength remained—

but dulled.

Everything was repairing.

Nothing fast enough.

Zee got one foot beneath himself.

His body shook.

He hated that.

He pushed harder.

The courtyard tilted.

He caught himself against a shattered pillar.

Breathing hurt.

That irritated him more.

“They walked away.”

His own voice sounded wrong.

Wet.

Rough.

Too tired.

He looked toward the broken arch where the surviving attackers had disappeared.

His good eye sharpened.

He remembered enough.

Boot patterns.

Voices.

Armor cuts.

Who favored which hand.

Which suppressor changed frequency before the next wave.

Who carried Witch restraints.

Who targeted his wings.

Who rotated out when Zee began adapting.

Not names.

Not yet.

But enough to begin later.

He reached for one of the abandoned restraint fragments.

His fingers closed.

Then stopped.

His hand was shaking.

Zee stared at it.

Rage came first.

Then humiliation.

They had beaten him.

Not tricked him into thinking he lost.

Not escaped because he chose mercy.

Not been secretly marked for death afterward.

They beat him.

Earned it.

Then left.

He hated them for it.

He hated himself more for understanding exactly how good they had been.

He tried standing again.

That was when the Dragon Soul Link screamed.

It had never felt like this.

This was not Zee winning badly.

This was not Zee surviving another impossible enemy.

This was defeat.

Teal-and-Gold wings opened.

Phoenix fire traced their edges.

Witch sigils flashed once.

Then he was gone.

The comparison did not forgive him.

It condemned him more cleanly.

His son had been afraid too.

Victor looked toward Darkheart again.

For the first time since his resurrection, approaching Zee frightened him more than staying hidden.

Not because Zee might kill him again.

Victor had already learned death could become something else.

Because Zee would look at him.

And Victor would have to stand there alive after everything he had done.

He took one step toward the road.

Stopped.

Not yet.

But closer than yesterday.
