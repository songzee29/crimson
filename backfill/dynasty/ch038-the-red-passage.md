Chapter Thirty-Eight

The Red Passage

The first rule of boarding a slave ship was apparently not to look like someone about to board a slave ship.

Caldrin informed them of this while painting dirt across Zee’s face.

Zee recoiled.

“What are you doing?”

“Improving you.”

“You made me dirty.”

“You were already dirty.”

“That was travel dirt.”

Caldrin stared.

“I apologize for disrespecting the distinction.”

Rez stood nearby in plain clothes, his smaller sword hidden beneath a dark coat. Ner wore gloves over his hands, every trace of Pure Golden Mana buried.

Aurelia had changed too.

No royal clothing.

No Darkheart symbols.

No visible witch crown.

Her hair was tied back, her Dragon nature buried so deeply Zee could barely feel its presence even knowing what to look for.

Theron remained beneath the ridge with Vale.

He hated that arrangement.

Everyone knew.

“I can walk.”

Vale folded his arms.

“You nearly fell mounting the horse.”

“I had been imprisoned for six years.”

“Yes.”

“So perhaps sympathy.”

“No.”

Theron looked toward Aurelia.

“She always chooses terrible people.”

Aurelia did not even turn.

“Victor was enough. I learned.”

Zee winced.

Rez whispered, “That was brutal.”

Ner nodded approvingly.

Above them, Zareth circled behind thick clouds.

The Dragon Link remained tense.

Boats. People. Blood.

Zee closed his eyes.

“Which ship?”

An image came.

The center vessel.

Lower deck.

Four distinct presences surrounded by many others.

Not enough for Zareth to identify blood.

Enough to know where people were confined.

Zee opened his eyes.

“Center.”

Mara nodded.

“Khell’s vessel.”

Aurelia looked toward her.

“You are certain?”

“The center ship always carries the broker.”

“Always?”

Mara corrected herself immediately.

“It did when I worked these routes.”

Aurelia nodded.

Better.

Truth precise enough to trust.

The three black ships drifted slowly down the Marrow River.

Red lanterns hung along their decks.

Small boats ferried wealthy buyers toward them.

No kingdom flags.

No royal insignia.

That was the point.

No crown could be blamed for what happened on water nobody claimed.

Zee watched men and women climb onto the center vessel.

Witches.

Vampires.

Humans.

One wolf-blooded noble.

Everyone dressed too well.

Everyone pretending this was business.

His fangs sharpened.

Rez touched his shoulder.

“Control.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

“You say that every time.”

“Because you keep needing it.”

Ner looked toward Rez.

“You sound seventy.”

Rez glared.

“I lived seventeen years.”

“Historically.”

Rez took one slow breath.

“I am saving all of this.”

“For when?”

“When I am taller again.”

Ner smiled.

“That assumes you grow faster than me.”

Zee laughed.

Aurelia looked at them.

“Are we finished?”

All three answered at once.

“Yes.”

Zareth sent amusement through the Link.

Aurelia sighed.

Then pointed toward the river.

“We go in two groups.”

“Caldrin, Mara, Rez.”

Rez nodded.

“You board through the buyer entrance.”

Zee frowned.

“Why Rez?”

“Because no one there expects Crown Prince Rez Vesper to look ten.”

Rez smiled.

Finally.

Aurelia continued.

“Zee, Ner, and I use the maintenance skiff beneath the stern.”

Ner looked toward her.

“And Zareth?”

Aurelia glanced upward.

“He waits.”

The Dragon Link exploded with rejection.

No.

Zee looked up.

“He says no.”

“I assumed.”

“He says he can help.”

“He can help by not setting the ship on fire while prisoners are below deck.”

A pause.

Then:

Small fire.

Zee covered his mouth.

Aurelia narrowed her eyes.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Zee.”

“He said small fire.”

Rez laughed first.

Aurelia looked toward the sky.

“Tell him if he burns that ship before I give the signal, I will personally send him back to his mother.”

The Link went silent.

Zee smiled.

“He understands.”

“Good.”

They moved.

Rez boarded the Red Passage as a servant.

He hated that less than being treated like an actual child.

Caldrin played the role of minor merchant.

Mara acted as broker.

That part required very little pretending.

The guard at the boarding ramp barely looked at Rez.

“Boy carries?”

Caldrin handed him a small case.

Rez accepted it with exaggerated effort.

Caldrin nearly smiled.

The guard waved them through.

The moment they crossed onto the deck, Rez changed.

Not visibly.

Inside.

Seventeen years of training settled behind ten-year-old eyes.

He counted guards.

Doors.

Stairs.

Weapons.

Possible exits.

Two vampires on deck.

Three witches.

Six humans.

One buyer carrying silver restraints.

One servant watching too carefully.

Mara whispered without moving her lips.

“Khell’s people use red cord on the wrist.”

Rez glanced.

Three guards.

Two servants.

One man near the stern.

Red cord.

Good.

Caldrin murmured, “Do not stare.”

“I am not.”

“You stare professionally.”

“That is not a thing.”

“It is now.”

The auction bell rang again.

A buyer laughed nearby.

“Veyrath, they say.”

Another answered.

“Real?”

“Supposedly.”

Rez’s jaw tightened.

“Dragon blood?”

“Better.”

“Dragon mixed with witch.”

Rez looked toward the river instead.

If he turned now, he would hit someone.

Caldrin noticed.

“Later.”

Rez nodded.

The buyers moved toward the lower auction chamber.

Rez followed.

⸻

Beneath the stern, Zee discovered that infiltration through a maintenance hatch was far less impressive than stories made it sound.

The hatch smelled terrible.

Ner climbed through first.

He immediately looked back.

“I hate this plan.”

Zee whispered, “You agreed.”

“I was younger then.”

“That was ten minutes ago.”

“A lot has happened.”

Aurelia entered last.

The maintenance corridor was narrow enough that Zee’s horns scraped the overhead beams twice.

He stopped.

“This place was designed badly.”

Ner looked back.

“It was designed for people without horns.”

“Exactly.”

Aurelia pushed both forward.

They descended.

Zee listened.

Heartbeats above.

Below.

Ahead.

Many.

Then four weak rhythms behind iron.

“Here.”

Aurelia stopped.

“Certain?”

“Yes.”

The corridor ended at a locked service door.

Ner removed one glove.

Pure Golden Mana gathered around his fingertips.

Zee looked at him.

“Careful.”

Ner raised an eyebrow.

“You are telling me that?”

“Yes.”

“That is alarming.”

Ner touched the lock.

Golden Mana moved through it.

Not forcing.

Reading.

The mechanism opened.

No alarm.

Ner smiled.

Zee frowned.

“You are pleased with yourself.”

“Yes.”

“Annoying.”

“You would be worse.”

True.

The next chamber contained cages.

Not cells.

Cages.

Seven spaces arranged around a central platform.

Three empty.

Four occupied.

Aurelia stopped at the entrance.

Zee had seen his mother angry.

Terrified.

Heartbroken.

He had never seen her look like this.

The four captives were alive.

Barely.

An old man lay curled inside the first cage.

A woman with silver-red hair occupied the second.

A younger Dragon-blooded man sat shackled in the third.

And in the fourth…

A woman lifted her head.

Dark red eyes.

Long hair nearly white from stress and age that Zee could not guess.

Thin.

Scarred.

Alive.

Aurelia whispered:

“Seraphine.”

The woman stared.

Nothing happened.

No recognition.

Then Aurelia stepped closer.

“Seraphine.”

Her voice broke.

The captive’s eyes widened.

She stood too quickly and stumbled against the bars.

“No.”

Aurelia reached the cage.

“It is me.”

“No.”

“Seraphine.”

“No.”

Tears appeared instantly.

“You are dead.”

Aurelia wrapped both hands around the bars.

“So are you.”

Seraphine laughed once.

Then sobbed.

“Aurelia?”

“Yes.”

The word barely left Aurelia before Seraphine reached through the bars.

Aurelia caught her hands.

For several breaths, Zee forgot where they were.

Forgot the auction above them.

Forgot Khell.

Forgot guards.

He watched his mother become someone’s sister.

Not queen.

Not survivor.

Not Dragon-Witch.

Little flame.

Seraphine pressed her forehead against the iron.

“You got old.”

Aurelia laughed through tears.

“You too.”

“I hate you.”

“I know.”

“You left.”

“You told me to run.”

“I was eight!”

“So was I!”

Seraphine stared.

Then laughed again.

It hurt to hear.

Zee looked toward Ner.

Ner’s eyes had gone wet.

He immediately looked away.

Zee did not tease him.

Not this time.

Then Seraphine noticed Zee.

Her face changed.

She stared at the horns first.

Then the Crimson scales.

Then Aurelia.

“Aurelia…”

“My son.”

Seraphine looked back at Zee.

Zee lifted one hand awkwardly.

“Hi.”

Seraphine stared.

Ner whispered, “Powerful introduction.”

Zee elbowed him.

Seraphine laughed weakly.

That seemed to decide something.

“He looks like trouble.”

Aurelia wiped tears from her face.

“He is.”

Zee frowned.

“Mother.”

Seraphine looked at him again.

“What is your name?”

“Zee.”

Her expression softened.

“I am Seraphine.”

“I know.”

“Apparently.”

He stepped closer.

“I came to get you out.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“We came.”

Zee nodded.

“We.”

Better.

Seraphine looked between mother and son.

Then her face hardened.

“Khell.”

Aurelia’s grief vanished.

“Where?”

“Above.”

“Does he know we are here?”

“No.”

A voice came from the doorway.

“Yes.”

Everyone turned.

Damaris Khell stood there.

He did not look like a blood broker.

That annoyed Zee.

He looked ordinary.

Middle-aged.

Expensive coat.

Dark beard.

No visible supernatural traits.

No theatrical weapon.

Just a man who had built a fortune by turning people into inventory.

Six guards stood behind him.

Khell looked at Aurelia.

Then Zee.

Then Ner’s uncovered hand.

His expression became very still.

“Well.”

Zee hated that word.

Khell smiled.

“Tonight has become valuable.”

Aurelia moved in front of the cages.

“You have exactly one chance.”

“To?”

“Open them.”

Khell looked toward Seraphine.

“I spent years assembling this collection.”

“They are people.”

“To you.”

Ner’s Golden Mana ignited.

“To everyone.”

Khell looked at him.

Interest sharpened.

“Pure.”

Zee stepped in front of Ner.

“No.”

Khell’s eyes moved toward him.

Crimson.

Horns.

Scales.

Fangs visible now.

He frowned.

“I know you.”

Zee’s expression hardened.

“No, you do not.”

“The Darkheart child.”

“That is not knowing me.”

Khell smiled.

“Fair.”

Then he lifted one finger.

Every cage ignited with red runes.

The captives screamed.

Aurelia turned.

Khell backed into the corridor.

“One step and the restraints constrict.”

Ner’s face tightened.

“Again?”

Zee looked toward him.

“We are getting good at these.”

“This is not the time.”

“I know.”

Khell smiled.

“I assume Morcant failed.”

Aurelia stared.

“She is alive.”

His smile vanished.

Interesting.

Caldrin’s voice sounded from behind Khell.

“And disappointed.”

Khell turned.

Too late.

Caldrin’s blade touched his back.

Rez stood behind him.

Mara beside Rez.

The guards spun.

Blue-White Mana exploded.

Rez changed the corridor in one move.

Two guards were thrown into the wall.

A third lost footing as Blue-White frost spread beneath him.

Caldrin disarmed a fourth.

Mara drew a short blade and held it against Khell’s ribs.

Khell stared at her.

“You.”

Mara looked at him.

“Me.”

“You were paid.”

“I was.”

“You profited.”

“I did.”

“You do not get to become righteous because guilt became inconvenient.”

Mara’s face hardened.

“No.”

She pressed the knife harder.

“But I can become useful.”

Khell smiled.

Then vanished.

Not magic.

Trapdoor.

The floor beneath him dropped.

Caldrin swore.

Mara nearly fell after him.

Rez grabbed her.

Zee heard Khell landing below.

Alive.

Running.

Aurelia turned toward the cages.

“Let him go.”

Zee stared.

“What?”

“For now.”

“He has—”

“Our family is here.”

That decided it.

Ner moved toward the runes.

“I can break them.”

Seraphine grabbed the bars.

“No.”

Ner stopped.

“Why?”

“They are linked.”

“Everything here is linked.”

Seraphine looked toward Aurelia.

“Khell built failure into every cage.”

Aurelia’s face hardened.

“So we do not break them.”

Zee looked at the floor.

Then the walls.

Then smiled.

Ner immediately understood.

“Oh no.”

“What?”

“You have the wall idea again.”

“It worked.”

Aurelia stared at both boys.

“Explain.”

Zee pointed around the cages.

“We take the room.”

Rez blinked.

“What?”

“The cages are linked to the ship.”

“So?”

Zee smiled.

“Zareth is outside.”

Aurelia’s face changed instantly.

“No.”

“Mother.”

“No.”

“We do not burn anything.”

“No.”

“We remove the roof.”

“No.”

Rez looked toward Ner.

“This is going to happen.”

Ner nodded.

“It always happens.”

Zee sent one thought through the Dragon Link.

Careful.

Zareth answered.

Small fire?

“No fire.”

Disappointment.

Then:

Break.

Zee smiled.

Aurelia saw.

“Zee Darkheart—”

The ship shook.

Everyone looked up.

A massive impact struck the upper deck.

Buyers screamed.

Wood splintered.

Zareth roared.

The cages remained intact.

The ceiling did not.

A crimson Dragon claw tore through the roof.

Rez looked upward.

“He actually listened.”

Zee grinned.

“Mostly.”

Aurelia covered her face.
