Chapter Sixty-Seven

The Hand on the Dragon Link

Zareth was not captured.

That was the first thing Zee learned.

The second was worse.

Someone had found a way to touch the Dragon Link from outside.

Not control it.

Not enter it fully.

Touch.

Enough to send pain.

Enough to make Zee think Zareth was hurt when he was not.

Enough to prove the bond had become visible to someone else.

Zareth arrived at Darkheart before midnight.

Furious.

Uninjured except for one missing shoulder scale.

The same scale inside the box.

Zee stood in the courtyard.

The Link opened instantly.

Zareth showed him what happened.

Drakonis council chamber.

A visiting envoy.

Human-shaped.

Face hidden behind white cloth.

No heartbeat Zee could detect through the memory.

The envoy asked to see the Crown Prince privately.

Zareth refused.

The figure threw powder.

Not poison.

Something that made Dragon Mana flare uncontrollably.

Then touched one scale.

Cut it free.

Escaped through a collapsing shadow.

Zareth chased.

Lost the trail.

Then pain hit the Link.

Artificial.

Inserted.

Ner listened carefully.

“So they did not hurt you.”

Zareth growled.

Zee translated.

“No.”

Rez folded his arms.

“They wanted Zee to react.”

Zee looked at the scale.

“Or prove they could reach the bond.”

Ner stepped closer.

Pure Gold traced around the metal box.

“Something is attached.”

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“What?”

“Not spellwork I know.”

“Blood?”

“No.”

“Dragon Mana?”

“Partly.”

Ner looked toward Zareth.

“Your scale is carrying an imprint of the Link.”

Zareth’s head snapped toward him.

Zee went still.

“Can you remove it?”

“I do not know.”

Good.

Ner studied.

Rez paced once.

“Do not destroy the scale.”

Zee looked toward him.

“Why?”

“It is bait.”

“Yes.”

“Which means destroying it might trigger whatever they expect.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“Future king.”

Rez glared.

“Current idiot.”

Ner almost laughed.

Zareth did not.

The Link had become violated territory.

Dragons understood that differently.

Zee felt his anger beneath everything.

Not fear.

Offense.

Possession of self.

Someone had touched what belonged only to them.

Zee understood.

He did not need to calm him.

He felt the same.

⸻

Ner discovered the first rule an hour later.

The imprint reacted to Zee.

Not proximity.

Attention.

When Zee focused directly on the scale, the hidden pattern brightened.

When he looked away, it faded.

Ner frowned.

“It is reading recognition.”

Rez looked at him.

“Meaning?”

“It wants Zee to know it is there.”

Zee leaned against the table.

“Dramatic.”

Ner stared.

“You are one to talk.”

Fair.

Zareth sent impatience through the Link.

Zee looked at him.

“You want to hunt.”

Immediate agreement.

Wolf instinct inside Zee answered.

Yes.

Rez saw.

“No.”

Zee frowned.

“No?”

“You have no trail.”

“I have the scale.”

“You have bait.”

Ner nodded.

“Rez is right.”

Zee looked betrayed.

Ner continued.

“We need to make it think the bait worked.”

Silence.

Then Zee smiled.

That one was darker.

Rez noticed.

“There.”

“What?”

“That face means you like the plan before we have one.”

“I like deception.”

“Nanda Parbat ruined you.”

“Improved me.”

Zareth’s amusement finally entered the Link.

Good.

Still them.

Ner placed both hands over the box.

“If this imprint reads Zee’s attention, I may be able to make it believe he is focused on it even when he is not.”

Rez understood.

“Fake the signal.”

“Yes.”

Zee stepped closer.

“And when someone checks?”

“We follow.”

Rez pointed.

“Not you.”

Zee’s expression cooled.

“Explain.”

“If the trap is designed for you, you are the obvious thing they will watch.”

Zee hated when logic worked against him.

Rez continued.

“Ner and I follow the magical trace.”

“Zareth takes sky.”

“You remain visible.”

Zee stared.

“At Darkheart.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

Ner looked at him.

“You are King.”

“That is not an argument.”

“It is today.”

Zee’s jaw tightened.

Zareth sent agreement.

Traitor.

Zee sighed.

“Fine.”

Rez blinked.

“That was easy.”

“I am growing.”

“No.”

Zee smiled.

“Also I already thought of another way.”

Everyone looked at him.

Of course.

⸻

Zee stayed publicly visible.

Very publicly.

He held court.

Annoying.

Useful.

Every visitor saw him.

Darkheart nobles watched him hear petitions, settle disputes, and threaten one merchant with seizure after the man tried bribing a border judge.

Zee barely raised his voice.

“Return the money.”

The merchant swallowed.

“I—”

Zee looked at him.

Human pupils shifted into slits.

“Return it.”

Compulsion pressed through the room.

The merchant’s resistance lasted three seconds.

Then:

“Yes, Majesty.”

Good.

Zee continued court.

Meanwhile, Ner created a false recognition pulse around Zareth’s stolen scale.

Rez followed the reaction through prepared observers.

Zareth flew high enough not to be seen from ground trails.

No one needed Zee physically.

That irritated him more than he expected.

Good.

His people were capable.

His friends were not extensions of him.

He could sit on a throne while they hunted.

Still hated it.

⸻

The trap led to a river shrine thirty miles east.

Not ancient.

Not divine.

Recently abandoned.

Rez entered first.

Ner behind.

Zareth remained above.

The shrine contained one person.

A woman in white cloth.

Same envoy.

She looked toward Rez.

“You are not him.”

Rez smiled.

“Disappointed?”

She moved.

Fast.

Not Human.

Ner’s Gold snapped around the doorway.

The woman hit the barrier.

Her body distorted.

Not shapeshifting.

More like her edges refused to stay fixed.

Rez drew his sword.

“What are you?”

No answer.

She touched the barrier.

It began blackening.

Ner’s face changed.

“Do not let her touch me.”

Rez moved immediately.

Blue-White Mana froze the floor beneath the woman.

She slipped.

Ner tightened the Gold.

Zareth crashed through the roof.

Careful enough not to crush Rez.

Barely.

The woman looked up.

Too late.

Zareth pinned her beneath one claw.

The Link exploded back toward Zee.

Found.

Alive.

Unknown.

Zee stood from the Darkheart throne in the middle of a tax dispute.

Everyone stopped talking.

He looked at Lord Merek.

“Continue without me.”

Merek blinked.

“Majesty?”

Zee was already gone.

Vampire speed.

⸻

He reached the shrine before the woman escaped.

Barely.

She had burned through half of Ner’s Golden boundary.

Rez had blood on one sleeve.

Zareth’s claw smoked where she touched him.

Zee entered silently.

The woman did not notice.

Good.

Assassin.

He stood behind her.

Knife touched the base of her skull.

“Don’t move.”

She froze.

Not from compulsion.

Calculation.

Then laughed.

“You came.”

Zee’s voice stayed calm.

“You wanted me.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“To see whether the stories were true.”

Zee’s expression changed.

That was almost disappointing.

“Which stories?”

“That you killed a God.”

“Yes.”

“That your blood changed.”

“Yes.”

“That Dragon blood lives inside the Tribrid.”

Zareth growled.

The woman continued.

“That the bond between you can be reached.”

Zee pressed the knife slightly harder.

“By you?”

“By anyone who knows where to cut.”

Ner’s face hardened.

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“Bad answer.”

The woman smiled beneath the cloth.

“You are becoming visible.”

“Everyone knows me.”

“No.”

She turned her head just enough to look sideways.

“Not like this.”

Zee smelled no ordinary blood.

No heartbeat.

No Witch Mana.

No Demon signature.

Something else.

Unknown.

Good.

The world still had surprises.

He asked:

“Who sent you?”

“No one.”

Truth?

His blood sense could not tell.

Interesting.

“What do you want?”

“To test the link.”

“Why?”

“To know whether it breaks.”

Zareth’s anger hit the bond like fire.

The woman smiled.

“It doesn’t.”

Zee looked at Zareth.

Then Ner.

Then Rez.

No mastermind.

No kingdom.

No obvious political motive.

Just someone testing.

That was somehow worse.

Zee’s voice dropped.

“You cut a scale from my brother to satisfy curiosity.”

The woman shrugged.

“Yes.”

Wrong.

That line was enough.

Zee removed the knife.

Everyone noticed.

The woman smiled.

Thought mercy.

Then Zee caught her throat.

Vampire strength.

Lifted.

Her body distorted.

He held anyway.

Crimson Demonic Dragon heat surged through his palm.

The distortion stabilized.

She looked surprised.

Zee smiled.

“There.”

New weakness.

Not copied.

Observed.

He drove her against the wall.

“Tell me what you are.”

“No.”

He compelled.

“Tell me.”

Nothing.

No effect.

Interesting.

Stronger resistance.

Or no blood pathway compatible with his dominance.

Zee smiled wider.

The woman looked unsettled.

“You enjoy failure?”

“I enjoy learning.”

That answer bothered her.

Good.

She struck him.

Her palm phased through his chest—

almost.

Crimson scales flared beneath the skin.

His Demonic Dragon adaptation resisted the distortion enough to force her hand partially solid.

Zee caught the wrist.

Broke it.

She screamed.

Finally.

He liked that.

Ner watched.

No comment.

Zee leaned close.

“You touched my family.”

The woman’s breathing remained strange.

No heartbeat.

Still pain.

“Do you know what happens now?”

She smiled weakly.

“You kill me.”

“Yes.”

No threat.

Fact.

He drove the knife into her heart position.

Nothing.

No heart.

Of course.

She laughed.

Zee laughed too.

Then Wolf instinct noticed something.

Not heartbeat.

Center of movement.

Every time she twisted, her weight protected the lower left ribs.

Weak point.

Assassin Zee saw it.

He struck there.

The woman screamed louder.

Something dark spilled from the wound.

Not blood.

Liquid shadow.

Zee’s Vampire hunger reacted anyway.

Curiosity.

Ner immediately said:

“Do not.”

Zee looked at him.

Ner’s face was serious.

“We do not know what that is.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“Exactly.”

Rez groaned.

“Zee.”

For once, Zee listened.

Not because they controlled him.

Because the argument was good.

He did not feed.

He killed her another way.

Crimson Witchcraft closed around the wound.

“Burn.”

Dark crimson fire consumed the liquid shadow from inside.

The woman convulsed.

Then collapsed.

Dead.

No blood.

No drain.

No answer to species.

Permanent progress anyway.

They knew the Dragon Link could be externally sensed under certain conditions.

They knew it could not be broken easily.

They knew unfamiliar entities had begun testing Zee directly.

And Zee learned something else.

His new evolution could force certain unstable supernatural bodies into solidity through contact and heat.

Not a copied power.

Another Crimson interaction.

Useful.

Dangerous.

He stood.

Rez looked at the body.

“Who was she?”

Zee wiped his knife.

“No idea.”

Ner sighed.

“You are getting too comfortable with that.”

“Yes.”

Zareth lowered his head.

The Link carried irritation.

Then one more image.

The severed scale.

Zee looked toward him.

“You want it back.”

Immediate.

Ner smiled.

“I can remove the imprint now.”

Good.

They returned to Darkheart before dawn.

Ner cleansed the scale with Gold and Bloodsong together.

Zareth pressed it against the damaged spot on his shoulder.

Dragon healing began pulling it back into place.

Zee watched.

Then felt something at the edge of his territory.

Not the Link.

Wolf instinct.

Smoke.

Far west.

Too much.

A messenger entered seconds later.

“Majesty.”

Zee turned.

“Now what?”

The man swallowed.

“Three villages.”

“Burning.”

Rez closed his eyes.

Ner muttered something rude.

Zareth’s Link carried exhausted amusement.

Zee looked toward the western windows.

Another attack.

Another reason.

Another direction.

He did not sigh.

Did not complain.

His pupils narrowed.

“Find out who started it.”

The messenger bowed and ran.

Zee looked toward the others.

No rest.

No peace.

Fine.

The world wanted pressure.

It would get pressure back.

And slowly, almost invisibly, Zee Darkheart was beginning to stop thinking like a man waiting for danger to arrive.

He was starting to think like a King deciding which dangers should be allowed to exist at all.
