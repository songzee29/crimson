Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Four

The Blood That Chose Its Own Shape

Zayrlen developed a habit of waking before sunrise.

Not crying.

Watching.

Zee discovered this on the third morning after returning from Vesper.

He entered the nursery quietly.

Zayrlen was already awake.

Crimson-Gold eyes followed him across the room.

No sound.

No movement except one tiny hand opening and closing against the blanket.

Zee stopped beside the cradle.

“You are supposed to be asleep.”

Zayrlen stared.

Zee stared back.

The baby blinked.

Then a tiny patch of Crimson-and-Gold scales appeared along his temple.

Gone.

Zee smiled.

“Terrible argument.”

Ner’s voice came from the doorway.

“You are debating a newborn again.”

Zee looked over.

“He started it.”

“He cannot speak.”

“That has not stopped him.”

Ner entered.

Recovery had gone well.

Tessa had finally stopped threatening to physically lock him in bed.

Mostly.

Zayrlen saw Ner.

His heartbeat changed.

Wolf recognition.

Then he looked back at Zee.

Same response.

Both fathers.

Pack.

Ner noticed.

“He does that every morning now.”

“Yes.”

“He checks for us.”

Zee’s expression changed slightly.

Ner saw that too.

“Do not make it sad.”

“I was not.”

“You were.”

Zee looked down at Zayrlen.

Then reached into the cradle.

The baby immediately caught his finger.

No fear.

No hesitation.

Public child.

Known child.

Protected child.

Loved without concealment.

Zee exhaled.

“Fine.”

Ner smiled.

“Progress.”

⸻

Darkheart’s defenses had become aggressive enough to annoy its own citizens.

That meant Rez considered them successful.

A merchant arrived at the eastern gate and spent twenty minutes arguing with a ward that refused to believe his mule was not enchanted.

It was not.

The mule simply hated everyone.

Elias inspected the ward.

Then the mule.

“The ward is correct.”

The merchant stared.

“What?”

Elias nodded toward the animal.

“There is definitely evil here.”

The mule kicked him.

Elias looked offended.

Rez laughed from the gatehouse.

“Excellent detection.”

Elias raised one hand.

Deep-blue Mana formed.

Black demonic sigils appeared.

The mule backed away immediately.

Rez pointed.

“You are threatening livestock.”

“It started first.”

Darkheart had returned to something resembling normal.

That never lasted.

⸻

The first indication came from Rhyven.

He was patrolling the western forest when his Phoenix-Wolf nature reacted to something underground.

Not danger.

Heat.

He stopped.

His claws ignited.

Then extinguished.

Amara noticed.

“What?”

Rhyven crouched.

Placed one hand against the soil.

“Something is warm.”

“Burrow?”

“No.”

“Magic?”

“Maybe.”

Amara shifted partially.

Wolf senses opened.

She smelled nothing unusual.

Rhyven’s Phoenix side felt it instead.

Heat moving beneath the earth in deliberate lines.

Not lava.

Not fire.

Something traveling.

He stood.

“It is going toward Darkheart.”

Amara’s expression changed.

“Call Zee.”

⸻

By the time Zee arrived, the heat had crossed half a mile.

Phoenix blood recognized it immediately.

Not Phoenix.

Something adjacent.

Artificial heat.

Contained.

Moving through stone.

Zee’s Witch sight opened.

There.

A narrow line beneath the soil.

Runes.

Not attacking.

Transporting something.

Kael arrived next.

Then Elias.

Zareth landed in Human form.

Rez was still in Vesper.

Ner remained at the keep with Zayrlen.

For now.

Zee crouched.

“Someone built another route beneath us.”

Amara frowned.

“Not another tunnel.”

“No.”

Elias studied the runes.

“A spellway.”

Zee looked toward him.

“Old?”

“Yes.”

“How old?”

“Older than Darkheart’s current borders.”

Good.

Not another enemy who had built beneath them yesterday without detection.

This had existed before Zee expanded.

Kingdom progression had simply reached it.

Zareth touched the soil.

His Witch senses reacted.

Then his Dragon side.

“Something living traveled through here.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“What?”

Zareth shook his head.

“Not sure.”

Rhyven’s Phoenix heat surged again.

Then a roar came from beneath the ground.

Not loud.

Deep.

Everyone stopped.

Zareth’s eyes widened.

“Dragon.”

Zee looked at him.

“Drakonis?”

“No.”

“Veyrath?”

Zareth listened.

“No.”

Another Dragon bloodline.

Ancient.

Independent.

Good.

The world remained larger than their family.

⸻

They opened the spellway.

Carefully this time.

Mostly because Elias threatened to leave if Zee simply punched through another ancient magical structure.

The entrance appeared beneath three layers of stone.

A circular shaft lined with red-metal runes.

Warm air rose from below.

Then came the smell.

Dragon.

Blood.

And something else.

Wolf.

Zee froze.

Amara noticed.

“You smell it too.”

“Yes.”

Not one body.

Two.

One Dragon.

One Wolf.

Both injured.

Badly.

Zee descended first.

Phoenix wings carried him through the shaft.

Zareth followed by magic.

Rhyven and Amara climbed.

Elias created steps for the others.

The chamber beneath had been sealed for centuries.

Broken chains lined one wall.

Old blood covered another.

At the center—

a Dragon lay in Human form.

Female.

Dark copper skin.

Black hair.

Burned red scales across both arms.

One horn broken.

Beside her—

a Wolf man.

Barely conscious.

His body covered in old scar tissue.

They were not attacking.

They were trying to survive.

Zee listened.

Two heartbeats.

Weak.

The Dragon opened one eye.

Saw Zee’s horns.

Saw Zareth.

Then smelled Crimson Phoenix blood.

Her face changed.

“Not them.”

Zee crouched.

“Who?”

She tried moving.

Failed.

“The collectors.”

Zee’s expression hardened.

Again?

Then the Wolf man spoke weakly.

“Not blood.”

Different.

Good.

Zee looked toward him.

“What do they collect?”

The Wolf swallowed.

“Bonds.”

Silence.

Zareth’s expression changed.

Dragon Link.

Zee immediately looked at him.

The Dragon woman saw it.

Then laughed once despite the pain.

“You have one.”

Zareth went very still.

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“You know what this is?”

She looked between them.

“Yes.”

“What is it called?”

“A soul-link.”

Zee’s expression did not change.

Zareth’s did.

Because that sounded too close to something neither wanted casually named by strangers.

The Dragon continued.

“Rare. Natural. Cannot be manufactured correctly.”

Good.

“People have been trying.”

Worse.

⸻

They brought both survivors above.

Tessa arrived from Darkheart personally.

Ner came too.

With Zayrlen.

Zee saw him and immediately frowned.

Ner stared.

“Do not.”

“There are injured strangers with unknown bloodlines.”

“Yes.”

“And you brought our son.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because Tessa needed my Bloodsong to stabilize one of them.”

Zee looked at the Wolf.

His heartbeat was collapsing.

Ner was right.

Annoying.

Again.

He handed Zayrlen to Elias.

Elias blinked.

“Why me?”

“Because your hands are clean.”

“That is disturbingly rare.”

Zee ignored him.

Ner knelt beside the Wolf.

Pure Gold Mana opened.

Bloodsong reached into the damaged rhythm.

The heartbeat steadied.

Barely.

Tessa worked.

The Dragon woman watched Zayrlen.

Her eyes widened.

“What is that child?”

Every member of Zee’s family turned toward her at once.

She noticed.

Good.

Ner answered first.

“Our son.”

The Dragon woman looked at Zee.

Then Zayrlen.

Her senses reacted.

Dragon.

Witch.

Wolf.

Natural.

Her face went still.

“They will come for him.”

Zee’s expression flattened.

“Who?”

“The Bond Collectors.”

“That is a terrible name.”

Elias muttered:

“Agreed.”

The Dragon woman ignored them.

“They do not take bloodlines.”

“What do they take?”

“Relationships.”

Ner’s expression changed.

The woman looked at Zee and Zareth.

“They find rare supernatural bonds. Dragon Links. Pack-binding. Witch soul-pacts. Bloodsong resonance. Sire connections.”

Rez was absent.

But Zee immediately thought of him.

Aidan.

Uncle Rez.

Memory.

No.

Not relevant.

Not exposed.

He pushed it aside.

The Dragon woman continued.

“They cut the bond out of one person and force it into another.”

Zareth’s face became murderous.

Zee’s wings opened.

Crimson fire moved.

That was enough explanation.

⸻

The collectors arrived before sunset.

Fast.

Too fast.

Meaning they had been tracking the survivors.

Not Darkheart.

Good.

Independent event.

No conspiracy swallowing everything.

Five black riders crossed the western boundary.

No banner.

No masks.

No attempt at diplomacy.

Each carried a long hooked weapon made from silver-black bone.

Zareth saw them.

His Link with Zee flared instinctively.

The lead rider smiled.

“There.”

Bad.

He could perceive it.

Zee stepped forward.

“Leave.”

The rider looked at him.

“Zee Darkheart.”

“Yes.”

“We only want what belongs to us.”

Zee looked toward the survivors.

“They do not.”

“The Dragon carries a severed bond.”

Zareth’s expression sharpened.

“Severed from whom?”

The woman behind them answered.

“My brother.”

Silence.

The collector smiled.

“He died.”

Her face broke for half a second.

Then hardened.

“And you kept the link.”

“Part of it.”

Zee understood immediately.

The spellway.

The heat.

The suffering.

They had been trying to take an inherited echo of a dead Dragon Link.

Not blood.

Not race.

A relationship made supernatural.

Disgusting in a new direction.

The collector pointed toward Zareth.

“But yours is better.”

That was the last useful sentence he ever spoke.

Zee killed him.

Immediately.

The other four attacked.

⸻

One hook struck toward Zareth.

Zee caught it with a Phoenix wing.

The weapon bit through three feathers.

Then something tried pulling.

Not flesh.

Link.

Zee felt the tug inside his skull.

His eyes changed.

Zareth felt it too.

Rage struck through the Link.

They touched it.

Zee’s answer came colder.

I know.

The hook tried extracting the connection.

The Dragon Link stretched.

Not broke.

Zee grabbed the weapon.

Crimson Phoenix flame burned along it.

The collector holding the other end screamed.

Zee pulled him forward.

Dragon strength.

Vampire speed.

Wolf aggression.

Witch precision.

One movement.

He tore the man’s arm off.

Then killed him.

Zareth shifted.

True Dragon form erupted.

Teal-and-Gold scales.

Phoenix fire.

Witch sigils.

He hit another collector hard enough to bury him in the ground.

No humor.

No mercy.

They had tried to touch the Link that had existed since birth.

Wrong family.

⸻

Ner protected the survivors.

And Zayrlen.

Pure Gold Mana formed a dome.

Bloodsong destabilized one rider who crossed too close.

The collector stumbled.

Rhyven hit him in true Wolf form.

Crimson fire followed claws.

The man survived the first impact.

Elias ended the second.

Deep-blue Witchcraft formed around black Demon sigils.

“Down.”

The collector slammed into stone.

Kael moved with Vampire speed.

Ended him.

The last rider turned toward Ner.

Saw Zayrlen in Elias’s arms.

His expression changed.

Opportunity.

He moved.

Zee saw it.

So did everyone else.

The man never got within twenty feet.

Amara hit first.

Wolf speed.

Rhyven hit second.

Phoenix-Wolf fire.

Elias’s Demon-Witch restraint locked the legs.

Kael broke one arm.

Ner’s Bloodsong stopped the heart.

The body collapsed.

Zee had not touched him.

Good.

He did not need to.

That was the point.

Zayrlen was surrounded by monsters.

Plural.

⸻

The Dragon woman stared at Ner.

“You killed him.”

Ner looked at the body.

“He was moving toward my son.”

Fair.

Tessa continued healing the Wolf man without looking up.

“Can everyone stop fighting near my patients?”

Zareth returned to Human form.

“You chose the battlefield badly.”

“They came here.”

“Still.”

Tessa glared.

Zareth stopped speaking.

Even Dragons learned eventually.

⸻

The collectors were dead.

Arc over.

No surviving master.

No secret larger order.

The two survivors gave names.

Not exposition.

People.

The Dragon woman was Selyra Kaen.

The Wolf was Torren Vale.

Brother and sister by choice, not blood.

They had spent twelve years surviving together after Selyra’s actual Dragon brother died.

The collectors wanted the remaining echo of her original sibling Link.

Torren had helped her escape.

That was why he had almost died.

Zee looked at him.

“Why?”

Torren frowned.

“Why what?”

“You are Wolf.”

“Yes.”

“She is Dragon.”

“Yes.”

“No blood relation.”

“No.”

“You nearly died anyway.”

Torren looked offended.

“She is family.”

Zee smiled faintly.

Correct answer.

⸻

Selyra recovered enough to leave three days later.

She refused Darkheart sanctuary.

That pleased Zee.

Not because he wanted her gone.

Because independence mattered.

She would return to her own people in the western volcanic territories.

Before leaving, she looked toward Zayrlen.

The baby was awake in Ner’s arms.

“Your son’s Dragon nature is unusual.”

Zee stared.

“Everyone keeps saying that.”

“It is not a warning.”

“Good.”

Selyra looked at Zareth.

Then Zee.

“The thing behind him is not a Link.”

“We know.”

“It is blood memory.”

Zee’s expression changed.

“Meaning?”

“Dragon inheritance can carry ancestral instinct without carrying ancestral consciousness.”

Good.

No ghosts.

No Aurelia resurrection.

No hidden memory spirit.

Just blood.

Selyra continued.

“He may express more of Veyrath than you expect.”

Zee looked toward Zayrlen.

Crimson-Gold eyes.

Tiny scales.

Heat.

Something enormous waiting.

“Good.”

Selyra almost smiled.

Then left.

⸻

That night, Zayrlen developed another problem.

He refused sleep.

For three hours.

Ner blamed Zee.

Zee blamed genetics.

Ner stared.

“Whose?”

Zee paused.

“Yours.”

Ner threw a pillow.

Zayrlen laughed.

Not a real laugh yet.

Closer to a breathy sound.

But both fathers froze.

Again.

The baby did it a second time.

Ner’s face changed completely.

Zee stared.

“Was that—”

“Yes.”

Zayrlen made the sound again.

A tiny Crimson-and-Gold spark escaped his hand.

Ner smiled.

Zee smiled too.

Outside, kingdoms hunted new blood.

Collectors hunted bonds.

Ancient forests suppressed supernatural life.

Darkheart kept expanding.

The world remained dangerous.

Inside the nursery—

their son had almost learned how to laugh.

For tonight, that mattered more.

⸻
