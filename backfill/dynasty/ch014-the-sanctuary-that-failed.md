Chapter Fourteen

The Sanctuary That Failed

House Vesper sent no banners.

That was Lucien’s first decision.

No royal carriage.

No mounted guard dressed in blue and silver.

No obvious procession announcing that Queen Aurelia Darkheart and the most hunted child in the known kingdoms had left the capital.

They departed before dawn under rain.

Aurelia rode beneath a dark traveling cloak with Zee secured against her chest and the crimson hatchling tucked close beneath the same heavy fabric. Captain Vale rode beside her. Caldrin took the lead. The eldest royal witch followed behind while four Vesper soldiers traveled in plain leather armor without crests.

King Lucien came too.

That surprised everyone except Rez.

Prince Rez Vesper had argued for nearly an hour when told he would remain behind.

He lost.

Then argued again.

He lost more loudly.

Now he stood atop the western wall of Vesper’s capital, arms folded, watching the group disappear into the rain.

His blue-white mana flickered around his fingers.

“You promised,” he muttered.

Lucien had promised nothing.

Rez had chosen to remember the conversation differently.

Far below, Lucien glanced once toward the wall.

He could barely see his son through the rain.

Aurelia noticed.

“You could have stayed.”

“So could you.”

“That is different.”

“Every parent believes that.”

Aurelia looked down at Zee.

He slept through the movement of the horse.

The crimson hatchling did not.

His head remained outside Aurelia’s cloak, golden eyes scanning the trees. His injured wing had healed almost completely, though he still favored it whenever he shifted.

The Dragon Link remained restless.

Zee slept.

Zareth watched.

Whenever distance opened even slightly between their bodies, unease moved through them both.

Near.

Stay.

Together.

Aurelia had begun recognizing the pattern even if she could not feel the Link herself.

“They hate being separated.”

Lucien looked toward the hatchling.

“I noticed.”

“Someone tried taking him from Zee yesterday.”

“What happened?”

“He screamed until every lamp in the room shattered.”

Lucien raised an eyebrow.

“Which one?”

“Both.”

Caldrin looked back from the front of the line.

“That should simplify childcare.”

Vale ignored him.

The road narrowed.

The Vesper foothills rose ahead, thick with pine forests and old stone formations covered in moss. No villages remained this far northwest. No farm smoke. No roadside shrines.

Only forest.

That was intentional.

The sanctuary had once been built far from major roads.

Lucien explained as they rode.

“It was established during my grandfather’s reign.”

“For dangerous children?” Aurelia asked.

“For children other kingdoms considered dangerous.”

“There is a difference.”

“Yes.”

Lucien’s expression tightened.

“Some were born with unstable magic. Others belonged to hunted bloodlines. A few were heirs whose enemies found children easier to assassinate than kings.”

Caldrin glanced back.

“A lovely place.”

“It was.”

“What happened?”

Lucien remained silent for several strides.

Then:

“One winter, every caretaker died.”

Vale looked toward him.

“At once?”

“Over three nights.”

“Children?”

“Gone.”

Aurelia frowned.

“Gone how?”

“No bodies.”

“Tracks?”

“None.”

“Blood?”

“Yes.”

Lucien looked ahead.

“A great deal.”

Caldrin sighed.

“And this is the safer option.”

“No.”

Lucien’s voice hardened.

“It is the option we can investigate without placing ten thousand Vesper civilians around Zee.”

That answer ended the argument.

The sanctuary appeared near midday.

It sat between two ridges beneath towering pines.

Old gray walls surrounded a manor built partially into the mountainside. Ivy had overtaken the northern towers. Iron gates leaned crooked beneath twenty years of rust.

No birds nested there.

No animals crossed the road.

The hatchling noticed immediately.

His body stiffened.

Zee woke.

Crimson eyes opened.

Aurelia slowed her horse.

“What is it?”

The hatchling stared at the manor.

The Dragon Link carried something unfamiliar.

Not danger.

Not yet.

Empty.

Zee responded with unease.

Lucien raised one hand.

Everyone stopped.

“From here, we walk.”

Caldrin dismounted.

“Because horses are difficult indoors?”

“Because something frightened every animal away.”

Caldrin stopped smiling.

They entered through the broken gate.

The courtyard was covered in weeds.

Children’s toys remained where they had fallen decades earlier.

A wooden sword.

A stone doll.

A little iron ring used for rolling games.

Aurelia stared at them.

“Twenty years.”

Lucien nodded.

“No one took anything.”

Vale crouched near the old fountain.

“Someone has been here.”

Lucien turned.

“How recently?”

Vale touched disturbed moss.

“Days.”

Caldrin drew his sword.

The Vesper soldiers followed.

The royal witch raised both hands.

Aurelia remained behind them with Zee.

The hatchling climbed onto her shoulder.

They entered the manor.

Dust covered the entrance hall.

Not evenly.

Footprints crossed it.

Many.

Bare feet.

Small.

Aurelia’s stomach tightened.

“Children?”

Vale shook his head.

“Too narrow.”

Caldrin looked toward the ceiling.

“Not all footprints belong to feet.”

A scraping sound came from above.

Everyone raised their weapons.

Nothing moved.

Lucien whispered, “Stay together.”

They crossed into the central hall.

Old portraits lined the walls.

Children.

Dozens.

Every age.

Every race.

Witch children.

Wolf-blooded children.

Pale vampire-born descendants.

Humans.

Others Aurelia did not recognize.

Every portrait had been scratched across the face.

Not by age.

Deliberately.

The royal witch approached one.

“These scratches are fresh.”

Lucien’s expression darkened.

“Someone is using this place.”

Zee stirred.

His vampire hearing caught something.

Far beneath them.

Heartbeat.

One.

Then another.

Then dozens.

He turned his head downward.

The hatchling reacted through the Link.

Below.

Aurelia followed Zee’s gaze.

“What?”

Lucien noticed.

“He hears something.”

Caldrin crouched and pressed one ear to the floor.

Nothing.

Vale did the same.

Then shook his head.

Zee could hear farther.

Deeper.

Something scratched beneath the floor.

Everyone heard that.

Vale stepped backward.

“Now I hear it.”

The floor collapsed.

Three Vesper soldiers disappeared.

Aurelia screamed.

Lucien grabbed her and pulled her back.

Black shapes erupted through the opening.

Not monsters.

Children.

At least they had once been.

Thin bodies climbed over broken stone with impossible speed.

Their skin was gray.

Their eyes clouded white.

Their mouths had been sewn shut with black thread.

The royal witch froze.

“Oh, gods.”

The first child-creature attacked.

Vale blocked.

His sword stopped inches from its throat.

He hesitated.

That nearly killed him.

The creature slammed into his chest and knocked him down.

Caldrin struck it with the flat of his blade.

“Do not think of them as children!”

“They are children!”

“Then find out later!”

The manor filled with movement.

More emerged from below.

Lucien cast.

Dark blue mana struck the floor in a ring around Aurelia, creating a barrier.

The creatures hit it.

Some bounced away.

Others clung to it with skeletal fingers.

Zee stared.

Not afraid.

Confused.

They smelled wrong.

Alive.

But not fully.

The hatchling hissed.

One creature pressed its face against the barrier.

Its white eyes fixed on Zee.

Something changed.

Every creature stopped.

The room went silent.

Lucien kept the barrier raised.

Vale pushed himself upright.

Caldrin whispered, “Why did they stop?”

The creature nearest Zee tilted its head.

The black stitches across its mouth began tearing.

Slowly.

Blood ran down its chin.

Aurelia looked away.

The mouth opened.

The voice that emerged did not belong to a child.

“Three.”

Everyone froze.

The creature looked at Zee.

“Three.”

Another repeated it.

Then another.

Soon the entire hall whispered.

“Three.”

“Three.”

“Three.”

Aurelia’s grip tightened.

“Do not listen.”

The nearest creature crawled closer.

Lucien strengthened the barrier.

It did not attack.

It placed one palm against the magical wall.

Then pointed toward Zee.

“Not one.”

The royal witch’s face drained.

Caldrin looked toward her.

“Do not.”

“I was not going to explain.”

“Good.”

The creature trembled.

Then screamed.

Every child-thing in the hall collapsed.

A black sigil ignited beneath their skin.

Lucien recognized it.

“Back!”

The same kind of death seal used on the assassin.

But larger.

Older.

The sigils burned across dozens of bodies.

Aurelia shielded Zee.

Nothing exploded.

Instead, the creatures began turning to ash.

One after another.

The nearest reached toward Zee before its fingers disappeared.

Its final sound was barely human.

“Run.”

Then it vanished.

Ash filled the hall.

Silence returned.

Lucien lowered the barrier.

No one moved.

Vale looked toward the pit beneath the floor.

“What was done here?”

Lucien’s expression became murderous.

“This was not what killed the sanctuary.”

Caldrin looked at the ash.

“No.”

“Someone came afterward.”

The royal witch crouched near the remains.

She examined the black residue without touching it.

“Those children were altered.”

Aurelia’s voice hardened.

“By whom?”

“I do not know.”

“Can you tell what they were?”

“Some witches.”

She pointed toward another pile.

“Some wolf-blooded.”

Another.

“Possibly vampire.”

Lucien stared at the old portraits.

A sanctuary for hunted children.

Abandoned.

Then repurposed by someone experimenting on them.

Aurelia looked down at Zee.

Suddenly the assassins made more sense.

The scholars.

The questions.

Someone did not simply want Zee dead.

Someone wanted to understand combinations of blood.

Caldrin saw the thought forming.

“Do not jump too far.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“I know.”

He nodded.

Good.

No certainty.

Only pattern.

Then a scream came from below.

Human.

Adult.

Alive.

Vale raised his sword.

Lucien looked toward the collapsed floor.

Caldrin smiled grimly.

“Well.”

He stepped toward the opening.

“The sanctuary has a basement.”

⸻

They descended.

The lower level was not part of the original manor.

That much was obvious.

The stone changed.

So did the air.

Iron tables lined the walls.

Broken chains lay across the floor.

Glass vessels contained dried remains of substances no one recognized.

Notes had been burned.

Most.

Not all.

The royal witch picked up a surviving fragment.

“Blood ratios.”

Lucien looked toward her.

“Whose?”

“No names.”

Caldrin examined another.

“Physical responses to mixing.”

Vale frowned.

“Mixing what?”

“Blood.”

Aurelia’s expression went cold.

The scream came again.

Farther ahead.

They followed.

A man lay chained inside the final chamber.

Middle-aged.

Emaciated.

Alive.

Barely.

Lucien approached.

“Who are you?”

The prisoner lifted his head.

His eyes focused on Aurelia.

Then Zee.

Terror consumed him.

“No.”

Aurelia stopped.

The man pulled against his chains.

“No, no, no.”

Caldrin crouched.

“You know him?”

“No!”

“Then why are you afraid?”

The prisoner looked at Zee.

“Because they wanted him.”

Silence.

Aurelia stepped forward.

“Who?”

The man began crying.

“They said there would be one.”

Lucien’s expression hardened.

“Who said?”

“I do not know their names.”

“Describe them.”

“Witches.”

“Which coven?”

“They never gave one.”

“Symbols?”

The prisoner shook his head.

“They removed them.”

Aurelia stared.

“How long have you been here?”

“I do not know.”

Caldrin looked at the man’s condition.

“Months.”

The prisoner whispered, “They were preparing.”

“For what?”

His eyes went to Zee again.

“For something they had not found yet.”

The room chilled.

Aurelia’s voice lowered.

“My son?”

“I do not know.”

“Then stop looking at him as though you do.”

The man flinched.

“You asked.”

“And you answer only what you know.”

Lucien glanced toward her.

Good.

Fear would not be allowed to become false certainty again.

The prisoner closed his eyes.

“They took children with mixed blood.”

“Witch and wolf.”

“Some.”

“Vampire and witch?”

“Yes.”

“Wolf and vampire?”

“I think.”

Caldrin’s expression darkened.

“And what happened?”

“Most died.”

Silence.

The hatchling moved closer to Zee.

The prisoner looked toward him.

“They wanted combinations.”

The royal witch whispered, “Why?”

“I do not know.”

The man opened his eyes.

“But they kept saying the same thing.”

Aurelia waited.

“What?”

“That nature had limits.”

He looked toward Zee.

“And one day something would be born that did not.”

No one spoke.

Not prophecy.

Not knowledge of Zee.

Theory.

Obsession.

Someone had been searching for impossible blood before Zee existed.

That did not mean they predicted him.

It meant his birth had given monsters a new target.

Lucien turned toward Vale.

“We destroy this place.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“After we learn everything we can.”

He nodded.

The prisoner began laughing weakly.

Caldrin frowned.

“What?”

“You are too late.”

Lucien’s face changed.

“Why?”

The prisoner stared toward the corridor.

“They came back yesterday.”

Aurelia froze.

“For what?”

“To clear everything.”

Caldrin looked around.

“Then why leave you?”

The prisoner smiled through cracked lips.

“Because I am bait.”

A sound clicked behind them.

The royal witch shouted.

“DOWN!”

The entire underground chamber ignited.

Black magical fire raced across the walls.

Lucien threw up a shield.

The ceiling cracked.

Aurelia fell.

Zee slipped in her arms.

The hatchling launched himself toward him.

Through the Dragon Link came one overwhelming command.

Hold.

Zee reacted.

Crimson Mana exploded.

The collapsing ceiling stopped.

Not all of it.

Enough.

Stone hovered over Aurelia.

Lucien stared.

The royal witch screamed.

“MOVE!”

Everyone ran.

Zee’s mana strained.

The stones began falling again.

Caldrin grabbed Aurelia.

Vale carried the prisoner.

Lucien held the rear.

The hatchling stayed beside Zee.

They reached the stairs seconds before the laboratory collapsed.

The manor shook.

Flames erupted from the lower windows.

Everyone spilled into the courtyard.

Then the sanctuary began falling in on itself.

Lucien stared at it.

Twenty years of secrets vanished beneath fire and stone.

The prisoner coughed.

“They will know.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“Know what?”

“That you came.”

“How?”

He smiled weakly.

“They always know.”

Then he lost consciousness.

Aurelia looked at the burning manor.

The sanctuary was gone.

So was any chance of using it.

The world had hunted Zee with armies.

Then monsters.

Mercenaries.

Assassins.

Now someone had begun preparing places before he ever arrived.

Not because they understood him.

Because they understood fear, blood, and opportunity.

Aurelia held Zee tighter.

“We go back to Vesper.”

Lucien nodded.

Caldrin looked toward the smoke.

“No.”

Everyone turned.

He pointed toward the ridge.

A rider stood there.

Watching.

The figure wore black.

No crest.

The moment Caldrin moved, the rider turned and vanished into the forest.

Vale cursed.

Lucien’s face hardened.

“They found us.”

Caldrin sheathed his sword.

“They never lost us.”
