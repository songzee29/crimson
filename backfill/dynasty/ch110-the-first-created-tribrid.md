Chapter One Hundred Ten

The First Created Tribrid

Rhyven woke burning.

Not dying.

Burning.

Tessa found him standing barefoot in the Darkheart medical courtyard before sunrise with crimson fire crawling across both arms.

His claws were extended.

His breathing was heavy.

His heartbeat was stronger than the day before.

And the deep wound Pyrathis had carved through his left thigh was gone.

Not healed badly.

Gone.

Tessa stopped ten feet away.

“Are you in pain?”

Rhyven looked at the flame.

“Yes.”

“Bad?”

“Yes.”

“Do you feel like you are dying?”

Rhyven considered.

“No.”

Then his eyes sharpened.

“I feel like something inside me refuses to.”

Tessa did not like that answer.

Neither did the three healers behind her.

Rhyven suddenly turned toward the eastern wall.

Wolf senses caught something.

His head tilted.

“Someone is running.”

Tessa heard nothing.

Then the alarm bell rang.

⸻

Zee reached the courtyard from above.

Crimson Phoenix wings opened against the morning sky.

He descended hard.

Not gracefully.

He was still learning that landing with enormous burning wings required more space than walking through a door.

He folded them behind him.

Rhyven stared.

“You kept yours.”

Zee looked at the crimson feathers along Rhyven’s shoulders.

“You kept part of yours.”

Rhyven’s expression tightened.

“I do not have wings.”

“No.”

“Good.”

Zee raised an eyebrow.

Rhyven looked at his wings.

“They look inconvenient.”

“They are.”

That was all the humor the morning received.

A Darkheart scout entered the courtyard bleeding from the chest.

“Eastern convoy.”

Zee’s expression changed.

“What happened?”

“Attacked.”

“By whom?”

“Unknown.”

“How many citizens?”

“One hundred twenty-three from Pyrathis recovery camps. Thirty Darkheart escorts.”

Zee’s wings opened.

The scout swallowed.

“They knew what was in the convoy.”

Zee went still.

“What?”

“The rescued bloodlines.”

That was enough.

⸻

The attackers called themselves nothing.

At least not to Darkheart.

They wore white bone masks and armor covered in anti-hybrid scripture.

Not an empire.

Not a kingdom.

A militant temple force from beyond the southeastern mountains.

They had heard what survived Pyrathis.

Phoenix blood.

New hybrids.

Experimental captives.

Things the surface world had never classified.

So they came to cleanse them.

By the time Zee arrived, twelve Darkheart soldiers were dead.

Three wagons burned.

The surviving civilians had formed a circle around the children.

And Rhyven reached the battlefield seconds behind him.

Not by horse.

Running.

Fast enough that the ground cracked beneath his feet.

His Wolf nature had changed.

Phoenix vitality drove it.

His body did not tire correctly.

Heat rolled from his muscles.

His senses cut through smoke.

He smelled blood.

Found the attackers.

Then saw one masked soldier raise a spear over a Wolf child.

Rhyven changed.

Not into Phoenix.

Wolf.

His true Wolf form erupted through him.

Larger than before Pyrathis.

Dark fur.

Crimson feathering along the shoulders and spine.

Fire moved beneath the coat without consuming it.

His claws struck the ground—

and ignited.

He hit the soldier before the spear descended.

The man’s armor broke.

Rhyven’s jaws closed around his throat.

Crimson flame traveled through his teeth.

The wound burned from inside.

Rhyven released him.

Then hunted the next.

Wolf instinct.

Phoenix heat.

One race.

Not two powers taking turns.

⸻

A masked warrior drove a blessed iron blade through Rhyven’s side.

Deep.

The point exited beneath his ribs.

Rhyven howled.

The warrior twisted.

Then smiled.

“Die, aberration.”

Rhyven’s eyes burned.

The flesh around the blade ignited.

Not uncontrolled fire.

Regeneration.

Phoenix vitality flooded damaged Wolf tissue.

Muscle rebuilt around the wound while the sword was still inside him.

Rhyven caught the attacker’s wrist with one clawed hand.

Pulled himself farther down the blade.

The man’s smile vanished.

Rhyven bit his face.

Then tore the weapon out.

Blood poured for three seconds.

Crimson flame followed.

The wound closed enough for Rhyven to keep moving.

Not perfect.

Not immortal.

He could still die.

But injuries that would have crippled an ordinary Wolf became temporary problems if his Phoenix vitality had enough strength left to rebuild him.

Rhyven stared at the blood on his hand.

Then at Zee.

Zee saw it too.

Stable.

Pyrathis had failed through torture.

Crimson blood had succeeded through survival.

Rhyven remained the first genuinely new race of Zee’s expanded creation era.

But he was not the only person connected to Zee who would be tested that day.

⸻

The battlefield shifted.

A scream came from the eastern wagons.

Zee turned.

Captain Serayne Voss fell.

She was one of Darkheart’s oldest settlement Witches.

Not related to Elara.

No grand bloodline.

No secret destiny.

She had spent twelve years building defensive wards around refugee roads and teaching children how to recognize hostile spellwork.

Now a white spear protruded through her abdomen.

The weapon was wrong.

Zee smelled it immediately.

Three layers.

Soul-severing Witchcraft.

Dragonbane mineral.

And something taken from dead Phoenix ash.

Designed to prevent supernatural healing by forcing every form of recovery to destroy another part of the victim.

Serayne collapsed.

Tessa reached her.

Gold-white healing struck the wound.

Serayne screamed.

Her skin split along the ribs.

Tessa stopped.

“No.”

Zee landed beside them.

“What?”

“The wound punishes healing.”

Serayne coughed blood.

Tessa pressed both hands around the spear.

“If I repair the body, the magic tears her Witch core.”

Serayne gasped.

“And if you stabilize the Witch core?”

“The body dies.”

Zee listened to her heartbeat.

Failing.

Fast.

Then weaker.

An ordinary healing spell would not be enough.

Vampire regeneration would trigger the curse.

Wolf vitality would trigger it too.

Phoenix alone might rebuild flesh while the curse destroyed her Witch nature.

Dragon reinforcement might keep the body intact while the soul-severing spell killed her magic.

But Zee did not need to create another race to save her.

He needed precision.

He reached through the wound with Witch sight, found the curse’s central knot, and closed his hand around the spear.

“Hold her still.”

Tessa looked at him.

“What are you doing?”

“Removing the part that is killing her.”

The spear resisted.

Its magic had been designed to punish healing, but it had not been designed to resist Crimson Phoenix blood guided by a Sovereign Witch.

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“Sever.”

The curse split.

Serayne screamed once.

Then the spearhead came free.

Tessa immediately flooded the wound with gold-white healing.

This time, the magic did not recoil.

The torn flesh closed.

The damaged Witch core steadied.

Serayne’s heartbeat remained weak, but it no longer fell.

Zee caught her shoulder before she collapsed.

“Stay awake.”

Serayne opened one eye.

“Was that your medical advice?”

“Yes.”

“It is terrible.”

“It worked.”

Tessa continued healing.

Serayne survived through her own Witch nature, Tessa’s skill, and Zee’s spellwork.

No transformation.

No new bloodline.

No racial creation.

She remained Serayne Voss.

A Darkheart Witch.

That was enough.

⸻

A roar shook the battlefield.

Not Wolf.

Not Phoenix.

Dragon.

The sound came through the Dragon Link before Zee saw its source.

Zareth.

Pain struck Zee’s chest.

Not his own.

Zareth’s.

The Link had existed between them since birth, but it had never carried anything like this.

Zee staggered.

Rhyven turned toward him.

“What is it?”

“Zareth.”

The pain intensified.

Zee felt scales splitting.

A heartbeat stuttering beneath ancient Dragon blood.

Teal-and-Gold Mana flaring wildly.

Then something cold and metallic entered the Link.

Dragonbane.

Not the crude mineral in Serayne’s spear.

Something older.

A weapon designed specifically to poison a true Dragon’s internal fire.

Zareth’s voice reached him through the Link.

Zee.

The word was not spoken.

It was dragged through pain.

Zee looked toward the eastern ridge.

A black spear of light had struck the ground beyond the convoy.

Dragon magic exploded outward.

The anti-hybrid force had not come only for the rescued captives.

They had brought a weapon for the Crown Prince of Drakonis.

Zee’s wings opened.

“Stay with Serayne,” he told Tessa.

Then he launched.

⸻

Zareth stood in the center of a crater.

His true Dragon form towered over the burning road.

Teal-and-Gold scales were cracked across his chest.

His original Dragon wings remained spread, one torn near the membrane.

Black veins of Dragonbane energy crawled beneath his scales.

Every breath came out broken.

Around him, the masked warriors kept their distance.

They had not defeated him through strength.

They had poisoned the foundation of his nature.

Zareth’s Dragonfire sputtered between his teeth.

Zee landed in front of him.

The Dragon Link became unbearable.

He felt every fracture.

Every failed heartbeat.

Every instinct inside Zareth screaming to burn the poison out.

But the Dragonbane was feeding on that resistance.

Do not fight it alone, Zee sent through the Link.

I am a Dragon.

I know.

Then let me remain one.

Zee looked at him.

Even dying, Zareth’s first fear was not death.

It was losing himself.

“You will remain a Dragon,” Zee said aloud.

Zareth’s eye fixed on him.

“Then why do you sound like you are about to do something reckless?”

“Because I am.”

The masked commander raised his weapon.

“Let the Dragon die.”

Zee did not look away from Zareth.

“Not today.”

⸻

The commander ordered the next strike.

Zee moved first.

Crimson Phoenix wings exploded outward.

One shielded Zareth’s body from the incoming Dragonbane bolts.

The other struck the attackers hard enough to throw them across the ridge.

Zee landed beside Zareth’s head.

His Witch sight entered the Dragon’s body.

The poison had reached the core.

Zareth’s Dragon nature was trying to burn it away, but every attempt fed the weapon.

His body was too durable to die quickly.

That made the suffering worse.

Zee felt Zareth’s pain through the Link as if it were inside his own bones.

“Your Dragon nature cannot solve this alone.”

Zareth’s eye narrowed.

“I know.”

The admission nearly broke the Link.

Zareth had never needed to say it before.

Zee placed one burning hand against the cracked scales above his brother’s heart.

“There is one way.”

Zareth understood.

“No.”

“Listen.”

“No.”

“Phoenix vitality can rebuild the damaged core.”

“Your blood could kill me.”

“Yes.”

“Your Witch nature could overwrite mine.”

“I will not give you mine.”

Zareth’s breath shook the ground.

“Then what are you doing?”

“Creating one inside you.”

The Dragon Link surged.

Zee felt Zareth’s terror.

Not fear of pain.

Fear of becoming something unrecognizable.

Zee pressed harder against his scales.

“You are not a stranger. You are my brother. I would never attempt this on someone who could not choose it.”

Zareth’s Dragonfire flickered.

“What happens if I refuse?”

“I find another way.”

“There may not be another way.”

“I know.”

The poison climbed higher.

Zareth’s heartbeat faltered.

Zee felt the exact moment the Dragon core began to collapse.

Zareth looked at him.

“Do it.”

Zee’s voice lowered.

“Say it clearly.”

Zareth’s eye burned Teal and Gold.

“I consent.”

The Dragon Link opened completely.

Zee felt him.

Zareth felt him.

No distance remained.

⸻

Zee cut his palm.

Crimson Phoenix blood rose in a burning stream.

He pressed it against Zareth’s chest.

The blood entered through the scales.

Zareth screamed.

The sound became a shockwave.

The remaining masked warriors fell to their knees.

Zee nearly lost his grip.

The Dragonbane reacted immediately.

It attacked the Phoenix blood as an invading force.

Zareth’s Dragon nature joined it.

Not willingly.

Instinctively.

The ancient Dragon structure rejected everything foreign.

Zee felt Zareth’s body trying to burn his blood out.

“Stay with me!”

Zareth’s pain slammed through the Link.

It is inside my core!

I know!

It is tearing me apart!

I know!

Phoenix blood spread through the Dragon’s veins.

It did not replace the Dragon.

It fought for space beside it.

Zareth’s scales ignited.

Teal-and-Gold fire erupted across his body, but Crimson sparks moved through the flames.

His original Dragon wings remained spread behind him.

They did not become Phoenix wings.

They burned along their edges with feather-like patterns of fire, but the wings remained unmistakably Dragon.

Zee reached deeper.

Phoenix first.

Renewal.

Vitality.

The ability to rebuild what the Dragonbane had poisoned.

But Phoenix alone would not be enough.

The Dragon nature was too old, too proud, too complete.

It would reject any structure that threatened its identity.

Zee needed something that could give the new bloodline shape without forcing it to dominate.

Witch.

Not Zee’s Witch nature.

A new one.

Zee gathered Crimson Mana around his hand.

Then pushed it into the Dragon core.

Zareth convulsed.

His Teal-and-Gold Mana exploded outward.

For one terrible instant, it became nothing but raw Dragon power.

Then the color changed.

Not away from Teal and Gold.

Deeper into it.

The Mana began forming patterns.

Circles.

Lines.

Runes.

Structures.

Zareth’s Dragon magic had always been Teal and Gold.

Now it gained a true Witch expression.

The Mana no longer merely flowed from his Dragon nature.

It began to think.

To shape.

To answer.

Zareth’s Witch nature was forming.

The Dragon resisted violently.

His claws dug trenches into the stone.

His tail struck the ground hard enough to split the crater.

Zee felt the resistance through the Link.

I am losing myself.

No. You are changing.

That is not the same thing.

Then hold on to who you are.

Zee forced his own memory through the Link.

Zareth as a child.

Zareth standing beside him beneath the Drakonis moon.

Zareth taking the first blow meant for Zee.

Zareth laughing when Zee had not yet learned how.

Zareth calling him brother without needing blood to prove it.

The memories became anchors.

The forming Witch nature reached for them.

Not to erase Zareth.

To understand him.

The Teal-and-Gold Mana stabilized around the Dragon core.

Phoenix blood surged again.

The Dragonbane screamed through the Link.

Zareth’s heartbeat stopped.

Zee felt the silence.

His own heart seemed to stop with it.

“No.”

He poured more Phoenix blood into the core.

The Dragonbane consumed it.

Zee pushed harder.

Crimson fire entered the poison.

Phoenix renewal fought the weapon.

The new Witch nature formed a structure around both.

Dragon.

Phoenix.

Witch.

Three supernatural systems collided inside one body.

Zareth’s true Dragon form began to change.

His scales remained Teal and Gold, but thin lines of Crimson light appeared between them.

Phoenix fire moved beneath the scales like a second bloodstream.

His horns lengthened.

Teal-and-Gold sigils formed across his brow and throat.

His Dragonfire erupted from his jaws, then split into two colors—

deep Teal and molten Gold at the center, surrounded by a living Crimson edge.

His original Dragon wings burned brighter.

Feather-like flames traced their membranes without replacing them.

Zee felt the Dragon nature resist one final time.

Then Phoenix caught.

The new Witch nature stabilized.

The Dragon stopped fighting.

Not because it had been defeated.

Because it recognized the other two structures as part of the body now.

The Dragon Link became overwhelming.

Zee felt Zareth’s pain vanish into something larger.

Not silence.

Integration.

The instant all three natures synchronized, the Link struck Zee like thunder.

Dragon.

Phoenix.

Witch.

One heartbeat.

One body.

One stable race.

Zareth inhaled.

His true Dragon form rose from the crater.

The Dragonbane shattered beneath his scales.

Teal-and-Gold Mana spiraled around him in precise magical structures.

Phoenix fire burned through the cracks in his scales and sealed them.

His Dragon wings remained his own.

His eyes opened.

One held Teal-and-Gold Dragonfire.

The other carried a thin Crimson ring around the pupil.

Zee dropped to one knee.

The Link remained open.

Zareth’s voice reached him.

I am still here.

Zee breathed hard.

“Yes.”

I am still a Dragon.

“Yes.”

And something else.

Zee looked up at him.

“Two things else.”

Zareth’s Dragonfire rolled across his teeth.

“Then help me learn what they are.”

⸻

The masked commander tried to flee.

Zareth turned his head.

His new Witch sensing found the man through the smoke.

His Phoenix vitality surged through his Dragon body.

His true Dragon wings beat once.

The force threw the commander into the ground.

Zee stood.

The commander looked between them.

“What did you do?”

Zee answered.

“Saved my brother.”

Zareth’s Teal-and-Gold Mana formed a circle beneath the commander.

The spell was crude.

New.

But stable.

The commander tried to break it.

Zareth’s Witch instinct tightened the structure.

The circle held.

Zee looked at him.

“Good.”

Zareth’s voice came through the Link.

I do not know what I just cast.

“Then learn.”

The commander raised a weapon.

Zee killed him before he could use it.

Zareth watched.

Then lowered his head.

The battle resumed around them.

⸻

Zee returned to the convoy with Zareth walking beside him in human form.

The transformation had not removed his Dragon nature.

It had not made him humanoid-only.

His true Dragon form remained intact, larger and more dangerous than before.

But his human form now carried the same threefold structure.

Teal-and-Gold Mana moved beneath his skin.

Phoenix vitality repaired the last traces of Dragonbane.

His eyes still held the faint Crimson ring.

Rhyven approached first.

He looked at Zareth.

Then at Zee.

“What happened?”

Zareth answered.

“I became difficult to classify.”

Rhyven nodded.

“Good.”

Serayne sat against a wagon while Tessa finished binding her wound.

She looked at Zareth.

“Did he transform you too?”

Zareth glanced at Zee.

“No.”

Serayne looked relieved.

“Excellent. I am already having a terrible day.”

Zee crouched beside her.

“You survived.”

“Because you removed the spear.”

“Correct.”

“Then I am keeping my original race.”

“Correct.”

“Good.”

Tessa tied the bandage tighter.

Serayne winced.

“Your healer is cruel.”

“She is.”

“I heard that,” Tessa said.

Zareth looked toward the burning road.

The rescued children were alive.

The convoy had survived.

But the world had changed.

Rhyven remained the first Phoenix–Wolf hybrid created by Zee Darkheart.

Zareth Drakonis now stood as the first deliberately created Phoenix–Dragon–Witch Tribrid.

The first new-generation Tribrid.

Not born that way.

Not discovered.

Created through trust, consent, and survival.

The age of new blood had begun with the people who had already stood beside Zee.

⸻

Ner arrived after the fighting.

He had remained at Darkheart because for once he had looked at Zee and decided being several months pregnant during an unknown anti-hybrid ambush was unnecessary.

His choice.

Not Zee’s command.

He walked straight toward Zee.

Then stopped.

His eyes found Serayne.

Then Rhyven.

Then Zareth.

Then the bodies.

“What did you do?”

Zee looked at Zareth.

“Something new.”

Ner felt Zareth through Bloodsong.

Three structures.

One heartbeat.

His eyes widened.

“You made a Tribrid.”

“Zareth did not become one because I wanted to test something,” Zee said. “He was dying.”

Ner’s expression softened as he looked at Zareth.

Then slowly toward the life inside himself.

Zayrlen moved.

Gold rippled faintly beneath Ner’s skin.

Zee saw it.

Natural.

Created.

Two entirely different miracles.

Ner placed a hand over his stomach.

“Do not get ideas.”

Zee almost smiled.

“I already have enough.”

Good.

Because the world had not finished giving him reasons to use them.

⸻
