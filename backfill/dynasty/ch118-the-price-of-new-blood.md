Chapter One Hundred Eighteen

The Price of New Blood

The first corpse arrived from the north wrapped in silver cloth.

Zee knew the body before the cloth came off.

Not personally.

By bloodline.

Vampire.

Old.

Strong.

Dead in a way Vampires rarely died.

The heart remained.

The head remained attached.

No silver.

No fire.

No Witch curse.

The blood was simply gone.

Not drained.

Removed.

Every vessel inside the body had collapsed dry.

Kael stood beside the table.

“That is the third one.”

Zee looked toward him.

“Where were the others?”

“Northern border.”

“And this?”

“Twenty miles inside Darkheart.”

Ner entered carrying Zayrlen.

Zee looked immediately toward the child.

Ner sighed.

“I am not taking him into the autopsy room.”

“Good.”

“I came to tell you something.”

Zee stepped out into the corridor.

Ner held Zayrlen against his shoulder.

The newborn slept.

“What?”

“Elias felt it.”

Zee’s expression changed.

“Felt what?”

“Demon.”

⸻

Elias waited in the western courtyard.

His deep-blue Witch Mana had become easier around him now.

Black sigils no longer appeared accidentally every few minutes.

He controlled them.

Mostly.

Zee approached.

“You felt Demon?”

Elias nodded.

“Not ordinary Demon.”

“House Varkesh?”

“No.”

“Good.”

Elias looked toward the corpse being moved inside.

“Something older.”

Zee stared.

“That phrase is becoming irritating.”

“It is still accurate.”

Rhyven arrived from patrol.

He smelled the dead Vampire and immediately frowned.

“No blood.”

“Yes.”

“Phoenix?”

“No.”

“Demon?”

Elias nodded.

“Partly.”

Zee looked between them.

“Partly.”

Elias’s claws appeared.

Then retracted.

“My Demon side recognizes hunger.”

Rhyven’s expression hardened.

Zee’s Vampire senses did too, once he stopped treating the absence of blood as emptiness.

There was residue.

Something had not fed.

It had extracted.

Separated blood from supernatural identity itself.

A technique.

Not a natural predation.

Witchcraft had been involved.

And Demon.

Again.

The Age of New Blood was producing imitators faster than Zee could kill them.

⸻

The northern settlement disappeared the next night.

Not destroyed.

Gone.

Buildings remained.

Food remained on tables.

Animals remained in pens.

But every supernatural person inside the settlement vanished.

Sixty-three people.

Witches.

Vampires.

Wolves.

Two hybrid families.

No Humans.

Zee stood in the empty central road before dawn.

Ner had stayed behind with Zayrlen.

By choice.

This time Zee did not argue.

Kael touched an abandoned cup.

“Warm.”

“They were here recently.”

Amara crouched near the road.

“No tracks leaving.”

Elias closed his eyes.

Deep-blue Mana spread.

Black sigils formed inside the sensing spell.

Then he opened them.

“Below.”

Zee looked down.

The earth was solid.

Zareth arrived in true Dragon form overhead.

His Link opened.

Something moved beneath the settlement.

Zee looked toward the ground.

Alive?

No.

That answer was worse.

⸻

They opened the earth.

Not with excavation.

Zee simply broke it.

Dragon strength.

Witch precision.

Phoenix wings lifting rubble.

The tunnel beneath was enormous.

Artificial.

Recently made.

Walls lined with black glass.

Blood channels ran along the floor.

Every channel led north.

Kael stared.

“This was built underneath Darkheart.”

“Yes.”

“How long?”

Zee touched the glass.

“Months.”

Amara swore.

They had missed it.

That mattered.

Not because Zee believed he could know everything.

Because someone had built a predator’s road beneath his kingdom.

That would be corrected.

⸻

The tunnel ended beneath an abandoned fortress outside Darkheart’s recognized borders.

The people inside called themselves:

The Pale Court.

Not a kingdom.

A gathering of bloodline thieves.

Former alchemists.

Disgraced Witches.

Demon binders.

Vampire surgeons.

People who had watched Zee create new races and reached the obvious conclusion.

If bloodlines could be stabilized—

they could be harvested.

Not copied.

Harvested.

Taken from one body and forced into another.

The Pale Court did not want to become Zee.

They wanted to industrialize what they thought he had proven.

That distinction lasted until Zee saw the first chamber.

Sixty-three Darkheart citizens hung inside glass cylinders.

Alive.

Their blood circulated through black tubes.

Magic was being peeled away layer by layer.

Witch first.

Vampire second.

Wolf third.

Hybrids lasted longer.

The process was designed to separate each nature from the person carrying it.

Zee stopped.

The entire room felt his presence change.

Kael looked at him.

“Zee.”

“I am controlled.”

That was true.

The frightening part was what control chose.

Every person responsible was going to die.

⸻

The Court noticed them.

Alarm bells rang.

Glass doors sealed.

Elias stepped forward.

“Let me.”

Zee looked toward him.

“Why?”

Elias’s deep-blue Mana gathered.

Black sigils formed around his wrists.

“Because I know what it feels like when someone decides your body belongs to their experiment.”

Zee nodded.

“Go.”

Elias smiled without warmth.

Then raised both hands.

“Open.”

The first door shattered.

Not because his Witch magic overpowered it.

Because the Demon side in his magic found the binding material inside the black glass and turned it against itself.

Demon–Witch.

Not two powers.

One race.

One method.

Elias entered first.

Chosen family before novelty.

That mattered.

⸻

The Pale Court sent altered guards.

One carried Wolf muscle grafted onto a Vampire skeleton.

Another had Witch channels forced into Demon bone.

None stable.

None volunteers.

Zee smelled it.

Prisoners.

He did not kill them.

He broke control collars.

Severed command spells.

Rhyven restrained the ones who lost control.

Kael carried two injured experimental guards out himself.

Amara hunted the handlers instead.

Perfect.

The distinction remained.

People who had been broken were not enemies simply because someone pointed them toward Darkheart.

The people holding the chains were.

⸻

The first Pale Court architect tried surrendering.

Zee found him behind three blood barriers.

“I can undo it.”

Zee looked through the glass toward his people.

“You already should have.”

“I was following research orders.”

Zee’s expression did not change.

“That excuse is old.”

The man raised both hands.

“I surrender.”

“You built the tunnel.”

“Yes.”

“You selected Darkheart.”

Silence.

“You took sixty-three of mine.”

The man began crying.

Zee’s wings opened.

Crimson flame reflected against the glass.

“You die.”

One life ended.

Then another.

Then another.

The Court began fleeing.

The King’s Hunt found them.

Blood Mark remembered anyone Zee personally tasted.

Witchcraft cut escape wards.

Wolf instinct tracked those who hid among prisoners.

Phoenix flight made walls irrelevant.

Dragon strength made fortifications temporary.

Crimson Sovereign synchronization stayed flawless.

No struggle between natures.

No unfinished mastery.

Only execution.

⸻

At the center of the fortress, Zee found the reason three Vampires had arrived bloodless.

A machine.

Not mechanical in the ordinary sense.

A ritual engine.

Black glass.

Demon marrow.

Witch circles.

Vampire hearts suspended inside fluid.

It separated racial identity through blood.

Zee stared at it.

Elias came beside him.

“They were trying to create bloodlines without the people.”

“Yes.”

“Can it work?”

Zee looked at the dead Vampires embedded in the structure.

“No.”

Elias understood.

Not because the theory was impossible.

Because the Court misunderstood what Zee’s blood did.

Zee did not create by stripping people into ingredients.

His blood stabilized life around identity.

Person first.

Race second.

The Pale Court had reversed it.

Race first.

Person disposable.

That was why everything they made broke.

Zee raised one hand.

“Sever.”

Nothing happened.

He frowned.

Elias looked toward him.

“Ancient?”

“No.”

“Then?”

Zee studied the engine.

Too many interdependent structures.

Cutting one would trigger another.

Interesting.

The Court had actually learned.

Zee’s eyes sharpened.

Then Crimson Phoenix fire ignited across both hands.

“Fine.”

Elias stepped back.

Zee destroyed the entire room physically.

Sometimes sophistication was unnecessary.

⸻

The fortress burned by morning.

The Pale Court ended.

No extended mystery.

No secret master.

No surviving founder waiting for Chapter One Hundred Nineteen.

Done.

Darkheart recovered fifty-eight of the sixty-three people alive.

Five died before rescue.

Zee stood through all five names.

Remembered them.

Then returned home.

⸻

Ner was in the nursery.

Zayrlen slept against his chest.

Zee entered quietly.

Ner looked up.

“How bad?”

“Five.”

Ner closed his eyes.

Then opened them.

“The others?”

“Alive.”

“Good.”

Zee sat beside him.

Zayrlen stirred.

Then opened his crimson-gold eyes.

The moment he saw Zee, the baby reached.

Not far.

Barely coordinated.

But toward him.

Zee froze.

Ner smiled.

“He knows.”

Zee took him.

Zayrlen settled immediately.

That small trust felt heavier than any crown.

Ner watched Zee’s bloodstained hands holding their son.

“You washed.”

“Yes.”

“You always do now.”

Zee looked down.

“Yes.”

Ner leaned back.

“What happened?”

“People tried harvesting bloodlines.”

Ner’s expression hardened.

“Because of what you can create.”

“Yes.”

“Will it stop?”

“No.”

Zee did not lie.

The Age of New Blood had begun.

The world would keep trying.

Some would create.

Some would break.

Some would hunt.

Some would beg Zee to change them.

Some would attack Darkheart hoping to steal the secret.

Zee looked at Zayrlen.

His son yawned.

Tiny Crimson-and-Gold magic moved around him.

Dragon.

Witch.

Wolf.

Natural.

Untouched by laboratories.

Born from love.

Zee’s expression changed.

“Then we get better at killing the ones who come for us.”

Ner smiled faintly.

“That sounded like you.”

“Good.”

Zayrlen made a small sound.

Then fell asleep.

Outside, Darkheart rebuilt the northern settlement.

Inside, Zee held the Prince they had been allowed to keep in the light.

And somewhere beyond Darkheart’s walls, the supernatural world continued learning a dangerous lesson.

New blood could be created.

But anyone who came to harvest it from Zee Darkheart’s family—

usually became the next thing Darkheart buried.
