Chapter Fifty-Two

The Country Without Roads

The first problem with trying to warn people about a place that folded distance was deciding where the place actually was.

The wolf clans wanted coordinates.

Rez wanted boundaries.

Ner wanted repeated measurements.

Zareth wanted everyone to stop pretending the land cared about their maps.

Zee wanted to go back.

That last opinion was unpopular.

They stood inside a long timber hall three days after escaping the deep wild, surrounded by wolf trackers, two witch surveyors, a Vesper cartographer, and one clan elder who had spent twenty minutes insisting that every region could be mapped if enough people survived measuring it.

Ner finally placed both hands on the table.

“No.”

The elder stared.

“No?”

“No.”

“You have been fifteen for exactly long enough to become disrespectful.”

Ner smiled.

“I was disrespectful before fifteen.”

Rez coughed.

Zee looked innocent.

The elder glared at all three.

Ner pointed toward the map.

“You keep asking where the boundary moved.”

“Yes.”

“We do not know.”

“You crossed it.”

“Yes.”

“You came back.”

“Yes.”

“Then mark the route.”

“We tried.”

Ner slid three pieces of parchment forward.

Each held a different version of the same journey.

On the first, the path curved six miles northwest.

On the second, the same landmarks appeared nearly fourteen miles apart.

On the third, the border stone sat less than a hundred yards from where the dead six-legged creature had been found.

The elder looked between them.

“That is impossible.”

Zee smiled.

“You are going to hear that a lot.”

Rez gave him a look.

Zee shrugged.

The Vesper cartographer leaned closer.

“What if the terrain physically moved?”

Ner shook his head.

“The trees changed position without moving through the space between positions.”

The witch surveyor frowned.

“How can you know?”

Ner lifted one hand.

Pure Golden Mana formed a thin line across the map.

“I anchored spell markers.”

“Some returned behind us.”

“Some appeared above us.”

“One remained exactly where I placed it while the path around it stopped leading to the same location.”

The hall went quiet.

The elder looked toward Zee.

“And you could see these… folds?”

“After one stabbed me.”

Several wolves looked toward his abdomen.

Zee frowned.

“It healed.”

“That was not the concern.”

“It usually is.”

Rez leaned against the table.

“What matters is this: do not call the region uninhabited.”

The elder’s expression hardened.

“We lost people there.”

“Yes.”

“To something dangerous.”

“Yes.”

“And you are telling me not to seal it?”

Rez shook his head.

“I am telling you that sealing and invading are different decisions.”

One wolf captain folded his arms.

“What would you call it?”

Rez looked toward Zee.

Zee thought about the eye beneath the ground.

The false Aurelia.

The eyeless animals.

The hunter confessing what his party had done first.

Then the path opening only after the stolen remains were returned.

“Inhabited.”

The elder frowned.

“By what?”

Zee looked toward Zareth.

The Dragon Prince had remained outside because the hall was too small, but his head rested beside an open wall section built for larger visitors.

The Link carried the same old Drakonis concept.

Deep wild.

Not a species.

Not a kingdom.

Not a name.

A warning category.

“Something that was there before us.”

“That tells me nothing.”

“Then write that.”

The elder stared.

Zee continued.

“Unknown inhabitants.”

“Unknown laws.”

“Do not kill anything unless it attacks first.”

“Do not cut pieces off things because you are curious.”

The surviving hunter from the expedition stood near the rear wall.

His face tightened.

Good.

He had chosen to testify publicly.

That mattered.

Zee looked toward him.

The man stepped forward.

“We caused what happened.”

A murmur moved through the wolves.

The hunter swallowed.

“We killed one of the creatures.”

“Unprovoked.”

“We opened it.”

“Took tissue.”

“And after that… the land changed.”

One clan warrior looked disgusted.

“You expect us to believe the forest judged you?”

The hunter shook his head.

“No.”

“I expect you to believe we did something stupid inside a place we did not understand.”

That answer was better.

The elder sat back.

“What do you recommend?”

Rez answered.

“Observation posts outside the last stable landmarks.”

“No patrols crossing the line without cause.”

“Nobody alone.”

“No hunters entering for trophies.”

Ner added, “No permanent magical anchors.”

The surveyors looked toward him.

“Why?”

“Because we do not know whether the region reads anchored magic as intrusion.”

Zee glanced at him.

“That is a guess.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Ner smiled.

The elder looked toward Zareth.

“And Drakonis?”

Zee opened the Link.

Question.

The answer came slowly.

Dragon history.

Old regions.

Avoidance.

Watching from high altitude.

No nesting nearby.

No claim.

Zee translated carefully.

“Drakonis will not claim it.”

The wolf elder raised an eyebrow.

“Zareth has that authority?”

The Dragon Prince’s Teal-and-Gold Mana flared outside the hall.

The elder looked toward the massive crimson head filling half the wall opening.

“Fair.”

Rez smiled.

Then the hall doors opened.

A young wolf messenger entered breathing hard.

“Elder.”

Everyone turned.

The messenger bowed.

“Something came out.”

Silence.

Zee straightened.

“From the deep wild?”

The messenger looked toward him.

“Yes.”

“What?”

“We do not know.”

Zee smiled.

Rez immediately said, “Stop smiling.”

“I am not.”

“You absolutely are.”

The elder stood.

“Where?”

“North watch.”

“Hostile?”

The messenger hesitated.

“No.”

That changed everything.

“What is it doing?” Ner asked.

The messenger looked deeply confused.

“Waiting.”

⸻

The thing waiting beyond the north watch had antlers.

That was where resemblance to any known animal ended.

It stood in a field of dead winter grass beneath a gray afternoon sky, nearly nine feet at the shoulder, with six narrow legs and a body covered in pale charcoal hide that seemed smooth from a distance and patterned with fine moving lines up close.

Its head was long.

Eyeless.

But unlike the smaller creatures from the deep wild, this one possessed a split crown of black antlers that curved backward and then inward as though framing empty space above its skull.

No mouth.

No visible nostrils.

No heartbeat Zee could detect.

No blood scent.

No obvious Mana.

Wolf warriors ringed the field at a distance.

None had attacked.

Good.

The creature stood perfectly still.

Zareth landed behind the group.

The animal reacted immediately.

Its antlers changed.

Not shape.

Position.

For a heartbeat Zee saw the crown in three places at once.

Then one again.

Zareth’s Link tightened.

Recognition?

No.

Caution.

Ner moved closer.

Rez caught his sleeve.

“Not yet.”

“I am not touching it.”

“That sentence has history.”

Ner glared.

Zee listened.

Nothing.

Then the strange Crimson perception in his blood stirred.

Faint lines appeared around the animal.

Not around its body.

Through it.

Several folds crossing the same space.

Zee inhaled.

“It is not fully here.”

Rez looked over.

“Meaning?”

“I do not know.”

Ner smiled faintly.

Zee frowned.

“Do not.”

Ner lifted one hand.

Pure Golden Mana formed a tiny sphere.

Not aimed at the creature.

He released it several feet to the left.

The sphere moved normally.

Then vanished when it crossed one of Zee’s perceived lines.

Ner’s expression sharpened.

“Again.”

He formed another.

This one he sent lower.

It passed.

Another higher.

Gone.

The creature finally moved.

One step.

Every wolf raised a weapon.

Rez raised one hand.

“Hold.”

The creature’s six legs folded oddly, each joint bending in different directions, yet the movement remained smooth.

It approached Zee.

Only Zee.

He did not move.

Aurelia was not here to tell him not to.

That made the choice more important.

Ner whispered, “Zee.”

“I know.”

Zareth’s Link filled with readiness.

Rez moved half a step closer.

The animal stopped six feet away.

Its antlers shifted again.

The air between them folded.

Zee saw a landscape.

Not around them.

Inside the space between the antlers.

Black grass.

Silver rain falling upward.

A distant mountain hanging upside down in a violet sky.

Then something moved across that landscape.

Hundreds of pale creatures.

Migrating.

Zee’s eyes widened.

Ner stepped closer despite Rez.

The image sharpened.

Not illusion.

A view.

Somewhere else.

The creature lowered its head.

One antler touched the ground.

The field beneath it changed.

Only a circle several feet across.

Winter grass became black.

For one heartbeat.

Then normal again.

Zee felt pressure against his bones.

Same impossible almost-language as before.

Not words.

Need.

Movement.

Blocked.

Danger.

Rez looked at Zee.

“You are getting something.”

“Not language.”

“What?”

Zee focused.

Images arrived.

A migration path.

Animals moving through deep wild folds.

Then a rupture.

Fire.

Not Dragonfire.

Different.

White-green.

A section of folded terrain collapsing.

Creatures scattering.

One route closed.

Then—

A thing Zee recognized.

Not species.

Structure.

A tower.

Human-made.

Built at the edge of unfamiliar land.

Ner saw only Zee’s face.

“What?”

“Someone built something.”

“Where?”

“I think inside.”

Rez frowned.

“Inside the region?”

Zee nodded slowly.

The antlered creature raised its head.

The view vanished.

Then it turned north.

Took three steps.

Stopped.

Looked back without eyes.

Zee stared.

Rez sighed.

“No.”

Zee smiled.

“It wants us to follow.”

“We do not know that.”

Ner looked toward the creature.

“Yes, we do.”

Rez turned.

“How?”

Ner pointed.

The animal had taken another three steps.

Stopped again.

Zareth’s Link supplied dry amusement.

Rez closed his eyes.

“Fine.”

The wolf elder, who had arrived with them, looked horrified.

“You are not going back in there.”

Zee turned.

“Probably.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It is the true one.”

The elder stared toward the creature.

“What if this is bait?”

Rez answered.

“Then we find out before sending anyone else.”

The elder looked toward him.

“You are all fifteen.”

Rez nodded.

“Yes.”

Nothing else.

The elder seemed to realize age was not going to win the argument.

Ner stepped toward the antlered creature.

It did not retreat.

“Whatever happened inside affected the migration.”

Zee nodded.

“And whatever caused it looks like something built.”

Rez’s expression darkened.

That was important.

The deep wild had not come looking for kingdoms.

Someone from the known world may have gone farther in.

Zareth lifted his wings.

Question through the Link.

Go?

Zee looked toward the others.

Rez sighed.

Ner smiled.

Of course.

Zee turned toward the wolf elder.

“Keep everyone outside.”

“You cannot order me.”

“No.”

Zee looked toward the strange creature.

“But if we are right, the thing inside may be dangerous because somebody already ignored the warning.”

The elder’s face tightened.

Then he nodded.

“Six hours.”

Rez frowned.

“What?”

“If you are not back in six hours, we do not send anyone after you.”

Zee smiled.

“Good.”

The elder blinked.

“You agree?”

“Yes.”

Rez looked toward him.

Zee continued.

“If this place folds people wrong, adding more bodies is stupid.”

Ner nodded.

“There is hope for you.”

Zee shoved him lightly.

The antlered creature started walking.

This time they followed.

The line between mapped kingdoms and everything beyond them opened without a door.

One step was winter field.

The next was black grass beneath silver rain.

No transition.

No warning.

No border stone.

Zee looked back.

The wolves were gone.

The sky had changed.

And the creature had already moved fifty yards ahead.

Rez drew his sword.

Ner’s Gold gathered around his hands.

Zareth spread enormous wings.

Zee’s Crimson lines returned around the world.

This time, they had not entered the deep wild because they were lost.

Something inside had asked for help.

Or something close enough to asking that Zee Darkheart was willing to find out.

And if someone from the kingdoms had brought fire into a place older than their maps—

then the monsters might not be the things with antlers.
