Chapter Seventy-Four

The Salt Cathedral

The western salt flats had no roads.

Only white.

Miles of cracked earth reflecting sunlight hard enough to hurt ordinary eyes.

Zee walked through it without squinting.

Ner beside him.

Rez had rejoined them at the border after Vesper’s aqueduct threat was contained.

Zareth arrived in human form.

That was still new enough that Rez stared.

Zareth noticed.

“If you keep looking at me like that, I am shifting back.”

Rez blinked.

“You talk more than I expected.”

Zareth’s expression went flat.

“I have always had opinions.”

Zee smiled.

“Unfortunately.”

Zareth looked toward him.

Through the Link:

You missed this.

Zee answered privately.

A little.

Warmth came back beneath the words.

Then smoke.

Drakonis.

Damage still under repair.

Responsibility waiting.

Zareth had come because Father Caldus threatened mixed bloodlines across kingdoms, including Veyrath and potentially Drakonis alliances.

Not because Zee summoned him.

Good.

The salt cathedral appeared near noon.

No towers.

No walls.

It had been carved downward.

A massive open pit cut into the flats, white stone descending in terraces toward a dark sanctuary beneath the earth.

Rez looked over the edge.

“I hate stairs.”

Ner glanced down.

“There are hundreds.”

“I hate them more now.”

Zareth smiled.

“You have legs.”

Rez stared.

“You had wings for most of your life.”

“Still do.”

“Exactly.”

Zee started down.

“Argue later.”

⸻

Nobody attacked them.

That was suspicious.

The first chamber contained no guards.

The second held candles.

The third held names.

Hundreds.

Painted across salt walls.

Zee stopped.

Wolf.

Witch.

Vampire.

Dragon-Witch.

Mixed blood.

Unknown blood.

Bloodsong.

Tribrid.

Ner went very still.

His name was there.

NEREUS AZRION.

Zareth found another.

AURELIA VEYRATH.

Dead.

Still listed.

Then:

THERON VEYRATH.

SERAPHINE VEYRATH.

Rez found his own.

Not because mixed.

Because proximity.

REZ VESPER — ENABLER.

He frowned.

“I am offended by how boring mine is.”

Ner looked toward Zee’s section.

His name occupied an entire column.

ZEE DARKHEART.

Below it:

PRIMARY CONVERGENCE THREAT.

ADAPTIVE BLOOD.

UNSTABLE SPECIES BOUNDARY.

DIVINE-KILLING CAPACITY CONFIRMED.

CRIMSON DRAGON MUTATION.

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“Very dramatic.”

Zareth folded his arms.

“You are one to talk.”

Zee looked at him.

“You have become irritating in human form.”

“I had to compensate for twenty-one years of you translating me badly.”

Rez laughed.

Ner actually stopped reading to look between them.

Zee pointed.

“I never translated you badly.”

Through the Link, Zareth sent Zee an old childhood memory.

Zee telling someone Zareth had said hello.

What Zareth had actually felt was suspicion, hunger, and a desire to bite the roof.

Zee frowned.

“That was close enough.”

“Liar.”

Good.

The humor lasted four seconds.

Then Ner touched the wall.

Pure Golden Mana spread.

His expression changed.

“These names are recent.”

“How recent?” Rez asked.

“Some days.”

Zee looked down the corridor.

“Caldus is here.”

“No,” Ner said.

Silence.

He studied the structure again.

“These walls are receiving information.”

Rez’s hand moved to his sword.

“From where?”

“I don’t know.”

Zee listened.

No heartbeat beneath them.

No blood.

No obvious living presence.

Zareth’s Dragon senses sharpened.

Then he spoke aloud.

“There is something lower.”

Zee looked toward him.

“What?”

Zareth’s eyes narrowed.

“Not someone.”

Through the Link came more.

Heat without fire.

A pressure beneath the salt.

Something old, but not divine.

Not friendly.

Not awake.

Zee felt the sensory layer beneath Zareth’s words.

Deep vibration.

Mineral.

Bone?

No.

Something structural.

Ner stepped away from the wall.

“The cathedral is built on something.”

Rez sighed.

“Of course it is.”

⸻

They found Father Caldus in the lowest chamber.

Alone.

Old.

Human.

No supernatural blood.

No armor.

No guards.

He sat at a stone table surrounded by paper.

He looked up when they entered.

“King Darkheart.”

Zee stopped.

“Father Caldus.”

Caldus looked toward Ner.

Then Rez.

Then Zareth.

His gaze lingered.

“Crown Prince Drakonis. Human form suits diplomacy.”

Zareth’s voice went colder.

“You cut Dragon bloodlines into categories.”

Caldus smiled.

“I classify risk.”

“You classify people you intend to erase.”

“Yes.”

No denial.

Rez looked disgusted.

“That saves time.”

Caldus folded his hands.

“I wondered which of you would say it first.”

Zee walked closer.

“Where are the kill orders?”

“Already distributed.”

Ner’s Gold flared.

Rez moved.

Zareth’s eyes sharpened.

Zee did not.

Calm.

“Targets?”

Caldus smiled.

“All over.”

Zee heard the heartbeat.

Steady.

No bluff.

Bad.

“How many?”

“Forty-three bloodlines.”

“Active strikes?”

“Seven.”

Rez swore.

Zareth’s voice dropped into something closer to Dragon thunder even in human shape.

“Drakonis?”

“One.”

“Ner?”

“One.”

“Veyrath?”

“Two.”

Zee’s pupils became slits.

“And Darkheart?”

Caldus smiled.

“Not the House.”

“You.”

Silence.

Zee looked at him.

“Where?”

“You are already here.”

That was the trap.

Ner realized first.

“Zee.”

The salt beneath them lit.

Not Witchcraft.

Something else.

Caldus stood.

“I could never overpower you.”

“No.”

“So I built a place that does not need to.”

The floor split.

Not downward.

Inward.

White salt opened into a black mineral chamber far beneath the cathedral.

Zareth’s voice sharpened.

“Move!”

Too late.

The entire sanctuary dropped.

⸻

They fell into darkness.

Zareth shifted midair.

Human body exploded outward into true Dragon form.

Wings opened.

Massive.

He caught Rez with one foreclaw.

Ner with the other.

Zee did not need catching.

He hit the sloped wall, claws digging in, slid twenty feet, then pushed off.

Dragon durability absorbed the rest.

He landed hard.

Caldus landed on a suspended platform above.

Protected by mechanisms.

Zee looked up.

“You planned that.”

“Yes.”

“I respect preparation.”

Caldus stared.

“That is not the response I wanted.”

Zee smiled.

Then the chamber woke.

Something moved beneath the salt.

Not a creature.

A network.

Black crystal roots spread through the cavern.

Every supernatural nature reacted differently.

Ner’s Gold flickered.

Rez’s Blue-White Mana weakened.

Zareth’s Dragonfire dimmed.

Zee felt all four natures strain.

Not suppression.

Separation.

The chamber was trying to make each supernatural force exist alone.

Zee’s Witch nature pulled away from Vampire.

Wolf from Dragon.

Not physically.

Structurally.

Caldus looked down.

“This is what the world should have remained.”

Zee’s face went cold.

“Separate.”

“Yes.”

Ner gritted his teeth.

“You built a weapon out of a natural formation.”

Caldus smiled.

“Finally.”

Rez looked around.

“Can it be broken?”

Ner studied.

“Not quickly.”

Zareth spoke through the Link while also growling aloud.

Three anchors. North wall. Under platform. Beneath us.

Zee received more than words.

Heat.

Positions.

A weak pulse under the salt.

He answered instantly.

Take the north. Rez gets the platform. Ner finds the lower anchor. I take Caldus.

Zareth’s mental reply came with irritation.

You always take the person.

He talks too much.

A wave of Dragon amusement broke through the strain.

Then they moved.

The adult Link made them terrifying.

No shouted explanation.

No delay.

Zareth blasted the north wall.

Rez used the opening to climb toward the platform.

Ner drove Gold into the floor in hair-thin lines.

Zee ran straight up the salt column.

Caldus saw him.

Panic finally entered his heartbeat.

Good.

Zee reached the platform.

Caldus triggered another mechanism.

Iron spikes fired.

Zee barely turned his wrist.

Silent magic.

The spikes shifted around him.

One embedded beside Caldus’s head.

Zee continued walking.

Caldus backed up.

“You are destabilizing everything.”

“Yes.”

“You could collapse the chamber.”

“Yes.”

“And kill us all.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“You keep forgetting.”

Caldus’s face changed.

Right.

Zee could not die.

Caldus could.

“Stop.”

“No.”

Zee crossed the last distance.

Caldus raised a poison blade.

Nanda Parbat read the shoulder.

Zee caught his wrist before the blade cleared cloth.

Twist.

Break.

Caldus screamed.

Zee’s smile vanished.

“Where are the seven strikes?”

Caldus spat blood.

“Too late.”

Vampire compulsion hit.

“Where?”

Caldus resisted.

Ordinary Human.

Extraordinary discipline.

Zee pushed harder.

“Tell me.”

The man shook violently.

Then laughed.

“Even if I tell you, you cannot reach them all.”

Zee looked toward Zareth.

The Link opened.

Not words first.

Drakonis.

Distance.

Air routes.

Then speech.

I can reach one.

Rez shouted from below.

“Vesper can reach another if we send now!”

Ner looked up.

“Veyrath survivors are scattered. We can warn both.”

Zee returned to Caldus.

“You were saying?”

Fear.

Good.

“Locations.”

Compulsion broke him.

Seven places.

Fast.

Specific.

Rez memorized.

Ner sent Golden signal marks through prepared communication charms.

Zareth’s Link carried one thought before he shifted toward flight.

I am taking Drakonis.

Zee answered.

Go.

No ownership.

No request to stay.

Zareth launched upward through the broken chamber.

Caldus watched.

His plan collapsing faster than he could process.

Zee leaned close.

“You chose bloodlines before they acted.”

Caldus glared.

“They would have.”

“Some.”

“Yes.”

“Not all.”

“Eventually.”

There it was.

Possibility as guilt.

Victor again.

Zee was tired of it.

He killed Caldus quickly.

One hand.

Neck.

No feeding.

He did not want the blood.

Caldus dropped.

The last principal name was finished.

Then the floor cracked.

Ner shouted.

“Zee!”

The chamber began collapsing.

Permanent progress had arrived.

Now survival.

Good.

Zee jumped.

⸻

They escaped through the upper shaft as the salt cathedral imploded behind them.

Rez emerged coughing.

Ner covered in white dust.

Zee barely singed.

Zareth already gone toward Drakonis.

Through the Link:

Still alive.

Zee answered.

Disappointing.

Zareth’s laughter came through with mountain wind and urgency.

Then he was gone.

Rez looked toward the horizon.

“Seven attacks.”

Ner nodded.

“Now six.”

Zee looked at him.

“How?”

Ner smiled faintly.

“Bloodsong warning reached one Veyrath enclave before the strike landed.”

Good.

Rez drew breath.

“Vesper will handle ours.”

“Drakonis handles theirs.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“And the others?”

Ner looked toward him.

“Darkheart has people.”

Zee understood.

Not everything required him personally.

King.

Network.

Influence.

He turned east.

“Then we move.”

The Covenant’s Five were finished.

But Caldus had already thrown seven knives into the world.

Now the question was not whether Zee could stop them all himself.

It was whether the world had finally learned something worse.

King Zee Darkheart was no longer one predator.

He had people who moved when he did.

And they were getting faster.
