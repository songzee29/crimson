Chapter Thirty-Three

The Blood Broker

Damaris Khell did not live inside a kingdom.

That was the first useful thing the Ashen Talon prisoner gave them.

The second was worse.

He moved.

Constantly.

Khell operated between vampire territories, human trade cities, outlaw ports, and neutral markets where kings pretended not to know what was sold after midnight.

Blood.

Relics.

Cursed weapons.

Supernatural remains.

Living captives.

Rare bloodlines.

House Veyrath had become merchandise.

Aurelia stopped sleeping.

Zee noticed on the second night.

On the third, he found her in the map room before sunrise.

She stood alone over a table covered in reports.

Zee entered quietly.

“You are doing the thing.”

Aurelia did not look up.

“What thing?”

“The thing you tell me not to do.”

She glanced toward him.

“Which one?”

“Not sleeping.”

Aurelia almost smiled.

“That is an unfortunately long list.”

Zee climbed onto the chair opposite her.

His horns were cool this morning.

Good.

His scales had remained at their normal permanent patches overnight.

Better.

“What did you find?”

Aurelia looked toward the maps.

“Three possible Khell routes.”

“Possible.”

“Yes.”

“Not known.”

“No.”

Zee nodded.

He liked hearing uncertainty named correctly.

“Are we going?”

“No.”

He stared.

Aurelia continued before he argued.

“Not yet.”

“That is different.”

“Yes.”

“What do we need?”

“Confirmation.”

Zee looked across the reports.

“From Sevran?”

“Among others.”

Sevran had spent two days answering questions and refusing others.

Aurelia tolerated it because every useful answer had been accurate so far.

House Veyrath remained scattered.

Some survivors had hidden.

Some might have been taken.

Some had changed names so completely even Sevran could not locate them.

Damaris Khell had purchased Veyrath captives at least twice.

Years ago.

Whether any remained alive was unknown.

Zee touched the edge of the map.

“If they are alive…”

Aurelia closed her eyes briefly.

He understood.

Hope was dangerous.

Not because it was bad.

Because it could make a person see certainty where there was none.

“We find proof first,” she said.

Zee nodded.

Then:

“I am coming.”

Aurelia stared.

“No.”

He sighed.

“We had one good moment.”

“You are ten.”

“So is Rez.”

Aurelia’s mouth opened.

Stopped.

Zee smiled.

“That does not work anymore.”

Aurelia looked deeply offended by reality.

Rez entered the room.

“It works on me.”

Zee turned.

Rez wore a new sword at his side.

Smaller.

Properly weighted for his current body.

He hated it.

Everyone knew.

He also trained with it six hours the previous day.

Aurelia pointed toward him.

“Thank you.”

Rez frowned.

“For what?”

“Proving age still matters.”

Rez walked to the table.

“I have seventeen years of memory.”

Zee looked at Aurelia.

“See?”

“That is not helping.”

Ner entered behind Rez carrying two books.

“You started without us.”

Aurelia looked toward the doorway.

“Why is everyone awake?”

Zareth pushed his head through the window.

Aurelia closed her eyes.

“Never mind.”

The Dragon Link delivered one cheerful concept.

Food.

Zee looked toward him.

“You ate.”

Again.

Rez smiled.

“Finally, someone with reasonable priorities.”

Aurelia folded the maps.

“You are all leaving.”

Ner placed one book on top.

“No.”

Aurelia stared at him.

Ner opened it.

A copied trader ledger filled the page.

One symbol had been circled in Gold.

A broken fang inside a square.

Aurelia’s expression changed.

“Where did you get this?”

“Vesper archives.”

“You searched the archives?”

“Lucien gave me permission.”

Rez looked betrayed.

“My father gives you permission to do things?”

“He likes me.”

“He likes me.”

Ner looked at him.

“Historically.”

Zee choked on a laugh.

Rez reached for him.

Ner moved behind Aurelia.

Aurelia ignored them and looked at the ledger.

“What is the symbol?”

Sevran answered from the door.

“Khell.”

Everyone turned.

He entered carrying another parchment.

“Or one of his broker marks.”

Aurelia looked toward Ner.

“How did you identify it?”

“I did not.”

Ner pointed toward several pages.

“I found the same symbol beside unusual cargo entries.”

“No descriptions.”

“Only weights.”

“Dates.”

“Routes.”

Sevran stepped closer.

“And one port.”

Aurelia’s eyes narrowed.

“Where?”

“Three days south.”

Rez looked toward the map.

“The River Market.”

Lucien entered behind Sevran.

“No one is going to the River Market.”

Zee looked at him.

“Did everyone plan this?”

“No.”

Lucien approached the table.

“The River Market sits outside direct Vesper authority.”

Rez folded his arms.

“That has never stopped you.”

“It should stop you.”

“I am Crown Prince.”

“You currently look ten.”

Rez stared.

Lucien immediately regretted it.

Silence fell.

Not humorous.

Rez’s expression closed.

Lucien softened.

“That came out wrong.”

“Yes.”

“I meant that anyone seeing you will recognize a vulnerability before they recognize your authority.”

Rez looked down at his hands.

He hated that.

Not being ten.

Being underestimated because of it.

Zee noticed.

“So we use it.”

Everyone looked at him.

Aurelia frowned.

“Use what?”

“They expect Rez to be seventeen.”

Rez slowly looked up.

Zee smiled.

“No one outside Vesper knows.”

Ner understood first.

“They will not recognize him.”

Rez’s expression changed.

Lucien hated where this was going.

Zee continued.

“A seventeen-year-old Crown Prince cannot walk through a criminal market.”

Rez smiled.

“A ten-year-old nobody can.”

“You are not nobody,” Lucien said.

“Exactly.”

Rez’s smile widened.

“That is why it works.”

Aurelia stared at Zee.

“You are planning infiltration.”

“Yes.”

“You are ten.”

“Yes.”

“You see nothing wrong with this?”

“Many things.”

Caldrin appeared in the doorway.

“That means he is learning.”

Aurelia looked toward the ceiling as though searching for patience there.

Caldrin continued.

“The plan has merit.”

Lucien glared.

“Of course you think that.”

“Children are routinely ignored by people who should know better.”

Zee looked toward Rez.

Rez shrugged.

“He is right.”

Ner closed the ledger.

“I am going too.”

“No,” nearly every adult said.

Ner blinked.

“Why?”

Aurelia pointed toward his hands.

“Pure Golden Mana is memorable.”

Ner lifted an eyebrow.

“So I do not use it.”

“You were hunted because of it.”

“I know.”

“That should make you more cautious.”

“It does.”

He looked at the ledger.

“Which is why I found Khell’s mark while everyone else was arguing.”

Rez whispered to Zee.

“He has gotten worse.”

Zee smiled.

“I know.”

Zareth shoved his head farther through the window.

Lucien looked at him.

“No.”

The Dragon Prince withdrew.

For three seconds.

Then he walked around the building and entered through the door.

Teal-and-Gold Mana shimmered.

Zee felt the Link.

Go.

Aurelia looked at all four.

Something in her expression changed.

Ten years ago, Zee had been carried between kingdoms.

Three years ago, adults still tried leaving all danger outside his room.

Now she had four children in front of her arguing strategy.

Except one carried seventeen years of experience.

One was a Dragon Crown Prince.

One possessed rare Golden Mana.

And one was Zee.

Perhaps childhood had never had a chance to look ordinary.

Lucien exhaled.

“Reconnaissance only.”

Aurelia turned sharply.

“Lucien.”

“Not all four.”

He looked toward Caldrin.

“You go.”

Caldrin nodded.

“Rez goes under disguise.”

Rez smiled.

“Ner stays.”

Ner’s face hardened.

“Why?”

“Because you are the one whose Mana appears in the ledger.”

Ner stopped.

That mattered.

Lucien continued.

“Zee stays.”

Zee opened his mouth.

Aurelia answered.

“Yes.”

Zee glared.

Then Zareth nudged him.

The Dragon Link carried:

Sneak later?

Zee almost smiled.

Aurelia pointed at him.

“No.”

He stared.

“I did not say anything.”

“I know your face.”

⸻

Rez hated the disguise.

That meant Caldrin considered it excellent.

His Vesper hair ornaments were gone.

The prince’s clothing had been replaced by rough brown trousers, worn boots, and a faded gray tunic.

His new sword remained hidden beneath a traveler’s cloak.

Caldrin looked equally unremarkable.

That was somehow more suspicious.

They entered the River Market near dusk.

No banners marked its borders.

Only wooden bridges crossing a wide black river and hundreds of stalls built beneath canvas roofs.

Normal goods occupied the outer market.

Fish.

Spices.

Cloth.

Iron.

Oil.

The deeper they walked, the quieter the merchandise became.

Blood bottles.

Cursed rings.

Wolf teeth.

Vampire ash.

Dragon-scale fragments of questionable origin.

Rez’s jaw tightened at that.

Caldrin noticed.

“Do not react.”

“I am not.”

“You are thinking loudly.”

Rez hated that phrase now.

They passed a stall selling silver restraints.

Another displayed magical suppressors.

Rez remembered waking inside a ten-year-old body.

For the first time, he understood something Zee had lived with from birth.

People looked at children and assumed powerlessness.

A merchant stepped directly into Rez’s path.

“Lost?”

Rez looked up.

“Yes.”

Caldrin nearly smiled.

The merchant crouched.

“Where are your parents?”

Rez pointed toward a random drunk man vomiting beside the river.

“He is busy.”

The merchant grimaced.

“Go find him.”

Rez walked on.

Caldrin caught up.

“That was cruel.”

“He will survive.”

“Probably.”

Rez’s seventeen-year-old mind fit differently inside the market now.

People ignored him.

Spoke around him.

Revealed things they would never say near Crown Prince Rez Vesper.

A pair of traders discussed a blood shipment arriving after moonrise.

Another complained that Khell’s buyers had increased prices.

Rez heard the name.

He did not look.

Caldrin did not either.

They continued.

A woman at a covered stall whispered:

“Khell is not coming.”

A man answered:

“He never comes anymore.”

“His man?”

“Blue dock.”

“When?”

“Second bell.”

Rez kept walking.

Caldrin bought an apple.

Neither spoke until three streets later.

Then Caldrin handed Rez the fruit.

“Blue dock.”

“I heard.”

“Second bell.”

“I heard.”

“You did well.”

Rez looked at him.

“You sound surprised.”

“I am enjoying the disguise.”

Rez bit the apple.

“I hate you.”

“Good.”

Then someone grabbed his shoulder.

Rez reacted automatically.

Too automatically.

He twisted.

Caught the wrist.

Dropped his weight.

Used a Vesper royal guard technique he had learned at fourteen.

The adult man hit the ground.

Hard.

The entire street went quiet.

Rez froze.

Caldrin closed his eyes.

The man stared up at the apparently ten-year-old child who had just thrown him.

“What the hell are you?”

Rez smiled nervously.

“Lucky?”

Caldrin grabbed him.

They ran.

⸻

Back in Vesper, Zee woke from sleep before the second bell.

He had finally managed to rest.

Then something pulled him awake.

Not Rez.

Not thought.

Not the strange broken boundary from his loss of control.

Nothing like that.

Zareth.

The Dragon Link.

The prince stood on the balcony staring south.

Zee sat up.

“What?”

An image came.

Rez.

River.

Danger.

Zee frowned.

“How would you know?”

Zareth showed something else.

Not knowledge.

Instinct.

Restlessness.

The Link had been filled with Rez all day because Zee had worried about him.

Zareth was reflecting Zee back at himself.

Zee groaned.

“That is not useful.”

Zareth huffed.

Then a knock came.

Ner entered without waiting.

Zee stared.

“Why knock?”

“My mother says it is polite.”

“You did not wait.”

“I completed the polite part.”

Ner closed the door.

His face was serious.

“I found something.”

Zee got out of bed.

“What?”

Ner held the copied ledger.

One page glowed faintly beneath Golden Mana.

“I tried tracing the ink.”

Zee frowned.

“You were told not to use magic on it.”

“I know.”

“That sounded like Rez.”

“Do you want the information?”

“Yes.”

Ner pointed.

Several numbers had appeared beneath the original writing.

Not hidden ink.

Impressions.

Older marks beneath newer entries.

Names.

Only fragments.

One stood clear.

VEY—

Zee’s expression changed.

“Veyrath.”

“Maybe.”

“Maybe?”

“Do not become Aurelia.”

Zee glared.

Ner continued.

“There are numbers beside it.”

“What kind?”

“I think people.”

Zee looked at him.

Ner swallowed.

“Seven.”

Silence.

Seven possible Veyrath captives.

Or seven blood samples.

Or seven shipments.

Or nothing.

They did not know.

Zee grabbed his coat.

Ner stared.

“No.”

Zee looked at him.

“You came to tell me.”

“Yes.”

“Then what did you think would happen?”

“That you would make a terrible decision and I would stop you.”

Zee smiled.

Ner groaned.

Zareth stood.

“No,” Ner said.

The Dragon Prince spread his wings.

“Nobody listens to me.”

Zee grabbed his boots.

Ner crossed his arms.

“We are telling Aurelia.”

Zee stopped.

Everything in him wanted to run south.

Immediately.

Find Rez.

Find Khell.

Find whoever had Veyrath blood.

But the last uncontrolled surge remained too close.

Rez’s younger face.

Aurelia saying we learn from it.

Zee looked toward the door.

Then sighed.

“Fine.”

Ner blinked.

“Really?”

“Yes.”

“I had three arguments prepared.”

“Save them.”

They found Aurelia before they reached the stairs.

She had already dressed.

Sword at her side.

Zee frowned.

“You knew?”

“Lucien received a message from Caldrin.”

“What message?”

Aurelia looked toward the southern window.

“They found Khell’s contact.”

Zee’s heart accelerated.

“Rez?”

“Safe.”

Ner showed her the ledger.

“That may change what we do next.”

Aurelia read the hidden impression.

Her face went completely still.

Seven.

Possible Veyrath.

For the first time since Sevran arrived, hope became specific enough to hurt.

Aurelia closed the ledger.

“We leave at dawn.”

Zee stared.

“We?”

“Yes.”

His eyes widened.

“You are letting me go?”

“I am bringing you.”

“That is basically the same thing.”

“No.”

Ner looked toward her.

“And me?”

Aurelia stared at him.

Ner lifted the ledger.

“I found it.”

She closed her eyes.

Zareth entered behind them.

Aurelia looked at all three.

Then toward the south where Rez was already moving through the River Market in a body no criminal there would recognize.

The answer changed the direction of the story.

“All of you.”

Zee smiled.

Ner smiled.

Zareth roared once.

Aurelia pointed at him.

“Quietly.”

He lowered his head.

By sunrise, the hunt would move south.

Not because someone had found Zee.

Not because another kingdom had raised a bounty.

Not because Victor had sent for him.

For once, Zee Darkheart was choosing the road.

Somewhere beyond Vesper, Damaris Khell possessed information about House Veyrath.

Perhaps captives.

Perhaps blood.

Perhaps only another lie sold for profit.

They would find out.

And the four who had spent their childhood being hunted were about to learn what happened when they became the ones doing the searching.
