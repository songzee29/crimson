Chapter Seventy-Six

The People Zee Could Not Reach

Winter reached Darkheart three months after the Covenant fell.

Zee turned twenty-two beneath a sky full of snow.

He did not celebrate much.

Darkheart did.

That was different.

The old Witch kingdom had changed around him.

Vampires now lived beyond the eastern market.

Wolf families had taken homes near the northern forest.

Witches still filled the oldest districts, but they no longer stood alone.

Mixed-blood families crossed the gates without hiding what they were.

Refugees arrived every week.

Some had heard stories about the Crimson Tribrid.

Others knew only one thing.

Darkheart did not punish blood.

Darkheart punished what people chose to do with it.

That reputation kept growing.

So did the kingdom.

And so did the attacks.

⸻

The first horn sounded before sunrise.

Western border.

Raiders.

Zee was already moving when the second horn came.

South.

Poison attack against a refugee caravan.

Then a third.

North.

A Darkheart village under siege.

Three attacks.

At once.

Zee stopped in the war room.

For one second.

Only one.

Merek looked at him.

“Majesty?”

Zee stared at the map.

He could reach the western border fastest.

Ner was already near the southern road inspecting new Bloodsong wards.

The north had Captain Elara Voss and forty Darkheart defenders.

Not enough.

Rez was in Vesper.

Zareth in Drakonis.

Different kingdoms.

Different responsibilities.

Zee hated the answer before he gave it.

“Send reinforcements north.”

Merek nodded.

“South?”

“Ner has it.”

“And you?”

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“West.”

Then he was gone.

⸻

The western raiders were Vampires.

Thirty-two.

Old enough to believe numbers mattered.

They entered through frozen woods and slaughtered the first patrol before anyone sounded the alarm.

Zee found them by blood.

Not tracks.

Not sound.

Blood.

His Vampire nature opened the forest around him.

Three wounded Darkheart soldiers.

One dead.

Thirty-two foreign bloodlines.

Two carrying silver.

Four recently fed.

One poisoned.

Zee shifted into Wolf form.

Near-black fur disappeared through snow.

He hunted without magic.

One raider vanished.

Then another.

Then a third.

By the time they understood the King was inside the forest, seven were already down.

A Vampire shouted:

“Form on me!”

Zee circled behind him.

Shifted human.

One hand lifted.

“Still.”

Four froze.

The others resisted.

Zee moved before resistance became action.

Vampire speed.

Assassin precision.

A throat.

A knee.

A wrist.

One Wolf-like strike with clawed fingers beneath a rib.

Then blood.

He fed from the strongest Vampire.

Deliberately.

The enemy’s blood carried an anti-compulsion tincture.

Zee tasted it.

His Crimson biology separated it before it spread.

Not immunity.

Recognition.

Another adaptation becoming familiar.

The Vampire weakened.

Zee released him alive.

“Who sent you?”

The man spat blood.

“No one.”

Lie.

Zee smiled.

“Again.”

Compulsion hit through voice and blood dominance.

The Vampire’s eyes widened despite trying not to look at him.

“Who?”

“Lord Arven.”

Different lord.

Different motive.

Border land.

Good.

No grand conspiracy.

Zee broke his neck.

Then finished the battle.

Twenty-seven minutes.

Too long.

Because when Zee returned to the war room—

the northern messenger was already there.

Blood on his cloak.

Zee stopped.

No one spoke.

He knew.

“How many?”

Merek swallowed.

“Eleven.”

Zee’s face emptied.

“Defenders?”

“Seven.”

“Civilians?”

“Four.”

Snow melted beneath Zee’s boots.

Heat.

Crimson Demonic Dragon heat.

Controlled.

Barely.

“Captain Voss?”

“Alive.”

That helped.

Not enough.

Zee turned toward the northern map.

He had killed thirty-two enemies.

Protected the western border.

Saved dozens.

And eleven people died somewhere else because he could not stand in two places at once.

The truth was becoming impossible to ignore.

Zee could survive the world.

His people could not always do the same.

⸻

He reached the northern village after sunset.

The battle was over.

Darkheart had won.

That word felt useless beside bodies.

Captain Elara Voss sat against a broken wall while a healer wrapped her ribs.

Thirty-three.

Witch.

Born Darkheart.

Had served under Victor.

Chose to stay under Zee.

She tried standing when he approached.

“Don’t.”

She stopped.

“Majesty.”

Zee looked around.

“Tell me.”

Elara did.

The attackers were Wolves hired by a northern claimant who wanted Darkheart forest territory.

No connection to the western Vampires.

No connection to the southern poisoners.

They struck because Zee was elsewhere.

Darkheart held.

Barely.

Elara killed four herself.

Lost seven soldiers.

Then something in her voice changed.

“Kael is dying.”

Zee turned.

“Who?”

“One of mine.”

Elara forced herself up despite his order.

“This way.”

⸻

Kael Marrin was twenty-four.

A Witch.

Not famous.

Not noble.

Not important to history.

Not yet.

He had been born in the northern village.

His mother made winter medicines.

His younger sister worked in the mill.

He joined Darkheart’s guard at eighteen because raiders had taken his father years earlier.

He was sarcastic when nervous.

Terrible at cards.

Apparently owed half the barracks money.

And he had spent the morning holding a cellar door while six children hid behind it.

A Wolf had ripped through his side.

Another had bitten deep into his shoulder.

The healer had stopped most of the bleeding.

Could not stop the poison.

Not normal venom.

Something made to kill Witches by turning their own Mana against damaged organs.

Kael’s mother knelt beside him.

His sister held his hand.

Zee stood in the doorway.

He smelled death approaching.

Slowly.

Certainly.

The healer looked at Zee.

“We cannot pull the poison without tearing his magic apart.”

Zee stepped closer.

Kael opened one eye.

“Majesty.”

His voice was barely there.

Zee crouched.

“You saved six children.”

Kael smiled weakly.

“Seven.”

Elara looked over.

“Seven?”

“Boy under the grain table.”

Even dying, correcting the count.

Zee almost smiled.

Then Kael coughed blood.

His mother broke.

Zee looked at the blood.

Witch.

Only Witch.

Strong.

Young.

Dying.

And suddenly—

Ner.

Fifteen.

Blood everywhere.

Heart stopping.

Zee biting open his own wrist because he had nothing else.

Take mine. Live.

Back then, Zee knew nothing.

Now he knew one thing.

His blood could change survival.

He still did not know how.

That made the choice worse.

Kael’s mother looked at him.

“Can you help him?”

Zee did not lie.

“I don’t know.”

She gripped Kael’s hand harder.

“Will it hurt him?”

“Yes.”

Kael laughed once.

“Bit late for that.”

Zee looked directly at him.

“This has to be your choice.”

Kael’s eyes focused.

Good.

Person first.

Always.

“If I do this, you may change.”

“Into what?”

“I don’t know.”

“Will I live?”

“I don’t know.”

Kael looked toward his mother.

Then his sister.

Then Zee.

“Do it.”

No hesitation.

His mother whispered:

“Kael…”

He squeezed her hand.

“I don’t want to die.”

That was enough.

Zee opened his wrist.

Crimson-and-gold eyes narrowed.

His blood welled dark red.

Hot.

Alive.

He held it to Kael’s mouth.

“Drink.”

Kael did.

One swallow.

Then another.

His Witch Mana convulsed.

The healer stumbled backward.

Kael screamed.

Pure Witch energy erupted across the room.

Zee grabbed his shoulders.

“Stay with me.”

Kael’s eyes snapped open.

Then his heart stopped.

His mother screamed.

Zee froze.

No.

Not again.

One second.

Two.

Three.

Then—

heartbeat.

Hard.

Different.

Kael gasped.

His back arched.

The Wolf poison inside him began burning out.

Not disappearing.

Losing.

His Witch Mana stayed.

Zee felt it clearly.

Still Witch.

But something else entered beside it.

Predatory.

Cold.

Hungry.

Kael’s eyes opened.

Dark red flashed beneath them.

Fangs descended.

His mother stopped crying.

For a different reason.

Kael stared at Zee.

“What did you do to me?”

Zee looked at him.

Alive.

Changed.

No answer simple enough.

“I saved you.”

Kael breathed.

His Witch Mana flared again.

Then his new Vampire senses hit.

Every heartbeat in the room.

Every blood scent.

His face twisted.

“Hunger.”

Zee understood immediately.

He looked at the healer.

“Animal blood first.”

Kael grabbed Zee’s wrist before he could rise.

Strong.

Stronger than before.

“Majesty.”

Zee looked down.

Kael’s fear was real.

Not of Zee.

Of himself.

“What am I?”

Zee did not give him a race name.

Not yet.

He squeezed Kael’s hand once.

“Kael Marrin.”

The young guard stared.

Zee continued.

“We figure out the rest after you stop dying.”

Kael laughed.

Weak.

Still himself.

Good.

That mattered more than history.

⸻

By morning, Kael was walking.

Barely.

His Witchcraft remained.

His Vampire nature was undeniable.

Fangs.

Healing.

Strength.

Blood hunger.

Enhanced senses.

No collapse between the two.

No Witch nature burned out.

No Vampire corruption erasing magic.

Two supernatural natures living together.

Naturally.

Because of Zee’s blood.

Ner arrived from the southern road before noon.

He had stopped the poison attack there without Zee.

Twenty-three people saved.

Two attackers captured.

One killed by Ner personally.

He entered Kael’s room and stopped.

Gold flashed in his eyes.

Then dark-red.

Bloodsong hummed beneath his breathing.

He looked at Zee.

“You did it again.”

Zee stared at Kael.

“No.”

Ner frowned.

Zee continued.

“This time I chose to.”

Silence.

That changed everything.

Ner walked to Kael.

“May I?”

Kael looked suspicious.

“Do what?”

“Listen.”

“That is somehow worse.”

Ner smiled.

“I like him.”

Kael sighed.

“Everyone says that until cards.”

Ner placed two fingers near Kael’s wrist.

Bloodsong.

Pure Gold.

His expression sharpened.

“Witch.”

“Yes.”

“Vampire.”

Zee’s face remained still.

Ner looked toward him.

“Both stable.”

There it was.

Proof.

Not theory.

Not Ner’s complicated Siren inheritance.

Something cleaner.

Something reproducible?

Maybe.

Too early.

Zee looked at Kael’s mother sleeping in the chair beside him.

His sister curled beneath a blanket.

Kael alive between them.

That was why this existed.

Not armies.

Not experiments.

Not history.

One person refusing to die.

Zee stood.

Outside, eleven graves were being prepared.

He could not save them.

But Kael would not join them.

Something inside Zee settled.

A new belief.

Cold.

Simple.

If the world would not stop trying to kill his people—

then he would make his people harder to kill.

⸻
