Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Seven

The Only Parent Left

Victor hated what Zee had done to Darkheart.

Then he hated that he did not entirely hate it.

The first thing he noticed was noise.

People spoke freely.

Too freely.

Witches argued with guards.

Vampires laughed near the fountain.

Two Wolves were openly criticizing a road decision while a Darkheart official wrote down complaints instead of threatening them.

A hybrid child ran past Victor without bowing.

Victor turned.

The child did not even notice him.

He looked toward Zee.

Zee noticed.

“What?”

“Your people have no discipline.”

“They are not yours.”

Victor’s jaw tightened.

Zee kept walking.

That answer explained more than Victor wanted.

⸻

They crossed the main courtyard.

Kael stood with Amara near a supply table.

Tessa was arguing with a soldier about healing protocol.

Rhyven sat on a wall sharpening a blade.

Elias passed with three different ledgers.

No one bowed when Zee approached.

Not immediately.

Some nodded.

One guard called:

“King.”

Zee responded:

“What?”

The guard lifted a broken gate hinge.

“This is your fault.”

Zee looked at it.

“How?”

“You broke it last week.”

“I broke several things last week.”

Victor stared.

The guard stared back at Zee.

“Exactly.”

Zee sighed.

“Send it to the smiths.”

“Already did.”

“Then why are you telling me?”

“So you know.”

Zee looked toward Victor.

“See?”

Victor did not.

Not yet.

The guard walked away.

Alive.

Unpunished.

Victor watched him go.

“Your subjects speak to you like that.”

“My people.”

Victor looked at him.

Zee did not correct himself.

That was the point.

⸻

Later, Victor watched Rez argue with Zee over trade routes.

Actually argue.

No lowered voice.

No careful phrasing.

No fear.

“You are wrong,” Rez said.

Zee looked at him.

“About?”

“The western route.”

“No.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

Rez unfolded a map.

“Look.”

“I have.”

“Then look better.”

Victor waited for the room to become dangerous.

It did not.

Zee leaned over the map.

Zareth entered halfway through and immediately disagreed with both of them.

Elias joined and somehow made the argument worse.

Ner passed through carrying Zayrlen and said:

“All of you sound stupid.”

No one died.

Victor found that deeply unsettling.

Zee looked toward him.

“You are staring.”

“I am observing.”

“You did enough of that recently.”

Victor looked away.

Fair.

⸻

The first real conversation happened that evening.

No council.

No guards.

No battle.

Just Zee and Victor on a balcony overlooking the lower city.

Victor stood at one end.

Zee at the other.

Distance chosen deliberately.

The silence lasted long enough to become hostile.

Victor spoke first.

“You still tap your thumb when you are angry.”

Zee looked down at his hand.

He was.

He stopped immediately.

Victor almost smiled.

Zee noticed.

“Do not.”

“You did it as a child.”

“I was a child.”

“You would do it whenever Aurelia told you no.”

Zee’s face changed.

Barely.

Victor continued before he could stop himself.

“You would sit beneath the eastern staircase afterward and refuse to speak to anyone.”

Zee looked toward him.

“How old?”

“Four.”

The answer came too quickly to be invented.

Zee’s expression tightened.

Nobody alive remembered that.

Not like this.

Aurelia would have.

Aurelia was gone.

That thought arrived without invitation.

Zee hated it.

Victor saw something shift and made the mistake of stepping closer.

Zee’s eyes snapped toward him.

“Stay there.”

Victor stopped.

“I was only—”

“I know.”

Silence.

Victor remained where he was.

Good.

⸻

Zee looked back over the city.

“You do not get to come back and act like my father.”

Victor’s jaw tightened.

“I am your father.”

“Biologically.”

“Yes.”

“Historically.”

Victor looked at him.

Zee continued.

“Those are not the same as being one.”

That landed harder.

Victor did not argue.

Unexpected.

Zee glanced over.

“You agree?”

“I know what I did.”

“That is not the same as understanding it.”

“No.”

Victor looked toward Darkheart.

“I am beginning to.”

Zee laughed once.

“Convenient timing.”

Victor’s old pride surfaced.

“I died.”

“You murdered me first.”

“I know.”

“You tried to control everything you feared.”

“I know.”

“You made my mother spend years protecting me from you.”

Victor’s expression hardened.

Not anger at Zee.

Pain.

“Aurelia chose you.”

“She was supposed to.”

“She chose you over me.”

Zee looked at him.

“She chose the child you tried to kill.”

Silence.

Victor had no answer.

Good.

⸻

Then he said something smaller.

“You hated milk.”

Zee blinked.

“What?”

“As a child.”

Victor looked almost irritated by the memory.

“You would drink anything else. Water. fruit juice. broth. But if Aurelia handed you milk, you would stare at the cup like it had insulted your bloodline.”

Zee stared.

“That is not true.”

“It is.”

“I do not remember that.”

“I do.”

That was the problem.

Victor remembered.

Before King.

Before gods.

Before hybrids.

Before Dragon awakening.

Before Crimson Sovereign.

Before the First Natural Abysscin.

Before Zee had become anything the world knew.

Victor remembered a four-year-old hiding under stairs because Aurelia said no.

Zee looked away.

He hated how much that mattered.

⸻

Ner found them eventually.

He did not interrupt immediately.

He looked at Victor.

Then Zee.

Then said:

“Zayrlen is awake.”

Zee turned instantly.

That ended the conversation.

Victor followed.

Not close.

He had learned at least one thing.

⸻

The nursery became dangerous the second Victor entered the doorway.

Ner stood beside the cradle.

Zee moved in front of him automatically.

Not dramatic.

Habit.

Victor stopped several feet inside.

Zayrlen lay awake.

Two months old.

Crimson-Gold eyes open.

Tiny hands moving.

No Phoenix power.

No impossible manifestation.

Just baby.

Victor looked at him.

Really looked.

No test.

No spell.

No plan.

The child’s Dragon-Witch-Wolf nature sat quietly beneath the surface.

Impossible blood.

Yet Zee had never feared him.

Victor understood the difference again.

Deeper now.

Zayrlen turned his head.

Saw Victor.

Stared.

Victor stared back.

Something about familiar blood perhaps.

Nothing more.

Zee watched every breath.

Victor took one step.

“No.”

Victor stopped.

“I moved one foot.”

Zee pointed.

“Move it back.”

Victor looked toward Ner.

Ner said:

“You heard him.”

Victor moved back.

Zayrlen blinked.

Victor stared at the baby again.

A minute later he adjusted his stance slightly.

Zee looked over.

“Victor.”

“I am standing.”

“Stand farther away.”

Rez, leaning against the wall near the doorway, turned his face to hide a smile.

Victor noticed.

“I see why you like him.”

Rez answered:

“I have known him longer than you have lately.”

That one hurt.

Victor said nothing.

⸻

Zayrlen made a small sound.

Victor’s attention snapped back.

Not fear.

Interest.

Then another sound.

Tiny.

Almost a coo.

Victor’s expression changed.

He stepped forward without thinking.

Zee’s voice sharpened.

“No.”

Victor stopped immediately this time.

No argument.

That mattered.

He looked at Zee.

Then Ner.

Then the child.

The distance between him and Zayrlen suddenly felt much larger than the room.

He had earned every inch.

Victor understood that.

Zee trusted Rez near the baby.

Zareth.

Elias.

Kael.

Tessa.

Amara.

Rhyven.

All of them.

Victor—

his own father—

stood at the edge of the room like an armed stranger.

Because he was.

Blood did not erase history.

Victor looked down.

For once, no defense came.

⸻

Zee lifted Zayrlen.

The baby settled immediately.

Victor watched how automatic it was.

Support head.

Check breathing.

Adjust against chest.

Listen.

Zee did not even know he was doing half of it.

Zayrlen grabbed his shirt.

Victor said quietly:

“He trusts you.”

Zee looked over.

“He should.”

Victor nodded.

“Yes.”

Simple.

Painful.

True.

⸻

Later, when Zayrlen slept, Victor found himself still in Darkheart.

That surprised him.

It surprised Zee more.

Zee passed him in the hall.

Stopped.

“Why are you still here?”

Victor looked at him.

“You said I could remain.”

“I said killing you was currently useless.”

“Which resulted in me remaining.”

Zee stared.

Victor waited.

Zee finally said:

“You are exhausting.”

Victor almost smiled.

“Also hereditary.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“Do not start.”

Victor did smile this time.

Zee walked away.

No murder attempt.

Progress.

Tiny.

Probably temporary.

Still real.

⸻

That night, Victor stood alone in a guest chamber he had not been offered so much as assigned under supervision.

He looked out over Darkheart.

The kingdom was louder than his.

Messier.

Freer.

More dangerous in some ways.

More loyal in others.

People loved Zee.

Not because he had trained them to fear displeasing him.

Because they chose him.

Victor understood that reluctantly.

Zee had become a different King.

A different father.

A different creature entirely.

The First Natural Abysscin.

And somehow still the same impossible child who once hid under a staircase after Aurelia told him no.

That contradiction sat painfully inside Victor.

⸻

Across the keep, Zee sat with Zayrlen asleep against his chest.

Ner beside him.

No Victor.

Good.

Zee looked toward the window.

Somewhere in the same building, his father was alive.

Again.

Aurelia was not.

That truth arrived quietly.

He did not say it.

Did not want it.

Could not remove it.

Victor was the only parent left who remembered Zee before everything.

That did not forgive murder.

Did not restore trust.

Did not make him safe.

It simply existed.

Zee looked down at Zayrlen.

Tiny hand curled against his chest.

Then he muttered:

“This family is getting ridiculous.”

Ner smiled.

“You made most of it.”

“Not him.”

“Unfortunately.”

Zee looked toward the hallway where Victor had disappeared.

For one second, irritation almost became something else.

He killed the thought before it formed.

Not tonight.

The new reality was already difficult enough.

Victor Darkheart was alive.

Still unwelcome.

Still dangerous.

Still unforgiven.

Still his father.

And for the first time in decades—

Zee had one parent left.
