Chapter Sixty-Eight

The Villages That Burned First

The western villages were already burning when Zee arrived.

Three.

Not one line of fire.

Three separate strikes.

Different roads.

Different timing.

Same result.

People running.

Livestock screaming.

Darkheart soldiers dragging families from houses while witches fought flames that did not behave like ordinary fire.

Zee stopped at the edge of the first village.

Smelled everything.

Wood.

Oil.

Blood.

Spell residue.

Wolf.

Human.

And something metallic beneath it.

Rez arrived beside him.

“Raiders?”

“No.”

Ner looked toward the flames.

“Magic?”

“Yes.”

Zareth circled above.

The Dragon Link pushed a sharp impression.

Fire wrong.

Not Dragon.

Not demon.

Zee’s crimson-and-gold eyes narrowed.

“Someone made the fire spread after impact.”

Rez looked toward the second village in the distance.

“Meaning they did not need many attackers.”

“Exactly.”

A scream cut through the road.

Zee vanished.

Vampire speed.

He appeared beneath a collapsing roof and caught the burning beam before it crushed a child.

Dragon durability took the heat.

His scales darkened.

He threw the timber aside.

“Run.”

The child stared at his horns.

Then moved.

Good.

Another building cracked.

Zee barely lifted one hand.

“Hold.”

Crimson Witchcraft caught the wall long enough for three people to escape.

Ner rushed past him.

Pure Gold spread across the road in precise lanes.

“Everyone inside the light! Stay between the lines!”

His voice carried more than volume.

Bloodsong resonance.

Panic softened.

Not erased.

Directed.

People moved where he told them.

Rez took over the evacuation without hesitation.

“North road closed!”

“South bridge clear!”

“Wounded first!”

“Anyone who can walk carries someone who cannot!”

He did not need a throne to sound like a king.

Zareth landed beyond the village and dragged an overturned wagon away from a trapped family with one claw.

No one waited for Zee.

Good.

That meant Zee could hunt.

⸻

The first attacker was still nearby.

Zee knew because fire was loud.

Fear was louder.

He shifted.

Bones changed.

Body dropped.

Dark near-black fur replaced skin.

Four legs hit the earth.

Wolf Zee.

No horns.

No scales.

No Dragon distortion.

Just Wolf.

Complete.

He ran.

Through smoke.

Across burned fields.

One scent separated from fleeing villagers.

Ash.

Leather.

Fresh fear.

Zee followed.

The trail crossed water.

He did not slow.

The attacker had tried to break scent by moving upstream.

Smart.

Zee left the river twenty yards early and circled.

Wolf instinct.

Human habits.

People always checked behind themselves after crossing.

The attacker did.

Zee was waiting ahead.

The man stopped.

Saw crimson-and-gold eyes in the dark.

His face drained.

“No.”

Zee advanced.

The man reached for a flare.

Zee lunged.

One paw struck the wrist.

Bones snapped.

The flare fell.

The man screamed.

Zee shifted back before him.

Human-shaped again.

Blood-dark coat.

Mature horns.

Slit pupils.

The attacker stared.

“You are him.”

Zee crouched.

“Who sent you?”

The man clenched his jaw.

Zee smelled poison inside one tooth.

Nanda Parbat.

Suicide capsule.

Zee caught his face before he could bite.

Two fingers.

Pressure.

The jaw locked.

The man panicked.

Zee smiled.

“Not yet.”

Vampire compulsion pushed through voice.

“Open.”

Resistance.

Strong training.

Zee pressed harder.

“Open.”

The man’s mouth obeyed.

Zee removed the poison capsule.

Dropped it.

Crushed it beneath one boot.

“Now answer.”

The attacker shook.

“Red Covenant.”

Rez arrived through the smoke.

“What is that?”

The man laughed weakly.

“Not yours.”

Zee looked at Rez.

“Useful?”

“Maybe.”

Ner reached them next.

Gold still burning around his hands.

“We got everyone out of the first village.”

Good.

Zareth’s Link carried the same from the second.

No deaths there.

One at the third.

Zee’s face changed.

One was enough.

He looked back at the prisoner.

“Why the villages?”

The man swallowed.

“To draw you west.”

Rez frowned.

“From where?”

No answer.

Ner’s eyes sharpened.

“Darkheart.”

Zee stood.

Too late.

A messenger horn sounded from the east.

Then another.

Then silence.

His castle.

Zee did not swear.

Did not shout.

He simply disappeared.

⸻

The attack on Castle Darkheart lasted four minutes.

That was all it needed.

The western fires were distraction.

The real target had entered through the old lower aqueduct beneath Victor’s abandoned prison wing.

Six intruders.

Not soldiers.

Assassins.

They moved directly toward the royal archive.

Not Zee.

Not Ner.

Not Rez.

Not Zareth.

Records.

Blood records.

House genealogy.

Veyrath lineage documents.

Zee arrived as the last assassin reached the archive door.

The man heard nothing.

Zee stood behind him.

“Wrong room.”

The assassin spun.

Blade.

Fast.

Zee moved less.

One wrist turn.

Knife trapped.

Elbow broken.

Knee destroyed.

The man hit stone.

Zee looked toward the five others.

All trained.

All already committed.

Good.

No speeches.

The first attacked high.

Zee ducked.

Second came low.

Wolf instinct read the movement.

He stepped outside the angle.

Third threw poison.

Zee smelled it before release.

His body already recognized the family of toxins.

He held his breath.

Flicked two fingers.

The needle reversed.

Hit the thrower.

Fourth cast.

Zee raised his palm.

The spell stopped.

Not blocked.

Stopped.

Then he closed his fist.

The magical structure collapsed.

The Witch gasped.

Zee crossed the distance.

Pressure point.

Down.

Fifth tried fleeing.

Vampire speed.

Zee appeared in front of him.

“Stay.”

Compulsion struck.

The assassin froze.

Sixth went for the archive.

Zee smiled.

That one had priorities.

He liked that.

Then killed him.

One knife.

Heart.

No wasted motion.

Rez arrived seconds later.

He looked at the six bodies and prisoners.

“Fast.”

Zee wiped the blade.

“They were not here to fight.”

Ner entered behind him.

“Then what?”

Zee opened the archive door.

Inside, one cabinet had been disturbed.

Only one.

Aurelia’s lineage records.

Veyrath.

Ner’s face hardened.

“They wanted proof.”

“Of the Dragon blood.”

Rez looked toward Zee.

“The world already saw enough.”

“Rumors are not records.”

Zee pulled one parchment free.

Aurelia Veyrath.

Dragon-Witch.

His mother.

His blood.

The attackers had come for certainty.

Not fear.

Not revenge.

Information.

Zee looked toward the surviving assassin.

“Red Covenant?”

The man’s heartbeat changed.

Yes.

Zee crouched.

“What are they?”

The assassin smiled.

“You.”

Zee’s expression did not move.

“That was stupid.”

The man continued.

“People deciding what you become next.”

Silence.

There it was.

Not one kingdom.

Not one council.

A group afraid enough to research him before attacking.

Rez asked:

“Where?”

The assassin laughed.

“Everywhere.”

Zee smelled the lie.

“Wrong.”

He compelled.

“Where?”

Resistance.

Blood ward.

Training.

Zee pressed harder.

The man’s eyes rolled toward him despite himself.

“Where?”

“Greyhaven.”

One word.

Ner looked toward Rez.

Rez nodded.

Known town.

Two days north.

Zee stood.

The assassin smiled weakly.

“You will come.”

“Yes.”

That answer frightened him.

Good.

Zee looked toward the burning villages through the western windows.

Then toward Aurelia’s record.

The philosophy shifted again.

Someone had burned three villages to reach one archive.

They had already demonstrated what they were willing to spend.

Zee did not need a second demonstration.

He looked at Rez.

“Secure the villages.”

“Ner?”

“Stay if you want.”

Ner’s eyes narrowed.

“I am going.”

Zee nodded.

No argument.

Then he looked toward Zareth through the Link.

Drakonis still recovering.

Stay.

Zareth sent immediate irritation.

Then acceptance.

His kingdom first.

Good.

Zee sheathed the knife.

“Greyhaven tonight.”

Rez stared.

“Tonight?”

“Yes.”

“Without knowing how large the Covenant is?”

Zee looked at the surviving assassin.

Then the archive.

Then smoke beyond Darkheart.

“I know enough.”

Rez did not like that answer.

He also did not say Zee was wrong.

That mattered.

Because the world had just burned Zee’s people to test how fast he would react.

And for the first time, Zee Darkheart did not intend to react.

He intended to arrive before they finished preparing the next fire.
