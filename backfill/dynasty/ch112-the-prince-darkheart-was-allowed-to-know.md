Chapter One Hundred Twelve

The Prince Darkheart Was Allowed to Know

Darkheart learned about Zayrlen because Ner got tired of hiding him.

By the sixth month, hiding the pregnancy required more effort than either man considered reasonable.

Ner stood before the mirror one morning fastening a coat that no longer closed correctly.

He pulled once.

Nothing.

Again.

Still nothing.

Zee watched from the bed.

“Stop.”

Ner looked at him through the mirror.

“Do not start.”

“You are fighting clothing.”

“It started first.”

“You own other coats.”

“I like this one.”

Zee stood.

Crimson Phoenix wings shifted behind him before folding tightly enough to cross the room.

He reached toward the fastening.

Ner slapped his hand away.

“I can dress myself.”

“I was going to help.”

“You were going to listen.”

Zee paused.

Ner stared.

Zee listened anyway.

Two heartbeats.

Steady.

Ner’s eyes narrowed.

“You are terrible at pretending.”

“Yes.”

Zayrlen moved.

The Pure Gold Mana beneath Ner’s skin responded immediately.

A thin Crimson line followed.

Then heat.

Dragon.

Witch.

Wolf.

The pattern had become unmistakable.

Zee touched Ner carefully.

Zayrlen kicked directly beneath his hand.

Zee froze.

Ner smiled.

“He did that yesterday.”

“You did not tell me.”

“You were murdering people in a flying bone ship.”

“That is not an excuse.”

“It is literally an excuse.”

Zayrlen moved again.

Harder.

Zee’s entire expression changed.

Ner watched him.

Then his smile softened.

“You want everyone to know.”

Zee looked up.

“That is your decision too.”

“I know.”

Silence.

This child was different from the son Zee had hidden.

No veil.

No erased bloodline.

No false home.

Zayrlen could be named.

Recognized.

Loved in daylight.

Ner rested a hand over their son.

“I am tired of pretending I ate too much.”

Zee almost laughed.

Ner looked at him.

“We tell Darkheart.”

“Today?”

“Yes.”

“No giant ceremony.”

“Agreed.”

“No prophecy nonsense.”

“Obviously.”

“No one touches my stomach without asking.”

Zee nodded.

“Especially Zareth.”

“Why especially Zareth?”

“He looks curious.”

Zee considered.

“He does.”

“See?”

⸻

Darkheart received the news in the central courtyard.

No throne.

No royal announcement scroll.

Ner simply walked beside Zee onto the upper steps without the coat that had hidden his pregnancy.

The courtyard quieted gradually.

Then completely.

Kael already knew.

Tessa knew.

Amara knew.

Zareth stood nearby in human form.

Rez had arrived from Vesper that morning.

The rest of Darkheart did not.

Merek looked from Ner to Zee.

Then back.

His eyes widened.

“Oh.”

Ner folded his arms.

“That reaction is why we waited.”

Someone farther back whispered:

“Is he—”

“Yes,” Zee said.

Silence again.

Ner looked at him.

“I could have answered.”

“I know.”

“Then why did you?”

“They were taking too long.”

Ner sighed.

Zee addressed the courtyard.

“Ner and I are having a son.”

The reaction hit like thunder.

Not fear.

Joy.

Wolves howled.

Witches released sparks into the air.

Vampires shouted.

Someone started crying.

Zee immediately regretted telling anyone.

Ner did not.

He stood watching Darkheart celebrate a child they had never demanded.

Nobody shouted heir.

Nobody shouted succession.

One woman shouted:

“WHAT IS HIS NAME?”

Ner smiled.

“Zayrlen Keal Darkheart.”

That made it worse.

The courtyard erupted again.

Rez leaned toward Zee.

“You look horrified.”

“There are too many people.”

“This is your kingdom.”

“That remains an unfortunate design flaw.”

Zareth looked toward Ner’s stomach.

“Can I—”

“No,” Ner said.

Zareth frowned.

“I did not finish.”

“You were going to ask to touch him.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

Through the Link came:

This is discrimination against Dragons.

Zee ignored him.

⸻

The celebration lasted until evening.

Ner escaped after twenty minutes.

Zee lasted twelve.

The important part came later.

Darkheart children began leaving small things outside Zee and Ner’s rooms.

Wooden wolves.

Carved dragons.

Gold-painted stones.

One badly made crown.

Ner picked that one up.

“He cannot wear this.”

Zee looked at it.

“It might fit Rez.”

Rez threw something at him.

For once, Zee did not mind the noise.

His son had been named publicly.

Nothing came to kill him.

No sky split.

No kingdom issued a death decree.

No father looked at the child and thought he should not exist.

Zee stood in the hall surrounded by gifts meant for Zayrlen.

For one quiet second—

the difference hurt.

Then he let it.

Aidan had received protection through secrecy.

Zayrlen was receiving protection through belonging.

Neither had been loved less.

The world had simply allowed Zee different choices.

⸻

Three days later, someone tried to steal Zayrlen’s blood.

They never reached Ner.

That was the only reason Darkheart still had an eastern medical tower.

The attacker entered disguised as one of Tessa’s assistants.

Human heartbeat.

Human scent.

No visible magic.

But Zee’s Blood Mark had taught his Vampire nature to remember signatures.

This person carried traces of blood from the bone vessels.

Zee smelled it when they crossed the council corridor.

He stopped.

The false healer stopped too.

Ner kept walking for two steps.

Then looked back.

“What?”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“Do I know you?”

The healer swallowed.

“No, Majesty.”

Blood pressure changed.

Heart accelerated.

Zee’s Wolf instincts caught fear.

Witch sight caught the concealment woven into the skin.

One motion.

“Sever.”

The disguise fell.

A woman stood beneath it.

Shaved head.

Gray ritual scars.

A needle appeared in her hand.

She lunged toward Ner.

She never crossed half the distance.

Zee’s Phoenix wing slammed between them.

The needle struck a feather.

Crimson fire consumed the metal.

Ner lifted one hand.

Pure Gold Mana crushed the attacker against the wall.

Hard.

She coughed.

Ner looked at Zee.

“I had that.”

“I know.”

“Then why did you move?”

“She was closer to me.”

Ner stared.

“That is a terrible lie.”

“Yes.”

The woman tried biting through her own tongue.

Heart Command hit first.

“Stop.”

Her jaw froze.

Zee approached.

“What did you want?”

She resisted.

Ner felt the heartbeat through Bloodsong.

“She is terrified of answering.”

Zee crouched.

“Good.”

The woman’s eyes flicked toward Ner.

Then his pregnancy.

Zee noticed.

His expression disappeared.

“What did you want from my son?”

Nothing.

Zee’s voice carried heavier Vampire authority.

“Speak.”

Her resistance cracked.

“Blood.”

Ner’s Gold tightened around her.

“Why?”

“Creation.”

Zee went still.

She continued because the command held.

“The unborn blood of a naturally forming multi-blood child may stabilize artificial combinations.”

Ner stopped breathing for one second.

Zee’s face became empty.

The woman realized what she had admitted.

Too late.

“Who sent you?”

“The Order of the Hollow Crown.”

A name.

Independent.

Not Pyrathis.

Not the bone-vessel force itself.

Researchers who had purchased information from the wreckage network after rumors of Zee’s creation blood spread.

Zee did not care about their ideology.

They had come for his unborn son.

That simplified everything.

“Where?”

She gave him the location.

A monastery beneath the Glass Mountains.

Ner released her from the wall.

The woman collapsed.

She looked toward Zee.

“I told you.”

“Yes.”

“You can use me.”

“No.”

“I can show you the laboratories.”

“You already gave me the location.”

She stared.

“I surrendered.”

Zee’s pupils became slits.

“You came for my son.”

The woman died before she could answer.

⸻

Ner stood over the body.

No moral lecture.

No surprise.

Just anger.

“They knew.”

“Enough.”

“They know I am carrying something unusual.”

“Yes.”

“Then the announcement—”

“Did not cause this.”

Ner looked at him.

“You are certain?”

“They had the plan before Darkheart knew.”

Good.

Zee would not let fear rewrite Zayrlen’s life before birth.

He looked toward the eastern mountains.

Ner knew that expression.

“You are going.”

“Yes.”

“I am coming.”

“No.”

Ner stared.

Zee immediately corrected:

“They targeted you and Zayrlen specifically.”

“That is exactly why I am coming.”

“You are six months pregnant.”

“I remain aware.”

“Ner.”

“Zee.”

Silence.

Then Zayrlen moved.

Both felt it.

Ner put a hand over his stomach.

“Your son apparently has an opinion.”

Zee listened.

The heartbeat was fast.

Not distressed.

Wolf response.

The child had reacted to both fathers’ agitation.

Crimson and Gold shimmered beneath Ner’s hand.

Zee exhaled.

“You stay with the second line.”

Ner smiled.

“Negotiable.”

“Ner.”

“Fine.”

It was not fine.

Both knew that.
