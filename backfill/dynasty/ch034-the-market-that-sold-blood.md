Chapter Thirty-Four

The Market That Sold Blood

They reached the River Market before noon.

Not through the front road.

Aurelia refused that immediately.

The market had too many eyes, too many brokers, and too many people who made their living remembering faces other kingdoms wanted forgotten.

So they entered by river.

A narrow Vesper boat moved beneath hanging willow branches while the city rose ahead in pieces—wooden bridges, black stone warehouses, low taverns, smoke, bells, and hundreds of canvas roofs crowded against the banks.

Zee stood at the bow.

Ner beside him.

Rez sat farther back wearing the same rough clothes Caldrin had used for his disguise the previous day.

He hated them less now.

Only slightly.

Zareth flew too high to be immediately identified, circling behind cloud cover where his crimson shape looked like nothing more than a distant shadow.

The Dragon Link remained active.

Many. Loud. Smell bad.

Zee smiled faintly.

“You are not wrong.”

Aurelia looked toward him.

“What?”

“Zareth hates the market.”

“Good.”

Zee frowned.

“Why good?”

“Because I agree with him.”

Caldrin guided the boat.

Vale sat beside Aurelia.

Lucien had remained in Vesper.

Not because he trusted any of them.

Because someone had to rule the kingdom while half its problems went south looking for criminals.

Rez had objected.

Lucien had responded that being physically ten did not exempt him from sending reports.

Rez had threatened to resign.

Lucien told him princes did not resign.

Ner had called that unfortunate.

Now Rez stared toward the market.

“Blue dock is on the east side.”

Caldrin nodded.

“Khell’s contact appeared there last night.”

Zee looked at him.

“You saw him?”

“No.”

“You said you found him.”

“I found where he was supposed to be.”

“That is different.”

“Yes.”

Ner looked toward Zee.

“You sound like your mother.”

Zee ignored him.

Aurelia unfolded the copied ledger.

The hidden impression Ner had revealed remained faint beneath the page.

VEY—

Seven.

Nothing more.

No names.

No proof.

Only enough possibility to make sleep impossible.

Sevran had stayed in Vesper.

He had wanted to come.

Aurelia refused.

Too many people in the River Market might recognize a surviving Veyrath Dragon.

Sevran argued that Zee was considerably more recognizable.

Aurelia told him that was exactly why Zee would be covered.

Zee had spent most of the morning complaining about the hood.

He complained again.

“I cannot see.”

“You can see.”

“I can see fabric.”

“Then stop turning your head so fast.”

“My horns catch it.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“That is why we altered it.”

“Badly.”

Rez smiled.

“This is the first time your horns have inconvenienced you enough for me to enjoy them.”

Zee looked toward him.

“You are wearing child boots.”

Rez’s smile disappeared.

Ner laughed.

Caldrin raised one hand.

“Save the murder attempts for people outside the boat.”

The dock approached.

Aurelia looked at all three boys.

“Rules.”

Zee sighed.

“No magic unless necessary.”

Ner nodded.

“No names in public.”

Rez nodded.

“No wandering.”

Zee looked toward Zareth above.

“Does he count?”

“Yes.”

The Dragon Link sent deep offense.

Aurelia continued.

“Caldrin leads.”

“Vale covers the rear.”

“Rez stays with Zee and Ner.”

Rez folded his arms.

“I have seventeen years of—”

“Memory.”

“Yes.”

Aurelia looked directly at him.

“And a body that has had four days to relearn itself.”

Rez closed his mouth.

That still hurt.

Because it was true.

Aurelia softened.

“You are still the most experienced of them.”

Rez looked up.

“That is why you stay with them.”

His expression changed.

Not much.

Enough.

“Fine.”

Zee smiled.

Rez immediately pointed at him.

“Do not make me regret this.”

“I never do.”

Everyone stared.

Zee frowned.

“What?”

The River Market smelled like too many lives touching the same place.

Fish.

Wine.

Blood.

Wet wood.

Sweat.

Magic.

Animals.

Smoke.

Vampires.

Wolves.

Humans.

Witches.

Something demonic several streets away.

Zee hated the first minute.

His senses had been unstable since the temporal surge.

Not as violently as before, but enough that crowded places felt like standing inside a storm.

Rez noticed.

“Focus on one.”

Zee looked toward him.

“What?”

“One sound.”

Rez tapped the stall beside them.

A merchant was chopping dried herbs.

“Knife.”

Zee listened.

Chop.

Chop.

Chop.

Everything else moved farther away.

“Better?”

“A little.”

“Good.”

Ner looked toward Rez.

“You learned that?”

“With him.”

Rez nodded toward Zee.

“Years ago.”

Zee remembered.

Vampire hearing had nearly driven him insane at four.

Rez had sat beside him in a cellar and made him count dripping water until the world became manageable again.

Seventeen years were still inside him.

That mattered.

They moved deeper.

Zee kept the hood low.

Ner wore gloves to conceal accidental Golden Mana.

Rez looked like a local child until he moved.

That remained the problem.

His mind expected a seventeen-year-old body.

His body had begun adjusting quickly, but certain movements remained too trained.

Too controlled.

Too old.

A merchant watched him pass.

Rez saw.

Immediately changed his stride.

Slouched.

Dragged one boot.

Zee smiled.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That is your lying face.”

“You have one too.”

“No, I do not.”

Ner answered.

“You absolutely do.”

They reached the east side.

The blue dock was literally blue.

Not enchanted.

Painted.

Badly.

Half the boards had faded gray.

Caldrin stopped beside a fish stall.

“Contact should arrive after second bell.”

Aurelia looked toward the sky.

“Twenty minutes.”

Vale scanned the rooftops.

Zareth remained circling farther out.

The Link carried occasional images.

River.

Boats.

Too many people.

Then something sharper.

Watching.

Zee stopped.

“Zareth sees someone.”

Aurelia’s expression changed.

“Where?”

Zee closed his eyes.

The Link sharpened.

High.

Red roof.

North.

He looked.

A woman sat on the edge of a tiled building eating something from a paper wrap.

Ordinary clothing.

No obvious weapon.

Watching them.

Caldrin saw her.

“Do not react.”

Zee frowned.

“You reacted by saying that.”

“She cannot hear me.”

Zee listened.

Her heartbeat remained calm.

No fear.

No spell.

Could be anything.

Aurelia looked away.

“We stay.”

The second bell rang.

No one came.

Five minutes passed.

Then ten.

Rez looked toward Caldrin.

“Bad contact?”

“Or cautious.”

Ner glanced toward the ledger hidden beneath Aurelia’s coat.

“Maybe he knows we found the mark.”

Zee’s horns heated.

Not much.

A warning.

He touched one.

Rez noticed.

“You good?”

“Yes.”

“Truth.”

Zee exhaled.

“Something feels wrong.”

Vale moved closer.

“What?”

“I do not know.”

The river changed.

Zee heard it first.

Not water.

Heartbeats.

Several slowing at once.

People along the dock began yawning.

A fishmonger dropped his knife.

A sailor sat down heavily.

Ner looked around.

“What is happening?”

Aurelia inhaled.

“Airborne.”

Caldrin cursed.

“Sleep agent.”

Rez grabbed Zee’s sleeve.

“Move.”

They started toward the alley.

Too late.

A cloud of pale vapor rolled from beneath the dock.

Not enough to fill the market.

Only the blue section.

Targeted.

Aurelia threw up a barrier.

Her witchcraft caught the first wave.

The vapor clung to it.

Then began eating through.

“Not ordinary poison.”

Vale drew his sword.

“Ambush.”

Shapes emerged from the river.

Divers.

Six.

No royal colors.

No black robes.

Professional muscle.

One threw a weighted net.

It went toward Aurelia.

Not Zee.

The attackers did not know exactly who they had.

Good.

Aurelia burned the net apart.

Rez moved.

His smaller body crossed the dock differently than he expected, but this time he adjusted mid-step.

Blue-White Mana struck the nearest diver and threw him into the river.

Ner pulled off one glove.

Golden Mana flashed.

Three attackers immediately changed direction.

Toward him.

Zee saw.

“They know Ner.”

Aurelia did too.

So this contract had at least some information.

Not enough.

One attacker shouted.

“Take the gold alive!”

Ner’s face hardened.

“Of course.”

Rez stepped beside him.

“Stay behind me.”

Ner stared.

“You are the same height.”

“Still older.”

“Historically.”

“Not now!”

A chain whipped toward them.

Zee caught it.

The metal burned.

Not anti-Tribrid.

Not Dragonbane.

Just enchanted restraint.

He pulled.

The attacker flew forward.

Zee headbutted him.

His juvenile horn struck the man’s mask.

Crack.

Zee grabbed his forehead.

“I keep forgetting that hurts.”

Rez laughed during the fight.

“That is twice!”

“Shut up!”

Zareth dropped from the sky.

The entire dock erupted.

People screamed.

The attackers looked up.

Too late.

Teal-and-Gold Dragonfire swept across the river in a controlled line, not touching civilians.

One boat caught fire anyway.

Zareth landed beside Zee.

The dock bowed under his weight.

The Dragon Link carried satisfaction.

Found.

Aurelia pointed toward the remaining attackers.

“Alive if possible.”

Caldrin sighed.

“You always remove the fun.”

The fight ended quickly after Zareth arrived.

Four attackers surrendered.

One jumped into the river.

Vale followed.

Caldrin watched him go.

“I hate commitment.”

The sixth lay unconscious beside Rez.

Ner looked toward the pale vapor still rolling under the dock.

“It was for me.”

Rez shook his head.

“For Golden Mana.”

“That means me.”

“Not necessarily personally.”

Ner looked at him.

Rez sighed.

“Fine. Bad wording.”

Aurelia approached one captured attacker.

“Who hired you?”

The man spat blood.

“No idea.”

Caldrin smiled.

“That might actually be true.”

“Broker?”

“Yes.”

“Name?”

“Never got one.”

“What did they want?”

The man looked at Ner.

“Golden blood.”

Ner’s jaw tightened.

Zee’s horns heated again.

Rez immediately put one hand on his shoulder.

“Do not.”

“I am not doing anything.”

“You are glowing.”

Zee looked down.

Crimson energy had appeared beneath his scales.

He forced it back.

Slowly.

It obeyed.

Aurelia noticed.

Good.

Then the woman from the red roof dropped into the alley.

Everyone turned.

She raised both hands.

“No weapons.”

Vale emerged from the river behind her.

Soaked.

Furious.

He put a blade against her back.

She sighed.

“That feels unnecessary.”

Vale’s expression suggested otherwise.

The woman looked toward Aurelia.

“If you are looking for Khell, you missed him by three years.”

Silence.

Aurelia stepped closer.

“Who are you?”

“Someone who used to work for him.”

Caldrin’s eyes sharpened.

“Used to?”

“Yes.”

“Why stop?”

The woman looked toward the restrained attackers.

“Because one day I realized selling people was worse when you knew their names.”

No one trusted that answer.

But no one ignored it either.

Aurelia’s voice remained cold.

“Where is Damaris Khell?”

The woman looked at Zee.

Then Ner.

Then Rez.

Then Zareth.

Her face became more confused with each child.

“This is a terrible group to bring into the River Market.”

Zee frowned.

“We get that a lot.”

She looked back to Aurelia.

“Khell is east.”

“How far?”

“Depends which route he is using.”

“What does he have?”

The woman hesitated.

Aurelia stepped closer.

“Veyrath.”

That changed the woman’s face.

So she knew the name.

Not from legend.

From commerce.

“Yes.”

Aurelia’s breath stopped.

Zee went still.

“How many?” Aurelia asked.

The woman looked toward the ledger hidden beneath her coat as though guessing they already knew part of the answer.

“Seven were listed.”

Ner’s face changed.

The number.

Seven.

“Were?”

Zee asked.

The woman looked toward him.

“That record is old.”

“How old?”

“Five years.”

Aurelia closed her eyes.

Not certainty.

Not rescue.

Only a trail.

“Alive?”

“I do not know.”

The answer hurt everyone differently.

Zee hated it.

Still respected it.

The woman continued.

“Khell did not keep them all.”

“Where did they go?”

“Three sold.”

Aurelia’s face became stone.

“To who?”

“I know one buyer.”

“Name.”

“Lady Morcant.”

Caldrin frowned.

“Witch?”

The woman nodded.

“Eastern territory.”

“Two others?”

“Unknown.”

“And the remaining four?”

“They traveled with Khell.”

“Why?”

The woman looked toward Aurelia.

“Because one of them could identify Veyrath blood by scent.”

Sevran would hate that.

Aurelia understood the implication.

A living tracker.

A person being used to hunt their own bloodline.

Zee’s scales spread again.

This time he noticed immediately.

He closed his eyes.

Focused on Rez’s hand still on his shoulder.

One sound.

One heartbeat.

Rez.

Blue-White Mana nearby.

Steady.

Zee breathed.

The scales stopped spreading.

Aurelia watched.

Progress.

Then the woman said:

“If Khell learns Aurelia Veyrath is alive, he will move.”

Everyone froze.

She knew Aurelia’s true name.

Aurelia’s voice dropped.

“How do you know me?”

The woman’s expression changed.

“Because one of the captives never stopped talking about you.”

The market noise seemed to disappear.

Zee looked at his mother.

Aurelia’s face broke for one heartbeat.

Only one.

“Who?”

The woman hesitated.

“I never learned the real name.”

Aurelia’s eyes hardened.

“Then tell me what you did learn.”

The woman swallowed.

“He called you little flame.”

Aurelia went completely still.

Zee had never seen that expression before.

Not fear.

Not grief.

Recognition so deep it hurt.

“Who called you that?” Zee asked.

Aurelia did not answer.

She looked toward the east.

Then at the woman.

“Where is Khell going?”

The former broker pointed beyond the river.

“Blackglass Road.”

“Two days if you ride hard.”

Caldrin smiled faintly.

“We are riding hard.”

Aurelia looked toward Zee.

For once, she did not tell him no.

“Everyone back to the boat.”

Zee stared.

“That is it?”

“That is enough.”

“Who called you little flame?”

Aurelia looked at him.

“Someone I thought I lost.”

That was all.

For now.

But it was enough to change the road ahead.

The search for House Veyrath was no longer based on a broken ledger.

There were seven names once attached to Khell’s trade.

Three had been sold.

Four had traveled with him.

And at least one of those four had known Aurelia Veyrath personally.

The hunt now had direction.

East.
