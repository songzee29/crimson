Chapter Ninety-Nine

The People Behind the Crowns

Nymeris came to Darkheart six months later.

Without warning.

Zee found her in Southhold arguing with a mason.

He stopped in the street.

“Why are you here?”

Nymeris looked over.

“To see whether your kingdom actually functions.”

The mason glanced between them.

Zee frowned.

“You could have started at the castle.”

“I have seen castles.”

She gestured toward Southhold.

“I wanted this.”

Homes.

Markets.

Wolf children chasing Vampire children through muddy streets.

Witches repairing protective runes on the school.

Former Rell workers building their own shops.

Kael arguing with a guard.

Tessa yelling at three patients.

Amara carrying lumber despite not being asked.

Nymeris watched all of it.

“This is not what surface reports describe.”

“Surface reports are written by people who dislike me.”

“They describe an army of hybrids.”

“I have four.”

“Five, if you count Rez.”

“He is Vesper.”

Nymeris glanced at him.

That answer mattered.

“You could claim him.”

“He would punch me again.”

“Again?”

Zee said nothing.

Nymeris smiled.

“I need that story.”

“No.”

⸻

Ner found them near the school.

Nymeris greeted him before Zee.

That offended Zee unnecessarily.

“You like him more.”

Nymeris looked at Ner.

“Yes.”

Ner smiled.

Zee stared at both of them.

“This alliance is becoming hostile.”

“Good,” Nymeris said.

Ner’s Bloodsong reacted when she stepped close.

Her Siren resonance answered more clearly now.

Still different.

Ner listened.

“You carry something deeper than the surface Siren lines I have encountered.”

Nymeris’s expression sharpened.

“My House descends from the royal Abyss line.”

“Does every Thalryn inherit it?”

“No.”

“Every Queen?”

“No.”

Ner nodded.

No more questions.

Nymeris noticed.

“You stopped.”

“You would tell me if you wanted me to know.”

She looked at Zee.

“Keep him.”

Zee blinked.

“I was already planning to.”

Ner laughed.

⸻

Nymeris stayed three weeks.

Not as guest decoration.

She attended councils.

Argued over Safe Road expansion.

Insulted Darkheart drainage systems.

Improved them.

Visited Kael’s training yards.

Asked Tessa about hybrid medicine.

Let Amara take her hunting through the southern forest and returned covered in mud, furious, and apparently happier.

She spent one afternoon with Rez when he arrived from Vesper.

The two argued about trade law for four hours.

Zee left after twenty minutes.

Cowardice sometimes meant survival.

Zareth arrived on the fifth day.

He and Nymeris immediately disagreed about whether oceanic territory included Dragon flight paths over open sea.

Neither yielded.

Zee watched them for half an hour.

Ner stood beside him.

“This is your fault.”

“I did nothing.”

“You introduced them.”

“I did not control the result.”

“That is usually your defense.”

Fair.

⸻

The attack came on Nymeris’s seventeenth day.

Not against her.

Against Southhold.

Again.

A mercenary company from a western kingdom wanted refugees returned for unpaid taxes and military desertion.

Different motive.

Same ugly assumption.

That people remained owned after leaving.

This time Southhold responded before Zee did.

Bells rang.

Children entered shelters.

Witch barriers rose.

Wolf trackers moved outside the walls.

Vampire defenders took rooftops.

Kael commanded the eastern street.

Tessa moved medical teams.

Amara vanished into the trees.

Zee stood beside Nymeris on the tower.

She watched.

“You are not moving.”

“I am.”

“You are standing.”

“I am watching first.”

Old Zee would already have been in the enemy line.

Nymeris understood the change.

“Your people know what to do.”

“Yes.”

“That bothers you.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because they learned by being attacked.”

Nymeris looked toward the battlefield.

“So did mine.”

There.

Same burden again.

Then the mercenaries broke through the western barrier.

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“Now.”

He moved.

Nymeris moved too.

Not because Zee asked.

Because civilians were threatened.

⸻

Zee hit the first line in human form.

Crimson-black scales spreading over his arms.

One-word Witchcraft breaking weapons.

Vampire speed cutting through formation.

Wolf instinct reading motion.

Controlled.

Dark.

Efficient.

Nymeris fought differently.

Water erupted from underground wells.

Not enough to flood Southhold.

Enough to tear mercenaries off their feet.

Her voice carried through it.

Abyssal resonance.

Pressure.

Direction.

One man tried reaching a child shelter.

Nymeris slammed him into stone with a column of water.

Zee looked over.

“That was excessive.”

Nymeris stared.

“You broke someone’s leg with your face ten seconds ago.”

“Horn.”

“That does not improve it.”

Good.

They fought well together.

Not perfectly.

Zee pushed.

Nymeris contained.

Nymeris controlled space.

Zee hunted openings.

Neither commanded the other.

Neither needed to.

When the mercenary commander surrendered, Zee stopped.

Immediately.

That mattered after Rell.

Nymeris noticed.

He heard the commander’s heartbeat.

Smelled fear.

Wanted blood.

Still chose prison.

Nymeris said nothing.

She did not need to.

⸻

Afterward, they found six dead Darkheart citizens.

Three soldiers.

Three civilians.

Not clean.

Never clean.

Zee stood over the bodies.

Nymeris beside him.

No one spoke for a while.

Then she said:

“You count them all as yours.”

“Yes.”

“Even people who arrived months ago.”

“Yes.”

“Even if they were born elsewhere.”

“Yes.”

Nymeris looked toward him.

“That is why they keep coming.”

Zee’s jaw tightened.

“That is why they keep dying too.”

“No.”

He looked at her.

She did not soften the answer.

“They die because other rulers still think leaving is theft.”

Zee said nothing.

Nymeris continued.

“You are responsible for what happens under your protection.”

“Yes.”

“You are not responsible for the existence of cruelty beyond it.”

Zee almost argued.

Then stopped.

Not because she had absolved him.

She had not.

She was drawing the same distinction Ner often demanded of facts.

Responsibility without delusion.

Queen thinking.

Zee understood.

That was rarer than sympathy.

⸻

Later that night, Zee found Nymeris sitting alone on the southern wall.

No crown.

Hair loose.

She looked less like Queen of Nhalaris.

Still unmistakably one.

He sat beside her.

For once, neither began with an insult.

Nymeris spoke first.

“When I was nineteen, a pressure collapse killed two hundred people beneath the western shelf.”

Zee looked at her.

She had never told him anything personal before.

“I ordered the evacuation too late.”

“Why?”

“I believed the wardens when they said the barrier would hold.”

Silence.

“It did not.”

Zee understood immediately.

“How long did you blame yourself?”

Nymeris laughed without humor.

“I have not stopped.”

“There.”

She looked at him.

“That is stupid.”

“Yes.”

“Was that supposed to help?”

“No.”

He leaned back.

“I blame myself for people dying where I am not.”

“That is also stupid.”

“Yes.”

They sat in silence.

Then Nymeris smiled faintly.

“Good.”

“What?”

“Now we are both stupid.”

Zee laughed.

There.

Not diplomacy.

Not alliance.

Friendship.

Something deeper than usefulness.

Chosen slowly.

Earned.

⸻

Nymeris remained another week.

When she finally left, Darkheart children knew her name.

Tessa had forced her to take three medical books back to Nhalaris.

Amara had given her a hunting knife.

Kael had somehow lost money to her playing cards.

Rez sent trade proposals.

Zareth claimed he still disliked her.

Nobody believed him.

Ner walked with her to the river passage.

Zee followed.

Nymeris looked between them.

“Nhalaris will send a permanent diplomatic vessel to the coastal river.”

Zee raised an eyebrow.

“That sounds suspiciously like friendship.”

“It is administration.”

“Of course.”

She looked at Ner.

“And I want you to visit.”

Ner nodded.

“I will.”

Then at Zee.

“You too.”

“I just went.”

“Six months ago.”

“Recently.”

“No.”

Zee sighed.

Nymeris stepped toward the water.

Then paused.

“Zee.”

His expression changed.

She rarely used his name without sarcasm.

“What?”

“If Darkheart is attacked beneath the coastline, send for me.”

He stared.

Nymeris continued.

“If Nhalaris is attacked near the surface, I will send for you.”

Not treaty language.

More personal.

Trust.

Zee nodded once.

“Done.”

She entered the river.

Then looked back.

“And fix your drainage.”

Zee’s face hardened.

“I knew you ruined the moment on purpose.”

Nymeris smiled.

Then vanished beneath the water.

Ner stood beside him.

“You love her.”

Zee looked at him sharply.

“Not like that.”

“I know.”

Zee looked back toward the river.

Chosen family had never needed one shape.

Aurelia had taught him that blood mattered.

Rez had taught him blood was not required.

Zareth had taught him connection could begin before language.

Ner had taught him love could survive change.

Nymeris was teaching him something else.

Two rulers could become family without either surrendering a crown.

And somewhere in the months ahead, that friendship would continue growing.

Not toward prophecy.

Not toward destiny.

Toward choices neither of them had made yet.

For now, that was enough.
