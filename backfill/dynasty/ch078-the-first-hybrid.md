Chapter Seventy-Eight

The First Hybrid

By spring, Darkheart had stopped calling Kael Marrin an accident.

Not because anyone understood him completely.

Because he had survived long enough to become undeniable.

His Witchcraft remained intact.

His Vampire nature remained stable.

He could cast without weakening his healing.

Feed without losing magic.

Use Vampire speed while shaping Witch sigils.

Compel weak minds.

Sense blood.

Heal wounds that would once have ended his career.

He was still terrible at cards.

That mattered to Kael more.

“You owe me seventeen silver.”

Captain Elara stared across the barracks table.

“You cheated.”

“I evolved.”

“That is not the same.”

Kael smiled, fangs showing slightly.

“Sounds like jealousy.”

Elara threw a card at him.

Kael caught it before it crossed half the table.

Everyone went quiet.

He looked at the card.

Then sighed.

“I hate when that happens.”

The barracks laughed.

Good.

Normal was returning.

Different normal.

But normal.

⸻

Zee watched from the doorway.

Twenty-two now.

Older by months rather than years.

His face had matured slightly.

His presence had changed more.

Kinghood did that.

So did blood.

Dark crimson-black scales remained visible along his neck and forearms beneath his clothes.

His crimson-and-gold eyes were calm.

Human pupils.

Kael noticed him.

“Majesty.”

The room immediately straightened.

Zee frowned.

“You were all laughing two seconds ago.”

Elara smiled.

“You entered.”

“That sounds like a problem.”

“It is.”

Kael stood.

Zee looked at the cards.

“You really cheated?”

“No.”

Elara said, “Yes.”

Zee looked between them.

Then walked away.

“Handle your own crimes.”

Kael laughed.

That was when the horn sounded.

Not an attack.

Arrival.

Hundreds.

⸻

They came from the southeast.

Families.

Not soldiers.

Witches first.

Then Vampires.

Wolves.

Children.

Wagons.

People carrying everything they still owned.

Lord Merek met Zee at the outer gate.

“Three hundred seventeen.”

“From where?”

“Kingdom of Serren.”

Zee knew the name.

Old Witch monarchy.

Strict bloodline laws.

“They expelled them?”

Merek shook his head.

“Worse.”

A woman at the head of the refugee line stepped forward.

She was Vampire.

One arm missing below the elbow.

A Wolf boy slept against her shoulder.

“They started registration.”

Zee’s face changed.

“For what?”

“Mixed households.”

“Why?”

“Because of him.”

She looked toward Kael.

Silence.

Kael stood several feet behind Zee.

The woman continued.

“They heard Darkheart made a Witch who could remain Witch after becoming Vampire.”

Kael flinched at the phrasing.

She saw.

“I’m sorry.”

“It’s fine.”

“No.”

Kael stepped forward.

“It isn’t. But I know what you meant.”

The woman nodded.

Her name was Maraine Holt.

Her husband had been a Wolf.

Dead three years.

Their son carried dormant traits from both parents but had awakened only Wolf.

Serren had classified the boy as a potential hybrid risk.

Not because Zee touched him.

Not because Kael created him.

Because fear had learned a new word.

Hybrid.

And started applying it everywhere.

Zee looked at the line behind her.

“They came after children?”

“Yes.”

His pupils narrowed.

“What happened to the ones who stayed?”

Maraine looked down.

“Some were taken.”

There.

Another blade.

Raised because of fear.

Sharpening the thing it feared.

Zee opened Darkheart’s gates.

“All of you come in.”

Merek looked toward him.

“We will need housing.”

“Build it.”

“Food?”

“Open the winter stores.”

“Those are reserve stores.”

Zee looked at him.

“Reserve for what?”

Merek paused.

Then nodded.

Good answer.

The refugees entered.

⸻

The first attempt to steal Kael came six nights later.

Four kidnappers.

No banners.

No kingdom insignia.

Paid by a foreign alchemist.

They wanted blood.

Not Kael himself.

Enough to reproduce whatever Zee had done.

Kael caught two alone.

That was important.

The first tried striking from behind.

Kael heard the heartbeat change.

Turned.

Vampire reflexes.

Caught the blade.

Witch force threw the attacker into a wall.

The second used vervain mixed with Witch suppression dust.

Kael inhaled it.

Dropped immediately.

Not immune.

Not Zee.

Still vulnerable.

Good.

The kidnappers moved to take him.

Then Kael’s Witch nature reacted.

One shaking hand lifted.

“Burn.”

The suppression powder caught fire before entering his lungs deeper.

Kael rolled.

Vampire healing started.

He slammed the second attacker down.

By the time Zee arrived, Kael had both restrained.

The other two were dead.

Elara killed them.

Not Zee.

Not Kael.

Darkheart defenders had handled their own problem.

Zee liked that.

Then he looked at the surviving kidnappers.

“Who paid you?”

One answered immediately.

“Doctor Hest.”

“Where?”

“Across the Korr border.”

“Why Kael?”

The man looked confused by the question.

“For his blood.”

Zee’s expression went cold.

“He has a name.”

“Yes.”

“Use it.”

“Kael.”

Better.

Kael came forward.

Still pale from the suppression toxin.

“He hired you to take me alive?”

“Yes.”

“And if I resisted?”

The kidnapper looked away.

Kael’s Vampire senses heard the heartbeat change.

His face hardened.

“Kill anyone with me.”

“Yes.”

Kael looked toward Zee.

Zee did nothing.

Choice belonged to Kael.

Kael thought for several seconds.

Then said:

“Prison.”

The kidnapper looked shocked.

Zee did too.

Slightly.

Kael noticed.

“What?”

“I expected you to kill them.”

“I considered it.”

“Why didn’t you?”

Kael looked at the refugees sleeping behind newly built walls.

“Because I want people to understand something.”

Zee waited.

Kael turned toward the prisoners.

“I am not your experiment.”

His Witch Mana flared.

Dark-red Vampire veins showed briefly beneath his eyes.

“I am not your army.”

His fangs descended.

“I am not proof your king can buy.”

Then he looked at Zee.

“And I am not his property either.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“Correct.”

Kael turned back.

“If they come for me again, maybe I choose differently.”

Good.

His line.

His life.

⸻

Three days later, Kael asked for a name.

Not a personal name.

He already had one.

A word for what he was.

The question happened in council.

No ceremony.

No historians summoned.

Kael stood before Zee, Ner, Merek, Elara, and three healers.

“I am tired of everyone calling me ‘the Witch with Vampire blood.’”

Ner smiled.

“Understandable.”

Kael looked at him.

“You have a name.”

Ner frowned.

“Several, unfortunately.”

“Bloodsong stuck.”

“Yes.”

Kael folded his arms.

“So what am I?”

Zee leaned back.

“Kael.”

Kael gave him a look.

“We established that.”

Ner laughed.

Zee smiled.

Then became serious.

“Witch.”

“Yes.”

“Vampire.”

“Yes.”

“No third bloodline.”

“No.”

“No loss of either.”

“No.”

Zee shrugged.

“Witch-Vampire.”

Kael stared.

“That simple?”

Merek said, “Most historical names start simple.”

Kael considered.

“Witch-Vampire.”

Ner nodded.

“Accurate.”

Elara smiled.

“Terrible for poetry.”

Kael looked toward Zee.

“Fine.”

And just like that, history acquired a name.

Not through prophecy.

Not divine declaration.

Not a glowing sign in the sky.

A soldier wanted people to stop describing him like an experiment.

The first Witch-Vampire stood inside Darkheart and chose the words for what he had become.

⸻

The world reacted within a month.

Letters came.

Some peaceful.

Some terrified.

One kingdom requested formal assurances Zee would not transform foreign citizens.

Another offered gold for “controlled access” to his blood.

One threatened sanctions.

One offered an alliance.

A Vampire court asked whether Witch-Vampires could reproduce naturally.

Zee had no idea.

He burned that letter mostly because the wording annoyed him.

Then came the worst one.

A declaration from three minor Houses.

They demanded Darkheart cease “unnatural bloodline manufacturing.”

Zee read it twice.

Then handed it to Ner.

Ner read.

Looked toward Kael training in the courtyard below.

“He thinks you manufactured him.”

Zee’s expression darkened.

“They all do.”

“No.”

Ner folded the letter.

“Some know better.”

Zee looked at him.

“Doesn’t matter.”

“It does.”

“Why?”

“Because fear spreading is not the same as everyone believing the same thing.”

Fair.

Zee hated when Ner made distinctions during good anger.

Still useful.

Zee looked out over Darkheart.

New homes.

New training yards.

Witch wards beside Vampire night patrols.

Wolf sentries running forest edges.

Refugees becoming citizens.

Kael teaching two guards how to fight around enhanced Vampire speed without depending on it.

Darkheart was changing.

Not because Zee announced an empire.

Because people kept arriving.

And stayed.

Zee looked at the declaration again.

His pupils narrowed.

“The world thinks I’m building monsters.”

Ner stood beside him.

“Are you?”

Zee looked toward Kael.

“No.”

Then toward the refugee district.

“I’m building somewhere they can survive.”

That difference mattered.

The world might not care.

Darkheart would.
