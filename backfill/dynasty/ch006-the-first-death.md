Chapter Six

The First Death

The hidden passage beneath the nursery had not been opened in eighty-three years.

Dust filled the narrow stairwell. Ancient roots had pushed between the stones, twisting through the walls like petrified veins. The royal witch knelt beside the concealed entrance with both hands pressed against a faded seal while Queen Aurelia held Zee against her chest.

The crimson hatchling refused to leave him.

He clung to Aurelia’s shoulder with tiny claws, his tail wrapped around the cloth covering Zee. Teal-and-gold mana trembled across his scales whenever footsteps passed beyond the locked nursery doors.

Danger.

The feeling moved through the Dragon Link without words.

Zee felt it.

His crying had stopped.

That frightened Aurelia more.

He simply stared toward the door with wide crimson eyes.

“Can you open it?” she whispered.

The witch gritted her teeth.

“This passage was sealed by one of your husband’s ancestors.”

“Then break it.”

“I am trying.”

Purple mana spread through cracks in the floor. The old seal resisted, not because it possessed greater power, but because it had been woven into the foundations of Castle Darkheart.

Another impact from the siege shook the tower.

Dust fell.

The hatchling hissed.

Aurelia looked toward the nursery door.

“How long?”

“Minutes.”

“We do not have minutes.”

A sound came from the corridor.

Metal sliding against metal.

The locks.

Someone was opening them.

The witch looked over her shoulder.

“My Queen.”

Aurelia knew.

“Keep working.”

The first lock turned.

Then the second.

Then the third.

The witch increased the pressure of her spell.

The floor seal cracked.

Aurelia stepped backward until the cradle pressed against her legs.

The nursery doors opened.

King Victor stood beyond them.

Alone.

His cloak hung over one shoulder.

His face was pale.

His right hand remained hidden beneath the folds of fabric.

Aurelia’s eyes went immediately to it.

“No.”

Victor entered.

The guards outside remained motionless.

“Leave us.”

No one obeyed.

Captain Vale had arrived behind Victor.

He looked from the king to the queen and understood enough.

“My King…”

Victor did not turn.

“I said leave us.”

Vale’s hand rested on his sword.

“Your Majesty, perhaps—”

“Leave.”

The command struck the corridor.

Vale’s jaw tightened.

He bowed.

The guards withdrew with him.

Victor closed the doors himself.

Aurelia stared at him.

“You brought no witnesses.”

“I did not want witnesses.”

The hatchling climbed down from Aurelia’s shoulder and placed himself between Victor and Zee.

He was absurdly small.

His wings barely reached the width of Aurelia’s forearm.

His growl sounded more like a broken purr than a threat.

Victor looked at him anyway.

The Dragon Link flooded with simple instinct.

Protect.

Zee’s Crimson Mana stirred.

Aurelia saw it.

So did Victor.

“Do not,” she whispered.

“I have not done anything.”

“You came here with a weapon.”

Victor froze.

Aurelia’s voice broke.

“I know you.”

His hand emerged from beneath the cloak.

The dagger caught the firelight.

The witch stopped working on the passage.

For several seconds, no one breathed.

Then Aurelia laughed.

Not because anything was funny.

Because grief had become too large for silence.

“You actually brought it.”

Victor looked down at the weapon as though seeing it for the first time.

“Aurelia.”

“You held him.”

“I know.”

“He trusted you.”

“I know.”

“You called him your son.”

“He is my son.”

“Then put the dagger down.”

Victor’s fingers tightened around the hilt.

“I cannot.”

The queen’s face changed.

Whatever remained between husband and wife began dying in that moment.

“You mean you will not.”

Victor swallowed.

“There is a difference between fear and cowardice.”

“You are about to murder a newborn because a piece of bone frightened you.”

“I am about to prevent something none of us may survive.”

“You do not know that.”

“The prophecy—”

“Does not know him.”

“It knew enough.”

“Enough for you to finish the rest yourself.”

Victor stared at her.

She stepped closer.

“You imagined kingdoms burning.”

His face hardened.

“You imagined Zee destroying everything.”

“Aurelia—”

“You imagined him becoming a monster.”

“Stop.”

“And then you decided your imagination was evidence.”

“Stop.”

“You became afraid of what our son might become, so now you are trying to become worse first.”

Victor’s expression broke.

For one heartbeat, the king disappeared.

The father returned.

He looked at Zee.

The infant watched him.

No hatred.

No understanding.

Only recognition.

Victor’s hand began to lower.

Then another siege stone hit the castle.

The wall behind Aurelia split.

The hatchling cried out.

Zee startled.

Crimson Mana erupted.

This time, the force was stronger.

The cracked wall stopped moving.

Falling stone hung motionless for a breath.

Then every fragment shifted away from Aurelia and Zee, striking the opposite side of the room.

The witch stared.

Victor stared.

Zee began crying.

And Victor’s fear returned greater than before.

“You see?”

Aurelia looked at him in disbelief.

“He protected himself.”

“He is two days old.”

“He is terrified.”

“He is two days old!”

Victor shouted it now.

The chamber went still.

“No witch child does that.”

“No vampire child does that.”

“No wolf child does that.”

Aurelia’s voice lowered.

“He is not any one of them.”

Victor flinched.

The words confirmed what everyone already feared.

Three natures.

One child.

The First Tribrid.

Victor looked at the dagger.

Then at Zee.

The prophecy whispered inside his mind.

Every blade raised against him shall sharpen what it seeks to destroy.

He had spent the night fearing that line.

Now he made the cruelest possible decision.

He decided to test it.

Victor stepped forward.

The hatchling lunged.

Tiny teeth sank into Victor’s wrist.

He cursed and ripped his arm back.

Teal-and-gold mana flashed.

Not enough to seriously injure him.

Enough to draw blood.

Victor stared at the hatchling.

The creature stood over Zee.

Protect.

The feeling crossed the Link.

Zee’s crying stopped.

Aurelia saw Victor raise the dagger.

She moved before thought.

Her hand struck his wrist.

The blade missed Zee and sliced across Aurelia’s forearm.

Blood sprayed across the blankets.

Victor froze.

Aurelia did not.

She slapped him.

The sound cracked through the room.

“You cut me.”

Victor stared at the blood.

“I did not mean—”

“No.”

Her voice trembled with rage.

“You meant worse.”

The witch rushed forward.

Victor turned toward her.

“Stay back.”

She stopped.

Not from fear.

From calculation.

Aurelia held Zee tighter.

“You will have to kill me first.”

Victor looked at his wife.

Then his son.

Then the hatchling.

Something inside him shattered.

He hated what he was about to do.

That did not stop him.

“Aurelia…”

Tears filled his eyes.

“Forgive me.”

He struck her with magic.

A dark violet force hit Aurelia across the chest and threw her onto the bed.

Zee slipped from her arms.

The witch screamed.

The hatchling leaped.

Victor caught Zee before he hit the floor.

For one terrible moment…

Father held son again.

Zee stared up at him.

Crimson eyes.

Tiny fangs.

Small hands.

Victor remembered the first time.

He remembered saying my son.

Then Aurelia screamed his name.

“VICTOR!”

He closed his eyes.

And drove the dagger into Zee’s chest.

Silence.

The entire world seemed to stop.

The hatchling shrieked.

A sound too large for his tiny body tore from him as teal-and-gold mana exploded through the nursery.

Aurelia crawled from the bed.

“No.”

Victor opened his eyes.

The dagger remained inside Zee.

Blood spread across the infant’s clothes.

Crimson.

Ordinary-looking.

Terrible.

Zee’s mouth opened.

No cry came.

His heartbeat stumbled.

Once.

Twice.

Then stopped.

Aurelia reached him.

Victor did not resist when she tore Zee from his arms.

She fell to the floor holding her child.

“No.”

Her fingers pressed against the wound.

“Zee.”

Nothing.

The royal witch dropped beside her.

Magic flooded her hands.

Purple light entered the wound.

Nothing happened.

“Again!”

“I am trying!”

“Again!”

The hatchling pushed himself against Zee’s side.

Fear became panic through the Dragon Link.

Where?

Gone?

Come back.

The simple emotions struck something that no newborn should have known how to answer.

But Zee did not move.

Victor stood above them.

Blood covered his hand.

His son’s blood.

He had done it.

The room blurred.

“I…”

Aurelia looked up.

The grief in her face became hatred.

“You killed him.”

Victor’s lips parted.

No words came.

Outside…

The siege stopped.

Not gradually.

All at once.

Every wolf across the northern forests collapsed.

Vampires across the world clutched their chests as the impossible heartbeat disappeared completely.

Witches everywhere felt a pressure vanish that they had not realized still lingered since Zee’s birth.

The blood-soaked clouds above Castle Darkheart turned black.

The world did not know what had happened.

It only felt the absence.

Inside the nursery, the hatchling pressed his forehead against Zee’s.

His entire body shook.

Teal-and-gold mana wrapped helplessly around the dead infant.

Aurelia sobbed.

The witch continued healing a heart that no longer beat.

Victor stared at the prophecy fulfilled by his own hand.

Or so he believed.

Then Zee’s blood moved.

One drop crawled backward across his skin.

The witch stopped.

Aurelia did not notice.

Another drop rose from the blanket.

Then another.

Victor’s face drained of color.

The blood gathered around the dagger wound.

Not flowing outward.

Returning.

The torn flesh began closing.

Slowly.

Wrongly.

Beautifully.

Zee’s tiny fingers twitched.

The crimson hatchling froze.

A feeling crossed the Link.

There.

Aurelia gasped.

“Zee?”

The infant inhaled.

The breath came violently.

His back arched.

Crimson Mana exploded through the nursery with enough force to throw every adult backward.

Victor struck the wall.

The witch crashed into the floor.

Aurelia held onto Zee with everything she had.

The dagger tore free from his chest by itself and clattered across the stone.

Zee screamed.

This time the cry was not quiet.

It rolled through Castle Darkheart.

Across the walls.

Across the battlefield.

Across forests and mountains and buried vampire halls.

His heartbeat returned.

Once.

The vampires felt it.

Twice.

The wolves lifted their heads.

Three times.

Every witch inside Castle Darkheart felt their magic shudder beneath the sudden pressure.

Zee’s wound vanished.

Completely.

No scar remained.

But something had changed.

His Crimson Mana was denser now.

Not vastly stronger.

Not transformed into some understood new state.

Simply different.

As though his body had encountered death…

And refused to remember how to remain dead.

Victor stared from the floor.

The prophecy whispered again.

Every blade raised against him shall sharpen what it seeks to destroy.

He finally understood the horror of his mistake.

He had not prevented the prophecy.

He had become the first blade.

Aurelia rose with Zee in her arms.

The hatchling climbed against him immediately.

His tiny body pressed around Zee as though he could physically prevent death from returning.

Zee’s crying softened.

The Dragon Link carried one feeling between them.

Safe.

Victor slowly stood.

Aurelia looked at him.

“Do not come closer.”

He stopped.

The king’s face had become ash.

“I killed him.”

“Yes.”

“He came back.”

“Yes.”

Victor stared at Zee.

Fear unlike anything he had ever known entered his eyes.

Not because Zee had attacked him.

Not because Zee had threatened the kingdom.

Because Victor had just killed his own newborn son…

And death had failed to keep him.

The witch whispered,

“What is he?”

Aurelia looked at her sharply.

“No.”

The woman fell silent.

No one would answer that question tonight.

No one possessed the right.

No one possessed the knowledge.

Outside, foreign soldiers stared toward Castle Darkheart as the infant’s scream faded through the air.

The armies did not know what had happened inside.

They only knew that for several moments, the impossible presence they had feared had vanished.

Then returned.

Stronger.

And inside the nursery, King Victor Darkheart looked at the blood on his hands and realized that the first person to kill Zee…

Had also become the first person to teach his body how to survive death.
