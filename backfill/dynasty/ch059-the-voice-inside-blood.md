Chapter Fifty-Nine

The Voice Inside Blood

Ner woke screaming three nights later.

Every heart in the western wing answered.

Not metaphorically.

Zee heard it happen.

One second Vesper slept.

The next—

hundreds of heartbeats struck the same rhythm.

Once.

Hard.

Together.

Zee opened his eyes.

Ner screamed again.

The blood inside Zee’s body vibrated.

He was moving before consciousness fully caught up.

Silent feet.

No door opened loudly.

No servant saw him cross the corridor.

Nanda Parbat remained in everything.

He entered Ner’s room through the balcony because the hallway was crowded with panicking guards.

Ner stood in the center of the chamber.

Barefoot.

Shaking.

Pure Golden Mana flooded the walls.

Dark-red light threaded through it like veins.

Three servants were frozen near the door.

Not paralyzed.

Listening.

Their faces empty.

Zee looked at them.

Compulsion?

No.

Different.

Ner’s voice came again.

“GET OUT!”

The command hit physically.

The servants turned.

Walked out.

No eye contact.

No spell gesture.

No Golden structure.

Voice.

Resonance.

Blood.

Zee stared.

Ner slapped both hands over his mouth.

His eyes widened.

The servants continued down the hall until the command apparently released.

Then confusion hit them.

Rez arrived from the doorway.

Sword drawn.

He looked at Zee.

Then Ner.

Then the retreating servants.

“What happened?”

Ner shook his head.

Zee listened.

Ner’s heartbeat was wrong.

Fast.

Then slow.

Then producing a second resonance without a second beat.

Like music around a drum.

“I heard everyone.”

Ner’s voice trembled.

Rez lowered the sword.

“Heard what?”

“Their blood.”

Silence.

Ner looked at Zee.

“All of it.”

Zee approached carefully.

“Mine too?”

Ner nodded.

“What does it sound like?”

Ner’s expression changed.

“You do not want to know.”

That made Zee curious immediately.

Rez saw.

“No.”

Zee looked toward him.

“I did nothing.”

“You were about to.”

Ner sat heavily on the bed.

“My own blood woke me.”

Zee frowned.

“How?”

“I was dreaming.”

“About?”

Ner looked away.

Sea.

That was obvious from his scent before he said anything.

Fear mixed with salt.

“I was underwater.”

“I could breathe.”

“There were voices singing beneath me.”

“When I tried waking, something sang back.”

Rez sat across from him.

“Memory?”

“I do not think so.”

“Vision?”

“I do not know.”

Good.

Ner looked toward Zee.

“Then I could hear every heart in Vesper.”

“Human?”

“All.”

“Witches?”

“Yes.”

“Vampires?”

“Yes.”

“Wolves?”

“Yes.”

Zee’s expression sharpened.

“What about me?”

Ner hesitated.

“You were different.”

“How?”

“I heard four things.”

The room went still.

Zee’s Dragon ridges tightened.

Four.

Not four awake.

Four present.

Ner looked toward him.

“Three sounded like separate rhythms inside the same blood.”

“And Dragon?”

Ner frowned.

“Lower.”

“Like something beneath the music.”

Not awake.

But there.

Zee nodded.

Rez looked between them.

“And your command?”

Ner’s face darkened.

“I did not mean to control anyone.”

“But you did,” Zee said.

“Yes.”

No softening.

Truth.

Rez thought tactically.

“Did they understand the words?”

Ner looked toward the door.

“They moved before I finished speaking.”

Zee understood.

“Not language.”

Ner looked at him.

“Blood heard first.”

That sentence settled.

Something ancient.

Something not Witch.

Not Vampire.

A third thing.

Ner’s hidden nature awakening more clearly.

Zee sat beside him.

Their knees touched.

Neither moved.

“Try something small.”

Ner stared.

“After I just controlled three servants?”

“Yes.”

“No.”

“Fine.”

Zee did not push.

Ner looked surprised.

“Fine?”

“Yes.”

“You really have changed.”

Zee’s face closed slightly.

Ner immediately regretted the wording.

Zee looked toward the empty corner where Aurelia would once have stood during every crisis.

“Yes.”

No anger.

That hurt more.

Rez interrupted before grief swallowed the room.

“We need boundaries.”

Ner looked toward him.

“For what?”

“For whatever this is.”

He pointed toward the hallway.

“You can influence people through your voice.”

“Maybe.”

“You can feel blood.”

“Yes.”

“You still have Golden Mana.”

“Yes.”

“You now need blood to feed.”

Ner grimaced.

“Yes.”

Rez leaned back.

“Then we learn one thing at a time.”

Ner sighed.

“You make everything sound like military training.”

“It usually works.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“Until it doesn’t.”

“That is why I have you.”

There.

Brother.

Still.

Zee looked toward Ner.

“We test only with consent.”

Ner nodded immediately.

Good.

“Me first.”

Ner stared.

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because your blood is insane.”

Zee looked offended.

Rez laughed.

Ner continued.

“And your compulsion may interfere.”

“Also rude.”

“True.”

Rez raised one hand.

“Me.”

Zee looked at him.

Ner frowned.

“You sure?”

“No.”

“Wonderful.”

“But we need information.”

Rez stood.

“What do you want me to do?”

Ner thought.

Then:

“Lift your right hand.”

Rez lifted it.

Ner blinked.

“I did not use anything.”

Rez lowered his hand.

“Try again.”

Ner breathed.

Golden Mana stayed dormant.

He looked at Rez.

No special eye contact.

Then spoke softly.

“Lift your right hand.”

Something moved through the room.

Zee felt it.

Not spell.

Not sound only.

Rez’s blood pulse changed.

His hand lifted before his expression reacted.

Then he forced it down.

Ner immediately stopped.

Rez stared at his own fingers.

“That was unpleasant.”

“Sorry.”

“Useful.”

Zee stepped closer.

“Could you resist?”

“Yes.”

“Easily?”

“No.”

Interesting.

Ner looked sick.

“I do not like this.”

Zee understood too well.

Power touching another mind without permission.

Dangerous.

He had compulsion.

Now Ner had something adjacent but different.

Zee did not tell him to embrace it.

Did not tell him to fear it.

“It is yours.”

Ner looked at him.

“You decide what that means.”

Aurelia’s lesson.

Changed.

Not copied.

Zee’s now.

Ner nodded slowly.

Then his fangs descended.

His eyes flashed.

“Hungry.”

Rez sighed.

“Of course.”

⸻

They stopped using animals after the fourth night.

Not because Ner could not feed from them.

Because it left him weak.

Vampire blood changed the requirement.

Human blood worked better.

Witch blood—

different.

They discovered that accidentally when Seraphine offered.

Ner refused three times.

Seraphine became irritated.

“You nearly died because you were afraid of feeding.”

“I am not biting you.”

“You are fifteen, not five.”

“That is not relevant.”

“It is to me.”

Ner looked toward Zee for help.

Zee smiled.

“No.”

“Traitor.”

Seraphine held out her wrist.

“You need to know what you are.”

Ner’s face hardened.

“I know who I am.”

Seraphine paused.

Then nodded.

“Good correction.”

She cut her wrist herself.

Ner fed.

Small.

Controlled.

The moment Veyrath Witch blood touched his tongue, Pure Golden Mana erupted.

Not stronger.

Different.

His voice hummed involuntarily.

Seraphine’s blood rose toward the wound.

Not out.

Toward Ner.

Zee stepped closer.

Ner stopped immediately.

The blood settled.

Seraphine stared.

“Well.”

Rez leaned against the wall.

“That is becoming a complicated word.”

Ner wiped his mouth.

“I did not do that.”

Zee corrected:

“You did.”

Ner glared.

“Without meaning to.”

“Still you.”

Seraphine flexed her hand.

“Did you take my magic?”

“No.”

Immediate.

Ner could feel that much.

“My Gold reacted to your blood.”

“Nothing else.”

Good.

No copied Veyrath power.

No theft.

Zee recognized the distinction.

Blood could change without becoming acquisition.

His own body had taught him that.

He looked toward his wrist.

Since draining the six killers, his feeding had become different too.

Supernatural blood called louder.

Not because he needed it to survive.

Because his body had learned it could become more through it.

That was dangerous.

Zee liked it.

Also dangerous.

⸻

The request came that afternoon.

Not from a kingdom.

From a vampire settlement beneath the eastern cliffs.

Three bodies had been found drained.

Completely.

Everyone assumed Zee.

That was why the request came directly to Vesper instead of through ordinary channels.

Lucien read the letter once.

Then handed it to Zee.

Zee smiled.

“Someone is using my reputation.”

Rez leaned over.

“Or feeding badly.”

Ner’s eyes sharpened.

“Either way, if they think Zee did it, this spreads.”

Lucien looked toward Zee.

“What do you want to do?”

Not what should we do.

Zee noticed.

“Hunt.”

Simple.

Lucien nodded.

“Take Rez.”

Rez frowned.

“I am standing here.”

“Yes.”

“Ner stays.”

Ner immediately said, “No.”

Lucien looked at him.

“You died four days ago.”

“I got better.”

Everyone stared.

Ner closed his eyes.

“That sounded terrible.”

Zee almost laughed.

Lucien continued.

“You do not understand your new condition.”

“Neither does anyone else.”

“Exactly.”

Ner folded his arms.

“The drained bodies may tell me something.”

Lucien looked toward Zee.

Zee did not answer for Ner.

Good.

Lucien saw.

Then:

“Fine.”

Zareth’s roar sounded outside.

Rez smiled.

“And apparently four.”

The core moved again.

Not because grief was gone.

Because the world was still moving.

⸻

The eastern cliff settlement existed beneath enormous stone shelves where sunlight reached only for several hours each day.

Mostly Vampires lived there.

Some humans by choice.

Several witches.

No crown.

No House.

The settlement leader met them at the first bridge.

She was Vampire.

Older.

Scar over one eye.

Her attention went immediately to Zee’s mouth.

“You fed recently.”

Zee smelled her bloodline.

Old.

Not ancient.

Strong.

“Yes.”

Her expression tightened.

“On whom?”

Rez groaned.

Zee smiled.

“An attacker.”

“Alive?”

“No.”

The leader stared.

Zee did not apologize.

“I am not here for that.”

She led them to the bodies.

Three.

Two Vampires.

One human.

Completely drained.

Zee crouched.

Assassin observation first.

No defensive wounds.

No struggle.

Wolf scent second.

One unknown predator.

Vampire blood analysis next.

Wrong feeding pattern.

The wounds were tiny.

Too clean.

Ner approached.

His face changed.

“Blood is still here.”

The leader stared.

“They are empty.”

“I know.”

Ner looked confused.

“So why can I hear it?”

Zee stood.

“What do you hear?”

“A resonance.”

He followed it.

Not in the corpses.

Into the stone wall.

Rez drew his sword.

“Trap?”

“Maybe.”

Zee smiled.

Rez looked at him.

“Stop enjoying that word.”

“No.”

They followed the resonance into a cavern.

There they found the killer.

Not Vampire.

Not creature Zee recognized.

Humanoid.

Pale gray skin.

Mouth sealed by scar tissue.

Its chest pulsed with stolen blood visible beneath translucent ribs.

The thing saw them.

Ran.

Zee smiled.

Now the hunt made sense.

He disappeared.

Assassin Zee.

Vampire speed kept hidden until needed.

Wolf instinct tracked across branching tunnels.

The creature doubled back.

Countertrack.

Zee recognized it.

Changed route.

It climbed the ceiling.

Zee smelled the blood overhead.

Knife thrown.

Not to kill.

To force movement.

The creature dropped exactly where Rez had positioned himself.

Blue-White Mana slammed it sideways.

Ner sealed the opposite tunnel in Gold.

Zareth waited outside because the cavern could not hold him safely.

Not optional.

His Link supplied aerial awareness of secondary exits cut through the cliff.

One escape route remained.

Zee knew.

The creature took it.

Exactly as planned.

It emerged outside.

Zareth’s claw dropped in front of it.

Not striking.

Blocking.

The predator stopped.

Four roles.

One hunt.

Zee stepped from the tunnel silently.

The creature turned.

No mouth.

No speech.

No surrender.

It attacked.

Fast.

Stronger than it looked.

Its fingers opened into hollow needles.

Zee caught one wrist.

Blood inside the creature surged toward the arm.

Its strength increased.

Interesting.

It attacked again.

Zee let a needle pierce his shoulder.

Rez shouted.

Zee ignored him.

He wanted to feel what it did.

The needle pulled blood.

Zee’s Vampire nature reacted with fury.

His Wolf nature identified the feeding direction.

Crimson adaptation remembered the six killers.

His blood thickened around the needle.

Not physically enough to clot.

Supernaturally.

The creature tried drawing.

Failed.

Zee smiled.

“There.”

New possibility.

Not immunity.

Resistance to forced feeding.

The creature looked almost afraid.

Zee enjoyed that.

Then Ner spoke.

“Stop.”

One word.

Bloodsong resonance struck the creature.

Its stolen blood stopped moving.

The predator froze.

Ner froze too.

He had not expected that.

Zee looked toward him.

Ner stared at the creature.

“I can hear the blood it stole.”

The thing struggled.

Ner’s voice lowered.

“Release it.”

The blood inside its chest convulsed.

Not obeying Ner exactly.

Responding.

The creature screamed without a mouth.

Blood began leaking from old wounds in its torso.

Zee’s eyes widened.

“Ner.”

“I know.”

Ner immediately stopped.

The effect ended.

He breathed hard.

Rez looked between them.

“New rule.”

Ner nodded.

“Very new.”

The creature attacked again.

No intelligence left in the decision.

Only hunger.

Zee moved.

Knife beneath the ribs.

Twist.

Pressure point near the shoulder.

Vampire strength only at the exact instant needed.

The creature collapsed.

Still alive.

Zee crouched.

He could kill it.

Wanted to.

Not because it had attacked him.

Because it had drained three people.

He could smell them inside it.

Three stolen lives.

His fangs descended.

Rez looked at him.

No moral lecture.

Just:

“Do we need answers?”

Zee considered.

No obvious language.

No known species.

Ner looked toward the body.

“I might be able to learn something from the blood.”

That changed it.

Zee did not feed.

Not yet.

“Alive, then.”

The creature thrashed.

Zee broke both arms.

Efficient.

Necessary to contain.

Mostly.

He enjoyed the second break more than he needed to.

Noted.

No guilt.

The settlement leader arrived minutes later.

She stared at the captured predator.

“What is it?”

Zee smiled faintly.

“I do not know.”

Ner laughed.

Rez shook his head.

Some habits survived everything.

The important part came later.

Ner placed one hand above the creature’s chest.

Pure Golden Mana formed a boundary around the stolen blood.

Then he hummed.

One low note.

The blood answered.

Not leaving.

Showing.

Three separate resonance patterns.

Three victims.

Then a fourth.

Older.

Not from the bodies they found.

Ner opened his eyes.

“There was another.”

The settlement leader went pale.

“A missing girl.”

“How long?”

“Six days.”

Ner looked toward the tunnels.

“She may still be alive.”

Zee’s darkness shifted immediately.

Rescue first.

Predator later.

Good.

He stood.

“Where was she last seen?”

The hunt continued.

Not prolonged.

Not another arc.

They found the girl in a side cavern before nightfall.

Weak.

Alive.

The creature had been storing victims and feeding slowly.

Zee returned to the captive afterward.

No need for answers now.

No surrender.

No innocence.

He crouched.

The predator sensed what was coming.

Fear.

Zee smiled.

Not ashamed.

Fangs descended.

He fed.

Its blood was unlike anything he had tasted.

Metallic.

Thin.

Carrying echoes of other blood without becoming them.

Zee’s Crimson nature reacted.

Not copied needles.

Not stolen storage organs.

Instead his own blood sense widened.

For a moment, he could distinguish blood that belonged naturally inside a body from blood taken from elsewhere.

A new perception.

Crimson.

His.

Useful.

He drained the predator completely.

Then stood.

The settlement leader watched.

“You enjoyed that.”

Zee wiped blood from his mouth.

“Yes.”

She stared.

Waiting for apology.

None came.

Zee looked toward the rescued girl being carried home.

“He enjoyed them.”

The leader said nothing.

Zee’s expression remained calm.

“So I enjoyed him.”

That story spread before they even left the eastern cliffs.

By the time Zee returned to Vesper, people were already calling him something new.

Not prince.

Not Tribrid.

Not monster.

The Blood Hunter.

Rez hated the name.

Ner thought it sounded dramatic.

Zareth’s Link found it amusing.

Zee?

Zee decided he did not care whether it was accurate.

Only whether predators believed it.

And when the first feral nest abandoned the Vesper road before Zee ever arrived—

he learned reputation could hunt ahead of him.
