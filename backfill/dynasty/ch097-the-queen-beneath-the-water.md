Chapter Ninety-Seven

The Queen Beneath the Water

Her name was Nymeris Thalryn.

She did not offer it immediately.

First she ordered Zee away from Kharos’s corpse.

Zee looked at her.

“No.”

Nymeris looked at him.

“That was not a request.”

“That is unfortunate.”

“You are standing beside a divine rupture connected to every underground current beneath this mountain.”

Zee glanced downward.

“Still sounds like a request.”

Ner stood several feet away, enjoying this far too much.

Nymeris noticed.

“You find this entertaining?”

“Extremely.”

“Why?”

Ner smiled.

“People usually need longer before ordering him around.”

Zee looked at him.

“Whose side are you on?”

“Mine.”

Fair.

Nymeris stepped closer to the dead God.

The river responded.

Water curled around her wrist.

Not Bloodsong.

Not ordinary Witchcraft.

Older.

Oceanic.

Pressure moved through it.

Zee felt something beneath the surface.

Abyss.

Depth.

The sensation of water so deep sunlight had never reached it.

Nymeris touched two fingers to Kharos’s blood.

The river convulsed.

Her expression hardened.

“You killed the holder of a Tempest Throne beside a watershed connected to the deep currents.”

Zee leaned against a broken stone because his body had finally started objecting to the day.

“And?”

“And divine authority does not simply vanish politely.”

“What did it do?”

“It fell.”

Zee frowned.

“Into what?”

“The water.”

That got his attention.

Nymeris looked toward the river.

“Kharos’s death created a pressure fracture through the current network. My eastern boundary felt it before he finished dying.”

“Your boundary?”

Now she finally looked directly at him.

“I am Queen Nymeris Thalryn.”

The water behind her rose higher.

“Reigning Queen of Nhalaris.”

Ner’s expression changed.

Zee’s did not.

He had never heard the name.

Nymeris noticed.

“Of course surface kings forgot.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“You hid underwater.”

“We survived underwater.”

“Same result.”

“No.”

The answer carried enough authority that even Zee stopped smiling for half a second.

Good.

He liked her better already.

⸻

Nhalaris was older than several kingdoms Zee knew.

That was all Nymeris volunteered.

Not where it stood beneath the ocean.

Not how many people lived there.

Not how its armies moved.

Only enough.

An ancient underwater civilization.

Its own laws.

Its own military.

Its own magic.

Its own throne.

Its royal bloodline had carried a relationship with the Abyss longer than surface records remembered.

Siren blood lived inside that inheritance.

Old.

Hereditary.

Royal.

Not every bearer expressed it the same way.

Nymeris did.

But not like Ner.

Her voice did not command blood.

It affected water.

Pressure.

Resonance through deep currents.

The ocean heard her differently than people did.

Zee studied her.

She caught him.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You are staring.”

“I am looking.”

“That is what staring is.”

“No.”

Ner laughed again.

Nymeris looked toward him.

Then stopped.

Something passed through the air.

Small.

Almost inaudible.

Ner’s Bloodsong answered automatically.

One low resonance beneath his heartbeat.

Nymeris’s eyes sharpened.

Ner’s smile vanished.

“There is something in your voice.”

Nymeris studied him.

“There is something impossible in yours.”

Zee looked between them.

Ner stepped closer.

Not threatened.

Curious.

“Siren?”

Nymeris’s expression changed by only a fraction.

“Partly.”

Ner’s Gold moved faintly around his fingers.

“My mother’s line.”

Nymeris watched the resonance around him.

“That is not ordinary Siren blood.”

“No.”

“What happened to you?”

Ner glanced at Zee.

Nymeris followed the look.

Then closed her eyes briefly.

“Of course.”

Zee frowned.

“What does that mean?”

“It means I have known you less than an hour and you have already become responsible for two separate problems.”

“Only two?”

Nymeris stared.

Ner laughed again.

Good.

⸻

Then Nymeris noticed Zee’s wounds properly.

Her humor disappeared.

“What did that?”

“Godslayer.”

“And where is he?”

“Dead.”

She looked toward the ruined shrine above them.

Then at Kharos.

“And that?”

“Also dead.”

Nymeris stared at him.

Zee waited.

Finally:

“What?”

“You killed both today?”

“Yes.”

“You killed a Godslayer before sunrise.”

“Yes.”

“Then fought a true God while those wounds were still open.”

“Yes.”

“And killed him before sunset.”

“Yes.”

Nymeris looked at Ner.

“Is he always like this?”

Ner considered.

“Unfortunately.”

Zee looked offended.

Nymeris looked back at the black wounds.

“Dark Divinity.”

Zee’s eyes sharpened.

“You know it.”

“I know of it.”

“Godslayers?”

“Yes.”

“How many?”

Nymeris smiled faintly.

“Surface kings really do believe nothing exists until they personally meet it.”

Zee stood straighter.

“That was unnecessarily insulting.”

“It was accurate.”

“Worse.”

Nymeris’s gaze returned to the wounds.

“You are resisting it.”

“A little.”

“You did not absorb it.”

“No.”

“Good.”

Zee frowned.

“Everyone keeps saying that.”

“Because attempting to hold Dark Divinity without being chosen by it would be an excellent way to destroy yourself.”

“I was not planning to.”

Nymeris looked at the dead God.

“You drank him.”

“That is different.”

“How?”

“I wanted his blood.”

She stared again.

Ner stepped between the conversation before it became circular.

“He does not become what he drinks.”

Nymeris looked at him.

“No?”

“No.”

“He changes around pressure.”

Nymeris returned her attention to Zee.

That interest was different.

Not awe.

Assessment.

“Everything that tries to kill you teaches you how to survive it.”

“Usually.”

“That sounds exhausting.”

Zee paused.

Most people called it terrifying.

Monster.

Impossible.

Weapon.

Nymeris called it exhausting.

He looked at her more carefully.

She noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You did it again.”

“Still looking.”

She rolled her eyes.

⸻

Zareth arrived before full dark.

Human form this time.

He walked into the valley, saw Nymeris standing beside Zee, and immediately opened the Link.

Dangerous.

Zee answered mentally.

I noticed.

Zareth looked at him.

You smiled.

I smile around dangerous people.

That is the problem.

Nymeris looked toward Zareth.

“You are Drakonis.”

Zareth stopped.

“Crown Prince Zareth Drakonis.”

“I know the blood.”

Zareth’s eyes narrowed.

“You know Dragon blood?”

“My people know what reaches the deep.”

“Dragons do not live underwater.”

“No.”

Nymeris’s expression remained calm.

“But dead ones sink.”

Silence.

Zareth stared.

Zee looked at her.

“That was dark.”

Nymeris glanced toward him.

“You killed two immortal threats before dinner.”

“Fair.”

Zareth privately sent:

I like her less now.

Zee answered:

You like her more.

A pause.

Unfortunately.

⸻

The real problem surfaced after sunset.

The river split.

Not physically.

Magically.

One current flowed downstream.

Another pulled backward beneath it.

Nymeris knelt beside the water.

Her face changed.

Queen now.

No irritation.

No jokes.

Responsibility.

“My eastern ward is weakening.”

Zee crouched beside her despite the pain in his ribs.

“What happens if it breaks?”

“Pressure from the Abyss enters channels not built to hold it.”

“Meaning?”

“Villages beneath the shelf collapse.”

“How many people?”

Nymeris looked at him.

“Eleven thousand under the eastern reach.”

That number changed Zee instantly.

Not because they were his.

Because he understood the look on her face.

Calculation.

Distance.

Time.

People.

A ruler trying to decide which disaster could be allowed because not all of them could be stopped.

He knew that expression.

She saw recognition in him.

Not sympathy.

Recognition.

“You understand.”

“Yes.”

Nymeris looked back toward the water.

“Most kings ask what happens to their trade.”

“I hate trade meetings.”

“That may be the first sensible thing you have said.”

Zee almost smiled.

Then pain tore through his side.

He hid it badly.

Nymeris noticed.

“You are injured.”

“I remain aware.”

“And you are considering helping.”

“Yes.”

“You cannot even stand properly.”

“I am standing.”

“You are leaning on a rock.”

“The rock is supportive.”

Ner turned away because he was laughing.

Nymeris stared at Zee for a long moment.

Then something changed.

Not affection.

Respect.

Small.

Earned.

“You do not know my people.”

“No.”

“You have never seen Nhalaris.”

“No.”

“You owe us nothing.”

Zee looked toward the unnatural current.

“I killed the God.”

“Yes.”

“That made the fracture.”

“Yes.”

“Then part of it is mine.”

Nymeris studied him.

That answer mattered.

Not because Zee took blame for everything.

Because he took responsibility for what was actually his.

She understood that too.

⸻

They worked through the night.

Not to repair the entire oceanic boundary.

Impossible.

Only to stop the fracture from widening.

Nymeris controlled the water.

Ner used Bloodsong resonance to identify where living currents carried the divine disturbance.

Zee used Witchcraft to break divine residue from the stone without touching Nymeris’s Abyssal magic directly.

Zareth carried collapsed boulders from the river channel in human form until he became irritated and shifted into true Dragon form to move the rest.

By midnight, the reverse current weakened.

Nymeris stood waist-deep in the river.

Her voice changed.

Not louder.

Deeper.

The water responded like something remembering its Queen.

Ancient Siren resonance moved through the current.

Not compulsion.

Not Bloodsong.

A royal call through water itself.

The fracture closed.

Mostly.

Nymeris emerged from the river.

For the first time, she looked tired.

Zee noticed.

Did not comment.

Queens disliked obvious observations as much as kings did.

Good.

⸻

By morning, the rumors had already started.

Cassel Rhune.

True Godslayer.

Dead.

Kharos.

True God.

Dead.

Same day.

Killed by the same man.

Surface reports disagreed on everything else.

Some claimed Zee had stolen Dark Divinity.

False.

Some claimed he had become a god.

False.

Some claimed he had killed Kharos with Cassel’s weapon.

False.

One report called him:

THE MAN WHO KILLED GODS.

Zee read it and frowned.

“That is dramatic.”

Zareth looked over his shoulder.

“You say that like you are not.”

“I am tasteful.”

Ner laughed.

Nymeris looked genuinely confused.

“Do surface people always name everything?”

“Yes,” Zee said.

“No,” Ner said simultaneously.

Nymeris looked between them.

“I see.”

Zee burned the report.

⸻

Nymeris prepared to return to Nhalaris before noon.

The river rose around her again.

Zee stood nearby.

Still wounded.

Still bleeding slightly beneath fresh bandages.

She looked at him.

“You owe my kingdom a visit.”

Zee stared.

“I do not do underwater.”

“You will.”

“That sounded like an order.”

“It was.”

Zee smiled.

Nymeris did not.

Then almost did.

Almost.

Ner noticed.

Zareth noticed.

Zee did not care.

There was nothing romantic in it.

Something stranger had begun.

Two rulers who understood responsibility.

Two people accustomed to being obeyed.

Two kingdoms that had existed without needing each other.

Until one dead God connected them through water.

Zee folded his arms.

“What happens if I refuse?”

Nymeris stepped backward into the rising river.

“I will come back.”

“That sounds threatening.”

“It is diplomatic.”

“Surface diplomacy is better.”

“No.”

She sank without splashing.

Then stopped.

Only her face remained above the impossible water.

“Ner.”

He looked at her.

“We should speak again.”

Bloodsong hummed faintly.

Ner nodded.

“I think so.”

Then Nymeris looked at Zee one last time.

“Try not to kill another god before I repair the damage from this one.”

Zee considered.

“No promises.”

Her eyes narrowed.

“King Darkheart.”

“Queen Thalryn.”

Then she vanished beneath the water.

The river fell normally.

Silence returned.

Zareth looked at Zee.

“She is going to become a problem.”

Zee smiled.

“Probably.”

Ner stepped beside him.

“You like her.”

“As a person.”

“I know.”

Zee looked at him.

“No jealousy?”

Ner stared.

“You are gay.”

“Correct.”

“And I have eyes.”

“Also correct.”

Ner smiled.

“Besides, I like her too.”

Zareth groaned.

“Excellent. Now there are three of you.”

Zee looked toward the river.

Somewhere far beneath oceans surface kingdoms barely understood, Queen Nymeris Thalryn ruled an ancient civilization tied to the Abyss and old Siren blood.

Zee knew almost nothing about her.

She knew almost nothing about him.

That was fine.

They had time.

A reason to meet again was enough.

Behind them lay the blood of a Godslayer.

Ahead of them lay the corpse of a God.

Before the same sun had set, Zee Darkheart had killed both.

And before the next sun fully rose, another kingdom had entered his life.

Not through conquest.

Not prophecy.

Not destiny.

Through consequence.

The world had become larger again.

And darker with it.
