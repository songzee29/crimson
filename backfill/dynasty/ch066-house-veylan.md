Chapter Sixty-Six

House Veylan

House Veylan expected soldiers.

Zee brought six people.

Himself.

Rez.

Ner.

Two Darkheart scouts.

And one former prisoner who knew the southern estate.

That was enough.

The Veylan manor sat behind white stone walls covered in blood wards.

Ner studied them from the tree line.

“Layered.”

Rez crouched beside him.

“Alarm?”

“Alarm. Binding. Identification.”

Zee looked toward the western wall.

“And the weakness?”

Ner smiled faintly.

“You assume there is one.”

“There is always one.”

“There are three.”

Better.

Ner pointed.

“The upper wards identify blood.”

“Lower wards identify magic.”

“The center line reacts to movement.”

Rez looked toward Zee.

“You can bypass all three?”

Zee’s eyes shifted into slits.

“Yes.”

“Without smashing the wall?”

Zee looked offended.

“I have learned subtlety.”

Ner coughed.

Rez smiled.

Zee disappeared.

⸻

The first guard never saw him.

Zee crossed the blood ward using presence suppression.

His Vampire heartbeat slowed.

Body heat dropped.

Crimson Mana pulled inward until the ward barely registered him.

When the movement line reacted, he froze.

Not magically.

Perfectly.

The pulse passed over him.

Nothing.

Then Wolf Zee took over.

Scent.

Oil.

Leather.

Three guards.

One hidden above.

Two below.

Zee moved between them silently.

Pressure point.

One down.

Knife handle.

Second.

The third turned.

Zee caught his mouth.

“Sleep.”

Witchcraft touched the nervous system with surgical precision.

The guard collapsed.

The fourth saw everything.

He reached for a bell.

Zee looked up.

“Don’t.”

Vampire compulsion struck before the man met his eyes.

The hand froze.

Zee climbed the wall.

“Open the gate.”

The guard resisted.

Strong blood ward.

Zee pressed.

“Open it.”

The man’s face tightened.

Then his hand moved.

The gate unlocked.

Rez entered first.

“Subtle.”

Zee smiled.

“Told you.”

Ner followed.

“Terrifying.”

“Also told you.”

⸻

House Veylan was not empty.

That was the problem.

Servants.

Children.

Guards.

Healers.

Stable workers.

People who had nothing to do with blood experiments.

Zee smelled them all.

His first instinct was not slaughter.

Good.

His second was to find the people who mattered.

Better.

Rez unfolded the seized monastery ledger.

“Three names.”

“Lord Veylan.”

“Lady Coris.”

“Master Alven.”

Ner’s face hardened.

“Alven signed the orders involving me.”

Zee looked toward him.

“Then he is yours to question.”

Ner nodded.

No argument.

They split.

Rez handled guards and evacuation.

Ner took the eastern archive with one scout.

Zee went upstairs.

Alone.

⸻

Lord Veylan was eating breakfast.

He looked up when Zee entered.

Did not panic.

Interesting.

“King Darkheart.”

Zee closed the door.

“Lord Veylan.”

The man glanced toward the window.

Two exits.

One hidden knife.

Poison ring.

Old knee injury.

Recent Vampire blood in his system.

Not enough to turn.

Enough to enhance stamina temporarily.

Zee noticed everything.

Veylan smiled.

“I wondered when you would come.”

“Then you should have left.”

“I considered it.”

“Why didn’t you?”

The man lifted his cup.

“Because kings who run lose everything.”

Zee’s expression barely changed.

“Kings who experiment on captives lose more.”

Veylan set the cup down.

“So that is what Maera told you.”

“She did not get much time.”

The smile weakened.

Good.

Veylan stood.

“We studied a phenomenon.”

“Ner is not a phenomenon.”

“No.”

Veylan looked toward the door.

“He is proof.”

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“Careful.”

Veylan smiled again.

“There it is.”

“What?”

“The creature underneath the crown.”

Zee crossed the room without Vampire speed.

Slow.

Predatory.

“You funded seventeen deaths.”

Veylan’s expression cooled.

“Collateral.”

Zee stopped.

“That word usually means someone else paid.”

Silence.

Then Veylan moved.

Fast.

Not Human fast.

Vampire blood enhancement.

He threw the poisoned ring-blade.

Zee flicked two fingers.

The weapon turned in the air.

Passed Veylan’s face.

Embedded in the wall.

Zee kept walking.

Veylan cast.

Not Witchcraft.

Blood sigils flashed beneath his sleeves.

Foreign magic.

Zee smelled Demon residue.

“Interesting.”

The binding struck.

Crimson-black chains wrapped Zee’s arms.

Veylan smiled.

“I prepared for you.”

Zee looked down.

The chains tightened.

Pain.

Suppression.

Demon residue.

Blood magic.

All things his body had met before.

Not this exact combination.

But enough.

Crimson Adaptation stirred.

His scales darkened.

Heat rose.

The chains began smoking.

Veylan’s smile vanished.

Zee closed both hands.

“Break.”

The entire structure shattered.

Veylan backed away.

Zee grabbed him by the throat.

One-handed.

Lifted him.

No rage.

No speech.

Just strength.

Veylan choked.

Zee looked into his eyes.

“Who else knows about Ner?”

The man resisted.

Blood ward again.

Zee’s Vampire dominance pressed harder.

“Who?”

Veylan’s nose bled.

“Three Houses.”

“Names.”

Resistance cracked.

“Veylan.”

“Ors.”

“Mereth.”

Zee’s grip tightened.

“Who ordered the monastery deaths?”

Veylan’s heartbeat changed.

Fear.

Truth approaching.

“I did.”

Good.

Zee smiled faintly.

Veylan saw.

Panicked.

“Wait.”

Zee’s fangs descended.

“You had your warning.”

He fed.

Human blood mixed with foreign Vampire blood and Demonic enhancers.

Strange.

Zee’s body separated them almost immediately.

Human nourishment.

Artificial Vampire enhancement.

Demon contamination.

Three layers.

His blood sense had become frightening.

He drank until Veylan weakened badly.

Then stopped.

Not mercy.

Information first.

Veylan gasped.

Zee leaned close.

“You are going to watch your House survive without you.”

The man stared.

“What?”

Zee snapped his neck.

Final.

⸻

Ner found Master Alven alive.

Barely.

Golden Mana held him against a wall while blood floated in thin red strands around the room.

Rez entered just after Zee.

“What happened?”

Ner’s voice stayed calm.

“He tried to use my blood against me.”

Alven coughed.

“I almost succeeded.”

Ner looked at him.

Then hummed.

One low note.

Every stolen sample in the room trembled.

Alven screamed.

Ner’s Bloodsong pulled the samples out of their containers and into a suspended ring.

Not into himself.

Around him.

Control.

Precision.

Pure Gold separated each drop.

Zee watched.

Proud.

Not because Ner needed him.

Because he did not.

Ner looked toward Alven.

“How many people?”

The man said nothing.

Ner’s eyes darkened red beneath Gold.

“Answer.”

Bloodsong pressed through his circulation.

Alven’s body locked.

“Forty-two.”

Rez went still.

“Experiments?”

“Yes.”

“Deaths?”

“Twenty-nine.”

Ner’s face changed.

No softness.

“Why?”

“To reproduce you.”

Silence.

Zee’s darkness moved.

Ner saw.

Raised one hand.

Not to stop Zee.

To claim his own choice.

“This one is mine.”

Zee nodded.

Alven laughed weakly.

“You think killing me proves—”

Ner whispered:

“Quiet.”

His blood obeyed before his mouth did.

Silence.

Ner stepped closer.

“I am not killing you because I am unique.”

His Gold tightened.

“I am killing you because twenty-nine people died so you could satisfy curiosity.”

Alven’s eyes widened.

Ner’s voice dropped lower.

“Still.”

His heart stopped.

One command.

One Bloodsong strike.

No spectacle.

Ner released him.

The body fell.

Zee looked at him.

“You all right?”

“No.”

Good answer.

Ner looked toward Zee.

“But I do not regret it.”

Zee understood.

That mattered more.

⸻

They did not destroy House Veylan.

That surprised everyone.

Rez found the servants first.

Seventy-three people.

Only eleven had known what the ruling family funded.

Four had objected.

Two had secretly helped prisoners escape.

Zee heard all of it.

He did not punish bloodline.

He punished choice.

Lady Coris confessed before Ner questioned her.

She had known.

Signed payments.

Never visited the monastery.

Still guilty.

Zee sentenced her to imprisonment.

Not death.

Rez looked at him afterward.

“Why?”

“Veylan confessed.”

“Alven enjoyed it.”

“Coris enabled it.”

Different lines.

Rez nodded.

Reasoning.

Not softness.

The estate changed hands by sunset.

Not to Zee personally.

To the surviving Veylan council under Darkheart oversight until the prisoners’ families were compensated and the research network dismantled.

People whispered anyway.

House Veylan had been taken in one day.

Its lord dead.

Its secrets exposed.

Its power broken.

King Zee Darkheart had entered with six people.

The story spread faster than the truth.

By nightfall, some versions claimed Zee killed everyone.

Others said he drank the entire ruling family.

One claimed he transformed into a crimson beast with six horns.

Zee hated that one.

Rez loved it.

Ner laughed for ten straight minutes.

Then a Darkheart messenger arrived.

Blood on his coat.

“Majesty.”

Zee turned.

“What happened?”

“Eastern wall.”

“Attack?”

The messenger shook his head.

“No.”

That was worse.

He handed Zee a black metal box.

No seal.

No mark.

Only one thing inside.

A severed Dragon scale.

Zareth’s.

Zee’s face emptied.

Then the Dragon Link struck.

Pain.

Not Zareth.

Someone else forcing pain through the bond.

The world had found a new direction.

Zee stood.

No panic.

No shouting.

Just one sentence.

“Find the route.”

And everyone in the room understood that House Veylan had just become yesterday’s problem.
