Chapter One Hundred Eight

The Child Beneath Gold

Ner remained dangerous.

Pregnancy did not change that.

The first assassin learned this personally.

Darkheart had not announced anything.

Only Zee and Ner knew.

Which meant the attack had nothing to do with the child.

That made it almost refreshing.

The assassin entered through a servant corridor at midnight.

Silver-coated blade.

Presence suppression.

Breath controlled.

Professional.

He reached the bedroom door.

Ner opened it before he knocked.

The assassin froze.

Ner looked him over.

“You are bad at this.”

The man stabbed.

Ner caught his wrist.

Pure Gold Mana flared.

Bloodsong hit the assassin’s heartbeat.

One note.

The man collapsed.

Zee appeared behind him half a second later.

Barefoot.

Angry.

Ner looked over.

“You are slow.”

“I was asleep.”

“That sounds like an excuse.”

Zee looked at the unconscious assassin.

Then at Ner.

Then at Ner’s abdomen.

Ner’s eyes narrowed.

“Do not.”

“I said nothing.”

“You were about to.”

“I was checking.”

“You have checked forty-seven times today.”

“Forty-six.”

Ner stared.

Zee listened.

Two heartbeats.

Fine.

He relaxed.

Ner sighed.

“You are intolerable.”

Zee smiled.

“And yet.”

“Yes.”

The assassin groaned.

Ner looked down.

“Oh, right.”

Zee crouched.

“Who sent you?”

The man spat blood.

“Go to hell.”

Zee smiled.

“Wrong answer.”

Ner folded his arms.

“Please do not kill him in the bedroom.”

“Why?”

“I sleep here.”

Fair.

They dragged him elsewhere.

⸻

The first month passed quietly.

Mostly.

The second did not.

Ner’s Pure Gold Mana began changing around the pregnancy.

Not color.

Never color.

Gold remained Gold.

Pure.

Bright.

Unmixed.

But its behavior shifted.

Whenever Ner cast, a second pattern formed beneath his own.

Not a spell.

An echo.

Zee noticed immediately.

Ner noticed because Zee would not stop staring.

“You are doing it again.”

“You have two mana rhythms.”

Ner frowned.

“No, I do not.”

“Cast.”

Ner lifted one hand.

Pure Gold light formed.

Under it—

for less than a second—

a darker Crimson shimmer moved.

Then vanished.

Ner stared.

Zee did too.

“Mine?”

“Partly.”

“Partly?”

Zee stepped closer.

His Dragon blood warmed.

The unborn child responded.

Not with Dragonfire.

Not with scales.

Only heat.

Ner’s hand snapped to his stomach.

“There.”

Zee felt it too.

Warmth.

Small.

Localized.

Then gone.

Ner looked at Zee.

“Dragon.”

“Maybe.”

“You said maybe like you are lying.”

“I am not certain.”

Ner smiled.

“You hate uncertainty.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Zee frowned.

“Why good?”

“Because otherwise you become unbearable.”

“You keep saying that as if it is new.”

⸻

By the third month, the moon started affecting the heartbeat.

Not dramatically.

No transformation.

No awakening.

But on the full moon, Zayrlen’s rhythm changed.

Faster.

Stronger.

Ner woke Zee in the middle of the night.

“Listen.”

Zee was already listening.

Of course.

The smaller heartbeat carried something different.

Wild.

Not dangerous.

Instinctive.

Wolf.

Zee went completely still.

Ner looked at him.

“You know that one.”

“Yes.”

Zee’s own Wolf nature answered from somewhere deep.

Not dominance.

Recognition.

His pupils narrowed.

For one second, he could almost scent the child despite there being no ordinary scent to follow.

Then Crimson and Gold energy rippled faintly beneath Ner’s skin.

Both men saw it.

Gold first.

Crimson second.

Together.

Ner stared.

“That is new.”

Zee touched the place carefully.

The energy disappeared.

Then the heartbeat slowed.

Normal again.

Ner looked toward him.

“Wolf.”

“Probably.”

“Dragon.”

“Probably.”

“Witch.”

“Yes.”

Ner’s expression shifted.

“Not Vampire?”

Zee listened deeper.

Nothing predatory answered blood.

No feeding response.

No deadened pulse.

No Vampire rhythm.

He shook his head.

“Not that I can feel.”

Ner placed a hand over his stomach.

“So he is already different from you.”

Zee smiled.

“Good.”

That answer surprised Ner.

Zee saw it.

“What?”

“I expected you to become unbearable about him inheriting all of you.”

“He does not need all of me.”

The words came easily.

“He needs enough of himself.”

Ner smiled.

“That was almost wise.”

“Do not ruin it.”

⸻

They told Zareth first.

Mostly because he discovered it.

Zee had intended to wait another month.

Then Zareth arrived in Darkheart unannounced, walked into the private council room, looked at Ner once, and stopped.

Dragon senses.

Too sharp.

His head tilted.

Then his eyes widened.

“No.”

Ner sighed.

Zee closed his eyes.

Zareth pointed at him.

“No.”

Zee looked up.

“What?”

“You.”

Then at Ner.

“And you.”

Ner smiled.

Zareth stared between them.

“You made a child.”

Zee said nothing.

Zareth’s mouth opened.

Then closed.

Then opened again.

Through the Dragon Link came pure disbelief.

YOU MADE A CHILD.

Zee answered mentally.

Apparently.

APPARENTLY?

Ner winced.

“Can you two stop screaming silently?”

Zareth looked offended.

“I am becoming an uncle.”

“Yes.”

He stared at Ner’s stomach.

Then at Zee.

“Does Rez know?”

“No.”

Zareth smiled.

“Oh, this is excellent.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“No.”

“I am telling him.”

“No.”

“You cannot stop me.”

Zee smiled.

“Silence.”

Magical stillness dropped over the room.

Zareth stared.

Then pointed at Zee without speaking.

Ner started laughing.

Zee released the effect.

Zareth took a breath.

“That was abusive.”

“No.”

“I am still telling Rez.”

“You are not.”

“Then tell him yourself.”

Fair.

⸻

Rez reacted exactly as expected.

Badly.

He arrived from Vesper two days later.

Walked into Zee’s study.

Looked at Ner.

Looked at Zee.

Then punched Zee in the shoulder.

Zee blinked.

“What was that for?”

“You made me learn from Zareth that you are having another child.”

“I was going to tell you.”

“When?”

“Soon.”

“You are terrible at family announcements.”

Ner raised a hand.

“I agree.”

Zee looked betrayed.

Rez ignored him and went directly to Ner.

“How are you?”

Ner smiled.

“Fine.”

“Really?”

“Yes.”

“Is he unbearable?”

“Completely.”

Rez looked toward Zee.

“Expected.”

Then he crouched slightly.

Not touching.

“Can he hear me?”

Zee answered.

“Probably not.”

Rez frowned.

“That was not for you.”

Ner laughed.

Rez looked toward Ner’s stomach anyway.

“Hello, tiny future problem.”

Zee stared.

Rez continued.

“I am your Uncle Rez.”

Ner looked toward Zee.

“That sounds familiar.”

Zee’s expression changed for half a heartbeat.

Aidan.

Then gone.

Not forgotten.

Just not allowed to own this moment.

Rez noticed.

Said nothing.

Good brother.

⸻

Kael learned next.

Then Tessa.

Then Amara.

Not through announcement.

Through trust.

Tessa immediately began medical preparation.

Ner objected.

“I am not sick.”

“You are pregnant.”

“Yes.”

“With Zee’s child.”

Ner stared.

Tessa crossed her arms.

“That is clinically concerning.”

Kael laughed so hard he had to sit down.

Amara sniffed the air around Ner once.

Then frowned.

“Wolf.”

Zee looked toward her.

“You feel it too?”

“Yes.”

Her expression softened.

“Strong.”

Ner placed a hand over his abdomen.

“Good strong?”

Amara smiled.

“Very.”

Then she looked at Zee.

“Do not smother him.”

“I do not smother.”

Everyone in the room laughed.

Zee considered leaving his own kingdom.

⸻

Darkheart did not learn yet.

That decision belonged to Zee and Ner.

The world continued moving.

That mattered.

A border settlement near Rell reported disappearing livestock.

Then two patrols vanished.

Not enemy soldiers.

Something else.

Zee went personally.

Ner wanted to come.

“No.”

Ner stared at him.

Zee realized the mistake immediately.

“No?”

He corrected.

“I would prefer you stay.”

“Why?”

“You are carrying our child.”

“I am still Witch.”

“Yes.”

“Still Vampire.”

“Yes.”

“Still Bloodsong.”

“Yes.”

“Still capable of killing you if you become irritating enough.”

Zee smiled.

“That one is debatable.”

Ner stepped closer.

“I am coming.”

Zee looked at him.

Protective instinct screamed.

Wolf wanted him behind walls.

Dragon blood wanted him nowhere near danger.

Vampire senses wanted every threat dead before Ner reached the road.

Crimson Sovereign synchronization carried all of that at once.

For one second, the pressure around Zee deepened.

Then he controlled it.

Choice.

Not command.

Not ownership.

He exhaled.

“Fine.”

Ner smiled.

“Good.”

“You stay behind the first line.”

Ner raised an eyebrow.

“Negotiable.”

“Ner.”

“Fine.”

Progress.

⸻

The missing patrols were found alive.

Barely.

Something had built a nest beneath an abandoned quarry.

Not Demon.

Not Vampire.

Not any creature Darkheart knew.

Pale armor.

Four arms.

Bone jaws.

Territorial.

Three of them.

Zee entered first.

Ner behind him.

Kael and Amara flanking.

The first creature attacked.

Zee moved before thought.

Wolf instinct read the lunge.

Vampire speed shifted him aside.

Witchcraft severed the magical shell around its throat.

One motion.

Effortless.

Crimson Sovereign state no longer felt new.

It felt natural.

The creature hit stone.

Zee broke its neck.

Ner watched.

“You are smoother.”

Zee looked over.

“What?”

“Your three sides.”

Another creature lunged.

Ner turned.

Gold flashed.

Bloodsong found no ordinary heartbeat, but it caught internal fluid rhythm instead.

Enough to destabilize balance.

Amara tore its leg out.

Kael burned through the exposed joint.

Zee finished it.

No pause.

No separate mode.

All three natures simply moved with him.

Improving.

Naturally.

Exactly as they should.

Then the third creature struck Ner.

It never reached him.

Zee crossed the distance so violently the ground cracked.

Caught it by the head.

Crimson-black scales spread up his arms.

Dragon force entered the grip.

He crushed the skull.

Silence.

Ner stared.

Zee realized everyone else was staring too.

Kael looked at the dead creature.

Then at Zee.

“That one felt personal.”

Zee looked toward Ner.

“Yes.”

Ner sighed.

“I was fine.”

“It almost touched you.”

“I noticed.”

Zee stepped closer.

Listened.

Two heartbeats.

Still there.

Strong.

Ner’s irritation softened.

Only slightly.

“You checked again.”

“Yes.”

“Hopeless.”

“Yes.”

Ner took Zee’s hand and placed it where the smaller heartbeat rested.

“Then at least do it properly.”

Zee went quiet.

The world around them smelled of blood, dirt, dead monsters, and broken stone.

Darkheart soldiers were freeing the missing patrols.

Amara was already shouting for stretchers.

Kael was complaining about his coat.

Nothing had stopped.

War.

Monsters.

Borders.

Kingdom.

Family.

All existing together.

Zee listened.

Ner.

Then beneath him—

Zayrlen.

Alive.

Growing.

Crimson and Gold flickered once beneath Ner’s skin.

Zee’s Dragon blood answered with warmth.

His Wolf instinct settled.

His Witch senses recognized the shape of developing magic.

His Vampire ears held both heartbeats.

For the first time, Zee allowed himself to believe this child might truly grow up in Darkheart.

Openly.

With both fathers.

No erased records.

No hidden kingdom.

No pretending.

Ner looked at him.

“You are doing the thing.”

“What thing?”

“Thinking too much.”

Zee smiled.

“Probably.”

Ner leaned into him.

“Then think about this.”

Zee waited.

“Our son is fine.”

He listened.

Two heartbeats.

Yes.

For now, that was enough.
