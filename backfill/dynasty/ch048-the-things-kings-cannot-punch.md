Chapter Forty-Eight

The Things Kings Cannot Punch

Vesper smelled like bread again.

Zee noticed before the western gates fully opened.

Warm grain.

Yeast.

Smoke from the lower bakeries.

Something sweet from the market district.

For almost two weeks the city had smelled different.

Less food.

More rationing.

More boiled roots.

More people pretending reduced meals did not matter because House Vesper needed them to believe everything remained under control.

Now wagons rolled through three entrances carrying grain from the western covens.

Children followed them.

Adults tried pretending they were not doing the same.

Rez saw.

His expression changed.

Only slightly.

Pride looked strange on him when it had nothing to do with winning a sword fight.

Zee walked beside him.

“You did that.”

Rez looked over.

“We did.”

“No.”

Zee nodded toward the grain wagons.

“That part was you.”

Rez frowned.

“Ner found the western supply.”

“Ner found the lie.”

“You changed the routes.”

“Lucien approved them.”

“You decided first.”

Rez looked toward the city again.

Zee smiled.

“Just accept it.”

“I am accepting it.”

“You look uncomfortable.”

“I am uncomfortable because you are being sincere.”

Zee shoved him.

Rez laughed.

Ner walked several steps ahead carrying one of the recovered registry cases beneath his arm.

Zee’s eyes went toward him automatically.

Again.

This was becoming ridiculous.

Ner had soot still caught near one temple.

His coat had been cleaned badly after the registry fire.

One sleeve had a burn near the cuff.

He was arguing with Caldrin about whether Voss’s preservation jars should be destroyed immediately or kept as evidence until the Northern Council finished internal review.

Ner wanted them destroyed.

Caldrin wanted photographs, magical impressions, and documentation first.

They had been arguing for thirty minutes.

Neither had repeated an argument.

Zee had stopped understanding half of it after minute eleven.

He kept listening anyway.

Ner glanced back.

Caught Zee looking.

Again.

This time Zee did not turn away immediately.

That felt like progress.

Then Ner smiled.

Zee looked forward.

Progress ruined.

Rez whispered, “Embarrassing.”

Zee did not even bother turning.

“I will throw you into the canal.”

“You threaten that too often.”

“One day I will mean it.”

“You always mean it.”

Zareth landed beyond the palace courtyard.

His arrival scattered three pigeons, six guards, and one extremely offended horse.

Teal-and-Gold Mana rolled beneath enormous crimson wings before settling.

The Dragon Link struck Zee with immediate warmth.

Home.

Food.

Then an image of the northern registry on fire.

Zee frowned.

“You already know?”

Zareth lowered his head.

The Link delivered Drakonis couriers.

Rumor.

Northern traders.

A picture of Zee standing inside flames looking dramatic.

Zee stared.

“That did not happen.”

Ner walked up behind him.

“What?”

“Apparently someone in Drakonis says I walked through the entire burning registry while carrying six people.”

Rez smiled.

“Only six?”

Zee looked toward him.

“Do not encourage this.”

Zareth sent another image.

Zee breathing fire.

“No.”

Ner laughed.

“You should let that one spread.”

“I cannot breathe Dragonfire.”

“Yet.”

Zee looked at him.

Ner’s smile faded slightly.

Not because he was worried.

Because both of them knew the word mattered.

Yet.

Five years of Dragon development had given Zee more.

Horns.

Scales.

Durability.

Crimson Dragon energy.

Pressure beneath the shoulder blades.

Partial manifestations during extreme emotion.

But not completion.

Not wings.

Not full transformation.

Not the fourth awakening.

Zee had learned not to force that door.

Mostly.

Zareth nudged him with his snout.

The Link shifted.

Drakonis.

Problem.

Zee frowned.

“What problem?”

A richer impression followed.

Dragon council.

Merchants.

Vesper grain routes.

Northern complaints.

A map.

Three territories.

Rez’s attention sharpened.

“What?”

Zee looked toward him.

“Drakonis has traders demanding the northern suspension be punished.”

Rez sighed.

“I thought reopening the compact ended that.”

Zareth sent disagreement.

Zee translated.

“Apparently some Dragon merchants lost money when northern trade diverted.”

Rez rubbed his face.

“Of course they did.”

Ner looked toward Zareth.

“Do they want compensation?”

An image.

Gold.

A lot of it.

Rez groaned.

“Of course they do.”

Zee smiled.

“Kings cannot punch trade.”

Rez looked at him.

“That may be the wisest thing you have ever said.”

“I did not say it was fair.”

Aurelia’s voice came from behind them.

“You have been home less than three minutes.”

They turned.

She stood at the top of the palace stairs.

Theron beside her.

Seraphine leaned against one column.

Zee smiled immediately.

Aurelia noticed.

Her expression softened.

He climbed the stairs.

At fifteen he no longer ran into her arms every time he returned from a mission.

Usually.

Today he reached her and stopped.

Aurelia opened one arm.

Zee leaned into her.

No embarrassment.

No apology.

He had spent two days around politicians, burning archives, stolen Mana, and people trying to make moral certainty sound simple.

He wanted his mother.

So he took the comfort.

Aurelia kissed his temple beside one horn.

“You smell like smoke.”

“Registry.”

“I heard.”

“You always hear.”

“I am Queen.”

“That is not magic.”

“Sometimes it feels like it.”

Seraphine looked toward Zee.

“Did you kill anyone?”

Aurelia turned.

“Seraphine.”

“What?”

Zee answered.

“No.”

Seraphine nodded.

“Good.”

Then added:

“Disappointing.”

Theron laughed.

Aurelia stared at both siblings.

Zee smiled against her shoulder.

Family.

⸻

The first surprise arrived during dinner.

King Lucien did not attend.

That happened occasionally.

Not often.

Rez noticed before anyone else sat down.

“Where is he?”

A servant answered carefully.

“Private council, Your Highness.”

“With whom?”

The servant hesitated.

Rez looked up.

That was enough.

“With whom?”

“Representatives from House Darkheart.”

The room changed.

Zee stopped reaching for his cup.

Aurelia became completely still.

Theron’s expression hardened.

Seraphine stopped smiling.

Rez stood.

“When did they arrive?”

“An hour ago.”

“Why was I not informed?”

“The King ordered privacy.”

Rez looked toward Zee.

Zee was already standing.

Aurelia spoke.

“Sit.”

He looked at her.

“Victor sent people.”

“Yes.”

“I am not sitting.”

“You are not storming Lucien’s council chamber either.”

“I was not going to storm.”

Seraphine muttered, “He was going to storm.”

Theron nodded.

“Definitely.”

Zee glared.

Aurelia remained calm.

“Lucien will tell us when he is ready.”

Zee’s horns warmed.

Anger.

Fear.

Both.

He named them.

That helped nothing.

“Why now?”

No one knew.

That was worse.

House Darkheart had not disappeared from Zee’s life over the last five years.

Victor still ruled.

Still controlled what portions of the House could publicly say about his son.

Still rejected every attempt to place Zee near Darkheart succession rituals.

Still refused to acknowledge Zee’s expanding Dragon manifestations as anything but “corruption.”

He had not tried to kill Zee again.

Never.

That one act remained singular.

But fear had matured into policy.

Zee was permitted in Darkheart territory only under negotiated terms.

Aurelia’s authority remained contested whenever Victor wanted it contested.

Certain Darkheart nobles quietly supported Zee.

Others called him curse when they thought Vesper ears were absent.

Victor had built distance and called it order.

Zee had responded by living anyway.

But Victor rarely sent formal representatives to Vesper.

Not without reason.

Rez pushed his chair back in.

“I am going to my father.”

Aurelia nodded.

Different.

Rez belonged in that conversation.

Zee did not.

Not yet.

He hated knowing the difference.

Rez looked toward him.

“I will come back.”

Zee frowned.

“I did not ask.”

“You had the face.”

“Everyone needs to stop saying that.”

Rez left.

Ner sat beside Zee.

Not across.

Beside.

That did not help Zee think.

“Do you want to go after him?”

“Yes.”

“Will you?”

“No.”

Ner looked slightly surprised.

Zee frowned.

“Stop looking like that.”

“You are getting better.”

“I hate when people say that.”

“Why?”

“Because then I have to keep doing it.”

Ner smiled.

Zee looked down at his hands.

Crimson scales had appeared farther across his knuckles.

He breathed.

Named the feeling.

Waited.

They receded slightly.

Theron watched.

“You learned my breathing.”

“Some of it.”

“Badly.”

Zee looked toward him.

“You said I was improving.”

“I lied.”

Aurelia raised one eyebrow.

Theron corrected himself.

“I encouraged.”

Seraphine laughed.

The tension eased.

Then the dining room door opened.

Rez returned.

His face told them before he spoke.

Something had changed.

Zee stood.

“What?”

Rez looked toward Aurelia.

Then Zee.

“Victor issued a formal claim.”

Aurelia’s eyes hardened.

“For what?”

Rez answered.

“For Zee.”

Silence.

Not his throne.

Not land.

Not blood recognition.

Zee.

Rez placed a sealed document on the table.

“King Victor asserts that as Zee’s father, King of House Darkheart, and ruler of the kingdom where Zee was born, he retains sovereign authority over Zee until adulthood.”

Zee stared.

Ner’s face hardened.

Theron stood.

Seraphine said a word Aurelia usually disliked hearing at dinner.

Aurelia ignored it.

Rez continued.

“He is demanding Zee return to Castle Darkheart.”

“No,” Aurelia said.

Immediate.

Absolute.

Zee looked toward her.

Then the document.

“Why?”

Rez’s expression darkened.

“Because your reputation has become politically dangerous.”

Zee laughed once.

No humor.

“Now?”

“He cites the northern crisis.”

“The Veyrath resurgence.”

“Your intervention in foreign territory.”

“Reports of increasingly unstable Dragon manifestations.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“I am more controlled than I have ever been.”

“I know.”

“What else?”

Rez hesitated.

Zee saw.

“What?”

“Victor claims House Darkheart has a right to determine whether you remain heir.”

That hit differently.

Zee had never begged for Darkheart.

But the House was his.

Birth.

Blood.

Mother.

History.

Victor deciding whether Zee belonged to it felt like someone trying to cut a piece from him and call that law.

Theron looked toward Aurelia.

“He cannot compel Vesper.”

“No.”

Rez answered.

“But he can create a succession dispute.”

Aurelia picked up the document.

Read.

Her face became colder with every line.

“He wants control.”

Zee looked at her.

Not protection.

Control.

Familiar.

The old fear wearing legal clothing.

Zee’s Crimson Mana flickered.

The candles bent.

Ner touched his wrist.

Not stopping him.

Grounding.

Zee looked down at the contact.

Then at Ner.

The Mana settled.

Aurelia saw.

She folded the document.

“Lucien’s answer?”

Rez smiled faintly.

“He told the envoys Zee is not property.”

Zee looked up.

Good.

Rez continued.

“They replied that House Darkheart considers this an internal matter.”

Seraphine laughed without humor.

“Vesper has sheltered him for most of his life.”

Theron’s scales appeared faintly along one wrist.

“They lost the right to call him internal when Victor put a blade through him.”

Aurelia’s face changed.

Zee looked away.

That old wound never needed repeating.

Rez continued.

“There is one problem.”

Zee sighed.

“There is always one.”

“Victor did not ask Vesper to send you by force.”

“Then what?”

“He invoked a royal challenge of guardianship.”

Aurelia stared.

Old Witch Kingdom law.

Ancient.

Ugly.

Zee knew enough to recognize the phrase.

“If House Darkheart proves Vesper is unlawfully preventing access to its heir, Victor can bring the matter before the Witch Crowns.”

Ner frowned.

“And they decide?”

Rez nodded.

“Potentially.”

Zee looked toward Aurelia.

“What do you want?”

Her answer was immediate.

“You do not go.”

Zee nodded.

Then:

“What do I want?”

Aurelia stopped.

Important difference.

The table went silent.

Zee looked toward the document.

For fifteen years Victor had feared him from a distance.

Controlled him from a distance.

Called him monster through walls and law and title.

Zee had spent childhood running from people who came to decide his life.

At fifteen, he could choose whether to keep running.

“I want to answer him.”

Aurelia’s face tightened.

“Zee.”

“Not alone.”

“Not by fighting.”

Her shoulders eased a fraction.

Zee continued.

“I want to stand in front of him.”

That scared her more.

He saw.

“Mother.”

“You do not owe Victor confrontation.”

“I know.”

“You do not owe House Darkheart proof.”

“I know.”

“You do not have to return simply because he finally found a law that sounds respectable.”

“I know.”

Zee took the document from her hand.

Then looked at the royal seal.

“But he keeps deciding what I am from rooms I am not in.”

His crimson eyes hardened.

“I am tired of that.”

Ner watched him.

Rez too.

Aurelia looked at the boy she once carried away from Darkheart.

Except he was not that child anymore.

Not helpless.

Not safe.

Not hers to command simply because fear told her danger was coming.

That lesson was harder for mothers.

“I come with you,” she said.

Zee smiled faintly.

“I assumed.”

Theron stood.

“So do I.”

“No,” Aurelia and Zee said together.

Theron looked offended.

Seraphine smiled.

“Beautiful.”

Theron pointed at her.

“You are staying too.”

“I did not volunteer.”

“Good.”

Rez looked toward Lucien’s empty seat.

“I will represent Vesper.”

Zee frowned.

“You have a kingdom crisis.”

“I have a Crown Prince problem.”

“You are the Crown Prince.”

“Exactly.”

Ner stood.

“I am coming.”

Zee turned.

“No.”

Ner stared.

“What?”

Zee realized how that sounded.

He corrected himself.

“You do not have to.”

Ner’s expression softened.

“I know.”

Then:

“I am coming.”

Their eyes held.

Zee smiled.

“Fine.”

Above the palace, Zareth roared.

Everyone looked toward the ceiling.

The Dragon Link flooded Zee.

Darkheart.

Castle.

Zee.

Danger.

Then unmistakable resolve.

Zee laughed.

“Apparently Zareth is coming too.”

Aurelia closed her eyes.

Rez smiled.

“Of course.”

The childhood Zee had been dragged away from Castle Darkheart because adults knew he could not survive its fear alone.

At fifteen, he was choosing to return.

Not to reclaim a throne.

Not to kill Victor.

Not to prove the prophecy right.

To make one thing clear.

Victor Darkheart could fear his son.

He could reject him.

He could hate what he had become.

But he would no longer decide who Zee was without Zee standing there to answer.

And for once, the hardest fight waiting for Zee Darkheart might be the one he could not solve with his hands.
