Chapter Fifty-Four

The Things That Follow Rivers

The deep wild did not follow them home.

That was the first good sign.

The second was that the surviving researchers left when they said they would.

No hidden camp remained.

No replacement stabilization core appeared a week later.

No one tried turning the folded land into a kingdom.

For once, people learned before Zee had to break anything else.

He considered that progress.

The problem started with the river.

Three days after they returned to Vesper, an entire fishing village woke to find thousands of silver-black fish swimming upstream.

Not unusual by itself.

Fish did strange things.

Zee had learned not to assume every strange thing deserved him.

Then the fish climbed onto land.

That changed matters.

They did not die.

They moved.

Hundreds of sleek bodies pulled themselves through mud on narrow fin-like limbs that had not existed the day before according to every fisherman who had spent his life catching them.

They crossed the village.

Ignored people.

Ignored food.

Ignored dogs.

Then entered the western forest.

By noon, birds followed.

By evening, deer.

The next morning, a flock of black-winged creatures no one in Vesper could identify crossed the same river and went in the same direction.

Not fleeing.

Traveling.

King Lucien received six reports before breakfast.

Zee was eating when the seventh arrived.

A royal messenger entered the private dining chamber soaked to the shoulders.

“Your Majesty.”

Lucien looked up.

“What now?”

The messenger bowed.

“Something large crossed the lower Vesper.”

Rez stopped eating.

“How large?”

The messenger hesitated.

“Larger than the ferry.”

Zee smiled.

Aurelia looked toward him.

“No.”

“I said nothing.”

“You smiled.”

“That is not illegal.”

“Yet,” Rez muttered.

Ner covered his mouth.

Zareth was absent.

Drakonis had summoned him the previous night for a royal migration council concerning several Dragon territories where prey animals had begun abandoning traditional ranges.

At the time, Zee thought it unrelated.

Now he was less certain.

Lucien pushed away from the table.

“Did it attack?”

“No.”

“Damage?”

“One dock.”

“Intentionally?”

“We do not think so.”

Zee looked toward Rez.

Rez already had the same expression.

Pattern.

Not attack.

Movement.

Ner placed his cup down.

“Everything is going west.”

The messenger nodded.

“Yes.”

Aurelia frowned.

“Toward the deep wild?”

“Maybe,” Zee said.

Lucien looked toward him.

“Maybe?”

“The river route bends south before the old wolf border.”

Rez stood.

“But the migration path could still connect.”

Ner’s eyes sharpened.

“Unless the deep wild changed something outside itself.”

Silence.

Zee did not like that possibility.

Not because it meant danger.

Because they had assumed restoring the folds had finished the problem.

Maybe it had.

Maybe this was something else.

Good.

Unknown again.

Lucien looked toward the messenger.

“Any people missing?”

“No.”

“Deaths?”

“No.”

“Then no panic.”

He looked toward Rez.

“Send notice to river settlements.”

“No hunting unfamiliar migrations unless attacked.”

Rez nodded.

“Already drafting it.”

Zee looked at him.

“You were eating.”

“I can think while eating.”

“That seems arrogant.”

“It is a basic skill.”

Ner looked toward Zee.

“You cannot?”

Zee frowned.

“I hate both of you.”

Aurelia stood.

“You are not leaving until we know more.”

Zee sighed.

Then the Dragon Link struck.

Hard.

Zareth.

Far east.

Urgency.

Sky filled with wings.

Not Dragons.

Thousands of pale creatures moving above the mountains.

Then an image of a Drakonis elder pointing west.

Another.

A river seen from impossible height.

Black movement inside it.

Then Zareth himself.

Coming.

Fast.

Zee’s expression changed.

Aurelia noticed.

“Zareth?”

“Yes.”

“What?”

Zee looked toward the open terrace.

“He found the same thing.”

⸻

Zareth arrived before noon.

Not alone.

That was new.

Three Drakonis Dragons came with him.

All young adults.

All larger than Zareth.

All armored along the chest and neck with dark ceremonial plating.

They landed outside Vesper’s eastern walls with enough controlled force to shake windows.

Zee reached the field first.

Rez and Ner behind him.

Aurelia came later.

Lucien even later because kings apparently had to finish speaking to people before running toward unexpected Dragons.

Zareth lowered his head immediately.

The Link opened.

Mountain herds.

Sky-creatures.

A river changing course for several breaths before returning.

A Dragon patrol finding black scales floating in a lake.

One final impression.

Following something.

Zee frowned.

“The animals are following something?”

Zareth’s head shifted.

Not exactly.

The concept changed.

Being called.

Ner stepped closer.

“Called how?”

Zee relayed the question.

The Link returned sensation.

Pressure.

Direction.

Compulsion?

No.

Invitation?

No.

More primitive.

A pull like migration instinct.

Rez looked toward one of the older Dragons.

“Can they speak human form?”

The Dragon’s body shifted.

Bones compressed.

Wings folded inward.

Scales receded.

A tall woman with dark red hair stepped from the transformation wearing Drakonis armor.

Zee stared.

He had known older Dragons could shift.

Still strange seeing something the size of a building become a person in seconds.

The woman looked toward Zareth first.

“Crown Prince.”

Then Rez.

“Prince Vesper.”

Ner.

“Azrion.”

Zee.

Her gaze lingered on his horns.

“Darkheart.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“Everyone gets my name right now.”

Rez muttered, “Growth.”

The Dragon woman ignored him.

“My name is Commander Varessa Drakonis.”

No relation claimed beyond House and kingdom.

Good.

The world was allowed to have people without becoming family trees.

Varessa continued.

“For two weeks, migratory patterns across Drakonis territory have changed.”

“How many species?” Ner asked.

“Twenty-seven confirmed.”

Rez whistled softly.

“Direction?”

“West.”

Zee looked toward Zareth.

The Dragon Link sent agreement.

Varessa continued.

“Predators too.”

That mattered.

Prey moved for food.

Predators followed prey.

But if predators and prey moved together—

Ner understood.

“They are not following the food chain.”

“No.”

“Do they attack each other during migration?”

“Rarely.”

Zee’s Wolf nature reacted to that.

Wrong.

Not impossible.

Wrong.

“Something is suppressing hunting instinct.”

Varessa shook her head.

“We do not know.”

Good.

She did not pretend.

Rez asked, “Why come to Vesper?”

Varessa looked toward Zareth.

“He insisted.”

Zee smiled.

Zareth’s Link carried pride.

Varessa did not.

“He believes this connects to whatever you encountered west of wolf territory.”

Ner frowned.

“We do not know that.”

“No.”

Varessa looked toward him.

“But Drakonis records contain one old pattern.”

There.

Not full explanation.

Fragment.

“What pattern?” Zee asked.

Varessa looked toward the western horizon.

“Mass migration before a region changes.”

Rez went still.

“Changes how?”

“We do not know.”

Zee sighed.

Varessa looked at him.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Ner smiled.

“She gave the correct answer.”

Varessa looked confused.

Good.

Zee continued.

“What do Dragon records actually say?”

“Very little.”

“Three events across several thousand years.”

“Each involved unrelated species moving in the same direction.”

“Each ended with the territory afterward being different.”

“Destroyed?”

“No.”

“Dead?”

“No.”

“Different how?”

Varessa hesitated.

“Maps stopped agreeing.”

Ner’s face changed.

Rez looked toward Zee.

Deep wild.

Not necessarily the same thing.

But related enough to matter.

Varessa continued.

“One record describes a mountain that became a lake.”

“Another describes a valley appearing where no valley existed.”

“The third says an entire forest migrated.”

Zee stared.

“The forest?”

“Those are the words.”

Rez looked toward Ner.

“Can forests migrate?”

Ner gave him a flat look.

“We just spent two days somewhere rain falls upward.”

“Fair.”

Zee looked toward Varessa.

“Where are the animals going exactly?”

She unfolded a Drakonis map.

Three migration lines.

Then Vesper reports.

Rez added the river settlements.

Ner added wolf territory.

The lines did not converge at the deep wild.

They converged south of it.

Farther west than any of the four had traveled.

Beyond maintained kingdom roads.

Beyond Drakonis patrol routes.

Beyond the wolf maps.

A region labeled with nothing.

Not because empty.

Because unknown.

Zee stared.

Then smiled.

Aurelia arrived in time to see.

“No.”

Zee looked toward her.

“I said nothing.”

“You smiled.”

“That is getting old.”

“You keep earning it.”

Rez leaned over the map.

“If the migration continues at current rate, whatever is happening reaches the convergence point in four days.”

Ner frowned.

“You said reaches.”

Rez looked at him.

“Wrong?”

“We do not know whether the animals are arriving.”

“Or leaving something else.”

Silence.

That was worse.

Zee looked toward Zareth.

The Link carried the same restless urge.

Go.

Find.

Understand.

This time Zee agreed.

Aurelia saw.

Her expression tightened.

“You are going.”

Not question.

“Yes.”

She exhaled.

No argument yet.

Progress on both sides.

“Who?”

Zee looked toward Rez.

Rez nodded.

Ner too.

Zareth’s wings spread.

All four.

Varessa frowned.

“You are fifteen.”

Zee looked toward her.

“So is Zareth.”

She stared at the Crown Prince.

Zareth stared back.

Varessa sighed.

“That argument is deeply irritating.”

Rez smiled.

“We get that often.”

Aurelia stepped toward Zee.

“This is not another political border.”

“I know.”

“Not a rescue we understand.”

“I know.”

“Not something you can assume wants help.”

“I know.”

She searched his face.

Zee let her.

Then:

“If it becomes too strange—”

“Mother.”

“No.”

He stopped.

Aurelia’s eyes hardened.

“You do not get to become older by interrupting me.”

Ner looked away to hide a smile.

Zee sighed.

“Fine.”

“If it becomes too strange, you leave.”

Zee thought of the deep wild.

Of antlers through his body.

Of false Aurelia calling him from the trees.

Of learning that retreat was not failure.

“I will.”

Aurelia did not fully believe him.

Neither did Zee.

But he meant it now.

That mattered.

⸻

They left before dawn.

No army.

No convoy.

The four.

Varessa and one Drakonis scout accompanied them until the final Dragon patrol line, then turned back by agreement.

After that, there were no kingdom markers.

No road.

Only migration.

The farther west they traveled, the stranger it became.

Wolves walked beside deer.

Vampires would have called the scent unbearable.

Zee did.

Thousands of heartbeats layered across valleys.

Animals that should never share space moved together without fighting.

Birds traveled at ground level.

Fish swam through flooded ditches beside hoofed creatures.

One enormous reptile with a translucent spine crossed their path, ignored Zareth completely, and continued west.

Ner stared.

“I want to study everything.”

Zee looked at him.

“That sounds dangerous now.”

“I know.”

“That did not stop you.”

“No.”

Rez smiled.

“Good. Self-awareness.”

By afternoon, the land began losing sound.

Not silence.

Distance.

A bird flapped ten feet away.

No sound.

Rez dropped a stone.

They heard it only after it struck.

Ner raised one hand.

Golden Mana pulsed.

The light appeared before the magical pressure reached them.

Zee frowned.

“Sound is slow.”

Ner corrected him.

“Maybe.”

Zee sighed.

“Of course.”

Then Zareth stopped.

The Dragon Link went rigid.

Ahead.

Something enormous crossed the horizon.

At first Zee thought it was a mountain.

Then it moved.

Not walking.

Drifting.

A black shape taller than castle towers passed behind distant hills.

No wings.

No obvious limbs.

Its upper body vanished into cloud.

Animals below did not flee.

They moved toward it.

Zee’s Vampire senses found no heartbeat.

Wolf instinct found no category.

Witch perception found no ordinary magic.

Dragon nature—

Dragon nature bowed.

Not Zee.

The blood.

Something deep inside his developing fourth nature pulled tight with instinct older than his own consciousness.

Not submission.

Recognition of scale.

Zee whispered:

“What is that?”

Zareth’s Link gave him nothing.

Not even a fragment.

For the first time since Zee had known him, Zareth had no Dragon memory to offer.

Ner’s Golden Mana flickered uncertainly.

Rez slowly drew his sword.

Then stopped halfway.

Put it back.

Zee looked toward him.

Rez stared at the moving shape.

“If that thing notices us, the sword is decorative.”

Zee laughed quietly.

Not because it was funny.

Because fear had returned.

Real fear.

The world had gotten bigger again.

And whatever crossed the horizon did not know or care that kings existed.
