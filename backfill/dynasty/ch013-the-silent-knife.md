Chapter Thirteen

The Silent Knife

The nursery door opened without making a sound.

Captain Vale struck first.

His sword cut through empty air.

Nothing stood beyond the threshold.

The corridor was vacant.

Caldrin stepped beside him.

“That is irritating.”

Rez remained near Aurelia.

His blue-white mana had begun gathering around his fingertips despite Lucien’s warning.

Aurelia noticed.

“Rez.”

“I know.”

“You clearly do not.”

“I am not using it.”

“You are glowing.”

“That is different.”

The crimson hatchling hissed.

Everyone stopped.

He was staring upward.

Ceiling.

Vale looked up.

A shadow moved.

Fast.

Something dropped.

Caldrin pushed Aurelia aside.

A figure landed where she had been standing.

Black cloth.

No armor.

No visible face.

One thin blade in each hand.

The assassin moved immediately.

Not toward Aurelia.

Not toward Vale.

Toward Zee.

Rez reacted.

Blue-white mana exploded from his palm.

The assassin twisted.

The spell struck the wall and froze a section of stone beneath a layer of pale frost.

Rez stared at his own hand.

“I missed.”

Caldrin blocked the first blade.

Vale caught the second.

The assassin used both men as leverage, kicked off the wall, and landed behind them.

Too fast.

Aurelia moved.

The assassin followed.

The hatchling launched himself.

He bit into the assassin’s sleeve.

The attacker spun and struck him away with the flat of a blade.

The dragon crashed into the bed.

Zee screamed.

The Dragon Link flooded with pain.

Rez’s expression changed.

Blue-white mana surged violently.

This time he did not miss.

A narrow bolt struck the assassin in the shoulder.

The figure hit the floor.

Caldrin was on them immediately.

One sword at the throat.

Vale kicked both blades away.

“Do not move.”

The assassin laughed.

A woman’s voice.

“You think I came alone?”

Vale’s face changed.

Then the windows shattered.

Three more assassins entered.

The room erupted.

Lucien’s guards rushed from the corridor.

One assassin threw a black powder into the air.

The nearest soldiers collapsed.

Not dead.

Sleeping.

The second went for Rez.

Lucien arrived in time to intercept.

Dark blue mana crashed through the room and sent the attacker through a table.

“Rez!”

“I am fine!”

“You are not supposed to be here!”

“That feels less important now!”

Lucien looked ready to argue.

Then another assassin attacked.

He turned and fought.

Aurelia backed toward the wall.

Zee in one arm.

The hatchling scrambling back toward him.

The first captured assassin moved.

Caldrin saw too late.

She bit down.

Something cracked between her teeth.

Poison.

Caldrin grabbed her jaw.

“No.”

She smiled.

Black veins spread across her neck.

Aurelia looked away.

The assassin convulsed.

Then went still.

Caldrin cursed.

“No answers.”

Vale blocked another strike.

“Survive first!”

The room became too crowded.

Too many blades.

Too much magic.

Zee heard everything.

Heartbeats.

Footsteps.

Steel.

Breathing.

The vampire senses that had begun emerging on the road sharpened again beneath danger.

One heartbeat moved differently.

Slower.

Not fighting.

Waiting.

Zee turned his head.

A fifth assassin stood outside the shattered window.

Hidden.

Watching.

Crossbow.

The weapon lifted.

Aurelia did not see.

Zee did.

The bolt fired.

His body reacted before thought.

Crimson Mana snapped outward.

The bolt changed direction.

Not stopped.

Turned.

It struck the wall.

The assassin froze.

Zee stared at them.

The attacker reloaded.

The hatchling saw now.

Through the Dragon Link came a simple image.

There.

Rez followed the hatchling’s gaze.

“Window!”

Lucien turned.

The assassin fired.

Rez cast first.

Blue-white mana froze the crossbow.

The weapon shattered in the attacker’s hands.

Caldrin threw a blade.

The assassin vanished below the window.

Vale rushed forward.

“Roof access!”

Two guards followed.

The battle inside ended quickly after that.

One assassin died.

One escaped.

One surrendered only after Lucien broke both weapons and pinned them beneath a containment spell.

The final attacker tried to reach Zee again.

Aurelia moved backward.

The assassin lunged.

Zee’s wolf nature reacted.

Not with a growl this time.

With scent.

He recognized the attacker’s blood before the blade reached him.

Fear.

Adrenaline.

Poison.

Something foreign beneath it.

His tiny fangs extended.

Crimson Mana surged.

The assassin stopped in place.

Not frozen by spell.

Held by pressure.

Lucien stared.

Rez stared.

The attacker strained.

Could not move.

Zee began crying.

The pressure increased.

Aurelia whispered, “Zee.”

He heard her voice.

The force released.

The assassin collapsed.

Caldrin kicked the weapon away.

Silence followed.

The nursery was destroyed.

Again.

Lucien looked around.

Then at Aurelia.

“I apologize.”

Caldrin blinked.

“For what?”

“The hospitality.”

Aurelia laughed.

It came from exhaustion more than amusement.

“You are still doing better than my husband.”

No one answered that.

⸻

The surviving assassin was taken below the palace.

Lucien questioned them personally.

No torture.

Not yet.

Aurelia remained present.

Vale too.

Caldrin leaned against the wall with his arms folded.

The assassin sat chained to a stone chair.

Young.

Male.

No crest.

No obvious allegiance.

Lucien placed the black needle from the nursery door on the table.

“What is this?”

The assassin smiled.

“You know.”

“I want to hear you say it.”

“Sleepvenom.”

“Made where?”

No answer.

Caldrin spoke.

“West coast.”

The assassin looked toward him.

Caldrin smiled.

“Thank you.”

Lucien continued.

“Who hired you?”

Silence.

Aurelia stepped closer.

“You failed.”

The assassin looked at her.

“Today.”

Vale’s hand moved toward his sword.

Aurelia raised one hand.

“No.”

She studied the man.

“You are not afraid of death.”

“No.”

“Good.”

His smile faltered.

“Then tell me what you are afraid of.”

Silence.

Aurelia saw it.

Tiny.

But there.

“Whoever hired you.”

The assassin looked away.

Caldrin straightened.

“That is useful.”

Lucien leaned forward.

“Name.”

The man’s jaw tightened.

He said nothing.

Then a sound came from inside his chest.

A click.

Caldrin moved first.

“Back!”

The assassin convulsed.

Dark symbols appeared beneath his skin.

Aurelia stepped away.

Lucien raised a shield.

The man’s body collapsed inward.

Not exploding.

Erasing.

Within seconds, the chair was empty except for chains and ash.

Vale stared.

“What was that?”

Lucien’s expression darkened.

“A death seal.”

Caldrin crouched near the ash.

“Triggered by questioning?”

“Possibly.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“Witch work?”

Lucien answered carefully.

“Yes.”

“Which witches?”

“I do not know.”

She nodded.

No one pretended certainty.

That had become a rule of survival.

Caldrin touched the ash with the tip of a knife.

“Someone spent a great deal of money making sure these people could not talk.”

Vale frowned.

“So not bounty hunters.”

“No.”

Lucien looked toward the ceiling above them.

“Something organized.”

Aurelia’s expression tightened.

The physical hunts had changed again.

Armies.

Mercenaries.

Monsters.

Now assassins trained to infiltrate fortified palaces and die without revealing employers.

The world was learning faster.

So was Zee.

⸻

That night, Lucien moved Aurelia and Zee into an older section of the palace.

No windows.

Two exits.

Four layers of wards.

Rez objected to being excluded.

Lucien ignored him.

Zee lay awake in a new cradle.

The crimson hatchling curled beside him.

Their Link remained restless.

Images now came occasionally.

Not clear memories.

Simple impressions.

Window.

Blade.

Pain.

Friend.

The hatchling showed Zee the place where the assassin had stood.

Zee answered with the sound he remembered.

Heartbeat.

Neither understood what the other was truly doing.

But the bond had grown.

Outside the room, Lucien spoke quietly with Aurelia.

“This cannot continue.”

Her face hardened.

“You are sending us away.”

“No.”

“Then what?”

“We stop waiting for the next attack.”

Aurelia stared.

“You want to hunt them.”

“I want to know which roads are no longer safe.”

“That sounds similar.”

Lucien looked toward the sealed door.

“Zee is too young to remain at the center of a palace under constant siege.”

“There is nowhere else.”

“There may be.”

Aurelia’s expression changed.

“Where?”

Lucien hesitated.

“Northwest of Vesper territory is an old sanctuary.”

Caldrin, standing nearby, frowned.

“I dislike that word.”

“It was built before my grandfather’s reign.”

“For what?”

“Children born into dangerous bloodlines.”

Vale raised an eyebrow.

“That sounds extremely convenient.”

“It is not.”

Lucien looked toward him.

“It has been abandoned for twenty years.”

Caldrin smiled.

“Better.”

Aurelia crossed her arms.

“Why abandoned?”

Lucien did not answer immediately.

That told them enough.

Vale sighed.

“What happened there?”

“Something killed the caretakers.”

Silence.

Caldrin looked delighted.

“Perfect.”

Aurelia stared at Lucien.

“And you are suggesting I take my newborn son there?”

“No.”

“Good.”

“I am suggesting we investigate it first.”

That was different.

Aurelia looked toward Zee’s door.

“What if it is not safe?”

Lucien answered.

“Then we make it safe.”

The plan began there.

Not escape.

Not hiding blindly.

Preparation.

For the first time since Zee’s birth, the people protecting him would choose where the next danger happened.

That mattered.

⸻

Far away, inside a chamber lit by red candles, a woman received news of the failed assassination.

She wore no crown.

No royal colors.

Only black robes stitched with silver thread.

A messenger knelt before her.

“All five failed.”

“Dead?”

“Four.”

“The fifth?”

“Gone.”

The woman’s expression did not change.

“And the child?”

“Alive.”

A pause.

“Again.”

The messenger lowered his eyes.

“Yes.”

She walked toward a table covered in reports.

Blood Rain.

Wolf madness.

Vampire disturbance.

The siege.

The resurrection rumor.

The Gravehound shrine.

The failed mercenary ambush.

Vesper sanctuary.

None offered answers.

Only patterns.

She touched Zee’s crude bounty sketch.

“Stop sending killers.”

The messenger looked up.

“My Lady?”

“They teach us nothing if they die before observing him.”

“What do you want instead?”

She smiled faintly.

“Eyes.”

“Spies?”

“Scholars.”

The messenger hesitated.

“Witches?”

“Some.”

“Why?”

The woman looked at the child’s crimson eyes drawn badly across the parchment.

“Because everyone keeps asking how to kill him.”

She folded the report.

“I want to know what survival is doing to him.”

The hunt had changed again.

And somewhere inside House Vesper, Zee Darkheart slept beside his dragon, unaware that the next enemy coming for him would not arrive with a blade.

They would arrive with questions.
