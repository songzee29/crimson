Chapter Fifty

Where Kingdom Maps End

The trees stopped growing in a straight line three miles before the border stone.

Zee noticed first.

Not because the others were distracted.

Because the forest offended every predatory sense he possessed.

Pines twisted east while their roots pushed west. Moss grew on both sides of trunks. Snow clung beneath branches but not above them. One dead tree stood upside down in the earth, roots reaching toward the sky while blackened branches disappeared beneath frozen ground.

Zee slowed.

Rez noticed immediately.

“What?”

Zee looked around.

“I do not like this forest.”

Ner glanced toward the upside-down tree.

“That is unusually reasonable.”

Zareth circled overhead.

The Dragon Link pressed against Zee with a sensation he had rarely felt from the Crown Prince of Drakonis.

Uncertainty.

Not fear.

Not yet.

Zee looked up.

“What do you feel?”

The Link answered with fractured impressions.

Old stone beneath mountains.

Dragon elders refusing to fly farther west.

A childhood memory that did not belong to Zee: Zareth, smaller, listening while an elderly Drakonis warrior scratched a warning symbol into ash.

Then a concept.

Hungry land.

Zee frowned.

Rez heard.

“Hungry?”

“Something like that.”

Ner pulled one glove tighter.

“We are not calling a forest hungry until we know what that means.”

Zee looked at him.

“You object to everything interesting.”

“I object to terrible descriptions becoming facts.”

Rez smiled.

“He has a point.”

Zee sighed.

They continued.

No royal escort.

No Aurelia.

No Vesper soldiers.

This mission had started simply enough.

Three hunting parties had vanished near the western edge of wolf territory.

Not children.

Not merchants.

Experienced supernatural hunters.

The wolf clans had found abandoned weapons, intact supplies, and tracks ending in places where nothing should have been able to remove a body without leaving blood.

They had asked neighboring kingdoms for help.

Two refused.

One sent scouts.

The scouts did not return.

Then a wolf envoy reached Vesper carrying a piece of black antler that no one recognized.

Zee wanted to go before the envoy finished speaking.

Rez made them wait for the rest.

That had turned out to be useful.

Now the four of them followed the last recorded trail beyond the final maintained road.

The border stone appeared after noon.

It had once marked the end of a wolf kingdom.

Years of weather had erased most of the carved crest.

Beyond it lay no road.

No patrol markers.

No kingdom banners.

Nothing.

Rez stopped beside the stone.

“This is where their maps end.”

Ner examined the ground.

“No.”

Rez looked toward him.

Ner pointed.

“Their maps stop here.”

A thin line of pale residue cut across the soil.

Zee crouched.

“Magic?”

Ner did not answer immediately.

Pure Golden Mana gathered along two fingers.

He did not touch the line.

He brought his hand close.

The residue moved away.

Ner froze.

Zee leaned closer.

“It moved.”

“I saw.”

Rez crouched opposite them.

“Spell?”

“No.”

“You sound sure.”

“I am sure it did not respond like one.”

Ner shifted his hand left.

The pale line retreated right.

He moved upward.

It flattened into the soil.

Zee smiled.

“It does not like you.”

Ner stared.

“I refuse to take offense from dirt.”

Zareth landed beyond the border stone.

The ground trembled beneath his weight.

Teal-and-Gold Mana rolled once along his scales.

The pale line vanished completely.

All four noticed.

Zareth lowered his head.

The Dragon Link sharpened.

Below.

Zee touched the earth.

Nothing.

No heartbeat.

No blood.

No ordinary scent.

His Wolf nature found wet stone, old snow, dead leaves, animal trails.

Then something else.

Not scent exactly.

Absence.

A place where scent should have continued and did not.

Zee stood.

“There is something underground.”

Rez looked toward the trail ahead.

“Living?”

“I do not know.”

“Dead?”

“I do not know.”

Ner looked pleased.

Zee stared.

“Why are you smiling?”

“Because you finally understand how annoying that answer is.”

Zee almost shoved him.

Then the forest made a sound.

Not a roar.

Not a branch breaking.

A deep vibration moved through the trees.

Every bird went silent.

Zareth’s wings lifted.

The Link flashed.

Movement.

Many.

Not approaching.

Leaving.

Zee turned.

Deer burst through the undergrowth.

Seven.

Then wolves.

Wild ones.

Then animals Zee did not recognize at all—long-bodied gray things with six narrow legs and smooth faces where eyes should have been.

Rez drew his sword.

“Do we know those?”

“No,” Zee said.

Ner stared.

“Do not attack.”

One eyeless creature passed within ten feet of them.

It did not turn.

Did not react to Zee.

Did not react to Zareth.

It ran.

That frightened Zee more.

Whatever could make something unknown flee without even noticing a Dragon was worth respecting.

The ground vibrated again.

Harder.

Rez looked toward the fleeing animals.

“We move with them.”

Zee blinked.

“Back?”

“No.”

Rez pointed northwest.

“They are not running away from the whole region. They are angling around something.”

Ner understood.

“So we follow the edge.”

“Yes.”

Zee looked toward the deeper forest.

“I wanted to see what they were running from.”

Rez stared.

“I know.”

“That was almost insulting.”

“It was.”

Zareth’s amusement passed through the Link.

Zee sighed.

They moved northwest.

⸻

The first missing hunter was alive.

Barely.

They found him suspended six feet above the ground between two trees.

No rope.

No web.

No spell Zee could see.

His body simply hung horizontally in empty air.

Rez stopped everyone before they approached.

“Do not touch him.”

Zee looked toward the hunter’s chest.

Heartbeat.

Very slow.

“Alive.”

Ner stepped forward.

Golden Mana formed into a hair-thin line.

He passed it beneath the floating man.

Nothing.

Then above.

Nothing.

Around his left arm.

The Gold distorted.

Ner stopped.

“There.”

Zee saw nothing.

“What?”

“A boundary.”

Rez frowned.

“Shape?”

Ner extended more threads.

One disappeared.

Another bent.

A third reflected back toward him.

His expression changed.

“This is wrong.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“You say that often.”

“No.”

Ner looked at him.

“I mean the boundary does not have one side.”

Silence.

Rez lowered his sword slightly.

“Explain.”

“I cannot yet.”

“Try.”

Ner moved two fingers.

Golden threads mapped empty air.

Some lines bent around the suspended hunter.

Others passed through the same space from different angles.

One thread vanished entirely and reappeared three feet behind Ner.

He jerked backward.

Zee caught his arm.

Their hands stayed together a heartbeat too long.

Not now.

Ner pulled away.

“Thank you.”

Zee nodded.

Rez pretended not to see anything.

Poorly.

Ner looked back toward the hunter.

“It is not a cage.”

“What is it?” Zee asked.

“I do not know.”

Rez sighed.

Ner glared.

“You asked me not to lie.”

“I did.”

Zareth approached carefully.

The Dragon Link tightened around Zee’s senses.

Zareth smelled something.

Not through Zee.

His own Dragon senses.

A memory-impression passed through.

Hot caverns beneath Drakonis.

A mineral vein Dragons avoided because fire disappeared near it.

Then another.

Old Dragon word.

Not language Zee could hear.

Concept.

Fold.

Zee frowned.

“Zareth thinks the space is folded.”

Ner looked immediately toward the Dragon.

“Folded how?”

Zareth sent irritation.

If he knew, he would have sent more.

Rez walked around the suspended hunter.

“No blood.”

Zee nodded.

“No wound.”

“No binding marks.”

“No poison smell.”

“Nothing?”

Zee looked closer.

“One thing.”

A pale discoloration along the hunter’s forearm.

Not bruise.

Not frostbite.

The skin looked faded.

As though color itself had been removed.

Zee’s vampire senses sharpened.

The blood beneath that patch moved incorrectly.

Slow.

Then fast.

Then slow again.

He stepped closer.

Rez caught his shoulder.

“No.”

Zee looked over.

“I need to see.”

“See from here.”

“I cannot.”

“Then we learn another way.”

Zee’s jaw tightened.

He hated when Rez was right.

Ner pointed toward the hunter’s hand.

“His fingers.”

They were moving.

Not consciously.

One finger twitching in a pattern.

Three.

Pause.

Two.

Pause.

Three.

Rez crouched.

“Signal?”

Zee listened to the hunter’s heartbeat.

Each finger movement matched a change in pulse.

Three beats fast.

Two slow.

Three fast.

“He is trying to tell us something.”

Ner moved closer without crossing the invisible boundary.

“What?”

The hunter’s eyes opened.

Wild.

Terrified.

His mouth moved.

No sound came out.

Then Zee heard something.

Not with hearing.

Not thought.

A pressure against his fangs.

Against his bones.

Against the Dragon ridges beneath his shoulder blades.

One word that was not a word.

Below.

Zee staggered.

Ner grabbed him.

Rez’s sword came up.

Zareth roared.

The suspended hunter dropped.

Rez caught him before he hit the ground.

The invisible boundary vanished.

Ner immediately moved in.

Golden Mana checked the hunter’s body.

Zee grabbed a tree for balance.

“What was that?”

No one answered.

Because the ground opened.

Not cracked.

Opened.

A circle of forest floor six feet across folded downward without breaking soil or roots.

Zee stared.

There was no hole beneath it.

There was sky.

Dark violet sky filled with unfamiliar stars.

For one impossible second another place existed beneath their feet.

Then something looked up.

One enormous eye.

Pale.

Horizontal pupil.

Too large to belong to anything that should fit below the forest.

The opening snapped shut.

Earth returned.

Roots unbroken.

Snow undisturbed.

Silence.

No one moved.

Rez looked down.

Then at Zee.

“We are leaving the normal rules behind.”

Ner stared at the ground.

“That was not teleportation.”

Zee looked toward him.

“You know that?”

“Yes.”

“How?”

“Because nothing moved.”

Ner swallowed.

“The distance changed.”

Zareth’s Link exploded with Dragon instinct.

Danger.

Territory.

Not predator.

Not exactly.

Older.

Zee’s Crimson scales spread involuntarily along his ribs.

The Dragon ridges beneath his shoulders tightened.

Crimson energy moved through his blood.

Not awakening.

Reaction.

Recognition without knowledge.

Zee looked toward the unconscious hunter.

Then the silent forest.

Then the border stone far behind them.

For fifteen years he had believed kingdoms were large because wars could take months to cross them.

Now an eye had looked at him from a sky beneath the ground.

The known world suddenly felt very small.

And for the first time in years, Zee Darkheart smiled because he was afraid.

Not because fear felt good.

Because fear meant they had finally found something none of them understood.
