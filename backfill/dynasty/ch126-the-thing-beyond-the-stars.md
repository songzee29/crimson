Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Six

The Thing Beyond the Stars

The creature hit Zee hard enough to crater the field twice.

The first impact broke the ground.

The second broke three ribs.

Zee rolled through black grass.

Phoenix wings flared open before his body stopped moving.

Crimson fire burned across every feather.

His ribs began healing.

The creature did not give him time.

Six limbs drove into the ground.

Its black shell opened farther.

Blue light flooded through the translucent sections beneath its armor.

Then it moved.

Too fast for something that large.

Zee crossed both Phoenix wings in front of himself.

The creature struck.

Feathers tore loose.

Crimson fire scattered across the field.

Zee went backward.

Zareth roared.

True Dragon form descended from above.

Teal-and-Gold Dragonfire hit the creature’s exposed back.

Blue light swallowed half the flame.

Not extinguished.

Absorbed.

Zareth felt it immediately.

It ate that.

Zee pushed himself upright.

I noticed.

Rez’s Blue-White Mana formed a massive circle around the crater.

“Then stop feeding it energy.”

Zareth landed.

“Excellent suggestion after I already did.”

Elias stared at the creature.

His black demonic sigils moved inside deep-blue Witchcraft.

“It did not absorb the fire.”

Zee looked toward him.

“What?”

“The heat continued.”

The creature turned.

Blue light pulsed.

Elias’s eyes narrowed.

“It absorbed the supernatural structure inside it.”

Different.

Worse.

⸻

Kael moved first.

Vampire speed carried him around the creature’s blind side.

He drove a sword into the shell.

The blade entered half an inch.

Stopped.

The creature twisted.

Kael barely escaped the returning limb.

Amara shifted.

True Wolf.

She hit low.

Claws scraped black armor.

No penetration.

Rhyven came behind her.

Phoenix-Wolf fire wrapped around his jaws.

He bit directly into one joint.

The creature screamed.

Good.

Something hurt.

Rhyven tore backward.

Black shell cracked.

Blue fluid appeared beneath it.

Not blood.

Close enough.

Zee smelled nothing recognizable.

The creature kicked Rhyven.

His body flew across the field.

Phoenix vitality ignited through broken bone before he landed.

Amara was already back on the joint.

Wolf instinct.

Find weakness.

Widen it.

Zee saw.

“Joints.”

Everyone changed direction immediately.

Family had fought together long enough to move without discussion.

⸻

Zareth stayed airborne.

No more Dragonfire.

His Witch side created Teal-and-Gold force lines around the creature instead.

Not attacking it.

Controlling movement.

The thing absorbed one line.

Then another.

The third snapped around its rear limb before it adjusted.

Zareth understood.

It learns.

Zee answered through the Link.

So do we.

Rez attacked from the opposite side.

Blue-White Mana created false spell patterns around the creature.

The thing turned toward the largest concentration.

Trap.

Amara hit the exposed left joint.

Kael hit the right.

Rhyven bit through the cracked center.

Blue fluid sprayed.

The creature shrieked.

Elias raised one hand.

“Hold.”

Deep-blue Witchcraft formed.

Black demonic sigils reinforced it.

The creature absorbed the outer structure.

Then stalled.

The Demon element resisted whatever it was doing.

Elias smiled.

“Oh.”

Zee noticed.

“So it cannot eat everything.”

“Not cleanly.”

“Good.”

The creature tore through the binding.

But slower.

Enough.

Zee launched.

Not fire.

Not magic.

Phoenix wings for speed.

Dragon force through one arm.

Vampire speed in the final step.

Wolf instinct choosing the exact joint Rhyven had damaged.

His claws went through.

The limb came off.

The creature screamed loud enough to break every remaining window in the nearest Vesper farmhouse.

Rez stared.

“Progress.”

Zee dropped the severed limb.

“Five left.”

The creature did something none of them expected.

It spoke.

Again.

Not the same sentence.

Elias’s sigils reacted violently.

He staggered.

Zee looked toward him.

“What did it say?”

Elias stared at the creature.

Then slowly answered:

“Stop.”

Silence.

Rez looked at the massive off-world thing.

“It understands surrender?”

Elias frowned.

“No.”

The creature spoke again.

Shorter.

Elias listened through the pattern.

Then looked at Zee.

“It said…”

He hesitated.

“Pain.”

Everything stopped for half a heartbeat.

Not because they trusted it.

Because that word changed the fight.

⸻

Zee landed several yards away.

His claws remained extended.

The creature lowered its front limbs.

Not submissive.

Defensive.

Blue light flickered inside its chest.

Uneven.

Damaged.

Zee looked at Elias.

“Ask why it attacked.”

“I do not speak its language.”

“You understood it.”

“I recognize structure. Barely.”

“Try.”

Elias looked irritated.

Then stepped forward.

Deep-blue Mana formed slowly around his hand.

Not attack.

Pattern.

He repeated the creature’s sound badly.

The creature’s shell shifted.

Then answered.

Elias listened.

Black sigils moved.

“Threat.”

Zee looked at the creature.

“It thought we were a threat?”

Elias shook his head.

“Something else.”

The creature pointed one damaged limb toward the sky.

Then toward itself.

Then toward Zee.

Another sentence.

Elias’s face changed.

“What?”

“It did not come here alone.”

That was enough to make everyone look up.

The night sky remained quiet.

Stars.

Clouds.

Nothing else.

The creature spoke again.

Elias translated slowly.

“Flee.”

Rez’s expression sharpened.

“From what?”

The creature pointed upward again.

Then—

its chest split open.

Not attack.

A projection.

Blue light formed above the crater.

An image.

Stars.

Black space.

Then shapes.

Dozens.

No.

Hundreds.

Moving between them.

Not ships like any kingdom used.

Living structures.

Some enormous.

Some small.

All heading toward one point.

This world.

Zareth’s Link went cold.

That is not good.

Zee stared at the projection.

“No.”

The creature collapsed.

⸻

Tessa arrived too late to participate in the fight.

Exactly how she preferred it.

She knelt beside the off-world creature.

“I do not know how this body works.”

Rez crouched nearby.

“Can you keep it alive?”

“I just said I do not know how it works.”

“Still.”

Tessa glared.

Then examined the blue fluid.

The creature twitched.

Zee stepped closer.

Its shell reacted immediately.

Fear.

Interesting.

It remembered him.

Good.

Tessa looked up.

“If you want answers, stop looming.”

Zee stared.

“I am standing.”

“You loom while standing.”

Ner would agree.

Annoying.

Zee stepped back.

Tessa returned to work.

She could not use normal healing through the shell.

So she stabilized physically.

Pressure.

Wrapping.

Temperature.

No assumptions.

Practical.

The creature survived.

For now.

⸻

Back in Vesper, Ner knew something was wrong before anyone returned.

Zayrlen refused sleep.

Again.

But this time there was no laughter.

No growling at statues.

No random sparks.

He stared toward the western windows.

Crimson-Gold eyes wide.

His tiny hands opened.

Closed.

The Dragon heat in his body rose.

Not enough to burn.

Enough that Ner noticed.

“You feel something.”

Zayrlen made a small sound.

Ner looked toward the sky.

No supernatural bond connected the child to off-world life.

No cosmic destiny.

No prophecy.

Nothing like that.

But the Dragon inside him reacted to pressure.

The Wolf reacted to threat.

The Witch reacted to unfamiliar magic moving through the land.

Instinct.

That was enough.

Ner picked him up.

“You are two months old.”

Zayrlen stared.

“You are not allowed to worry about things in space.”

The baby blinked.

Ner sighed.

“Your father is ruining you already.”

⸻

Zee returned before dawn.

The moment he entered the Vesper keep, Zayrlen stopped fussing.

Ner noticed.

Again.

Zee approached.

“What happened?”

Ner handed him the baby.

“You first.”

Zee took Zayrlen.

The child immediately settled against his chest.

Wolf recognition.

Pack.

Father.

Zee looked toward Ner.

“We found something from beyond this world.”

Ner stared.

“You say things like that too casually.”

“It attacked us.”

“Of course.”

“It was afraid.”

Ner’s expression changed.

“Of you?”

“No.”

That answer mattered more.

Zee looked toward the window.

“Something followed it.”

Zayrlen’s hand closed against his shirt.

Ner went quiet.

“How many?”

Zee looked at him.

“Potentially hundreds.”

Silence.

Then Ner looked at their son.

At Vesper.

At Darkheart beyond the horizon.

At the world.

“Then I suppose boring lasted one day.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“Yes.”

⸻

The off-world creature woke at sunrise.

Elias was there.

So was Zee.

Rez.

Zareth.

No one else.

The creature looked toward Zee.

Then toward Elias.

It spoke.

Elias listened.

His black sigils shifted.

“What?”

“It says its name.”

Zee waited.

Elias repeated it badly.

“Vhal.”

The creature made a harsh sound.

Elias frowned.

“Apparently that was insulting.”

Rez smiled.

“Good start.”

Elias tried again.

“Vha’el.”

The creature stopped correcting him.

Close enough.

Zee looked at Vha’el.

“What is coming?”

Elias translated piece by piece.

The answer took time.

Not because the story slowed.

Because the language was genuinely different.

By the end, they understood enough.

Vha’el belonged to a species scattered across distant worlds.

Not rulers.

Not conquerors.

Survivors.

The things following were called something Elias translated roughly as:

The Devouring Choir.

Not because they sang.

Because they moved as one.

They did not steal bloodlines.

Did not create races.

Did not conquer kingdoms for crowns.

They consumed biological possibility.

The stranger a species became—

the more valuable it was.

Hybridization.

Mutation.

Multiple bloodlines.

Adaptation.

Evolution.

Zee’s expression changed.

Rez saw it.

“They are coming because of the Age of New Blood.”

Vha’el answered before Elias finished translating.

“No.”

Everyone stopped.

It had learned one word.

Then pointed toward Zee.

Elias listened to what followed.

His face hardened.

“They were already coming.”

Zee stared.

Vha’el pointed toward him again.

Then toward the world.

Elias translated.

“They noticed this world years ago.”

Zareth’s eyes narrowed.

“Why?”

The answer came.

God deaths.

Dragon awakenings.

Demonic disturbances.

Hybrid creation.

Unusual bloodline density.

Phoenix extinction and sudden Crimson Phoenix emergence.

The world had become louder.

Not because one event summoned them.

Because too many impossible things had happened over time.

Zee looked at the projection still burned into memory.

Hundreds moving toward the planet.

The world had finally become interesting to something beyond it.

That was unfortunate.

Vha’el looked directly at Zee.

Then said one final sentence.

Elias translated quietly.

“They will want you alive.”

Zee smiled.

“Everyone does lately.”

Rez looked toward him.

“This is different.”

“Yes.”

Zee’s Crimson Phoenix wings opened slightly.

Crimson-Gold eyes sharpened.

“Then they should have stayed farther away.”
