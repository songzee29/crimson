Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Eight

The Blood That Rose

Zee was alone when they came for him.

Not unguarded.

Not careless.

Alone.

There was a difference.

Darkheart sat hours behind him.

Vesper farther west.

Zareth had returned to Drakonis.

Rez was still dealing with the aftermath of the Vesper suppression.

Ner remained home with Zayrlen.

Exactly where Zee wanted them.

The road beneath Zee crossed an abandoned kingdom boundary that had not mattered for centuries.

Broken stone.

Dead watchtowers.

Forest reclaiming old walls.

No villages nearby.

No civilians.

Zee had come because three Darkheart scouts had reported finding something unusual in the ruins.

Not an enemy.

Not a body.

A set of metallic fragments that resembled material taken from Vha’el’s shell.

Maybe off-world.

Maybe not.

Worth checking.

Zee landed in the center of the ruined courtyard.

Crimson Phoenix wings folded behind him.

Nothing moved.

He smelled rain.

Old moss.

Rust.

Animal tracks.

No blood.

No unfamiliar heartbeat.

No Witchcraft.

No threat.

That was the first warning.

Zee stopped.

Too empty.

Then the first weapon hit him through the back.

⸻

It was not a blade.

A compressed force spike punched between two ribs and threw Zee twenty feet forward.

He hit stone.

Before he could turn—

a silver-black chain wrapped around one Phoenix wing.

Then another.

Three attackers pulled from different directions.

The wing opened violently.

Feathers tore.

Zee snarled.

Vampire speed moved him.

He crossed the courtyard before the attackers adjusted.

One throat broke beneath his hand.

Another body hit the wall hard enough to fold.

A third lost an arm.

Then something struck Zee’s temple.

White light.

Anti-Witch.

His vision vanished for half a second.

That was enough.

A hammer hit his ribs.

Crack.

Another.

Crack.

A spear entered beneath his shoulder.

Zee roared.

Dragon scales spread across his chest and arms.

He turned.

Broke the spear.

Caught the wielder.

Bit into his throat.

Blood entered Zee’s mouth.

Human.

Enhanced.

Wrong.

The man’s blood carried a suppression compound.

Zee spat immediately.

Too late.

His Vampire senses blurred.

Not gone.

Reduced.

Then four more attackers dropped from the ruined walls.

No battle cry.

No challenge.

No leader announcing themselves.

They attacked.

Together.

⸻

Zee opened his Phoenix wings.

Crimson fire erupted.

One attacker vanished beneath flame.

Another rolled through it wearing black mineral armor that drank heat without fully stopping it.

A third fired a net.

The mesh opened midair.

Witch-breaking runes.

Dragonbane wire.

Phoenix-ash coating.

Wolf-scent irritants.

Designed for him.

Zee twisted.

Almost cleared it.

Almost.

The edge caught one wing.

The runes activated.

Pain tore through his back.

Zee dropped.

Not all the way.

One knee hit stone.

He ripped through the net with Dragon strength.

The attacker who fired it was already moving.

Smart.

No waiting to see whether it worked.

Zee’s Witchcraft snapped outward.

“Break.”

Two weapons shattered.

“Down.”

One attacker hit the ground.

“Silence.”

Three spellcasters lost their next invocation.

Then something struck Zee in the side.

Not magic.

A physical bolt.

Thick.

Barbed.

It entered between scales.

Zee looked down.

The shaft detonated internally.

His entire side opened.

Blood hit stone.

His Phoenix regeneration ignited immediately.

Then slowed.

The bolt carried something.

Not poison.

A healing disruptor.

Zee’s expression changed.

They knew.

They knew too much.

⸻

Wolf instinct found the nearest threat behind him.

He spun.

Caught a knife wrist.

Broke it.

Claws through the chest.

Dead.

Vampire speed took him to the second.

He tore the man’s jaw off.

The third struck him with a weighted chain.

Zee ducked.

The fourth anticipated the dodge.

A boot hit his face.

His head snapped sideways.

Blood filled his mouth.

He grabbed the ankle.

Pulled.

The attacker fell.

Zee crushed his knee.

Then his throat.

Another chain wrapped around Zee’s horn.

He snarled.

Pulled forward instead of resisting.

The attacker stumbled toward him.

Zee headbutted him.

Skull broke.

But the chain remained.

A second attacker grabbed it.

Then a third.

They pulled.

The horn cracked.

Pain flashed white.

Zee roared and ripped free.

Part of the horn remained.

Not gone.

Cracked badly near the outer ridge.

He touched it once.

Then smiled through blood.

“Good.”

That frightened them.

It should have.

Because Zee stopped fighting cleanly.

⸻

He hit the next man hard enough to remove half his face.

Wolf aggression entered everything.

Vampire speed stopped being elegant.

It became lunging.

Snatching.

Closing distance before weapons could reset.

Witchcraft stopped being broad.

One-word interruptions.

“Fall.”

“Blind.”

“Open.”

“Stay.”

Dragon force shattered armor.

Phoenix wings became blades of heat and bone.

Crimson Sovereign synchronization remained perfect.

That was the cruel part.

Nothing inside Zee was failing to cooperate.

He was doing everything correctly.

There were simply too many answers demanded at once.

One attacker forced Witchcraft.

Another attacked the wings.

Another used anti-Vampire stakes.

Another triggered a sonic weapon tuned against Wolf hearing.

Another fired Dragonbane splinters.

Another carried heat-suppression plates.

Another moved only when Zee committed attention elsewhere.

Every time Zee solved one problem—

two more hit him.

⸻

A sonic pulse exploded.

Zee’s Wolf hearing went white.

He staggered.

A blade entered his thigh.

He tore it out.

A Witch slammed a restraint around one wrist.

Zee broke her neck.

The restraint stayed.

Anti-Witch iron crawled up his forearm.

His casting weakened on that side.

He switched hands.

“Sever.”

The restraint cracked.

Then a Dragonbane spear struck his chest.

Not through.

Scales stopped most of it.

Most.

The point entered.

Zee grabbed the shaft.

Pulled the attacker closer.

Bit through his throat.

Blood.

Different this time.

No suppression.

He fed.

Hard.

Fast.

Strength returned for three seconds.

Enough.

Zee lifted the dying man and threw him into two others.

One broke his back.

The other lost his footing.

Zee was on him instantly.

Claws.

Blood.

Bone.

Then a metal fist slammed into Zee’s damaged side.

Everything went red.

He dropped to one knee.

Another hammer hit his back.

His Phoenix wing buckled.

A chain tightened across it.

Then pulled.

Feathers ripped out by the handful.

Zee screamed.

Crimson fire exploded across the courtyard.

Three attackers burned.

One died immediately.

Two kept moving.

Prepared.

Again.

Always prepared.

⸻

Zee launched upward.

Or tried.

His good wing opened.

The damaged one failed halfway.

The torn feathers could not stabilize.

He rose six feet.

Then a weighted restraint struck his ankle.

Pulled.

Zee hit the ground face-first.

Stone cracked beneath him.

Before he could rise, something heavy landed on his back.

Dragon-strength attacker.

Not true Dragon.

Something enhanced enough to matter.

A fist struck the back of Zee’s skull.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

Zee reached backward.

Caught fingers.

Broke them.

Rolled.

The attacker moved with the roll instead of fighting it.

Training.

Zee hated competent people.

He drove an elbow into the ribs.

The man grunted.

Another attacker stabbed Zee through the abdomen.

Zee looked down.

Then at the person holding the blade.

Wrong choice.

Zee pulled himself farther onto it.

The attacker’s eyes widened.

Zee grabbed his face.

Crushed it.

The blade remained inside.

Blood poured down Zee’s legs.

His healing fought.

Slow.

Too slow.

The next strike caught his already damaged eye.

Something blunt.

Hard.

His left eye flooded red.

Vision vanished.

Swelling began immediately.

Zee roared and swung blindly.

Hit someone.

He heard bone break.

Good.

Then another strike hit his mouth.

A tooth cracked.

Blood filled everything.

He spat onto the ground.

Then laughed.

Low.

Ugly.

Not because he was winning.

Because they had finally made him desperate.

⸻

The attackers did not laugh back.

They had lost people.

A lot.

Bodies covered the courtyard.

Seven.

Nine.

Twelve.

More wounded.

Zee had made them pay for every yard.

But they kept rotating.

Fresh attackers forward.

Injured attackers back.

Suppressors changing frequency.

Weapons changing material.

No repeated pattern long enough for Zee to dominate.

They had studied adaptation too.

That was the part that impressed him.

Somebody had understood the real problem.

Zee adapted.

So they refused to give him one stable problem.

⸻

He heard movement to the right.

Turned.

Too slow.

A restraint collar struck his throat.

Zee grabbed it before it locked.

Dragon strength fought the mechanism.

The attacker on the other end screamed from the strain.

Then two more joined.

The collar closed.

Witchcraft dimmed.

Not gone.

Phoenix fire weakened.

Wolf hearing dulled.

Vampire senses blurred.

Dragon blood remained strongest.

Zee ripped one chain loose.

Killed the attacker holding it.

Another slammed a suppression rod into his spine.

His body convulsed.

The collar locked completely.

Zee hit the ground on one hand.

The attackers moved in.

Bad.

Very bad.

He tried Vampire speed.

Got half a step.

A hammer broke another rib.

Tried Witchcraft.

“Brea—”

A fist hit his throat before the word finished.

Tried Phoenix flame.

A heat plate crushed against one wing.

The fire suffocated beneath it.

Tried Dragon strength.

Three attackers hit his already damaged knee from different angles.

Something tore.

Zee dropped.

This time both knees touched stone.

⸻

He looked up through one working eye.

Twenty still standing.

Maybe more beyond.

He had killed enough to make the courtyard look like a massacre.

Still not enough.

His healing continued.

Ribs knitting too slowly.

Eye repairing beneath swelling.

Wing trying to rebuild while feathers were still being ripped free.

Scales closing over one wound while another opened.

His body was surviving.

Not controlling.

That difference finally became undeniable.

An attacker stepped forward with a mace.

Zee spat blood at his boots.

The mace hit his face.

Everything disappeared.

Then returned sideways.

Zee was on the ground.

He did not remember falling.

A boot hit his ribs.

Another.

Another.

He caught one ankle.

Twisted.

The attacker screamed.

Zee dragged him down and bit into the thigh.

Fed.

Blood.

Strength.

He tore open the artery.

Then three people pulled him off.

One arm around his neck.

One chain around his waist.

One hooked weapon through the damaged Phoenix wing.

Zee snarled.

Animal now.

No elegance.

No King.

No court.

No throne.

He bit whoever came close.

Kicked.

Clawed.

Headbutted.

Used broken stone as a weapon.

Drove his thumb into an eye.

Tore an ear off.

Crushed fingers.

Anything.

Everything.

Survive.

⸻

A blade entered his back.

Missed the heart.

Another entered his side.

A third struck his shoulder.

The world narrowed.

Blood.

Stone.

Breathing.

Pain.

His own heartbeat.

Still there.

Always there.

Death had already ended once.

They could not have him that way.

But they could hurt him.

Gods, they could hurt him.

⸻

A hammer came down on his damaged wing.

Bone cracked.

Zee screamed.

The wing collapsed under him.

Crimson feathers scattered across black stone.

His Phoenix fire surged instinctively.

Weak.

Fighting.

Trying to restore.

Trying to stabilize.

The attackers saw it.

“Again.”

A suppression plate slammed onto the wing.

The Crimson flame compressed.

Zee’s body reacted.

Not enough.

His Phoenix blood burned harder.

Still not enough.

Then—

something beneath it answered.

Zee froze.

Not the attackers.

Not the pain.

Something inside his blood.

Old.

Deep.

Buried beneath everything he had ever known himself to be.

His Crimson Phoenix fire changed.

For one heartbeat.

Only one.

Crimson became scarlet.

Deeper.

Thicker.

Almost blood instead of flame.

Then something black-crimson appeared inside it.

Not smoke.

Not corruption.

Phoenix.

Different.

Older.

The fire moved beneath the existing flame like an ancient second heartbeat.

Zee’s Crimson-Gold eye flared.

Both colors became violently bright.

The attackers nearest him stopped.

One whispered:

“What is that?”

Zee did not know.

That frightened him more than anything else had.

The black-crimson Phoenix energy pulsed once.

Something in his bones recognized it.

Not acquired.

Not stolen.

Not created.

Inherited.

Then—

gone.

The fire returned to normal Crimson.

Weak.

Suppressed.

The moment passed.

It did not heal his wing.

Did not restore his ribs.

Did not break the restraints.

Did not save him.

Good.

Because the next hammer hit his skull.

And Zee went down again.

⸻

He tried standing.

Once.

Made it to one knee.

Blood ran from his mouth.

One eye swollen shut.

Horn cracked.

Chest torn.

Scales split open across the ribs.

One wing hanging wrong.

Clothes shredded.

Hands soaked red.

He breathed.

The attackers watched.

Some of them were afraid now.

That almost made Zee smile.

He rose another inch.

A spear struck behind the knee.

He dropped.

Someone drove a restraint into the ground over his wrist.

Another over the opposite arm.

One across his throat.

One pinned the damaged wing.

Zee pulled.

Stone cracked.

The restraints held.

The attackers did not cheer.

Smart.

They had seen too many friends die.

One approached carefully.

Zee snapped at him.

Fangs bloodied.

Still dangerous.

Still Zee.

The man stepped back.

“Do not get close.”

Good advice.

Zee laughed once.

It came out wet.

Then a boot hit his face.

Hard.

The world blurred.

He remained conscious.

Barely.

That was worse.

He understood everything.

The weight on his body.

The blood beneath him.

The sound of his own breathing.

The fact that he could not stand.

The fact that somebody had finally done it.

Not killed him.

Not broken his immortality.

Not proven stronger in some glorious duel.

They had simply planned better.

Hit harder.

Worked together.

Refused to let him control the fight.

And Zee Darkheart—

First Natural Tribrid.

Crimson Sovereign.

Crimson Demonic Dragon.

Crimson Phoenix.

King.

Monster.

God-killer.

Father.

Had lost.

Completely.

⸻

No family came.

No Dragon roar split the sky.

No Blue-White Mana.

No Pure Gold Bloodsong.

No Demon-Witch sigils.

No Wolf cavalry.

No rescue.

The loss remained his.

The attackers began withdrawing.

Not carrying Zee.

Interesting.

They had not come to capture him.

Only beat him.

Maybe kill him if possible.

Maybe prove something.

Zee did not know.

Did not care yet.

He memorized what he could.

Footsteps.

Voices.

Patterns.

Blood scents beneath suppressants.

Any face his functioning eye caught.

Later.

Not now.

Now he lay there.

Alive.

Furious.

Humiliated.

Bleeding into ancient stone.

The last attacker stopped several feet away.

Looked at him.

“You are not untouchable.”

Zee tried to smile.

Blood ran between his teeth.

“No.”

The attacker waited.

Zee’s working eye sharpened.

“You just made yourself touchable.”

The man left.

Good.

Because Zee could not have stopped him.

That truth sat heavier than the pain.

⸻

Minutes passed.

Maybe more.

Zee could not tell.

His healing worked.

Slow.

Ribs beginning.

Eye trying.

Wing twitching.

Scales closing.

Immortality refusing the only ending his attackers might have preferred.

But beneath the familiar repair—

something else remained.

A warmth.

Not Crimson Phoenix.

Not Dragon.

Not Witch.

Not anything Zee had consciously possessed.

Black-crimson.

Scarlet.

Ancient.

Sleeping again.

But no longer entirely silent.

Zee stared at his own blood pooling beneath one hand.

For one impossible second, the reflection looked almost black-red.

Then ordinary Crimson returned.

He frowned.

“What the hell was that?”

No answer.

⸻

Far away from Darkheart—

far from Vesper—

far from every kingdom Zee currently watched—

a man woke screaming inside a ruined stone sanctuary.

His eyes opened.

Older.

Harder.

Alive.

A scar remained across his throat.

Another across his chest.

Marks left by the son who had killed him years ago.

Victor Darkheart sat upright.

Breathing.

Still.

He had been breathing for a long time now.

Long enough to understand the impossible.

Long enough to understand that his death had been real.

Long enough to understand that something buried in his blood had answered only after it.

Death.

Then fire.

Then return.

He had hidden after waking.

Not from a plan.

Not to manipulate kingdoms.

Not to rule from shadows.

Because he had not understood what he had become.

Because the last face he remembered before death—

was Zee’s.

Victor lifted one hand.

For a moment, black-crimson fire crawled across his fingers.

Scarlet beneath it.

Heavy.

Ancient.

Phoenix.

Then it vanished.

Victor stared.

The dormant bloodline finally given the condition it had waited for his entire life.

Death.

Rebirth.

Awakening.

The name had come to him only afterward.

Not a title.

Not a crown.

Blood.

Abyssal Titan Phoenix Sovereign.

Victor whispered it into the dark.

Then froze.

Because for the first time since his resurrection—

something far away had answered.

Not through a Link.

Not through thought.

Through blood.

His blood.

His son.

Victor slowly stood.

⸻

Zee knew none of this.

He remained alone in the ruined courtyard.

Broken.

Alive.

Furious.

His fingers finally moved.

Then his hand.

He tried pushing himself up.

Failed.

The humiliation burned hotter than his wounds.

Good.

He would remember that too.

For now—

there was no revenge.

No victory.

No new form.

Only Zee Darkheart lying in his own blood, forced to accept that overwhelming him had always been possible if enough people were willing to pay the price.

And somewhere beneath the Crimson Phoenix blood struggling to keep him together—

something inherited from Victor Darkheart had opened its eyes for the first time.
