Chapter One Hundred Twenty-One

The First Month of the Prince

By the end of Zayrlen’s first month, Darkheart had learned three things.

The Prince hated being cold.

He liked Wolf howls.

And he had already destroyed four blankets.

The fifth died during breakfast.

Ner stared at the new hole.

Zee looked at Zayrlen.

The baby looked back.

A tiny wisp of Crimson-and-Gold heat rose from his mouth.

Ner pointed.

“That was Dragon.”

“Yes.”

“He burned another blanket.”

“Yes.”

“You are smiling.”

“No.”

“You absolutely are.”

Zee stopped smiling.

Poorly.

Ner sighed.

“We need fireproof fabric.”

Zee considered.

“I know a Dragon.”

“You know several.”

“I know one who owes me.”

Through the Link came instantly:

I owe you nothing.

Zee looked toward the ceiling.

You are listening.

Unfortunately.

Ner shook his head.

“Your family is exhausting.”

Zayrlen sneezed.

Another spark.

Ner closed his eyes.

“So is yours.”

⸻

The first month did not bring another kidnapping attempt.

It brought something almost stranger.

Diplomats.

Dozens of them.

Kings wanted to know what Zayrlen was.

Witch councils wanted bloodline records.

Wolf clans wanted to know whether his future true Wolf would count as a pack-born Alpha by ancestry.

Dragon courts wanted confirmation of Veyrath blood.

Vampires wanted to know why Zee’s son carried no Vampire nature.

One extraordinarily stupid ambassador asked whether Zayrlen would be available for future marriage negotiation.

Zee stared at him.

Ner stared too.

The ambassador slowly realized both fathers were deciding which one would kill him.

Rez intervened.

For diplomatic reasons.

“He is one month old.”

The ambassador swallowed.

“I meant eventually.”

Zee’s Crimson Phoenix wings opened.

Rez sighed.

“You should leave.”

The ambassador left.

Quickly.

Ner looked toward Rez.

“You saved his life.”

“I save many disappointing people.”

Zee frowned.

“I was only going to scare him.”

Rez stared.

“No.”

Fair.

⸻

The important diplomatic meeting came from Drakonis.

Not because Zareth demanded it.

Because his court did.

Three elder Dragons arrived in Human form.

They carried no threats.

No weapons.

That was respectful.

They requested formal recognition of Zayrlen’s Veyrath descent.

Zee’s expression cooled.

“Why?”

The eldest Dragon answered:

“Because a naturally born child carrying Dragon blood outside an active Dragon House has not occurred in recorded generations.”

“He is Darkheart.”

“Yes.”

“Then recognize that.”

The Dragon inclined his head.

“We do.”

Good answer.

Ner held Zayrlen.

The elder looked toward him.

“May I sense the bloodline?”

Zee said:

“No.”

Ner said:

“Yes.”

They looked at each other.

Ner raised an eyebrow.

“He asked.”

Zee frowned.

“He is staring.”

“He is a Dragon.”

“That is not an excuse.”

Zareth stood nearby.

“It often is.”

Zee ignored him.

Ner looked toward the elder.

“No touching.”

The Dragon nodded.

Teal light gathered around his eyes.

Not Zareth’s exact Teal-and-Gold Mana.

His own.

He sensed.

Then went completely still.

The other Dragons noticed.

“What?”

The elder looked at Zee.

“Veyrath.”

Zee said nothing.

“Strong.”

Still nothing.

“Stronger than expected.”

Zee’s pupils narrowed slightly.

“Meaning?”

The Dragon hesitated.

Then wisely chose precision.

“The line did not weaken passing through you.”

That mattered.

Aurelia’s blood had not survived inside Zee as some hidden remnant.

It remained real.

Complete enough to pass naturally into his son.

Zayrlen shifted against Ner.

Heat moved through the room.

The elder Dragon took one instinctive step backward.

Zareth smiled.

“You felt that.”

The elder looked toward the child.

“Something looked back.”

Silence.

Zee’s expression changed.

Not alarm.

Attention.

“What?”

The Dragon slowly answered:

“Not conscious.”

Good.

“Not awake.”

Good.

“But large.”

Zee remembered the kidnapping attempt.

That enormous Dragon presence flashing behind the cradle.

Something far too vast for the tiny body currently chewing on Ner’s sleeve.

Ner looked down.

Zayrlen continued chewing.

The absurdity did not lessen the truth.

Something enormous existed inside him.

Waiting.

⸻

The Dragons left without titles.

Without claims.

Without trying to make Zayrlen Drakonis.

Zareth made certain of that.

“He is Darkheart.”

The eldest Dragon had nodded.

“Of course.”

Zareth’s expression remained hard.

“And Veyrath through Zee.”

“Yes.”

“Not Drakonis.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Zee looked at him afterward.

“You threatened them politely.”

“I am Crown Prince.”

“Barely.”

Zareth smiled.

“You sound jealous.”

Zee’s wings opened.

Zareth left before the argument became structural damage.

⸻

That evening, Elias found Zee in the nursery.

Zayrlen was asleep.

Ner had gone to bathe.

Elias leaned against the door.

“You look strange.”

Zee looked toward him.

“I always look like this.”

“No.”

Elias approached.

“You look peaceful.”

Zee stared.

Elias smiled.

“I know. Horrifying.”

“Leave.”

“No.”

He sat in the chair opposite.

For a few minutes, neither spoke.

Then Elias looked at Zayrlen.

“He has your eyes.”

“Yes.”

“And Ner’s attitude.”

“He cannot speak.”

“Still obvious.”

Zee almost smiled.

Elias’s expression became quieter.

“You know I remember when you said you would never have this.”

Zee looked at him.

Years earlier.

Before the crown.

Before hybrids.

Before the God.

Before Phoenix blood.

Before Aidan.

Before Ner had ever told Zee he wanted his child.

Elias had known him long enough to remember versions of Zee most people never met.

“You said the world would turn anything you loved into leverage.”

Zee looked down at Zayrlen.

“It tries.”

“Yes.”

“And?”

Elias waited.

Zee touched one small hand.

Zayrlen closed around his finger without waking.

“It has not convinced me to stop.”

Elias smiled.

“Good.”

That was all.

Old friends did not always need speeches.

⸻

The next serious problem arrived from Vesper.

Rez received the message first.

Then came to Zee.

His face told Zee enough.

“Vesper?”

“Yes.”

“What?”

Rez handed him a sealed report.

Three villages near Vesper’s northern edge had gone silent.

Not destroyed.

Not kidnapped.

People remained.

But something had taken their magic.

Witches could not cast.

Vampires could not accelerate healing.

Wolves could not shift.

Every supernatural nature had been suppressed simultaneously.

Zee read the report twice.

“Permanent?”

“We do not know.”

“Dead?”

“Not yet.”

Ner entered carrying Zayrlen.

Rez looked toward the baby.

Then back at Zee.

“I need to go.”

“Obviously.”

“I want you there.”

That made Zee look up.

Rez rarely asked.

He usually informed.

“Why?”

“Because one survivor said the thing that touched them was looking for new blood.”

The room changed.

Ner heard it.

“So this is connected to the Age of New Blood.”

Rez shook his head.

“Maybe.”

Good.

No assumptions.

Zee handed the report back.

“When?”

“Now.”

Zee looked at Ner.

Ner looked at Zayrlen.

Then at Zee.

“Go.”

Again.

Zee hated how much trust required leaving.

But he understood it now.

Ner smiled faintly.

“I am not going anywhere.”

Zee looked toward the nursery wards.

Elias’s detection.

Amara’s rotations.

Kael’s guards.

Rhyven’s patrol.

Zareth could reach them through the sky.

Tessa was nearby.

Ner himself could kill most things foolish enough to enter the room.

Zee touched Zayrlen’s cheek.

The baby’s eyes opened.

Crimson-Gold.

Recognition.

Zee whispered:

“I will be back.”

Zayrlen grabbed his finger.

Then let go.

Zee stood.

Phoenix wings opened.

Rez watched him.

“Ready?”

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“Yes.”

⸻

They reached Vesper before sunset.

What waited there was not an army.

Not a laboratory.

Not a kingdom.

The first village looked normal.

People stood outside homes.

Witches held dead spell circles in their palms.

A Vampire had cut his hand and watched the wound remain open.

A Wolf stood beneath the moon—

unable to shift.

Rez’s Blue-White Mana flared.

It still worked.

Zee’s abilities remained.

Whatever had happened was selective.

One elderly Witch approached them.

“It came from the forest.”

“What?”

“We never saw it.”

Zee crouched beside the dead magic burned into the soil.

His Witch nature touched it.

Nothing.

His Vampire senses found no blood.

Wolf found no scent.

Dragon found no heat.

Phoenix found no life-resonance.

Interesting.

Rez looked at him.

“You have that face.”

“What face?”

“The one you make when something is finally difficult.”

Zee smiled faintly.

Then looked toward the forest.

The King’s Hunt had nothing to claim yet.

No confirmed prey.

No scent.

No blood.

No heartbeat.

No magic signature.

For the first time in a long while—

Zee Darkheart looked into a forest and could not immediately identify what was looking back.

Something moved between the trees.

Not visible.

Felt.

The nearest Witches collapsed.

Rez’s Blue-White Mana flickered.

Zee’s Crimson fire dimmed for one second.

Only one.

Then returned.

His expression changed.

Whatever lived in the northern Vesper woods could suppress supernatural bloodlines.

And it had just tried him.

Zee smiled.

Darkly.

“Well.”

Rez looked toward him.

“What?”

Zee’s Crimson-Gold eyes sharpened.

“It knows I am here.”

The forest answered.

Every tree bent toward them at once.

And somewhere far away in Darkheart—

Zayrlen Keal Darkheart suddenly opened his Crimson-Gold eyes.
