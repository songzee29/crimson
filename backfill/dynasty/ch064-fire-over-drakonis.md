Chapter Sixty-Four

Fire Over Drakonis

Zareth did not ask Zee to come.

The Dragon Link did not work that way.

It gave Zee heat.

Smoke.

Blood.

A mountain collapsing beneath claws.

Then Zareth’s rage.

That was enough.

Zee left Darkheart before the eastern gates finished opening.

Rez came with him.

Ner followed.

No argument.

They reached Drakonis under a red sky.

Not sunset.

Fire.

Three mountain watchtowers burned along the western ridge.

Dragons filled the air.

Not fleeing.

Fighting.

Zareth was already among them.

He struck another Dragon from the sky.

The enemy twisted.

Black armor covered its chest.

Not Drakonis.

Zee smelled foreign Dragon blood before they landed.

“Who are they?”

Rez looked upward.

“Not here to negotiate.”

Ner’s Gold spread over his hands.

“That narrows it down.”

Zee’s crimson-and-gold eyes shifted.

Slits.

The Link opened.

Zareth sent images fast.

Foreign Dragon mercenaries.

Hired.

Targeting royal nesting grounds.

Not him specifically.

Destroy hatcheries.

Break Drakonis succession strength.

Force the Dragon Queen into border concessions.

Zee’s face hardened.

“Someone hired them to hit the hatcheries.”

Rez stopped.

“That is war.”

“Yes.”

Zareth hit the ground nearby.

His true Dragon body was bloodied.

One wing membrane torn.

Still standing.

Still furious.

Zee approached.

Zareth lowered his head.

The Link carried one clear demand.

Do not take this from me.

Zee understood.

This was Drakonis.

Zareth’s kingdom.

His fight.

Zee nodded.

“Lead.”

The Dragon Prince’s eyes burned.

Then he turned.

Good.

⸻

They did not fight as rescuers.

They fought as reinforcements.

Zareth commanded the air.

Three Drakonis squadrons split at his signal.

One drove attackers high.

One cut off retreat.

One protected the lower nesting valleys.

Zee stayed on the ground at first.

Wolf senses.

Blood.

Smoke.

Movement.

He found six foreign fighters trying to enter through a narrow ravine.

Two Dragons in human form.

Four armed mercenaries.

The first man never saw Zee.

Knife.

Silent.

The second did.

Barely.

Zee caught his wrist.

Twisted.

One short movement.

Done.

The Dragon shifted halfway.

Scales spread.

Zee hit him before transformation completed.

Vampire speed.

Assassin placement.

One precise strike beneath the jaw.

Then Witchcraft.

“Still.”

Crimson force locked the Dragon’s body for one second.

One second was enough.

Zee drove him into the cliff.

The second Dragon attacked from behind.

Zee smelled the blood before hearing movement.

Wolf instinct turned him.

Claws met claws.

Dragon force slammed into Zee’s ribs.

His dark crimson-black scales held.

Pain.

Not failure.

Zee smiled.

The attacker saw the horns.

The eyes.

The heat.

Then froze.

“You.”

Zee’s expression did not change.

Recognition again.

Reputation.

Useful.

“The Crimson Demonic Dragon.”

“I really hate that name.”

The Dragon backed away.

Good.

Fear without effort.

Zee stepped forward.

The Dragon attacked anyway.

Better.

Zee caught the throat.

Vampire strength.

Wolf balance.

Dragon durability.

He drove the enemy down.

Fangs descended.

The Dragon’s eyes widened.

Zee smelled old enchanted blood in him.

Witch mixture.

Foreign.

Interesting.

He fed.

Not because necessary.

Because he wanted to know.

Dragon blood entered hot.

His evolved Dragon nature reacted immediately.

No copied flame.

No new species trait.

Just adaptation pressure.

His scales warmed.

His body recognized the foreign enchantment in the blood and rejected it before it could spread.

Zee drank until the Dragon weakened badly.

Then stopped.

The Dragon stared.

Surprised to be alive.

Zee wiped his mouth.

“Who hired you?”

Silence.

Zee crouched.

“Do not make me ask twice.”

The Dragon tried to resist compulsion by looking away.

Zee barely cared anymore.

“Answer.”

Predatory pressure entered the command.

Vampire will.

Blood recognition.

Voice.

The Dragon’s jaw tightened.

Resistance.

Strong.

Zee pushed harder.

Blood vessels burst in one eye.

The Dragon gasped.

“Lord Sevrik.”

Rez arrived behind Zee.

“Where?”

“Eastern Vale.”

“Why Drakonis?”

“Trade passage.”

“Whose passage?”

The Dragon shook.

“His.”

Zee looked at Rez.

Different attacker.

Different motive.

Good.

No grand alliance.

Just greed.

Rez’s face hardened.

“Keep him alive.”

Zee stood.

“He is yours.”

The prisoner stared at him.

Then realized Zee had decided not to kill him.

Relief.

Zee noticed.

Enjoyed that too.

⸻

The hatcheries survived.

Barely.

One lower nesting chamber collapsed.

No hatchlings died.

That mattered most.

Three Drakonis guards did.

Seven attackers surrendered.

Twelve died.

Five escaped.

Zareth landed on the highest ridge after the battle.

Blood covered his claws.

One side of his face was burned.

He looked older than twenty-one.

Not weaker.

More royal.

The Dragon Queen arrived behind him.

She did not ask Zee what happened.

She asked Zareth.

Good.

Zareth shifted through the Link and Dragon gestures.

Attack route.

Numbers.

Casualties.

Names.

Lord Sevrik.

Trade passage.

The Queen listened.

Then looked toward Zee.

“You fed from one.”

“Yes.”

No apology.

“Why?”

“Information.”

“And?”

Zee looked toward the surviving foreign Dragon being guarded below.

“He lived.”

The Queen’s expression changed slightly.

Not approval.

Evaluation.

Then she looked back at her son.

“What do you recommend?”

Zareth went still.

Zee did too.

This was new.

Not because Zareth had never advised.

Because the Queen asked in front of everyone.

The Crown Prince looked over the damaged ridge.

Then sent his answer.

Not revenge.

Not immediately.

Secure hatcheries.

Close western air route.

Send demands to Sevrik.

Prepare force.

If refused—

strike.

Zee smiled faintly.

Rez noticed.

Future ruler.

The Queen nodded.

“So ordered.”

Zareth’s posture changed.

Pride.

Responsibility.

Weight.

Zee knew that feeling now.

King recognized Crown Prince.

No one needed to say it.

⸻

They left Drakonis after midnight.

Not because everything was safe.

Because Zareth stayed.

His kingdom needed him.

Zee did not ask him to come back to Darkheart.

That would have been wrong.

The Link stretched as Zee, Rez, and Ner traveled west.

Zareth remained behind.

Still connected.

Still his own.

Rez rode beside Zee.

“You are quiet.”

“I am thinking.”

“Dangerous.”

Ner looked over.

“About Sevrik?”

Zee nodded.

Rez frowned.

“Drakonis will handle him.”

“Yes.”

“And you are going to let them.”

“Yes.”

Rez looked suspicious.

Zee smiled.

“I am King now. I understand jurisdiction.”

“That may be the scariest thing you have ever said.”

Ner laughed.

Then suddenly stopped.

Zee turned.

Ner’s face changed.

Pure Gold flashed beneath his skin.

“What?”

Ner touched his throat.

“Blood.”

“Whose?”

“I do not know.”

His voice deepened involuntarily.

A low resonance moved through the night.

Zee felt it in his own veins.

Then Ner looked toward the road ahead.

“Mine.”

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“What?”

Ner lifted one hand.

A thin red line had appeared across his palm.

No wound.

No blade.

Blood beaded anyway.

Rez stopped his horse.

“That is not normal.”

Ner stared at the line.

“It is calling me.”

Zee went completely still.

Not panicked.

Not loud.

Worse.

Calm.

Someone had reached into Ner’s blood from a distance.

That was enough.

The road home could wait.

Zee turned east.

Rez sighed.

“Where are we going?”

Zee’s voice became very quiet.

“To find who touched him.”

And this time he did not intend to wait for a second attempt.
