Chapter Ninety-Five

Dark Divinity

Cassel Rhune made it twelve miles.

Zee gave him six.

Then stopped chasing.

Cassel realized too late.

The Godslayer entered an abandoned mountain shrine and found Zee already sitting on the altar.

Cassel stopped.

“How?”

Zee smiled.

“Assassin.”

“I left false trails.”

“I know.”

“Three.”

“I know.”

Cassel looked toward the entrance behind him.

Then back.

“You stopped following.”

“Yes.”

“When?”

“Six miles ago.”

For the first time, Cassel looked annoyed.

Zee liked that too.

“Your mistake,” Zee said, “was assuming hunting means following.”

Cassel drew his blade with his unbroken hand.

His other arm hung badly.

Dark Divinity crawled through the fracture.

Repairing it slowly.

Not healing.

Forcing function.

Interesting.

Zee stood.

The wounds Cassel gave him still had not fully closed.

Face.

Arm.

Side.

Black edges.

Pain remained.

He had not felt this damaged for years.

It made him vicious.

Not uncontrolled.

Vicious.

Different.

⸻

Cassel attacked first.

The shrine exploded.

Black Divinity cut through old wards.

Zee threw himself sideways.

Silent magic collapsed a pillar.

Cassel sliced through it.

Zee expected that.

The falling stone hid the knife.

Assassin blade entered Cassel’s thigh.

Cassel snarled.

Zee appeared behind him.

Claws opened his back.

Cassel spun.

Black blade through Zee’s stomach.

Both stopped.

Close.

Bleeding.

Cassel whispered:

“You cannot heal.”

“Currently.”

“You cannot adapt to Dark Divinity.”

“Yet.”

Cassel’s eyes hardened.

“That word is why I came.”

Zee grabbed his wrist.

“Then you still do not understand me.”

He slammed Cassel into the altar.

Again.

Again.

Stone broke.

The Godslayer drove black force through Zee’s chest.

Zee staggered.

Cassel pulled the blade free.

For the first time since his newborn death—

Zee’s body felt quiet.

No rapid healing.

No immediate adaptation.

No easy answer.

Cassel saw it.

“You feel mortal.”

Zee looked down at the wound.

Then laughed.

Cassel frowned.

“What is funny?”

“I was never immortal because I healed quickly.”

The shrine shook.

Crimson-and-gold eyes lifted.

“I am immortal because death already tried.”

Zee stepped forward.

“And lost.”

Cassel struck.

Zee caught the sword between both hands.

Dark Divinity burned through his palms.

Flesh split.

Smoke rose.

Zee did not release it.

Cassel pushed harder.

“You cannot overpower this.”

“I do not need to.”

Zee twisted.

Not strength.

Angle.

Nanda Parbat.

The blade moved half an inch.

Cassel’s balance broke.

Zee headbutted him.

Horn opened his brow.

Then knee.

Elbow.

Throat.

Pressure point.

Cassel’s sword dropped.

Zee kicked it across the shrine.

Without the weapon, Cassel was still dangerous.

Dark Divinity erupted directly from his hands.

Zee crossed his arms.

The force hit.

Scales shattered.

He flew into the altar.

Cassel moved to retrieve the blade.

Zee smelled blood.

Tracked the heartbeat.

Saw the limp.

Calculated.

Decision.

He threw his own knife.

It entered Cassel’s sword hand.

Pinned it to the floor.

Cassel screamed.

Zee walked toward him.

Slowly.

Cassel looked up.

“You enjoy this.”

“Yes.”

“You learned nothing from Rell.”

Zee stopped.

That irritated him.

Then he smiled.

“No.”

Cassel pulled against the knife.

Zee crouched.

“I learned exactly what I needed.”

He grabbed Cassel’s hair and forced his face upward.

“At Rell, violence chose for me.”

Zee’s fangs descended.

“Right now?”

His eyes became slits.

“I am choosing every second.”

Cassel finally understood the difference.

And feared it.

Zee smelled that.

Loved it.

Accepted that he loved it.

Then continued.

⸻

Cassel ripped his hand free.

Dark Divinity detonated.

The shrine roof shattered.

Night opened above them.

Zee landed outside among snow and broken stone.

Cassel emerged with his black blade again.

Blood everywhere.

Darkness crawling over him.

Not Demon darkness.

Not corrupted God power.

Something rarer.

Older.

Anti-divine.

It reacted to the remnants of the dead god Zee had consumed years ago.

Not because Zee had become divine.

Because traces of that history remained written into his evolved blood.

Dark Divinity burned toward it.

Crimson biology responded.

Not by absorbing.

Not copying.

Rejecting access.

Zee felt the difference.

His blood could not claim Dark Divinity.

But it could begin learning not to let that force reach so deeply.

Tiny.

Incomplete.

Enough.

The next black strike entered his shoulder.

Still terrible.

But healing did not stop completely.

Cassel saw.

“No.”

Zee smiled.

“There it is.”

“You adapted.”

“A little.”

“That should be impossible.”

“So am I.”

Cassel roared and attacked.

The final fight became ugly.

No clean magic.

No speeches.

Cassel shattered two of Zee’s ribs.

Zee broke Cassel’s knee.

Dark Divinity burned half the scales from Zee’s left arm.

Zee tore three fingers from Cassel’s sword hand.

Cassel stabbed Zee through the heart.

Zee looked down.

Then at him.

Cassel froze.

The heart kept beating around the blade.

Damaged.

Slower.

Still beating.

Zee smiled.

“That usually works better.”

Cassel tried pulling away.

Zee caught him.

“No.”

One hand closed around Cassel’s throat.

The other seized the blade still through Zee’s chest.

Zee pulled it sideways.

Out of Cassel’s grip.

Then threw it beyond the cliff.

Cassel stared.

His Godslayer weapon disappeared into darkness.

Zee’s hand tightened.

Cassel whispered:

“You should kill me.”

“Yes.”

“You know what I am.”

“Yes.”

“If you let me live, I come back.”

“I know.”

Cassel smiled through blood.

“Then do it.”

Zee considered him.

Evidence.

Choice.

Threat.

Cassel had come specifically to kill Zee for what he might become.

He had attacked first.

Would return.

No confusion.

No rage deciding for him.

Zee made the decision.

Then stopped.

Not mercy.

Information.

“How does someone become a Godslayer?”

Cassel laughed weakly.

“You want it.”

“No.”

“Everyone does.”

“I asked how.”

Cassel studied him.

Then answered.

“Darkness.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“I have that.”

“Not enough.”

Cassel’s gaze dropped toward Zee’s blood.

“Dark Divinity chooses very few.”

“Divinity?”

“Anti-divinity.”

“Can it be stolen?”

“No.”

“Drained?”

“No.”

“Passed through blood?”

Cassel smiled.

“You tried.”

Zee’s expression flattened.

Fair.

Cassel continued.

“It requires a heart dark enough to hold it without becoming its servant.”

Zee looked at the black burns across his body.

“Sounds irritating.”

Cassel actually laughed.

Then coughed blood.

“You are not a Godslayer, Zee Darkheart.”

“I know.”

“You have no Dark Divinity.”

“I know.”

“And killing me will not give you mine.”

Zee smiled.

“Good.”

Cassel frowned.

“Good?”

“I already have enough problems.”

Then Zee broke his neck.

Quick.

Deliberate.

Cassel Rhune died.

The darkness inside him did not enter Zee.

It withdrew.

Black light left the corpse like smoke moving backward and vanished into the night.

Zee watched.

No feeding.

No attempt to take it.

No need.

Some powers were not his.

That was fine.

⸻

Zareth found Zee before dawn.

Human form.

He stared at the wounds.

“You look horrible.”

Zee smiled.

“Ner said the same.”

“Ner has eyes.”

Zareth touched one black wound near Zee’s shoulder.

Zee slapped his hand away.

“That hurts.”

Zareth stared.

“You are complaining about pain?”

“I complain constantly.”

“Not accurately.”

Through the Link came concern beneath the humor.

Zee felt it.

Then Zareth felt Zee’s answer.

Pain.

Satisfaction.

Control.

Choice.

All present.

You did not disappear.

Zee looked toward him.

No.

You enjoyed killing him.

Yes.

Zareth’s mouth twitched.

Good.

Zee raised an eyebrow.

Zareth continued aloud.

“Because you knew why.”

There.

Exactly.

⸻

Darkheart healers needed nine days to close every wound Cassel left.

Nine days.

For Zee.

That terrified the kingdom.

A weapon existed that could slow him.

A force existed that could scar supernatural bodies in ways ordinary magic could not.

Godslayers existed.

Plural, according to Cassel’s journals.

Rare.

Not unified.

Not an order.

Not one organization.

Individuals.

Different motives.

Different gods.

Different darkness.

That was worse.

And better.

The world remained larger than Zee.

He preferred it that way.

Ner found him reading Cassel’s recovered journal late one night.

“Planning to become one?”

“No.”

“Good.”

“I hate gods enough already.”

Ner sat beside him.

“Could you?”

Zee thought about Cassel’s words.

Dark heart.

Dark Divinity.

Rare selection.

Then shook his head.

“Doesn’t matter.”

“Why?”

Zee closed the book.

“Because I do not need every power that can hurt me.”

Ner smiled faintly.

“Growth.”

Zee glared.

“Say that again and I bite you.”

Ner leaned closer.

“You say that like a threat.”

Zee kissed him instead.

Then returned to the journal.

Outside, Darkheart continued growing.

Inside, Zee carried three black scars that had not disappeared.

For the first time in years, an enemy had left permanent marks on his body.

Zee did not hate them.

He liked remembering.

Not that Cassel almost killed him.

He could not.

But that something in the world had still been strong enough to make King Zee Darkheart bleed and stay bleeding.

The world remained dangerous.

Good.

So did he.
