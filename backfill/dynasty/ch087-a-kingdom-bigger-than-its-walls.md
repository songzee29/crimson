Chapter Eighty-Seven

A Kingdom Bigger Than Its Walls

Darkheart rebuilt the eastern village before summer.

Then ran out of houses.

People kept coming.

Thirty Wolves from a broken northern pack.

Seventeen Vampires expelled by a religious court.

Forty-three Witches whose kingdom had outlawed mixed marriages.

Children.

Parents.

Grandparents.

People carrying furniture on carts because they intended to stay.

Merek placed the numbers before Zee.

“We cannot keep putting everyone inside the old borders.”

Zee looked at the map.

“Then stop.”

Merek waited.

Zee pointed beyond Victor’s original boundary stones.

“Build there.”

“That land was never formally part of House Darkheart.”

Zee looked through the window.

Families were already putting tents on it.

“It is now.”

Merek sighed.

“You make territorial law sound very simple.”

“People live there.”

“That is not how borders work.”

Zee smiled.

“It is becoming how mine work.”

⸻

The new settlement had no name yet.

Kael suggested one.

Everyone hated it.

Amara suggested another.

Worse.

Tessa banned all three from naming anything.

So the people called it Southhold until someone came up with something better.

Darkheart soldiers helped build.

Witches raised stone.

Vampires worked through the night.

Wolves cleared forest and roads.

Nobody waited for Zee.

That was the point.

Zee visited three days later.

A little girl handed him a hammer.

He stared at it.

“What am I supposed to do with this?”

“Help.”

Zee looked toward Ner.

Ner smiled.

“You heard her.”

Zee Darkheart.

King.

First Natural Tribrid.

Crimson Demonic Dragon.

Blood Hunter.

Spent the next hour building a roof badly.

Zareth arrived in human form halfway through.

He watched Zee hit his thumb.

Then said:

“I have seen gods fight with greater dignity.”

Zee looked at him.

“Leave.”

“No.”

Through the Link came pure amusement.

Zee sent back an image of throwing him into the river.

Zareth answered mentally:

You know I can fly.

Not in human form.

Zareth smiled aloud.

“I can shift.”

Ner looked between them.

“You are doing the private argument again.”

“Yes,” Zee and Zareth said together.

Ner sighed.

Then the first explosion hit.

⸻

The western edge of Southhold disappeared in fire.

Zee was moving before the sound finished.

Not kingdom troops.

Hunters.

Thirty.

Maybe forty.

White masks.

No banners.

They were not trying to conquer land.

They were killing refugees.

Different motive.

Again.

Zee reached the first street.

Too late for one house.

A Wolf father lay dead beside the door.

His wife was alive.

Two children under the table.

Zee smelled them.

Alive.

Good.

He stepped back outside.

Human pupils gone.

Slits.

“Who?”

A captured attacker laughed.

“The Pureborn Hand.”

Zee frowned.

Never heard of them.

Good.

The world remained large.

“They hunt mixed households,” Tessa said as she arrived.

“How do you know?”

“I found their marks on bodies last year.”

Zee looked toward her.

“You never mentioned them.”

“They were small.”

Another explosion shook the settlement.

Tessa’s face hardened.

“Apparently not anymore.”

⸻

Zee moved toward the fire.

Then stopped.

Another horn.

Darkheart north wall.

Different attack.

Merek’s signal.

Monster breach.

Two places.

Again.

Old instinct pulled Zee north.

Southhold burned in front of him.

Kael appeared beside him.

“Go.”

Zee looked at him.

Kael’s Witch Mana darkened around his hands.

“Southhold is ours.”

Tessa joined him.

Amara dropped from a rooftop.

Ner stepped beside Zee.

“We can hold this.”

Zee hated the sentence.

Not because he doubted them.

Because he believed them.

That was new.

Zareth’s voice entered through the Link.

I can reach the north wall faster.

Zee received wind, distance, position.

Zareth was already shifting.

Then take north.

Done.

Zee looked at Ner.

“Southhold.”

Ner nodded.

Zee looked toward Darkheart Keep.

A third signal rose.

Red.

Not attack.

Medical emergency.

Too many wounded arriving from both sides.

He made the decision.

“Merek gets the keep.”

Kael smiled faintly.

“And you?”

Zee looked toward the Pureborn hunters.

“You already told me to leave.”

Kael frowned.

“I did not think you would listen.”

“Neither did I.”

Zee stepped backward.

Then stopped.

“Do not die.”

Tessa laughed once.

“Excellent command, Majesty.”

Zee left.

Not to save another battlefield.

To organize the kingdom between them.

That was harder.

⸻

Southhold survived without him.

Kael broke the attackers’ Witch suppressors.

Tessa found civilians through smoke.

Amara hunted the masked commanders.

Ner used Bloodsong carefully enough to freeze attackers without catching terrified civilians in the same command.

Twenty-one hunters died.

Fourteen surrendered.

Darkheart lost six people.

Not none.

Six.

But the settlement remained standing.

At the northern wall, Zareth arrived in true Dragon form.

His voice thundered across the battlements.

“MOVE!”

Darkheart soldiers moved.

Dragonfire struck the monsters climbing from the ravine.

Zareth did not need Zee.

The soldiers did not either.

They held.

At the keep, Zee coordinated healers, reinforcements, evacuation routes, food, replacement patrols, and prisoner separation.

No glorious battle.

No blood hunt.

King work.

He hated parts of it.

Needed all of it.

By midnight, the attacks were over.

Zee reached Southhold last.

Kael sat against a wall.

Tessa was stitching her own arm.

Amara had blood across half her face.

Ner stood beside six covered bodies.

Zee stopped.

Six.

He could have killed every attacker personally and six might still have died.

That truth no longer broke him the same way.

It changed what he built.

He crouched beside the first body.

Then the second.

Learned every name.

⸻

The next morning, Southhold began rebuilding.

Again.

A refugee asked Zee whether they should leave.

Zee looked at him.

“Do you want to?”

“No.”

“Then stay.”

“They will attack again.”

“Yes.”

The man waited.

Zee looked over the damaged settlement.

Kael helping repair a wall.

Tessa arguing with a healer.

Amara carrying lumber.

Ner organizing frightened children.

Zareth in human form speaking with Darkheart officers.

His kingdom.

Not one species.

Not one bloodline.

Not one wall.

Zee’s voice became quiet.

“Then next time we are better.”

The man nodded.

That answer spread.

By autumn, Southhold had doubled.

The Pureborn Hand expected terror to empty it.

Instead, survivors brought cousins.

Friends.

Packmates.

Exiles.

People who heard Darkheart had been attacked and stayed anyway.

Zee stood on the new southern tower when Merek brought the population report.

He read it once.

Then again.

“This cannot be right.”

“It is.”

Darkheart had grown by nearly a thousand people in less than a year.

Merek looked toward the streets below.

“You should probably decide what this kingdom is becoming.”

Zee watched Witch children playing beside Wolf children while a Vampire merchant shouted at both for stealing fruit.

He smiled faintly.

“No.”

Merek frowned.

“No?”

“They are already deciding.”

Below them, Southhold kept building.

And for the first time, Darkheart did not look like an old Witch kingdom changing.

It looked like the beginning of something that had never existed before.
