Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Two

The Father Who Watches

Victor saw Zee holding Zayrlen before Zee ever knew he was there.

Darkheart had changed so much that Victor almost did not recognize it.

The old kingdom had been narrower.

Quieter.

More obedient.

This place breathed differently.

Witches crossed the courtyard beside Wolves.

Vampires stood watch beside Humans.

Children ran between soldiers without fear.

Phoenix feathers occasionally passed overhead when Zee moved between towers.

Darkheart had become something Victor never built.

Something he would once have called unstable.

Something alive.

Victor remained beyond the inner grounds beneath concealment older than most of the wards surrounding the keep.

His evolved Witchcraft bent attention away from him.

Black-crimson flame remained buried beneath his skin.

The Abyssal Titan Phoenix Sovereign inside him stayed quiet.

Mostly.

He had not come to attack.

Not yet.

He had come to see.

Then Zee entered the western courtyard carrying his son.

Victor stopped breathing.

⸻

Zee still looked injured.

The ambush had not vanished from him.

One Phoenix wing remained uneven where feathers continued regenerating.

A dark fracture line still marked one horn.

The bruising around his left eye had almost faded but not completely.

New Dragon scales were still filling in along his ribs beneath the open edge of his coat.

He moved normally.

Almost.

Victor noticed the difference.

A father notices things even after failing to deserve the word.

Zee carried Zayrlen against his chest.

One hand supported the baby’s head automatically.

The other held beneath him.

No servant.

No nurse.

No guard.

Zee.

Zayrlen moved.

Zee adjusted instantly.

Tiny change.

Barely visible.

Victor saw it.

Then the baby grabbed Zee’s finger.

Zee stopped walking.

Looked down.

“Sir.”

Zayrlen tightened his grip.

Ner walked beside him and laughed.

“He is not listening.”

“He is disrespecting the King.”

“He cannot sit upright.”

“That is unrelated.”

Zayrlen released Zee’s finger.

Then reached higher.

His tiny hand found Zee’s horn.

Victor’s eyes widened.

Zee froze.

The baby grabbed it.

Hard.

Ner started laughing immediately.

Zee stared at his son.

“Absolutely not.”

Zayrlen pulled.

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“Sir.”

Ner laughed harder.

“He likes it.”

“It is attached to my skull.”

“He does not care.”

Zayrlen pulled again.

Zee sighed.

Then adjusted his grip so the baby’s neck remained supported.

He did not move the hand away.

Did not frighten him.

Did not snap.

Did not even raise his voice.

He simply stood there while his two-month-old son held one of the most threatening parts of his body like a toy.

Victor felt something inside his chest twist.

Years ago, he had looked at impossible eyes.

Tiny fangs.

Blood rain.

A child that frightened the world by existing.

And he had chosen murder.

Zee looked at an impossible child and let him pull on his horn.

The difference hurt worse than Victor expected.

⸻

Ner leaned closer.

“He is getting sleepy.”

“I know.”

“You heard it?”

“His breathing changed.”

Victor’s expression tightened.

Zee had noticed before Ner finished speaking.

The baby’s heartbeat.

Breathing.

Tiny movement.

Everything.

Zee lowered his voice.

“Sleep.”

No Witch command.

Just father.

Zayrlen stared at him.

Then yawned.

Zee smiled.

Small.

Barely there.

Victor almost hated him for that smile.

Not because it was weakness.

Because Zee had something Victor had thrown away.

⸻

Victor left the observation point before Zee could look toward him.

Not because Zee had sensed him.

Because Victor needed distance.

He walked deeper into the old forest beyond Darkheart’s western edge.

Pride fought guilt.

Guilt fought habit.

Habit won first.

He needed certainty.

That was what Victor told himself.

He needed to know whether what he had seen was real.

Whether Zee’s gentleness survived pressure.

Whether the monster disappeared when the child became inconvenient.

Whether fear changed him.

Victor had spent years building excuses around his own failure.

The prophecy.

The blood rain.

The world’s reaction.

Zee’s immortality.

The impossible combination inside a newborn body.

All true.

None enough.

Still, Victor clung to one final lie.

Perhaps anyone would have broken under enough fear.

Perhaps Zee simply had not been tested correctly.

Victor looked back toward Darkheart.

Then black-crimson flame rose over his fingers.

Scarlet beneath.

He closed his hand.

One test.

Deeply wrong.

He knew that.

He did it anyway.

Because Victor Darkheart was still Victor Darkheart.

⸻

The first fire appeared in the eastern watchtower.

No spark.

No caster.

No projectile.

One second the tower stood cold.

The next—

black-crimson flame erupted across the upper stone.

Scarlet depth burned inside it like blood trapped beneath darkness.

The fire moved against the wind.

Darkheart alarms rang instantly.

Then three more fires appeared.

Southern wall.

Old council roof.

Western barracks.

No conventional trajectory.

No visible attacker.

Witch sigils burned inside the flames.

Ancient.

Unfamiliar.

Every nearby ward reacted differently.

Some cracked.

Some dimmed.

One completely reversed itself and tried sealing the fire inside.

The fire ate the ward.

Zee looked up from the courtyard.

Zayrlen still in his arms.

His expression changed instantly.

Father vanished behind King.

Not completely.

Never completely.

“Ner.”

Ner was already moving.

He took Zayrlen.

Pure Gold Mana rose around them.

Rez appeared from the lower passage.

“What?”

“Unknown fire. Multiple positions.”

Elias arrived seconds later.

Deep-blue Mana spread through the courtyard.

Black sigils appeared.

His face changed.

“That is not Phoenix ash behavior.”

Zareth’s presence struck Zee through the Dragon Soul Link from elsewhere in Darkheart.

I feel it.

Zee looked toward the western tower.

Do you recognize it?

No.

Good.

Bad.

Both.

⸻

“Rez,” Zee said.

“Evacuating affected sections.”

“Keep civilians away from fire.”

“Already.”

“Elias.”

“Studying.”

“Ner.”

“I have Zayrlen.”

Zee’s eyes sharpened.

That was all he needed.

He launched.

His damaged Phoenix wing opened.

Pain.

Then something strange happened.

The wing caught black-crimson fire.

Not Victor’s flame.

Zee’s.

Scarlet blood-fire spread through the damaged feathers.

The torn sections regenerated faster.

Not instantly.

Far faster than they had the day before.

Zee stopped midair.

His heartbeat changed.

One deep strike.

Then another.

He felt it beneath his ribs.

Ancient heat.

Different from Dragon.

Different from acquired Crimson Phoenix.

His cracked horn burned.

The fracture line sealed another inch.

New feathers emerged along the injured wing.

Not Crimson.

Black-crimson.

Scarlet veins glowing through them.

Zee stared.

“What the hell?”

Then another tower ignited.

No time.

He moved.

⸻

Zee hit the first fire with Crimson Phoenix flame.

His normal fire should have collided.

Instead—

the two flames recognized each other.

Not identical.

Related.

The black-crimson fire curved toward him.

Zee’s own Phoenix blood surged.

Scarlet spread across his wings.

His eyes flared violently Crimson-Gold.

The heat doubled.

Then tripled.

Stone beneath him cracked.

Elias shouted from below:

“Zee!”

“I know.”

“No, you do not.”

Fair.

Zee forced the fire outward.

Too much.

A wave of scarlet-black Phoenix flame exploded across the roof.

It burned through the strange attack.

Then kept going.

Zee had to slam both hands together.

“Stop.”

Witchcraft caught his own fire.

Barely.

The flames shuddered.

Then folded inward.

Zee landed hard.

His expression was furious.

Not because of the attack.

Because his own power had failed to obey cleanly.

⸻

Victor watched from beyond the outer wall.

He had expected reaction.

He had expected recognition.

He had not expected this.

His own Abyssal Titan Phoenix Sovereign fire had touched Zee’s presence—

and Zee’s blood had answered like dry forest meeting lightning.

Black-crimson feathers.

Scarlet flame.

Accelerated regeneration.

Phoenix pressure thick enough that Victor felt it through concealment.

His son’s biology was not simply awakening the inheritance.

It was changing it.

Victor’s eyes narrowed.

“No.”

Not rejection.

Disbelief.

The ancient bloodline inside Victor had always felt vast.

Old.

Complete.

Zee’s version did not feel complete.

It felt hungry.

Transformative.

His acquired Crimson Phoenix blood was fusing with the inheritance instead of yielding to it.

Victor understood one thing immediately.

This was becoming something the original bloodline had never been.

⸻

Zee extinguished the third fire.

Then the fourth.

No civilians dead.

Several wounded by collapsing wards.

Darkheart intact.

Then Victor moved the attack.

Closer.

A black-crimson sigil appeared above the royal residence.

Zee saw it.

His entire body changed.

The sigil turned.

A spear of scarlet-black flame formed beneath it.

Pointed directly toward the nursery level.

Zee did not calculate.

Did not assign defense.

Did not think about walls.

“Ner.”

Bloodsong answered from inside.

Here.

Zee moved.

⸻

He entered through the balcony.

Ner already had Zayrlen.

Pure Gold barriers layered around them.

The baby had started fussing.

Not combat.

Not power.

Just instinct responding to the pressure.

Tiny Crimson-Gold scales flickered across his shoulder.

Zee stepped between the balcony and his family.

“Behind me.”

Ner did not argue.

He moved one step back.

Not because he was helpless.

Because Zee was physically between the attack and the child.

That was the correct position.

Zee opened both Phoenix wings.

One still healing.

Not anymore.

The strange paternal heat surged.

Black-crimson feathers tore through the remaining damaged sections.

Scarlet fire spilled between them.

His wing regenerated almost completely in seconds.

Zee gasped.

The cracked horn sealed.

Blood from a reopened wound along his ribs ignited before reaching the floor.

Scarlet.

His Dragon scales glowed from beneath.

His heartbeat thundered.

Deeper.

Older.

The air around him changed.

Then something enormous appeared behind him.

A Phoenix.

Not physical.

Not complete.

A silhouette made from black-crimson blood-flame.

Predatory.

Colossal.

Head lowered.

Wings wider than the room could contain.

Crimson-Gold eyes burning inside the shape.

Victor saw it from outside.

And went completely still.

That was not his Phoenix.

⸻

The attack descended.

Zee raised Witchcraft first.

“Hold.”

The scarlet-black spear slowed.

Crimson Sovereign followed.

Witch precision.

Vampire reaction.

Wolf protection instinct.

All three unified.

Dragon force grounded his body.

His existing Crimson Phoenix fire rose.

The inherited fire erupted through it.

Zee’s wings wrapped backward around Ner and Zayrlen.

His own body stood in front.

No hesitation.

The spear hit.

The balcony exploded.

Stone shattered.

Wards broke.

Flame swallowed Zee.

Ner tightened Gold around Zayrlen.

Zee took the rest.

The impact forced him backward one step.

Then another.

His feet dug trenches into the floor.

His skin burned.

Regenerated.

Burned again.

Black-crimson feathers grew through the damage as fast as the attack tore them.

His voice changed.

A Phoenix cry lived beneath the sound when he shouted:

“NO.”

The strange fire bent.

Not mastered.

Not controlled elegantly.

Overpowered by instinct.

Father before power.

Zee drove everything outward.

The incoming attack broke around him.

Never touched Ner.

Never touched Zayrlen.

Victor diverted the final force before impact could become lethal.

Zee never knew.

To Zee—

he had simply stopped it.

With his body.

⸻

Then the attack vanished.

All of it.

No final strike.

No visible caster.

No signature remaining long enough to follow.

Zee stood in the ruined nursery balcony breathing hard.

Scarlet-black fire still crawled over his wings.

Some feathers had turned almost entirely black-crimson.

His eyes remained unnaturally bright.

The giant Phoenix silhouette flickered behind him.

Then disappeared.

Ner stepped forward.

“Zee.”

“I am fine.”

Ner stared at the burned remains of Zee’s coat.

“Not doing this again.”

Fair.

Zayrlen cried.

That sound cut through everything.

Zee turned immediately.

Ner handed him over.

The baby settled against Zee’s chest within seconds.

Heartbeat.

Recognition.

Father.

Zee’s expression softened despite the fire still moving across his feathers.

He rocked him carefully.

“Nothing touched you.”

Zayrlen made a tiny sound.

Zee looked down.

“I know.”

Ner looked around at the destroyed balcony.

Then back at Zee.

“You are on fire.”

“Yes.”

“Different fire.”

“Yes.”

“Can you put it out?”

Zee tried.

Nothing happened.

He frowned.

Ner almost laughed.

Did not.

Not yet.

⸻

Far beyond the courtyard, Victor watched one last time.

Zee’s wings still carried black-crimson feathers.

Scarlet fire curled around them without burning the child.

Zayrlen slept against his chest.

Zee adjusted the baby’s head automatically.

Gentle.

Exact.

Victor closed his eyes.

That was the answer.

Threaten the kingdom—

Zee became King.

Threaten his son—

Zee became something else.

Worse.

Better.

Everything Victor had not been.

He turned away.

Then stopped.

Because Zee looked suddenly toward the western forest.

Not directly at Victor.

Close enough.

Zee’s Wolf instincts stirred.

Vampire senses sharpened.

Witch Mana resonated.

Phoenix fire rose without permission.

His blood reacted.

Known.

Impossible.

Known.

Zee frowned.

He stared into the dark.

“What are you?”

Victor left before Zee could find out.

⸻
