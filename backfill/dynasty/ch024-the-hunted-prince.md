Chapter Twenty-Four

The Hunted Prince

Seven Years Later

Zee Darkheart was halfway up the eastern tower of House Vesper when someone tried to shoot him out of the sky.

He heard the bowstring before the arrow left it.

The sound reached him through wind, rain, bells, soldiers shouting from the courtyard below, and the violent beating of wings directly above his head.

Zee twisted.

The arrow passed close enough to cut several strands of black hair from beside one developing horn.

“Rude.”

A second arrow came.

This one never reached him.

Zareth dropped from the clouds like a crimson blade.

His enormous wing swept between Zee and the distant rooftop, knocking the projectile aside before it struck. Teal-and-gold Dragon Mana flashed across crimson scales as the ten-year-old Crown Prince of Drakonis released an angry roar that shook windows throughout the upper palace.

Zee winced.

“I heard it too.”

Zareth’s response entered the Dragon Link immediately.

Arrow. You. Again.

The meaning arrived without true words—anger wrapped around an image of Zee falling from the tower.

Zee rolled his crimson eyes.

“I was not going to fall.”

The Link answered with another image.

Zee.

Falling.

Headfirst.

Three summers earlier.

Into King Lucien’s ornamental pond.

Zee’s expression darkened.

“That was different.”

Zareth sent amusement.

Zee jumped from the tower.

Someone screamed below.

Zareth screamed louder.

For several heartbeats Zee dropped freely through open air, black coat snapping behind him. The small crimson-black horns growing from his temples cut through his windblown hair, and patches of permanent crimson scales along the sides of his neck caught the gray morning light.

He had no wings.

Not yet.

That did not stop him from doing stupid things involving heights.

Crimson Mana gathered beneath his boots.

The air compressed.

Zee hit the roof below, rolled once, and came upright in a crouch.

The assassin was already running.

“Got you.”

The figure glanced over one shoulder.

Black hood.

Leather armor.

Human heartbeat.

No spellwork Zee could sense.

Not every person hunting him was supernatural.

Sometimes ordinary people were simply greedy.

Zee ran.

At ten years old, his body remained small compared with the soldiers training throughout Vesper’s courtyards, but nothing about the way he moved resembled an ordinary child. His vampire nature sharpened the world around him. His wolf instincts read every shift in the assassin’s balance. Crimson Mana moved beneath his skin without waiting for conscious command.

And something hotter lived underneath all three.

Something newer.

Dragon.

Zee felt it whenever Zareth came close.

Whenever anger rose.

Whenever the strange scales along his shoulders expanded farther toward his arms.

He did not understand it.

He hated not understanding things about himself.

The assassin vaulted a stone railing.

Zee followed.

Below them, merchants crossing the lower palace square scattered as both figures landed among overturned baskets and startled horses.

“Move!” the assassin shouted.

A fruit seller threw an apple at him.

Zee almost laughed.

The assassin reached the alley.

Zee accelerated.

Too fast.

His shoulder struck the man’s back.

Both crashed through stacked wooden crates.

The assassin rolled first and drew a short blade.

Zee stood.

Blood ran from a shallow cut across his cheek.

The wound had already begun closing.

The man saw it.

Fear entered his heartbeat.

Zee heard that too.

“You should have stayed on the roof.”

The assassin backed away.

“You really are the monster they say.”

Zee’s smile vanished.

That word still worked.

Monster.

Abomination.

Curse.

Different kingdoms preferred different names.

None bothered him as much as monster.

Because Victor used that one.

Zee’s crimson eyes narrowed.

“Who paid you?”

The man attacked.

Zee moved before the blade reached him.

One hand caught the assassin’s wrist.

Too strong.

The man gasped.

Zee twisted.

Steel hit stone.

The assassin threw a fist.

Zee ducked and drove his shoulder into the man’s stomach.

The impact sent him backward.

Crimson scales spread farther across Zee’s forearm.

The assassin stared.

“What in the hell are you?”

Zee looked down.

More scales.

They always appeared when he became angry.

He hated that too.

“I asked first.”

The assassin reached for something beneath his coat.

Zee smelled metal.

Poison.

He reacted.

Crimson Mana struck the man’s chest and pinned him against the wall.

Not enough to crush.

Enough to stop movement.

The assassin strained.

Zee walked closer.

His juvenile horns glimmered dark crimson near their bases, fading almost black toward their tips.

“Who paid you?”

The man laughed nervously.

“You think there is only one bounty anymore?”

Zee’s face hardened.

Wrong answer.

Zareth landed behind him.

The alley shook.

The assassin looked up.

Then farther up.

Zareth had become terrifying over seven years.

Still young by Dragon standards, but already massive beside human children. Crimson scales covered his body from horn to tail, and teal-and-gold Dragon Mana moved beneath them like living fire.

The Dragon Link carried one simple intention.

Bite?

Zee almost smiled.

“No.”

Disappointment.

The assassin stared between them.

“You speak to it?”

Zee’s expression changed instantly.

“Him.”

The man swallowed.

Zareth lowered his head.

The assassin went pale.

Zee leaned closer.

“That is Crown Prince Zareth Drakonis.”

The man’s heartbeat accelerated.

“And if you call him it again, I might let him bite you.”

Zareth sent delight through the Link.

Zee ignored him.

The assassin looked toward the alley entrance.

Soldiers were coming.

Too late.

He smiled.

“Doesn’t matter.”

Zee frowned.

“What?”

“The arrow was not for you.”

The world stopped.

Zee’s eyes sharpened.

“Who?”

The assassin grinned.

Then bit down.

Zee heard the capsule crack between his teeth.

“No!”

He grabbed the man’s jaw.

Too late.

The assassin collapsed.

Not dead.

His breathing continued.

Sleeping poison.

Zee stared.

A distraction.

The arrows.

The chase.

The whole attack.

Zareth felt Zee’s realization through the Link.

Ner.

Zee turned.

They ran.

⸻

Nereus Azrion hated royal ceremonies.

He hated court robes.

He hated formal introductions.

He especially hated being called Nereus by people who had known him since he was three.

“Stand straight,” the palace steward whispered.

Ner remained exactly as he was.

The steward closed his eyes.

“Nereus.”

“Only my mother gets that tone.”

“You are addressing foreign delegates.”

“They know my name.”

“That is not the point.”

Ner looked across the Vesper receiving hall.

Delegates from three southern witch kingdoms stood beneath banners embroidered with old magical symbols. They had not traveled to see Zee.

For once.

They had come for him.

Pure Golden Mana had become harder to hide as Ner matured. At ten, he could summon it deliberately now, shape barriers with it, and open magical seals ordinary witchcraft could barely touch.

That had created opportunities.

And enemies.

Mostly enemies.

Rez stood beside him wearing Vesper blue and silver.

At seventeen, the prince had become tall enough that Ner hated looking up at him during arguments.

Blue-White Mana moved faintly along Rez’s wrist.

“You look miserable.”

Ner whispered, “I am.”

“Good.”

“Why?”

“Character building.”

Ner glared.

Rez smiled.

Seven years had turned him into exactly what Zee once accused him of becoming.

An older brother.

Annoying.

Protective.

Always present when trouble appeared.

And frequently responsible for the trouble.

A southern witch stepped forward.

Her hair had been braided with small copper charms.

“Nereus Azrion.”

Ner sighed internally.

“Yes?”

“We appreciate House Vesper permitting this meeting.”

Rez answered before Ner could.

“House Vesper permitted nothing.”

The witch looked toward him.

Rez smiled pleasantly.

“Nereus agreed to meet you.”

The distinction mattered.

Ner glanced toward him.

Rez gave the smallest nod.

Your choice.

Ner relaxed.

The witch noticed.

Good.

She changed approach.

“We have studied rare mana expressions throughout our kingdom for generations.”

Ner’s golden eyes narrowed slightly.

“I do not want to be studied.”

“We mean no harm.”

“That sentence usually comes before harm.”

Rez coughed.

The witch frowned.

Ner continued.

“What do you want?”

“To teach you.”

That surprised him.

“My Golden Mana?”

“Yes.”

“You have Golden Mana?”

“No.”

“Then how?”

The woman hesitated.

Ner smiled.

“That is what I thought.”

A second witch stepped forward.

“We know structures that may help you understand your gift.”

“That is different.”

“Yes.”

Ner considered.

He was interested.

That was dangerous.

His mother had taught him curiosity was not the same as trust.

He had learned the lesson beneath Vesper years earlier when a demon had hunted him for exactly what lived inside his hands.

“What would you want in return?”

The witches exchanged looks.

There.

Rez saw it too.

The first woman spoke carefully.

“Access.”

“To what?”

“Your mana.”

Ner’s face hardened.

“No.”

“For observation only.”

“No.”

“Nereus—”

“Ner.”

The room changed.

Only one person regularly called him that.

The southern witch did not understand.

Rez did.

He smiled faintly.

“Nereus is his name.”

The witch frowned.

Ner looked toward her.

“But Ner belongs to Zee.”

The woman clearly did not understand that either.

She would eventually.

A servant entered.

Fast.

Too fast.

Rez noticed.

His hand moved toward his sword.

The servant bowed.

“My Prince.”

“What happened?”

“There has been an attack near the eastern tower.”

Ner stood.

“Zee?”

“Alive.”

Neither boy relaxed.

“Zareth?”

“Alive.”

Rez’s jaw tightened.

“Then why are you running?”

The servant looked toward Ner.

“The palace wards around the southern wing just failed.”

Golden Mana erupted around Ner’s hands.

The foreign witches stepped backward.

Rez drew his sword.

Then every door slammed shut.

The copper charms woven into the visiting witch’s hair ignited.

She looked horrified.

“That is not ours.”

Rez turned.

“Everyone down!”

The floor exploded.

⸻

Zee felt Ner’s fear through no supernatural link.

He did not need one.

He knew his friend.

Seven years gave people a different kind of connection.

Zee and Zareth burst into the receiving hall through the upper balcony just as golden light flooded the chamber.

“Ner!”

Ner stood inside a sphere of Pure Golden Mana.

Rez was beside him.

Three foreign witches were trapped against the far wall beneath collapsing stone.

Something moved beneath the shattered floor.

Not human.

A hand reached upward.

Then another.

Stone-colored creatures climbed from beneath Vesper.

Constructs.

No heartbeats.

No scent.

No blood.

Exactly the kind of enemy Zee’s usual senses hated.

Rez saw him.

“About time!”

Zee jumped.

“You said I was banned from diplomatic meetings!”

“This is no longer diplomatic!”

“That happens around you a lot!”

Rez blasted a construct apart with Blue-White Mana.

Ner shouted, “Can you argue later?”

“No!” both answered.

Ner rolled his eyes.

The nearest construct attacked.

Zee blocked.

Its fist struck his forearms.

Pain traveled through bone.

His scales spread immediately.

Crimson-black plating covered more of his skin.

The impact still hurt.

But less than it should have.

Dragon durability.

Incomplete.

Growing.

Zee shoved the creature back.

Crimson Dragon energy mixed into his Mana for a heartbeat.

The effect shocked him.

Heat.

Dense.

Ancient.

The construct’s stone chest cracked where Zee touched it.

He stared at his own hand.

The scales had spread almost to his elbow.

Zareth saw.

The Dragon Link filled with recognition.

Like me. Not me.

Zee looked at him.

That was the closest Zareth had ever come to describing what was happening.

Not Zareth.

Dragon.

But Zee’s.

“Later.”

Zareth roared.

Together they attacked.

The constructs had been sent for Ner.

That became obvious quickly.

Every one ignored Zee whenever Ner moved.

The southern witches realized it too.

“We did not do this!” one shouted.

Rez looked toward them.

“I believe you!”

The witch blinked.

“You do?”

“If you wanted Ner kidnapped, burying yourselves under a ceiling would be extremely committed!”

Fair.

One construct reached through Ner’s golden barrier.

Its stone hand began absorbing light.

Ner’s expression changed.

“It is eating my magic.”

Zee saw the golden glow draining into carved symbols along the construct’s arm.

Not ordinary stone.

A magical vessel.

Someone had built it specifically for Ner.

Zee’s anger rose.

The crimson scales reached his temple.

His horns burned.

Not with fire.

With pressure.

Zareth felt it.

Warning.

Zee ignored him.

He grabbed the construct.

The thing immediately tried absorbing Crimson Mana instead.

Bad decision.

Its runes flared.

Then cracked.

Zee’s Mana did not behave like Ner’s.

Something crimson and draconic moved through the contact.

The construct’s entire arm shattered.

Zee ripped it away.

Rez stared.

“You have been practicing that?”

“No.”

“That is worse!”

Another construct struck Zee from behind.

He crashed across the floor.

Pain exploded through his ribs.

He heard something crack.

Then begin repairing.

He pushed himself up.

No death.

Never death.

Pain remained.

He hated pain.

Ner’s Golden Mana surged.

“Zee!”

“I am fine!”

“You are bleeding!”

“That happens!”

Ner looked furious.

His sphere expanded.

Golden light struck every construct simultaneously.

Not destroying them.

Revealing something.

Threads.

Thin magical lines stretched from the constructs down through the broken floor.

Control lines.

Ner saw them.

“So that is how.”

He closed his fist.

Pure Golden Mana traveled backward along every thread.

Far beneath the palace, someone screamed.

Every construct stopped.

Rez looked toward Ner.

“What did you do?”

Ner stared at the glowing threads.

“I found who was holding them.”

Zee smiled.

“That was good.”

Ner smiled back.

“Thank you.”

Rez looked offended.

“I have been complimenting you for years.”

“No, you have been insulting me educationally.”

“That is affection.”

Zareth smashed the final construct.

The floor shook.

The three boys looked toward him.

Zareth lifted his head proudly.

Zee laughed.

Then Aurelia entered.

The laughter died immediately.

She saw Zee.

Blood.

Broken ribs healing.

Scales spread across his neck and arms.

Horns glowing faintly crimson-black.

She stopped.

Something old moved behind her eyes.

Recognition.

Fear.

Zee noticed.

“Mother?”

Aurelia crossed the chamber.

She touched one horn.

Very gently.

Zee stiffened.

“You know what this is.”

Her hand stopped.

Rez and Ner looked toward her.

Zareth became completely still.

Aurelia withdrew.

“I know more than I have told you.”

Zee’s expression changed.

“How much?”

“Not here.”

That answer would have worked seven years ago.

Not now.

Zee stepped backward.

“Why?”

Aurelia looked around the destroyed hall.

Foreign witches.

Vesper soldiers.

Lucien entering through the western doors.

Too many ears.

“Because some truths become dangerous once spoken.”

Zee’s crimson eyes hardened.

“So you do know.”

“Zee.”

“My horns started growing when I was five.”

Aurelia said nothing.

“The scales started before that.”

Still nothing.

“Zareth makes them worse.”

The Dragon Link flashed annoyance.

Zee corrected himself.

“Stronger.”

Zareth settled.

Ner stared at him.

“You have been keeping track?”

“Of course.”

Rez looked toward Aurelia.

“He deserves an answer.”

She turned.

“He deserves to survive the answer.”

The room went quiet.

Zee’s anger weakened.

Not disappeared.

Changed.

“Is it about me?”

Aurelia looked directly at him.

“No.”

That surprised everyone.

Then:

“It begins with me.”

Zee stared.

Something in the shape of Aurelia’s face suddenly seemed different.

Not physically.

In memory.

Her unusual resilience.

Her sensitivity to Zareth.

The way Dragon Queen Drakonis had once studied her without understanding why.

Aurelia stepped closer.

“When we are alone.”

Zee wanted to demand more.

He was ten.

So instead he crossed his arms and said the cruelest thing he could think of.

“I am not going to forget.”

Aurelia almost smiled.

“I know.”

Lucien approached.

“The people controlling the constructs escaped.”

Ner turned.

“No.”

Everyone looked at him.

His Golden Mana remained connected to one final thread.

Faint.

Moving.

Ner smiled.

“I can still feel them.”

Rez’s face lit up.

Zee immediately understood.

Aurelia did too.

“No.”

All three boys looked toward her.

Rez tried first.

“My Queen—”

“No.”

Ner tried.

“They attacked me.”

“No.”

Zee tried.

“They are getting away.”

“No.”

Zareth lowered his head.

Aurelia pointed at him.

“Especially no.”

The Dragon Link carried deep offense.

Caldrin Marr appeared in the shattered doorway.

He examined the room.

Then the glowing magical thread running into the underground passages.

Then the children.

Then Aurelia.

“No what?”

Zee smiled.

Aurelia closed her eyes.

Caldrin looked toward the glowing trail.

“Oh.”

His own smile appeared.

“No.”

Zee’s face fell.

Caldrin continued.

“Not without me.”

Aurelia stared at him.

And somewhere beneath House Vesper, the surviving controllers of the stone constructs believed their attack had failed and their escape remained secret.

They had made one mistake.

They attacked children who had spent their entire lives being hunted.

Now Zee Darkheart was ten.

Ner had a trail.

Rez had a sword.

Zareth had wings.

And for the first time, the hunted prince was old enough to decide that he was tired of waiting for the next enemy to find him.

This time…

Zee intended to find them first.
