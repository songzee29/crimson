Chapter Eighteen

The Prince in Crimson Scales

The messenger survived until sunrise.

That was longer than anyone expected.

The Vesper rider lay beneath layers of fur beside the fire while the royal witch worked carefully over wounds gathered across hundreds of miles of desperate travel. His horse had collapsed minutes after reaching the valley. His cloak had frozen solid along one side, and three deep cuts crossed his back where something had nearly pulled him from the saddle.

Aurelia remained beside him.

Zee sat in her lap wrapped in thick furs, old enough now to support himself without immediately falling sideways. The months in the mountains had changed him. His face had begun losing the softness of a newborn. His crimson eyes followed movement with unsettling precision, and his tiny fangs had grown more pronounced whenever hunger or anger stirred them.

Beside him, the crimson hatchling had changed even faster.

His wings had strengthened.

His body was nearly twice the size it had been when he first appeared beside Zee. Teal-and-gold mana occasionally slipped between his scales when he became excited, and he had developed the unfortunate habit of trying to fly indoors.

The tent had already been repaired twice.

Now he stared toward the mountain ridge.

He had not looked away since the messenger spoke.

Something had been killing the hunters before they reached the valley.

Aurelia touched Zee’s back.

“What do you feel?”

He could not answer.

But the hatchling could share enough.

Through their Link came fragments.

High.

Wings.

Watching.

Not fear.

Something stranger.

Recognition without memory.

Zee leaned toward him.

The hatchling pressed his forehead against Zee’s.

The messenger coughed.

Captain Vale immediately looked down.

“Stay with us.”

The man opened his eyes.

“Bodies…”

“We heard.”

“No.”

His breathing rattled.

“You do not understand.”

Caldrin stepped closer.

“That happens frequently. Try anyway.”

The rider swallowed.

“Not all were killed the same way.”

Aurelia frowned.

“What do you mean?”

“Some burned.”

“Some were torn apart.”

“Others…”

His face tightened.

“…were crushed into the mountain.”

Vale looked toward the distant ridges.

“One creature?”

“I never saw it clearly.”

“But?”

“Wings.”

Everyone looked toward the crimson hatchling.

He hissed indignantly.

Caldrin raised an eyebrow.

“No one accused you.”

The hatchling hissed again.

Zee laughed softly.

The messenger tried to continue.

“Last night I saw it.”

Aurelia’s amusement vanished.

“What?”

“A dragon.”

Silence settled around the camp.

Not the little creature beside Zee.

An adult.

Vale looked toward Caldrin.

“You have been scouting these mountains for months.”

“I have.”

“And never saw a dragon?”

“I saw shadows.”

“That would have been useful information.”

“I also saw eagles.”

Vale glared.

Caldrin shrugged.

“The difference becomes important only when one tries to eat you.”

The hatchling suddenly rose.

His wings spread.

Teal-and-gold mana slipped over his crimson body.

The Dragon Link changed.

Near.

Aurelia stood.

“What?”

Zee turned toward the ridge.

His vampire hearing caught it first.

Not a heartbeat.

Wingbeats.

Deep enough to move the air through the valley.

Vale drew his sword.

The royal witch lifted both hands.

Caldrin looked upward.

A shadow crossed the snow.

Then another.

Too large to belong to any bird.

The mountains disappeared beneath wings.

An enormous dragon passed over the valley.

Black scales covered its body, but silver ran along the edges of its wings and horns. Its tail cut through the clouds, and every beat of its wings sent snow exploding from the surrounding ridges.

The Vesper soldiers scattered.

Aurelia did not.

She held Zee tighter.

The crimson hatchling stepped in front of him.

Ridiculously small.

Completely unwilling to move.

The adult dragon circled once.

Then descended.

The impact shook the valley.

Snow rolled from nearby cliffs.

Vale took one step forward.

The dragon’s silver eyes moved toward him.

“Do not,” Aurelia said.

Vale stopped.

The dragon lowered its enormous head.

Not toward Aurelia.

Toward the hatchling.

The little crimson dragon froze.

Something passed through him.

Scent.

Heat.

A shape barely remembered from the first seconds after his shell broke.

The Dragon Link carried it to Zee.

Mountain.

Fire.

Dragons.

Home.

Zee stared at his companion.

The adult dragon released a low sound.

The hatchling answered.

Not with language.

A chirp.

Then a growl.

Then something almost uncertain.

The adult dragon’s body began changing.

Wings folded inward.

Black scales contracted.

Bone shifted beneath dark fire.

Within moments, a tall man stood where the dragon had been.

He wore armor made from overlapping black scales edged in silver. Long dark hair fell across his shoulders. His eyes remained unmistakably draconic.

No one lowered their weapons.

The stranger ignored them.

He looked only at the hatchling.

Then he dropped to one knee.

“Prince Zareth Drakonis.”

Silence.

Aurelia stared.

Vale slowly lowered his sword.

Caldrin blinked.

“Well.”

The hatchling tilted his head.

The stranger’s expression broke.

Not fear.

Relief.

“We found you.”

Aurelia looked down at the crimson dragon who had slept beside Zee from the moment of his birth.

“Prince?”

The man finally looked at her.

“Crown Prince.”

Caldrin quietly muttered, “Of course he is.”

Vale elbowed him.

The stranger rose.

“I am Vaelor Drakonis, Wing Commander of the Royal Guard.”

Aurelia’s eyes narrowed.

“You are certain?”

Vaelor’s expression hardened immediately.

“I watched his egg for three months before he hatched.”

He touched one fist to his chest.

“I stood inside the royal nesting chamber the day he entered the world.”

The hatchling—Zareth—stared at him.

The name seemed to stir something.

Zareth.

Through the Dragon Link came a tiny spark.

Self.

Zee reached toward him.

Zareth immediately turned.

Whatever fragile recognition had drawn him toward Vaelor disappeared beneath something much stronger.

Zee.

Near.

Together.

Vaelor saw it.

His expression changed.

“What is that?”

Aurelia’s body tightened.

“What is what?”

“The connection.”

“We do not know.”

Vaelor looked between them.

“You expect me to believe the Crown Prince of Drakonis vanished from a sealed royal nest and appeared beside your child for no reason?”

“No.”

Aurelia met his stare.

“I expect you to believe we do not know the reason.”

Vaelor studied her.

Then Zee.

The crimson eyes.

Small fangs.

Crimson Mana.

Rumor made flesh.

His hand moved instinctively toward the sword at his side.

Zareth growled.

The Dragon Link snapped with immediate warning.

Danger.

Zee’s Crimson Mana stirred.

Vaelor stopped.

Slowly, he moved his hand away.

“I did not come for your son.”

“Good.”

“I came for ours.”

Aurelia looked at Zareth.

The small dragon had pressed himself against Zee’s leg.

“He has remained beside Zee since the moment they were born.”

“And he belongs in Drakonis.”

“He belongs to himself.”

Vaelor’s eyes narrowed.

“He is an infant.”

“So is Zee.”

“That has not stopped kingdoms from deciding what belongs to them.”

The words silenced him.

Vaelor looked toward the distant mountains.

“The Dragon King and Queen have searched since the day he vanished.”

Something shifted inside Aurelia.

A mother.

A missing child.

For the first time, she saw the other side.

“What did they think happened?”

“We did not know what to think.”

“Did you know where he went?”

“No.”

“Why did it take months to find him?”

Vaelor’s jaw tightened.

“We followed rumors.”

“A crimson hatchling beside the Darkheart child.”

“Most sounded ridiculous.”

Caldrin smiled faintly.

“They usually are.”

Vaelor ignored him.

“Then hunters began carrying drawings.”

He reached beneath his armor and removed a folded bounty notice.

Two figures had been drawn across it.

Zee.

And the crimson hatchling.

Aurelia’s expression darkened.

Vaelor continued.

“Some wanted your child.”

“Others realized a dragon traveled beside him.”

Vale looked toward the ridge.

“The bodies.”

Vaelor met his eyes.

“Yes.”

“You killed them.”

“Some.”

Caldrin raised an eyebrow.

“Some?”

Vaelor looked toward the mountains.

“The others were not mine.”

Silence returned.

Aurelia looked toward the ridge where the mysterious shadow had appeared days earlier.

“If not you…”

“I do not know.”

That answer had become more frightening than most explanations.

Vaelor stepped toward Zareth.

“I am taking the prince home.”

Zareth immediately backed against Zee.

Vaelor stopped.

“Your Highness.”

The hatchling growled.

Caldrin looked delighted.

“I think His Highness disagrees.”

“This is not amusing.”

“It is slightly amusing.”

Vaelor took another step.

Zareth spread his wings.

Teal-and-gold mana flared.

The Dragon Link flooded Zee.

No.

Stay.

Zee reached forward.

Zareth moved to him immediately.

Vaelor stared.

Aurelia saw realization beginning.

Not understanding.

Only recognition that this was not ordinary attachment.

“Try separating them,” she said quietly.

Vaelor looked at her.

“I would not recommend it.”

He ignored the warning.

He moved slowly, kneeling beside Zareth.

The hatchling watched him.

Vaelor extended both hands.

“Come.”

Zareth recognized something in the voice.

Royal command.

Familiar scent.

His own people.

For a moment, he hesitated.

Then Vaelor lifted him.

The Dragon Link stretched.

Zareth cried out.

Zee’s entire body went rigid.

Distance.

Wrong.

Zareth twisted violently.

Vaelor nearly dropped him.

“Easy!”

The hatchling screamed.

Teal-and-gold mana exploded around his body.

Zee screamed too.

Crimson Mana surged through the camp.

The fire flattened.

Snow lifted from the ground.

Aurelia grabbed Zee.

“Bring him back!”

Vaelor stared.

The two infants were not injured.

Yet both reacted as though something essential were being torn apart.

He carried Zareth back.

The instant the hatchling touched Zee, the pressure vanished.

Zareth buried himself against the child.

Zee’s tiny hand closed around his neck.

Together.

The Dragon Link settled.

Vaelor remained motionless.

“What did your son do to him?”

Aurelia’s eyes became dangerous.

“Nothing.”

“He vanished from Drakonis and bonded himself to—”

“He was born the same day as Zee.”

“So?”

“He appeared beside him as a newborn.”

“I know.”

“Then perhaps stop blaming one infant for something neither could possibly understand.”

Vaelor opened his mouth.

Then stopped.

Aurelia continued.

“Your queen lost her son.”

“I understand that.”

“More than you know.”

Her voice softened.

“But if you tear Zareth away from Zee because you are frightened by what you cannot explain, you will become exactly like every king who came for my child.”

Vaelor stared at her.

The comparison offended him.

That was why it worked.

He looked toward the two infants.

Zareth had already calmed.

His wing rested over Zee’s legs.

Vaelor’s expression became grim.

“I cannot return without him.”

“Then do not.”

“My queen will come herself.”

Aurelia nodded.

“Good.”

Vaelor stared.

“You would welcome the Dragon Queen into this valley?”

“No.”

Aurelia looked toward the mountains.

“But I would rather speak to a mother than another soldier.”

Vaelor almost smiled.

Almost.

He stepped back.

Then raised one hand toward the sky.

Silver mana gathered above his palm.

The royal witch tensed.

“What are you doing?”

“Calling home.”

The silver light shot upward.

It passed through the clouds.

For a moment, nothing happened.

Then the heavens answered.

One roar.

Distant.

Another from farther east.

Then another.

Vale slowly looked upward.

Caldrin sighed.

“I miss the Gravehounds.”

Aurelia stared at the clouds.

Shapes began appearing beyond them.

Wings.

Many.

Zareth lifted his head.

The Dragon Link filled with emotions too large for him to understand.

Home.

Blood.

Fire.

Family.

And beside all of them remained one feeling stronger than the rest.

Zee.

The Dragon Kingdom had finally found its missing prince.

And it was coming to take him home.
