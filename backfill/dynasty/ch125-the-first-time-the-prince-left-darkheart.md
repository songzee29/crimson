Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Five

The First Time the Prince Left Darkheart

Zayrlen left Darkheart for the first time at two months old.

Zee hated the idea.

Which was exactly why Ner decided they were doing it.

“He cannot spend his entire infancy inside one kingdom.”

“He is two months old.”

“Yes.”

“He does not know what a kingdom is.”

“Then this will be educational.”

“He cannot hold his own head up for long.”

“Zee.”

“No.”

Ner stared.

Zee corrected immediately.

“I would prefer not.”

“Better.”

“Still no.”

Ner smiled.

“You do understand you are not winning.”

“Yes.”

That was the problem.

⸻

The destination was Vesper.

Safe.

Close.

Family.

Rez had been demanding a visit since the birth.

Demanding was generous.

Threatening was more accurate.

His last message had read:

If you do not bring my nephew soon, I am coming to collect him myself.

Zee had replied:

Try.

Rez responded:

I raised you better than this.

Completely false.

Still irritating.

So they went.

Ner carried Zayrlen.

Zee flew above part of the route.

Then landed because Ner accused him of scouting every bush.

He was.

Amara led a Wolf escort.

Kael rode near the rear.

Elias came because he claimed Vesper owed him replacement books.

Rhyven came because Rez had apparently promised him food.

Zareth joined midway through the route without invitation.

Naturally.

Zee looked at him.

“Why are you here?”

“I am traveling.”

“To Vesper.”

“Yes.”

“At the exact same time.”

“Yes.”

“To see Zayrlen.”

Zareth looked offended.

“That is speculative.”

Through the Link came:

Obviously.

Zee ignored him.

⸻

The road remained quiet.

No attack.

No hidden threat.

No kidnapping.

That itself felt suspicious.

Zee checked the tree line every few minutes.

Ner noticed.

“You are doing it again.”

“What?”

“Expecting the world to ruin something.”

Zee looked toward him.

Ner adjusted Zayrlen against his chest.

“He is allowed to have a boring trip.”

Zee looked at their son.

Zayrlen slept through the entire conversation.

“Boring feels irresponsible.”

Ner smiled.

“That might be the most Zee sentence you have ever said.”

⸻

Vesper saw them coming.

Mostly because Zee’s enormous Crimson Phoenix wings were difficult to miss.

The gates opened.

Blue-White banners moved in the wind.

Rez stood in the courtyard before their horses fully stopped.

He did not greet Zee.

Did not greet Ner.

He walked directly toward Zayrlen.

“Give him to me.”

Ner laughed.

Zee frowned.

“You could say hello.”

Rez looked at him.

“Hello.”

Then at Ner.

“Hello.”

Then back at Zayrlen.

“Give him to me.”

Ner handed him over.

Zee stared.

“That was fast.”

“You have had him for two months.”

“He is my son.”

Rez smiled.

“And my nephew.”

The word settled warmly.

No hidden meaning spoken aloud.

No dangerous truth.

Just Zayrlen.

Rez held him carefully.

Zayrlen opened his eyes.

Crimson-Gold.

Then grabbed Rez’s coat.

Rez smiled.

“There he is.”

Zee’s expression shifted.

Something old moved quietly in his chest.

Rez had done this before.

With another son.

Another child who knew him as Uncle Rez.

No one else remembered why that mattered.

Rez did.

He looked at Zee for half a heartbeat.

That was enough.

Then he turned his attention fully to Zayrlen.

No comparison.

No stolen moment.

Good.

⸻

Vesper welcomed the Prince without ceremony.

That had been Zee’s condition.

Lucien was gone from the story’s active center now, but Vesper still carried the culture he had built.

Blue-White flame lit the central courtyard.

Children gathered.

Witches whispered.

Some stared at Zee’s wings.

More stared at Zayrlen.

One child asked loudly:

“Is he dangerous?”

Zee answered:

“Yes.”

Ner answered:

“He is a baby.”

Both at once.

The child looked confused.

Rez smiled.

“Both answers are correct.”

Zayrlen sneezed.

A tiny Crimson-and-Gold spark hit Rez’s shoulder.

The child laughed.

“Dangerous.”

Zee looked smug.

Ner gave up.

⸻

The important moment happened in the old Vesper gardens.

Rez carried Zayrlen beneath trees Zee remembered from childhood.

Zee and Ner walked behind him.

Zareth had disappeared somewhere with Elias and Kael.

Probably causing expensive damage.

Amara and Rhyven were arguing near the wall.

For once, nothing was on fire.

Rez stopped beside a fountain.

Zayrlen stared at the water.

Zee stopped too.

Water.

Aidan.

Great Vesper River.

Maternal inheritance.

A boy saying:

It knows me.

The memory hit sharply.

Rez felt Zee go still.

Ner noticed only the change in posture.

“What?”

Zee looked at the fountain.

“Nothing.”

Ner studied him.

Different from years ago.

He knew when not to push.

Rez turned slightly.

Zayrlen reached toward the water.

Nothing happened.

Good.

No Ocean.

No Abyss.

No Siren inheritance.

Different son.

Different blood.

The fountain remained ordinary.

Rez looked at Zee.

Zee nodded once.

The moment passed.

⸻

Then Zayrlen growled.

Tiny.

Ridiculous.

Unmistakable.

Everyone froze.

Ner looked at him.

“Was that a growl?”

Rez smiled.

“Yes.”

Zee stepped closer.

Zayrlen stared at a statue beside the fountain.

A carved Wolf.

His pupils narrowed.

Slit.

Tiny claws appeared.

Then came the pack response.

Not distress.

Recognition.

The Vesper garden carried old Wolf scent from visiting allies.

Zayrlen reacted.

His true Wolf nature remained far from awakening.

But instinct was becoming more expressive.

Rez held him closer.

The growl stopped.

Zee looked at him.

“Do that again.”

Ner stared.

“He is not a performance.”

“I want to hear it.”

“He is two months old.”

“Still.”

Rez laughed.

Zayrlen immediately growled again.

Everyone stopped.

Rez looked smug.

Zee looked betrayed.

Ner laughed.

“Apparently he likes Uncle Rez.”

Zee folded his arms.

“Temporary.”

⸻

That evening, Vesper held dinner.

Nothing political.

Just family.

Rez sat with Zayrlen asleep against his chest for almost the entire meal.

Zee kept watching him.

Finally Elias noticed.

“You are jealous.”

“No.”

“You are.”

“No.”

Kael looked toward Rez.

“He has been holding the baby for an hour.”

“One hour and twelve minutes,” Zee said.

Silence.

Elias smiled.

“You counted.”

“No.”

Everyone laughed.

Zee regretted bringing any of them.

Zareth looked toward the sleeping Prince.

“He should visit Drakonis next.”

Zee answered immediately.

“No.”

Ner answered:

“Eventually.”

Zareth smiled.

Zee looked betrayed.

Again.

⸻

The attack came after dinner.

Not for Zayrlen.

Not for Zee.

Not for Vesper.

A meteor struck the western fields.

Actual meteor.

The impact shook the kingdom.

Every ward screamed.

Rez stood instantly.

Zayrlen woke and started crying.

Zee was beside him before the second tremor.

Ner took Zayrlen.

Rez’s Blue-White Mana filled the hall.

“What was that?”

Zareth’s Dragon senses reached farther.

His expression changed.

“Not stone.”

Zee looked toward him.

“What?”

Zareth turned toward the west.

“Alive.”

Silence.

Beyond the windows, something enormous moved inside the crater.

Not Demon.

Not Dragon.

Not Phoenix.

Not anything Zee recognized.

Good.

The world was still capable of surprising him without tying every threat to his bloodline.

Rez handed control of the inner keep to Vesper guards.

Zee looked at Ner.

Ner looked toward the crying baby.

Then at him.

“Go.”

Zee nodded.

No hesitation this time.

Progress.

He left his son with Ner in Vesper.

Protected.

Known.

Loved.

Then Zee, Rez, Zareth, Elias, Kael, Amara, and Rhyven went to find out what had fallen from the sky.

⸻

The crater was enormous.

The thing inside it was larger.

Black shell.

Six limbs.

Translucent sections showing blue light inside.

No recognizable blood.

No familiar Mana.

No scent Zee could classify.

Zareth stared.

“This is not from here.”

Rez looked toward the sky.

“Meaning?”

Zareth looked at Zee.

“Not this world.”

Silence.

Zee’s expression sharpened.

Beyond the stars.

The possibility had always existed.

Now something had landed in Vesper.

The creature rose.

Its shell unfolded.

Blue light spread across the field.

Zee’s Phoenix wings opened.

Crimson flame answered.

The creature looked directly at him.

Then spoke in a language none of them knew.

Except Elias.

His new Demon-Witch sigils reacted.

Not translation.

Pattern recognition.

He stared.

“What?”

Elias looked toward Zee.

“I think it asked what you are.”

Zee smiled.

Crimson-Gold eyes brightened.

“Tell it I am busy.”

Rez looked at him.

“You cannot be serious.”

The creature charged.

Zee launched.

“Apparently it understood.”

And for the first time in the Age of New Blood—

the danger coming toward Darkheart had not come from another kingdom.

It had come from beyond the world.
