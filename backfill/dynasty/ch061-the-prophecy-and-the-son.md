Chapter Sixty-One

The Prophecy and the Son

At twenty-one, Zee Darkheart entered Castle Darkheart without being invited.

No one stopped him.

That was the first difference.

At fifteen, guards had watched his horns.

At twenty-one, they watched his hands.

Mature crimson-black horns swept back from his temples in dark arcs polished naturally by age and battle.

Permanent Crimson scales covered his forearms, parts of his neck, shoulders, ribs, upper spine, and patches along his jaw.

His body had finished growing into something years of fighting had already prepared—tall, powerful without excess, every movement economical.

The ridges beneath his shoulder blades had become pronounced structures under skin and scale.

Still closed.

Still no wings.

Still no full Dragon.

Still the First Natural Tribrid carrying a fourth nature that had never needed a new title to make itself real.

His crimson eyes remained unmistakably his.

Everything else about the way he looked at people had changed.

A guard near the entrance shifted his grip.

Zee noticed.

Dominant right hand.

Old left shoulder injury.

Two knives.

One anti-Vampire charm.

Fear elevated pulse.

No intention to attack.

Zee walked past.

Twenty-one had made killing possibilities quieter.

He no longer needed to stare at them.

They existed in the background of every room.

Rez walked at his right.

Twenty-one.

Crown Prince of Vesper.

No boy remained in the way he wore blue-white ceremonial armor or measured the Darkheart court with a future ruler’s patience.

Ner walked at Zee’s left.

Twenty-one.

Pure Golden Mana barely visible beneath his skin.

Vampire stillness and Witch presence coexisting with something stranger in his voice.

Years of Bloodsong development had taught him enough control that entire rooms no longer accidentally matched his heartbeat when he became emotional.

Usually.

His hand brushed Zee’s once as they entered.

Deliberate.

Zee did not look over.

He squeezed Ner’s fingers once.

Released.

Zareth came through the sky.

Twenty-one.

No longer the young Dragon that had barely fit inside Vesper courtyards.

The Crown Prince of Drakonis crossed over Castle Darkheart like a living crimson storm, Teal-and-Gold power flowing between immense scales.

He did not land.

He circled.

Independent.

Watching.

The Dragon Link opened.

Victor.

Below.

Fear.

Zee felt it too.

Not magically.

His Vampire senses could hear his father’s heart from the throne hall.

Fast.

Too fast.

Victor knew.

Zee had brought no army.

That frightened the King more.

The throne room doors opened.

Zee entered.

Twenty-one years collapsed into one room.

Victor Darkheart sat beneath the blackened crest.

Older.

Much older.

The crown looked heavier than it had six years before.

The court gathered along both sides.

Some loyal to Victor.

Some secretly to Zee.

Some loyal only to survival.

The imprisoned supporters had been moved from the cells that morning.

Victor had expected an assault.

He had placed them behind the throne under guard.

Human shields without calling them that.

Zee saw.

His face remained calm.

Victor’s fingers tightened on the throne.

“You came.”

Zee stopped at the center of the hall.

“You knew I would.”

Victor looked toward Rez.

Then Ner.

Then upward as Zareth’s shadow crossed the high windows.

“Always surrounded.”

Zee’s expression did not change.

“No.”

He looked at the prisoners.

“I came for them.”

Victor laughed weakly.

“Of course.”

“Release them.”

“No.”

Zee tilted his head.

No anger.

No threat.

That was worse now.

Victor knew.

“Then we finish.”

A murmur moved through the hall.

Rez did not speak.

Ner did not.

This was Zee’s.

Victor stood.

“You believe this is about prisoners?”

“No.”

“Then what?”

“You.”

The answer landed simply.

Victor descended the throne steps.

Twenty-one years earlier he had carried a blade toward a newborn.

Now he approached a grown man who could hear every change in his heartbeat.

The irony was almost cruel enough to be magic.

It was not.

Victor stopped ten feet away.

“You have no idea.”

Zee frowned.

“About?”

Victor stared.

For a moment he seemed genuinely confused.

Then horrified by the confusion.

“You do not know.”

Zee looked toward Rez.

Rez knew nothing.

Ner too.

“What am I supposed to know?”

Victor laughed.

One broken sound.

“No.”

He turned away.

Then back.

“You cannot.”

Zee’s assassin instincts tracked every movement.

Victor was not reaching for a weapon.

He was unraveling.

“Father.”

Victor flinched.

Zee had not called him that in years.

“What?”

“What do I not know?”

Victor stared at his son.

Then whispered:

“The prophecy.”

Silence.

Zee’s face went blank.

“What prophecy?”

That question destroyed Victor more completely than any threat could have.

The King stepped backward.

“No.”

Zee looked toward Rez again.

Rez’s eyes sharpened.

Ner shook his head once.

None of them knew.

Zee returned his attention to Victor.

“What prophecy?”

Victor’s breathing changed.

Twenty-one years.

Every decision.

Every fear.

Every attempt at containment.

Every rejection.

Built around words his son had never even heard.

“You are lying.”

Zee’s expression darkened.

“I do not need to lie to you.”

“You must know.”

“I don’t.”

Victor stared.

Zee stepped closer.

“Tell me.”

“No.”

Crimson eyes narrowed.

Not compulsion.

Not yet.

“Tell me what you heard before you killed me.”

The court went utterly still.

Victor’s face collapsed inward.

There.

The newborn death.

The first blade.

The thing no crown could clean.

Zee continued.

“You heard something.”

“Yes.”

“And decided I deserved death before I could speak.”

Victor’s mouth tightened.

“It was not that simple.”

“It was exactly that simple to the infant.”

A sharp breath came from someone in court.

Victor looked around.

The House was hearing too much now.

Good.

No more private fear shaping public law.

Victor’s voice lowered.

“The words came after Blood Rain.”

Zee said nothing.

“The kingdom was still in panic.”

“Witches had lost control of magic.”

“Wolves were mad.”

“Vampires heard—”

“A second heartbeat,” Zee said.

Victor looked at him.

“Everyone knows what happened when I was born.”

“Not this.”

Victor’s face changed.

Then finally, after twenty-one years, he spoke the words that had ruled him.

“When blood falls upward and beasts forget their names, a child of crown and hunger shall open his eyes beneath the blackened throne.”

Zee went still.

Victor continued.

“Three lives shall breathe within one flesh, and every blade raised against him shall sharpen what it seeks to destroy.”

Rez looked toward Zee.

Ner’s breathing stopped for half a heartbeat.

Above them, Zareth’s Link tightened.

Victor’s voice began trembling.

“He shall not inherit the world as it was given.”

The throne room seemed to shrink around him.

“The world shall break before it teaches him how to die.”

Silence.

Nothing answered.

No thunder.

No magic.

No prophecy waking.

Just words.

Old words.

Zee stared at his father.

“That’s it?”

Victor looked as though Zee had struck him.

“That’s it?”

“Yes.”

“You heard that and killed me?”

“You do not understand what it meant.”

“No.”

Zee stepped closer.

“I understand exactly what you did.”

Victor’s face hardened defensively.

“It said every blade would sharpen you.”

“And you chose to become the first.”

Victor stopped.

Zee’s voice remained calm.

“It said I would change the world.”

“You decided that made me guilty of whatever change frightened you.”

“It said the world would break—”

“Before it taught me how to die.”

Zee smiled faintly.

No humor.

“You learned that part was true very quickly.”

Victor’s face whitened.

The newborn.

Blade.

Blood.

Stillness.

Return.

Death ended once.

Zee continued.

“I did not know.”

Victor shook his head.

“You became it anyway.”

“Became what?”

“The prophecy.”

Zee stared.

Victor pointed at him.

“The horns.”

“The blood.”

“The creatures following you.”

“The Houses bending.”

“The monsters fearing your name.”

“The people choosing you.”

“Everything.”

“You are what it warned me about.”

Zee looked down at himself.

Mature scales.

Blood-dark clothing.

Knives hidden where Victor could not see.

The body of a predator shaped by years of choices.

Then back at his father.

“I became me.”

Victor laughed bitterly.

“Same thing.”

“No.”

The word struck harder than a spell.

“The prophecy described possibilities.”

“You built your life around them.”

“I didn’t.”

Victor’s breathing became ragged.

Zee continued.

“I did not leave Darkheart because a prophecy told me to.”

“You drove me out.”

“I did not learn to fight because prophecy demanded it.”

“People hunted me.”

“I did not go to Nanda Parbat because some ancient voice wanted an assassin.”

“I wanted skill.”

“I did not start protecting people because destiny assigned them to me.”

“I chose them.”

“I did not drink supernatural blood because prophecy promised evolution.”

“I liked what it did to me.”

Ner’s eyes shifted toward him.

No judgment.

Truth.

Zee stepped closer.

“And I did not come here because some prophecy said I would.”

His crimson eyes hardened.

“I came because you imprisoned people for choosing me.”

Victor stared.

The throne room held its breath.

Zee spoke quietly.

“You spent twenty-one years obeying those words.”

“I spent twenty-one years trying to stop them.”

“Same thing.”

Victor recoiled.

There.

The irony finally became visible.

Not that Victor caused every event.

He had not.

Aurelia’s death belonged to seven killers.

Nanda Parbat belonged to Zee.

Ner’s transformation belonged to desperation and blood.

Zee’s growing darkness belonged to grief, choice, appetite, survival, and the world he lived in.

But Victor had shaped one part faithfully.

He had treated possibility as guilt.

Fear as proof.

Prophecy as permission.

And his son had grown without even knowing the script.

Victor whispered:

“If I had done nothing…”

Zee’s expression changed.

“You still would not have owned me.”

Victor looked up.

“That is what you still do not understand.”

Zee stepped within arm’s reach.

“I might have loved you.”

That hit.

Victor’s face broke.

Just once.

Small.

Zee saw the father who had existed before terror.

Too late.

“I did love you.”

Zee’s eyes hardened.

“You loved the version of me that had not frightened you yet.”

“No.”

“You killed him.”

Victor closed his eyes.

Zee let silence do the work.

Then Victor drew a knife.

Not to murder Zee again.

He knew better.

Not even truly to win.

Instinct.

King.

Father.

Man cornered by everything he had spent his life avoiding.

The blade came.

Zee caught his wrist.

Effortlessly.

Twenty-one years.

The first time, Victor’s blade had entered an infant.

This time it stopped inches from Zee’s chest.

Victor strained.

Zee did not move.

“You cannot kill me.”

Victor’s face twisted.

“I know.”

That mattered.

He finally knew.

Zee looked at the knife.

Then Victor.

“You taught me that.”

He broke Victor’s wrist.

The knife fell.

Guards moved.

Rez drew his sword.

Ner’s Gold spread.

Zareth roared above.

Zee lifted one hand.

Everyone stopped.

Not compulsion.

Authority.

His.

“This is between us.”

Victor clutched his wrist.

“You think killing me makes you king?”

“No.”

Zee removed his coat slowly.

Set it over the throne rail.

The court stared.

He wore no crown.

No ceremonial armor.

Just weapons.

Scales.

Bloodline.

Himself.

Victor looked toward the guards.

“Take him.”

No one moved.

That was the second difference.

Victor stared.

“Take him!”

Two guards lowered their weapons.

Then five.

Then twelve.

Lord Merek, bruised from imprisonment, stood behind the throne.

“Majesty.”

Victor turned.

Merek looked toward Zee.

Then lowered his head.

“House Darkheart has heard enough.”

Victor’s face went white.

More nobles followed.

Not all.

Some remained standing.

Some backed away.

Some still loved Victor.

Some feared Zee.

No unanimous magic.

No prophecy forcing knees.

Choice.

Exactly as Zee wanted.

Victor looked around at the fracture.

Then at Zee.

“You took my House.”

Zee shook his head.

“You lost people.”

Victor screamed and attacked.

No knife now.

Witchcraft.

Dark Mana tore across the chamber.

Zee did not cast.

Dragon durability took the first blast across his scales.

Stone exploded behind him.

Zee remained standing.

Victor stared.

Zee moved.

Vampire speed.

One instant ten feet away.

Next behind Victor.

Pressure point beneath the shoulder.

Victor’s casting arm died temporarily.

Wolf instinct read the turn before it happened.

Zee caught the elbow.

Assassin leverage drove Victor to one knee.

Still no Witchcraft.

Victor spat a spell through words.

Zee whispered one of his own.

One precise force.

The syllable died in Victor’s throat.

Not permanently.

Long enough.

Zee stepped back.

“Again.”

Victor looked up.

Humiliated.

Furious.

Terrified.

He attacked.

This time Zee let him.

For several minutes.

Not because the fight was close.

Because Victor deserved to understand.

Every blade sharpened what it sought to destroy.

Victor cast suppression.

Zee’s blood adapted around old suppression patterns years ago.

Victor used poison smoke.

Zee smelled it before release and held his breath longer than a Human body could.

Victor threw enchanted silver.

Zee’s accumulated resistance turned agony into damage he could fight through.

Victor bound Zee’s legs.

Crimson Mana surgically severed the weakest line.

No spectacle.

Victor summoned a blade.

Zee took it through the hand deliberately, trapped the weapon between reinforced bone and scale, then broke it.

Victor stumbled backward.

Finally afraid in a way Zee had never smelled from him before.

Not fear of prophecy.

Fear of the man.

Zee preferred that.

He approached slowly.

Victor backed toward the throne.

“You enjoy this.”

Zee stopped.

“Yes.”

No shame.

Victor’s face twisted.

“There.”

He laughed desperately.

“The monster.”

Zee’s expression became almost peaceful.

“You needed me to be one when I was a baby.”

“No.”

“You needed it.”

“Because otherwise what you did had no excuse.”

Victor stared.

Zee stepped closer.

“I am a monster now.”

Gasps moved through the hall.

Ner’s face changed.

Rez went still.

Zareth’s Link pulsed.

Zee continued.

“To some.”

He looked around.

“To traffickers.”

“Hunters.”

“Torturers.”

“People who take children.”

“People who threaten mine.”

Then back at Victor.

“And today?”

A faint smile.

“To you.”

Victor hit the throne.

No more room behind him.

Zee stopped inches away.

Victor whispered:

“Aurelia would hate this.”

Everything inside Zee froze.

Wrong name.

Wrong weapon.

Victor saw.

For one second thought he had found safety.

Zee’s voice dropped.

“Do not use her.”

“She would not want you to kill your father.”

“You do not know what she would want.”

“I knew her before—”

Zee hit him.

Once.

Victor fell across the throne steps.

Blood split his mouth.

Zee stood over him.

The darkness wanted more.

Slow.

Painful.

Nanda Parbat gave him hundreds of ways.

Wolf wanted the throat.

Vampire wanted blood.

Witch could stop the heart without touching him.

Dragon heat could burn.

Zee considered all of them.

Calmly.

Then chose.

Personal.

Controlled.

Final.

He lifted Victor by the front of his robes.

The King looked into his son’s crimson eyes.

Zee could compel him.

Make him kneel.

Make him apologize.

Make him beg.

That would have been easy.

Zee did not.

He wanted Victor’s final moment to belong to Victor.

No altered mind.

No false words.

“Do you regret killing me?”

Victor breathed hard.

Blood on his teeth.

“Yes.”

Zee waited.

Victor continued.

“Not enough.”

Truth.

Zee nodded.

“Good.”

Victor stared.

“Good?”

“I did not want a lie.”

Victor’s eyes filled.

Fear.

Grief.

Twenty-one years.

“I was your father.”

Zee’s expression shifted.

Pain.

Real.

Still.

“Yes.”

Victor whispered:

“And you are going to kill me.”

“Yes.”

No rage.

No accident.

Choice.

Victor closed his eyes.

Maybe expecting a blade.

Zee used his hands.

One around Victor’s throat.

One against the back of his neck.

The same son Victor had once held as a newborn before fear destroyed everything.

Zee leaned close.

“You killed me once.”

Victor opened his eyes.

Zee’s voice remained quiet.

“I came back.”

Then Zee broke his neck.

One motion.

Precise.

Victor Darkheart died.

No resurrection.

No second heartbeat.

No prophecy saving him.

No return.

His body fell against the throne steps.

Zee stood over his father.

Silence filled House Darkheart.

He felt something.

Not guilt.

Not triumph exactly.

Grief, perhaps.

For a father he had never really gotten.

For Aurelia.

For what fear had eaten.

But Zee did not regret the death.

That distinction remained.

Rez approached first.

Stopped several feet away.

Not touching.

Not fixing.

Just there.

Ner came next.

His Golden eyes found Zee’s.

Love.

Concern.

No attempt to replace anyone.

Zareth landed outside the throne hall so hard the high windows rattled.

The Link carried one overwhelming truth.

Finished.

Yes.

Victor’s reign was finished.

Then Lord Merek stepped forward.

He looked at Victor’s body.

Then at Zee.

“By blood, you are first heir of House Darkheart.”

Zee said nothing.

Another noble spoke.

“By challenge, the King fell to you.”

Another:

“By the House, there is no stronger claimant.”

Zee looked toward the throne.

Victor’s throne.

Black stone.

Old blood.

Old fear.

He did not want Victor’s crown.

Merek lifted it anyway.

Dark metal.

Heavy.

The same crown Zee had watched from below as a child.

“King Zee Darkheart.”

Zee stared at it.

Then took the crown from Merek.

The court waited.

Zee looked toward the throne.

Toward Victor.

Toward the banners.

Then closed his hands around the crown.

Metal bent.

Gasps.

Crimson Dragon heat moved through his palms.

The old crown cracked.

Zee broke it in two.

One half struck the throne steps beside Victor.

The other remained in Zee’s hand.

Rez smiled.

Ner did too.

Merek stared.

“What are you doing?”

Zee looked at the broken crown.

“His reign ended.”

He dropped the second half.

“I am not wearing it.”

Silence.

“Then how will the House recognize—”

Zee turned.

Every eye followed.

He climbed the throne steps.

Stepped over Victor’s crown.

Not Victor’s body.

That distinction mattered.

He moved the body aside first.

Carefully enough that several people noticed.

Then Zee sat on the black throne.

No crown.

Blood from his father still across one hand.

Crimson scales catching torchlight.

Mature horns framing a face the House had once been taught to fear.

Outside, Zareth roared.

Pure Gold flared once around Ner.

Blue-White Mana answered from Rez.

Not granting Zee authority.

Standing with him.

Lord Merek knelt.

Then another.

Then another.

Some slowly.

Some eagerly.

Some because they loved him.

Some because they feared him.

Some because bloodright demanded it.

Some because Victor was dead.

Some because they had already chosen Zee years ago.

Zee watched each one.

No illusion that everyone kneeling meant everyone loyal.

Future king.

Future assassin.

He would remember faces.

Hands.

Heartbeats.

The ones hesitating.

The ones relieved.

The ones lying.

By the time the last standing Darkheart noble made their choice, the throne room had changed.

Not magically.

Politically.

Personally.

Permanently.

Zee rested one blood-covered hand on the black stone armrest.

Twenty-one.

No children.

No sons.

No completed Dragon awakening.

No full Dynasty spanning the world.

Not yet.

But fully grown.

Fully dangerous.

And finally sitting beneath no father.

Rez looked up at him.

“Do I have to call you Majesty now?”

The throne room froze.

Zee stared.

“No.”

Rez smiled.

“Good.”

Ner laughed.

The sound loosened something in Zee’s chest.

Zee looked toward him.

His partner.

His best friend.

The first strange blood-born being Zee had ever created without knowing creation was possible.

Zee loved him.

Deeply.

That love did not soften the blood on his hands.

Both existed.

Zareth’s amusement rolled through the Link.

Zee looked toward the shattered crown below.

Victor had spent twenty-one years trying to escape a prophecy.

He had killed an infant because he feared the man that infant might become.

The infant returned.

The child survived.

The hunted boy learned to hunt.

The prince learned to kill.

The son learned he had never needed his father’s fear to become dangerous.

And at twenty-one, when Victor finally met the future he had spent his entire life trying to prevent, it was not destiny that entered the throne room.

It was Zee.

Now Victor Darkheart was dead.

The old crown was broken.

And beneath the blackened throne banners of House Darkheart sat a King who had no intention of asking the world whether it approved.

King Zee Darkheart looked over his House.

Then smiled.

Small.

Predatory.

His.

The prophecy had survived the man who feared it.

And House Darkheart had a new King.
