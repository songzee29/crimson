Chapter Thirty-Six

The House That Bought Dragons

Lady Morcant’s estate had no walls.

That was the first thing Zee disliked about it.

The second was the silence.

They reached the old quarry road near sunset and left the horses beneath a ridge thick with dead cedar. Beyond the trees, black farmland spread toward a manor built directly into the side of a pale stone hill.

No towers.

No soldiers on visible patrol.

No banners identifying the owner.

Nothing about the place looked important enough to hide stolen supernatural bloodlines.

Caldrin studied it from the ridge.

“Expensive.”

Zee frowned.

“It looks empty.”

“That is what makes it expensive.”

Ner crouched beside him.

“How?”

“People with real money pay to make danger look boring.”

Rez nodded.

“That is unfortunately true.”

He was adjusting his new sword belt again.

It had been five days since Zee accidentally changed him.

Five days of relearning reach, weight, stride, balance, stamina, and the humiliating fact that some horses now looked considerably larger than he remembered.

Rez hated all of it.

He had also improved frighteningly fast.

Seventeen years of memory did not make his ten-year-old body stronger than it was.

It did tell him exactly how to train it.

Zee looked toward him.

“Your belt is crooked.”

Rez looked down.

It was.

He fixed it.

“You saw nothing.”

Ner smiled.

“I saw everything.”

Rez pointed toward him.

“You have been waiting five days for revenge.”

“I waited seven years.”

“That is worse.”

Aurelia turned from the estate.

“Quiet.”

All three immediately stopped.

Zareth remained farther up the ridge, crimson body partially hidden beneath dark trees. He could not approach openly.

A Dragon Crown Prince landing beside a secret blood estate would announce them before anyone reached the front door.

The Dragon Link carried his displeasure.

Bad plan. No bite. Too far.

Zee answered silently with a simple image.

Wait.

Zareth sent one back.

Biting Caldrin.

Zee almost laughed.

Caldrin looked over.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“I dislike when you say nothing while looking at the dragon.”

“You should.”

Mara crouched beside Aurelia.

The former Khell broker had become increasingly nervous the closer they came to Morcant’s territory.

Aurelia noticed.

“You have been here.”

Mara did not answer.

Vale’s expression hardened.

“Mara.”

“Yes.”

Zee looked at her.

“You said you knew one buyer.”

“I did.”

“You did not say you delivered here.”

Mara’s jaw tightened.

“I delivered here.”

Aurelia became very still.

“What?”

“Blood.”

“People?”

Mara looked away.

Aurelia stepped closer.

“People?”

“Yes.”

Zee felt his horns warming.

Rez touched his sleeve.

Not restraining.

Reminder.

Zee breathed.

The heat steadied.

Aurelia’s voice became almost too calm.

“Veyrath?”

“Once.”

“How many?”

“Two.”

“When?”

“Six years ago.”

“Alive when you left?”

“Yes.”

Aurelia’s hands curled.

“You knew this.”

“I knew two captives bearing Veyrath markers came here six years ago.”

“And you waited until now?”

“I told you Morcant bought Veyrath.”

“That is not the same thing.”

“No.”

Mara did not defend herself.

That helped her more than excuses would have.

“I was not a good person then.”

Aurelia’s stare sharpened.

“That sentence does not return anyone.”

“I know.”

Zee watched Mara.

He had expected anger to sound louder.

Instead, guilt seemed to make her quieter.

Ner asked, “What did Morcant do with them?”

Mara swallowed.

“I never stayed long enough to know.”

Caldrin studied the manor.

“Then tonight becomes educational.”

Aurelia turned toward everyone.

“We are not storming the estate.”

Zee opened his mouth.

“No.”

He closed it.

Rez looked amused.

Aurelia continued.

“We confirm who is inside.”

“Where they are kept.”

“How many guards.”

“What protections.”

“Then we move.”

Zee looked toward the darkening sky.

“What if Khell arrives first?”

“We adapt.”

“What if Morcant moves them?”

“We adapt.”

“What if—”

“Zee.”

He stopped.

Aurelia stepped closer.

“I want them out too.”

That quieted him.

Not because she was Queen.

Because this was her family.

Her blood.

Her hope.

He nodded.

Aurelia looked toward Mara.

“How do we enter?”

Mara pointed toward the quarry behind the estate.

“Deliveries go below.”

Vale frowned.

“Of course they do.”

Caldrin smiled.

“I was worried we might use a normal door.”

The quarry entrance was hidden behind a curtain of dead vines.

No spell concealed it.

Only neglect.

That bothered Ner.

“Why hide something important this badly?”

Mara whispered, “Because most people who find it do not leave.”

Rez looked toward her.

“You really need to improve your comforting stories.”

They descended.

Aurelia.

Vale.

Caldrin.

Mara.

Zee.

Rez.

Ner.

Zareth remained outside, tracking them through the Dragon Link as far as distance allowed.

The tunnel smelled of limestone, iron, old blood, and witchcraft.

Zee’s vampire senses sharpened.

Heartbeats ahead.

Many.

He stopped.

“Twenty-three.”

Vale looked back.

“Guards?”

“Some.”

“How many?”

Zee listened.

“Eight walking.”

“Others slow.”

“Sleeping?”

“No.”

He frowned.

“Too weak.”

Aurelia’s face hardened.

Captives.

The tunnel opened into an underground gallery.

Glass chambers lined the walls.

Empty.

Mostly.

One held dark feathers floating in preserving liquid.

Another contained shed wolf claws.

Another displayed strips of scaled hide that Zee refused to inspect closely.

Ner’s Golden Mana stirred beneath his gloves.

“This is a collection.”

Mara looked sick.

“Yes.”

Rez stared toward a cabinet containing labeled blood vials.

“Not research.”

Caldrin looked around.

“No.”

“Ownership.”

Zee hated the room immediately.

Aurelia did too.

They continued.

Voices approached.

Vale pulled Zee and Ner into a side alcove.

Rez moved beside them.

Two guards passed.

Human.

Armed.

One complained about Khell.

“Shipment should have arrived already.”

The second answered, “Morcant is furious.”

“How many?”

“Four.”

Zee looked at Aurelia.

Four.

The remaining captives who had traveled with Khell.

Maybe.

The guards continued.

“Old woman says she wants all seven reunited before auction.”

Zee’s heart stopped metaphorically—not literally, never literally.

All seven.

Aurelia’s face changed.

Not hope now.

Certainty of one thing.

Someone had deliberately brought them together.

The guards disappeared around the corner.

Aurelia whispered, “Auction?”

Mara went pale.

“That is new.”

Caldrin glanced toward her.

“Meaning?”

“Morcant collects.”

“She does not sell.”

Ner looked toward the distant corridor.

“Then someone changed the deal.”

Rez’s eyes narrowed.

“Khell.”

Possibly.

Not certainty.

They moved faster.

The next chamber answered the question.

Seven iron doors.

Three open.

Four closed.

Names had been written above them in chalk.

Not true names.

Numbers.

One.

Two.

Three.

Four.

Five.

Six.

Seven.

Zee hated that more.

Aurelia approached the first closed door.

Her hand stopped inches from it.

Something inside moved.

A heartbeat.

Weak.

Dragon.

Witch.

Both.

Aurelia inhaled sharply.

“Open it.”

Ner approached the lock.

Golden Mana began gathering.

Mara caught his wrist.

“No.”

Everyone turned.

Ner’s face hardened.

“Remove your hand.”

Mara did immediately.

“The lock is linked to the prisoner.”

Aurelia looked toward her.

“If broken incorrectly?”

“The restraint tightens.”

“Can you open it?”

“No.”

Caldrin examined the frame.

“Can we remove the door?”

“Same result.”

Zee touched the wall.

“What about the room?”

Mara looked at him.

“What?”

“If the lock controls the door…”

He pointed toward the stone beside it.

“…then we do not use the door.”

Caldrin smiled.

“My influence.”

Aurelia looked toward Vale.

Vale sighed.

“Quietly.”

They began removing stone.

Not with explosions.

Not magic at first.

Caldrin found the mortar seams.

Vale worked a narrow blade into them.

Rez used controlled Blue-White cold to weaken specific sections.

Ner shaped Golden Mana into thin wedges.

Zee listened to the heartbeat inside.

Slow.

But steady.

When enough stone loosened, Zee pulled.

The block shifted.

Crimson scales spread across his fingers under the strain.

They removed another.

Then another.

A gap opened.

Darkness beyond.

A face appeared.

Male.

Older than Aurelia.

Gaunt.

Long dark hair streaked with gray.

Deep red eyes opened slowly.

Aurelia stopped breathing for one terrible moment.

The man stared through the gap.

Confusion.

Then disbelief.

His lips moved.

No sound.

Aurelia stepped closer.

“Theron?”

The man’s face broke.

Not magically.

Emotionally.

“Aurelia?”

Zee looked at his mother.

He had never heard anyone say her name like that.

Not Queen.

Not Veyrath.

Not Darkheart.

Little sister.

Aurelia tore the remaining stone out herself.

Witch Mana shattered mortar.

Dragon strength followed.

The opening widened.

She climbed through.

Theron Veyrath tried standing.

His legs failed.

Aurelia caught him.

For several seconds, no one else existed.

Theron held her as though checking whether she was real.

“You lived.”

Aurelia’s voice broke.

“So did you.”

He laughed once.

Weak.

“You got old.”

Aurelia pulled back.

“You look terrible.”

“That is more like you.”

Zee smiled despite the room.

Then Theron noticed him.

His gaze moved past Aurelia.

To Zee.

Crimson eyes.

Horns.

Scales.

Ten years old.

Theron stared.

Aurelia followed his gaze.

“My son.”

Theron looked back at her.

“You had a child?”

“Yes.”

“With Victor?”

Aurelia’s expression changed.

“That conversation is longer.”

Theron looked toward Zee again.

Dragon scent.

Veyrath blood.

Something else.

Several something elses.

His eyes narrowed.

“What is he?”

Zee crossed his arms.

“Zee.”

Theron blinked.

Rez smiled.

Ner looked delighted.

Aurelia almost laughed.

“This is Zee.”

“My son.”

“That answer is enough for now.”

Theron nodded slowly.

Then his face changed.

“The others.”

Aurelia looked toward the remaining cells.

“Alive?”

“Three here.”

“Four coming.”

“Yes.”

“You know?”

Theron’s expression darkened.

“Khell has been using me to identify Veyrath blood for years.”

Zee remembered the broker’s words.

One captive could identify their blood by scent.

Theron.

Aurelia looked as if she wanted to kill someone with her bare hands.

Theron grabbed her wrist.

“No.”

She looked at him.

“Not because he deserves mercy.”

His eyes sharpened.

“Because he is bringing four more of ours.”

That mattered.

Aurelia breathed.

Controlled.

“Who are the three?”

Theron looked toward the other occupied cells.

“Two Veyrath descendants.”

“One Dragon-Witch.”

“Family?”

“Distant.”

“Names?”

“Later.”

Footsteps echoed.

Many.

Mara looked toward the corridor.

“Too late.”

The estate had discovered them.

Vale drew his sword.

Caldrin smiled.

“Finally.”

Aurelia helped Theron against the wall.

“You stay here.”

Theron laughed weakly.

“I remember you being easier to order.”

“I was six.”

“You were never easy.”

Zee looked toward Rez.

“Sounds familiar.”

Rez glared.

Ner whispered, “Historically.”

Rez elbowed him.

Then the first alarm bell rang.

Zareth heard it outside.

The Dragon Link erupted.

Fight?

Zee looked toward the ceiling.

Aurelia saw his face.

“Tell him to wait.”

Zee frowned.

“Why?”

“Because we do not know what happens if an entire Dragon crashes through a manor built over prisoners.”

Zee sent the thought.

Wait.

Zareth answered with furious reluctance.

The first guards entered the gallery.

Aurelia turned.

Her grief disappeared beneath power.

“Alive if possible.”

Caldrin sighed.

“You say that every time.”

The fight began.

Lady Morcant came downstairs herself.

That told Zee more about her than the guards did.

She did not hide behind them.

She walked through the gallery while the battle still moved around her, dressed in dark green robes with silver embroidery and carrying no visible weapon.

Older.

White hair.

Black eyes.

Witch.

Powerful.

Vale fought two guards near the entrance.

Rez controlled another with Blue-White Mana, using his smaller body rather than fighting against it.

Ner kept the occupied cells behind a Golden barrier.

Caldrin had already disarmed three people.

Aurelia stood in front of Theron.

Zee stood beside her.

Morcant looked at Theron.

Then Aurelia.

Her eyebrows rose.

“Oh.”

Aurelia’s voice became ice.

“Lady Morcant.”

“You are difficult to purchase.”

Zee’s horns heated instantly.

Rez shouted from across the chamber.

“Control!”

Zee breathed.

Morcant noticed.

Her gaze shifted toward him.

Interest.

No recognition of everything he was.

Only visible traits.

“Your son?”

Aurelia stepped slightly forward.

“Yes.”

“Veyrath.”

“Partly.”

Morcant smiled.

“Enough.”

Zee hated her immediately.

Theron struggled upright.

“Morcant.”

She looked toward him.

“You look healthier than expected.”

“I hope your disappointment kills you.”

Morcant smiled.

“Still charming.”

Aurelia’s magic gathered.

“Open the cells.”

“No.”

“Then I open them.”

“You cannot without injuring the occupants.”

Ner spoke from behind his Golden barrier.

“I can.”

Morcant finally noticed him.

Pure Golden Mana shone around both hands.

Her expression changed.

“What are you?”

Ner looked offended.

“A person.”

Zee smiled.

Rez shouted, “Long story!”

Morcant raised one hand.

The floor sigils activated.

Every closed cell tightened.

Theron screamed.

Aurelia turned.

The prisoners inside cried out.

Ner’s Golden Mana surged.

“Stop!”

Morcant looked toward him.

“Open one lock and all four restraints contract.”

Ner froze.

Good trap.

Different problem.

Zee listened.

Heartbeats.

Pain.

Magic.

His own instincts began overlapping again.

He hated it.

He forced himself to focus.

One sound.

Rez’s voice.

“Zee!”

He looked.

Rez pointed toward the ceiling.

“Control lines!”

Zee saw nothing.

Ner did.

Golden Mana rose.

Thin magical connections became visible between Morcant’s ring and the four cells.

Not one lock.

One control source.

Ner smiled.

“There.”

Morcant’s expression changed.

She closed her fist.

Too late.

Rez struck first.

Blue-White Mana snapped around Morcant’s wrist and forced her hand open.

Ner sealed the space around the ring.

Aurelia attacked.

Violet witchcraft tore it free.

Zee caught the ring.

Morcant stared.

The object burned his palm.

He nearly dropped it.

Crimson scales spread.

The heat decreased.

Adapt.

He closed his fist.

The ring cracked.

All four cells unlocked.

Theron laughed.

“That was expensive.”

Morcant’s face twisted.

For the first time, she lost composure.

“You stupid child.”

Zee looked at the broken metal in his hand.

“People say that a lot.”

She cast.

Black-green witchcraft struck him squarely in the chest.

Zee flew backward.

Pain exploded through his ribs.

He hit the wall.

Hard.

Stayed conscious.

Stayed alive.

Always.

His body repaired what it could while he pushed upright.

Aurelia’s face transformed with rage.

Morcant saw it.

Too late.

Aurelia hit her with enough witchcraft to crack the floor.

Morcant countered.

The collision shook the gallery.

Dragon energy surfaced beneath Aurelia’s magic.

Dark crimson-red flashed through violet.

Morcant’s eyes widened.

“Dragon-Witch.”

Aurelia advanced.

“You should have learned what you were buying.”

The next spell broke Morcant’s defense.

Vale disarmed the remaining guards.

Caldrin put a blade to Morcant’s throat.

Aurelia stopped him.

“Alive.”

Caldrin sighed dramatically.

Theron laughed again.

“I like him.”

“You should not,” Aurelia said.

“Everyone says that,” Caldrin replied.

The battle ended.

Ner opened the remaining cells safely.

Three captives emerged.

Weak.

Bruised.

Alive.

Not seven.

Not yet.

But alive.

Aurelia stood among them.

For ten years, Zee had known his mother as someone who had lost almost everything behind her.

Now three pieces of that past were breathing in front of her.

Then Theron looked toward the far corridor.

“We need to leave.”

Aurelia turned.

“Khell.”

“He will know Morcant lost the estate.”

“How?”

Theron looked toward the shattered control ring.

“That ring was linked to his transfer contract.”

Caldrin frowned.

“So destroying it warned him.”

“Yes.”

Zee looked guilty.

Theron pointed at him.

“No.”

Zee blinked.

“What?”

“I know that face.”

Aurelia almost smiled.

Theron continued.

“You freed four people.”

“Whatever consequence follows belongs to the person who built the cage.”

Zee stared.

He liked his uncle immediately.

Aurelia looked toward the ceiling.

“Zareth.”

Zee grinned.

Finally.

He sent one thought through the Link.

Come.

The manor roof exploded.

Everyone ducked.

Stone rained into the gallery.

Zareth’s enormous crimson head appeared through the opening.

Teal-and-Gold Mana rolled between broken beams.

The Dragon Prince looked extremely pleased.

Aurelia stared at the destroyed ceiling.

“I said come.”

Zee looked at Zareth.

The Link answered:

Came.

Rez laughed.

Ner covered his face.

Theron Veyrath stared upward at the Crown Prince of Drakonis.

Then at Zee.

Then Aurelia.

“I have many questions.”

Aurelia sighed.

“So does everyone.”

They left Morcant’s estate with four Veyrath survivors.

Lady Morcant bound.

Mara under guard but no longer treated as a prisoner.

And one new certainty.

Theron Veyrath lived.

Four more captives were still moving with Damaris Khell.

The rescue had begun.

It was not finished.
