Chapter Thirty-One

Stay With Me

Rez had seen Zee lose control before.

He had seen Crimson Mana tear through walls when Zee was little.

Seen him wake from nightmares with fangs buried in his own lip.

Seen his Wolf instincts take over during hunts.

Seen Dragon scales spread across his body while rage burned through his horns.

This was different.

There was no single direction to the power.

No enemy.

No spell.

No target.

Zee stood at the center of the council chamber gripping Rez’s wrist while the world around them behaved incorrectly.

Dust rose several inches from the floor.

Stopped.

A candle flame bent toward its own wick.

Straightened.

A drop of spilled water crawled halfway up the side of a cup before falling again.

Zareth backed away on the balcony.

Not from Zee.

From whatever the Dragon Link was carrying.

Too much.

Images without source.

Zee as a newborn.

Rez at seven.

Rez at ten.

Zee laughing at three.

A battlefield that had not happened.

No—

Not future.

Not memory.

Possibility colliding with memory so violently neither child could understand it.

Zee cried out.

Rez grabbed both shoulders.

“Look at me.”

“I cannot stop it.”

“You do not have to stop everything.”

“That is not helpful!”

“I am improvising!”

The wall behind them cracked.

“Badly!”

“Still here!”

Zee looked at him.

Rez smiled despite the fear.

“There you are.”

For half a heartbeat, Zee focused.

Rez.

Seventeen.

Older.

Always older.

The boy who had been seven when Zee first arrived in House Vesper.

The prince who had put his finger into Zee’s newborn hand.

The teenager who taught him swordwork.

The idiot who cheated at games and called it strategy.

His best friend.

His brother.

Rez’s Blue-White Mana bent around them.

Not absorbed.

Not changed.

Caught inside Zee’s unstable field.

Rez felt something press against his thoughts.

He stiffened.

Zee did too.

“What?”

Rez stared.

“Did you…”

Zee’s face tightened.

“What?”

A thought had slipped.

Not spoken.

Not sent.

Just felt too loudly.

Rez tried pulling his mind back.

Impossible.

Zee’s uncontrolled magic had reached past the places where blood ended and thought began.

Not mind-reading.

Not a skill.

A boundary breaking because Zee had too many powers colliding at once.

Rez saw Zee realize something was happening and become more frightened.

“Get out.”

Rez blinked.

“What?”

“Go.”

“No.”

“Rez.”

“You are not getting rid of me that easily.”

“I can feel you.”

“That is creepy.”

“I am serious!”

“So am I.”

Zee released him and backed away.

The room pulsed.

Rez’s sword belt slid several inches across the floor without being touched.

Zee grabbed his head.

“I might hurt you.”

“You might.”

Zee looked shocked.

Rez shrugged.

“I am not going to lie just because you are scared.”

“Then leave!”

“No.”

“Why?”

Rez’s answer came immediately.

“Because I know you.”

“That is stupid.”

“Probably.”

“I cannot control this!”

“I know.”

“Everyone else left!”

“Because you needed them to.”

“You need to!”

“No.”

Zee’s eyes flashed.

Crimson Mana slammed into Rez.

He flew backward.

Hit the wall.

Dropped hard.

Pain shot through his shoulder.

Zee froze.

“Rez.”

Rez groaned.

“That one hurt.”

“I told you!”

Zee backed farther away.

Horror spread across his face.

“I told you to leave.”

Rez pushed himself up.

His shoulder screamed.

He ignored it.

“No.”

“Stop saying no!”

“No.”

Zee’s power burst again.

Rez braced.

This time Blue-White Mana caught him.

He slid instead of flying.

Zee was crying now.

Angry at himself for it.

“I do not want to hurt you.”

Rez walked forward.

“You already did.”

Zee flinched.

Rez continued.

“And I am still here.”

“Why?”

Because he was Rez.

Because he had known Zee since before Zee could talk.

Because Zee had spent his entire life expecting love to become fear eventually.

Victor had taught him that first.

The world reinforced it every year.

Rez refused.

He stepped through the pressure.

His Blue-White Mana crackled.

Zee’s Crimson Mana pushed.

Rez kept moving.

“I am not Victor.”

Zee stopped.

Rez regretted the name instantly.

But not the point.

“I am not leaving because you became frightening.”

Zee stared.

Rez reached him.

“I was here before the horns.”

He touched one lightly.

Zee almost laughed through tears.

“Before the scales.”

His hand moved to Zee’s shoulder.

“Before you could walk.”

“You were annoying then too.”

“There he is.”

Rez pulled Zee against him.

Not carefully enough to preserve dignity.

Exactly enough to hold him.

Zee resisted for one second.

Then collapsed into him.

The pressure changed.

Not gone.

Focused.

Rez held tighter.

Zareth crouched nearby.

The Link carried one thing.

Together.

Rez closed his eyes.

His own fear finally broke through.

Not fear of Zee.

Fear for him.

The boy in his arms was shaking so hard Rez could feel it through both bodies.

I can’t lose my best friend.

The thought came with seventeen years of memory behind it.

Zee as a newborn gripping his finger.

Zee at three stealing food from Rez’s plate.

Zee at ten insulting his sword stance.

Every hunt.

Every fight.

Every time Rez had stood between Zee and something larger.

Every time Zee had returned the favor.

Rez tightened his arms.

I wish I could stay with him.

He did not say it.

He did not cast.

He did not even realize how the words formed inside him.

But Zee heard them.

Not as sound.

Not as language.

As meaning.

Stay with him.

The unstable magic answered before Zee could understand.

The room changed.

The air became perfectly still.

Every flame stopped moving.

Dust hung above the floor.

Rez’s heartbeat thundered once.

Then changed.

Zee’s eyes opened.

“Rez?”

Something moved through their connection.

Not backward.

Not into history.

Not toward any moment already lived.

Into Rez.

Now.

His living body.

His present age.

Seven years stood between them.

Zee’s magic found that difference.

And removed it.

Rez gasped.

His grip loosened.

Zee stumbled backward.

“What—”

Rez’s coat shifted.

The sleeves dropped over his hands.

His shoulders narrowed beneath the fabric.

The collar slid loose around his neck.

His boots suddenly felt enormous.

Rez looked down.

“No.”

His voice cracked higher.

He froze.

“No.”

The sword belt lying across the floor seemed too large now.

The room appeared taller.

Zee appeared—

The same height.

Almost.

Rez’s body continued changing for several terrifying heartbeats.

Not disappearing.

Not becoming transparent.

No years playing backward around him.

His face softened.

His limbs shortened.

Muscle reshaped.

His seventeen-year-old body returned to a living biological state it had once passed through.

Ten.

Then it stopped.

The world resumed.

Dust fell.

Flames moved.

Sound returned.

Rez’s oversized coat slipped from one shoulder and pooled around him.

Silence followed.

Zee stared.

Zareth stared.

Rez looked at his hands.

Small.

Ten-year-old hands.

Blue-White Mana sparked between his fingers.

Still his.

Rez looked toward Zee.

Zee looked back.

They were eye level.

“What did you do?”

Zee’s face went white.

“I do not know.”

Rez looked down at himself again.

His trousers had gathered around his boots.

His belt slipped completely and hit the floor.

Zareth made a strange choking noise.

Rez slowly turned toward him.

“Do not.”

The Dragon Link exploded with amusement.

Zee slapped both hands over his mouth.

Not fast enough.

A laugh escaped.

Rez stared at him.

“You think this is funny?”

Zee tried desperately to stop.

“No.”

“You are laughing.”

“I am scared!”

“That is not what scared sounds like!”

Zareth lowered his head to inspect Rez.

Rez shoved his snout away.

“I swear to every god listening—”

His younger voice cracked.

Zee lost the battle.

He laughed.

Rez looked ready to kill him.

Then Zee’s laughter stopped as quickly as it began.

His eyes filled with fear.

“Rez.”

The room door opened.

Aurelia entered first.

Lucien behind her.

Ner followed.

Vale, Caldrin, Sevran, and the royal witch crowded the corridor.

Everyone stopped.

Lucien looked into the room.

Then looked again.

His gaze moved over Zee.

Zareth.

The oversized Vesper clothing pooled on the floor.

The ten-year-old boy standing where his seventeen-year-old son had been.

Lucien went completely still.

“Rez?”

Rez closed his eyes.

“Yes, Father.”

The voice made Lucien pale.

Ner stared.

His mouth slowly opened.

“No.”

Rez pointed at him.

“Do not.”

Ner’s expression became radiant.

“You are short.”

“I have always been taller than you.”

“Not anymore.”

“Nereus.”

“Oh, you definitely cannot use my full name while looking like that.”

Rez lunged.

His pants immediately slipped.

He nearly fell.

Ner laughed so hard he grabbed the doorway.

Even Caldrin turned away to hide his face.

Rez pulled the waistband up with both hands.

“I hate everyone.”

Zareth sent delighted approval.

Zee did not laugh this time.

He looked at Aurelia.

“I did something.”

She moved toward Rez carefully.

“What happened?”

“I do not know.”

Rez answered before Zee could.

“He lost control.”

“I stayed.”

“Then…”

He looked down at himself.

“This.”

The royal witch entered.

Lucien finally moved.

He crossed the room quickly and crouched in front of Rez.

Rez stiffened.

Lucien put both hands on his shoulders.

“Tell me something only you would know.”

Rez stared.

“Father.”

“Please.”

Something in Lucien’s voice erased the irritation.

Rez thought.

“When I was six, I broke the blue glass window in your private study.”

Lucien blinked.

“You blamed the western wind.”

“It was convincing.”

“It was summer.”

“I was six.”

Lucien’s eyes filled.

Rez continued quickly.

“When Zee was born, I was seven.”

Zee looked at him.

Rez pointed toward Zee.

“I met him when Aurelia brought him into Vesper.”

“I gave him my wooden wolf.”

“Zareth ate half of it.”

Zareth growled.

“You did.”

Rez looked at Ner.

“Nereus arrived when Zee was three.”

“Ner is Zee’s name for him.”

Ner’s teasing expression softened.

Rez looked back at Lucien.

“I remember turning eleven.”

“Twelve.”

“Thirteen.”

“I remember my first real diplomatic meeting.”

“I remember you making me redo sword drills for three hours because I used Mana after you told me not to.”

Lucien’s mouth twitched.

“You cheated.”

“See?”

Rez spread his smaller hands.

“I remember all seventeen years.”

The royal witch moved closer.

“Mana.”

Rez raised one hand.

Blue-White Mana formed immediately.

Clean.

Controlled.

No hesitation.

A narrow blade shaped itself across his palm.

Exactly the technique he had mastered years earlier.

Ner stopped smiling entirely.

“That is still you.”

Rez looked at him.

“Yes.”

Sevran stared from the doorway.

“I have never seen this.”

Aurelia turned sharply.

“No theories.”

“I was going to say exactly that.”

“Good.”

She approached Rez.

“May I?”

Rez nodded.

Aurelia touched his wrist.

Witch magic moved carefully through him.

Not forcing.

Reading.

Her expression became more unsettled the longer she worked.

Lucien watched her.

“Well?”

“His body is healthy.”

Rez looked down.

“For ten?”

Aurelia hesitated.

“Yes.”

The room went silent.

Ner’s eyes widened.

Rez stared.

Zee’s breathing changed.

“No.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“Zee.”

“No.”

He backed away.

“I did not—”

“I know.”

“I did not make him—”

“We do not know exactly what you did.”

Rez spoke.

“Ten?”

The royal witch checked independently.

Violet Mana moved around him.

Then faded.

She looked toward Aurelia.

“Approximately.”

Rez stared at Zee.

Zee stared back.

Same height.

Same apparent age.

Ten.

Ner stepped beside Rez.

He compared them openly.

Rez glared.

“If you say anything—”

“We are basically the same height.”

Rez lunged again.

Lucien caught the back of his oversized coat.

Ner grinned.

“Your father can pick you up again.”

“NER.”

The humor broke when Zee made a small sound.

Everyone turned.

He had started crying silently.

Rez stopped fighting Lucien.

“Zee.”

“I changed you.”

“We think.”

“Do not.”

Rez’s expression softened.

“Do not what?”

“Make it less.”

Zee wiped his face angrily.

“I changed your body without asking.”

No one argued.

Because he had.

Aurelia crossed the room and knelt beside him.

“Listen to me.”

“Can I put it back?”

Aurelia went still.

“Can I?”

“We do not know.”

“Then try.”

“No.”

Zee stared.

“Why?”

“Because we do not know what you did the first time.”

“But—”

“And I will not let you reach blindly into Rez again because you are frightened by the result.”

That stopped him.

Lucien looked toward his son.

Rez was silent.

Aurelia continued.

“Whatever happened, his history remains.”

“His memory remains.”

“His Mana remains.”

“His mind remains.”

She looked toward Rez.

“You are still seventeen years lived.”

Rez nodded slowly.

“But physically?”

“Ten.”

The word landed.

Rez sat down.

Mostly because the nearest chair was suddenly much taller than expected.

He climbed onto it with furious dignity.

Ner watched.

Rez pointed.

“Not one word.”

Ner covered his mouth.

Zareth failed to conceal amusement entirely.

Rez looked at Zee.

“Your dragon is a terrible person.”

“He is a dragon.”

“That does not help.”

For a few moments, nobody spoke.

Then the royal witch looked toward Aurelia.

“This was not reversal.”

Zee looked up.

“What?”

She chose her words carefully.

“I can still sense the years in him.”

Lucien frowned.

“What does that mean?”

“His life is intact.”

“Nothing was removed from what happened.”

She gestured toward Rez.

“But his living body no longer reflects seventeen years of biological age.”

Sevran spoke from the doorway.

“So the past remains.”

Aurelia gave him a warning look.

He lifted his hands.

“Observation. Not theory.”

Rez leaned forward.

“I remember everything.”

“The records still exist.”

“My birth year did not change.”

Lucien nodded slowly.

“I remember raising you.”

Ner said, “I remember you being annoyingly taller this morning.”

Rez glared.

Zee whispered, “I remember too.”

Aurelia looked at her son.

“Whatever this was…”

She paused.

“…it did not rewrite history.”

Zee looked at Rez.

“Then why ten?”

No one had an answer.

Rez did.

Not immediately.

But something returned to him.

The room.

Zee shaking in his arms.

His own fear.

The thought.

Rez’s expression changed.

“Oh.”

Zee noticed.

“What?”

Rez looked away.

“Nothing.”

Ner frowned.

“That is obviously something.”

Rez rubbed both hands across his suddenly younger face.

“I thought something.”

Aurelia’s eyes narrowed.

“When?”

“Right before this happened.”

“What?”

Rez looked toward Zee.

The room seemed too quiet again, though Zee’s surge had ended.

“I thought I could not lose him.”

Zee froze.

Rez continued reluctantly.

“And…”

He hated saying this in front of everyone.

Caldrin looked far too interested.

Rez glared at him.

“If you repeat this, I will stab you when I am tall enough again.”

Caldrin nodded solemnly.

“Understood.”

Rez looked back at Zee.

“I thought I wished I could stay with him.”

Zee’s face changed.

A memory that was not his surfaced.

Not memory.

The feeling he had touched.

Stay with him.

His uncontrolled power had heard it.

Felt it.

Answered it.

Aurelia understood the connection at the same moment.

“You were seven years older.”

Rez looked at her.

“Yes.”

Zee whispered, “I am ten.”

Rez stared.

“And now so am I.”

Ner slowly stopped smiling.

Lucien’s face became deeply unsettled.

Zee shook his head.

“I did not choose that.”

Rez slid down from the chair.

His coat nearly swallowed him again.

“I know.”

“What if I heard something else?”

No one answered.

“What if somebody thought something worse?”

Aurelia touched his shoulder.

“This happened while you were completely out of control.”

“That does not make it better.”

“No.”

She would not lie.

“It means we learn from it.”

“How?”

“First?”

She looked toward Rez.

“No one lets Zee attempt this again.”

Rez nodded.

“Strongly agree.”

Ner raised one hand.

“Second, someone finds him clothing before he trips and dies of embarrassment.”

Rez glared.

“I cannot die from embarrassment.”

Ner smiled.

“Shame.”

Rez threw the nearest cushion.

Ner ducked.

The pillow struck Sevran.

Sevran stared.

Caldrin finally laughed openly.

Even Lucien’s composure cracked.

The tension loosened.

Not vanished.

Could not.

Because beneath the absurdity remained a truth none of them could ignore.

Zee had touched something no one knew he could touch.

Not history.

Not the dead.

Not yesterday.

A living person.

Now.

Rez remained the prince who had lived seventeen years.

Every birthday still happened.

Every lesson.

Every fight.

Every mistake.

Every memory.

Nothing had been erased.

Yet his current body stood beside Zee’s at ten years old.

Zee looked at him.

“I am sorry.”

Rez sighed.

“I know.”

“You are angry.”

“Yes.”

“At me?”

Rez thought about it.

“Partly.”

Zee nodded.

That hurt.

Rez stepped closer.

“But I am not afraid of you.”

Zee looked up.

Rez was no longer up.

They were eye level.

That realization struck both at once.

Rez looked annoyed by it.

Zee looked devastated.

Then Rez put one smaller hand on Zee’s shoulder.

“You heard the stupidest possible version of what I meant.”

Zee’s mouth twitched.

“I did not mean make me ten.”

“I know.”

“I meant I did not want to leave you alone.”

“I know.”

Rez exhaled.

“Apparently your magic has terrible interpretation skills.”

Zee laughed once despite himself.

“Sorry.”

“Stop apologizing every five breaths.”

“Sorry.”

Rez stared.

Zee smiled weakly.

There.

Brotherhood surviving impossible magic.

Lucien watched his son.

Ten-year-old body.

Seventeen years behind his eyes.

He looked toward Aurelia.

“What happens now?”

Aurelia looked at Rez.

Then Zee.

Ner.

Zareth.

Four children standing at nearly the same physical age.

Except one of them had lived seven years more than the others.

“We do not force anything.”

She looked toward Zee.

“We do not experiment.”

Then Rez.

“And we do not pretend this is temporary just because we want it to be.”

Rez nodded slowly.

His expression turned strange.

“So I am ten.”

“Physically.”

“With seventeen years of memory.”

“Yes.”

Rez looked toward Ner.

Ner smiled.

Rez pointed at him.

“I am still older than you.”

Ner folded his arms.

“Historically.”

“I will take it.”

“You need a smaller sword.”

Rez looked horrified.

“My sword is fine.”

He walked toward it.

Tried lifting the weapon he had carried comfortably that morning.

The balance dragged his arm down.

Ner smiled wider.

Rez stared at the sword.

Then at Zee.

“This is your fault.”

Zee nodded miserably.

Rez sighed.

“I was trying to make you laugh.”

“It worked a little.”

“Good.”

Zareth walked over and lowered his head until he and Rez were almost eye level.

The Dragon Link sent Zee an image.

Rez.

Small.

Zee immediately turned away to hide his face.

Rez noticed.

“What did he say?”

“Nothing.”

“What did he say?”

Ner looked delighted.

“I want to know too.”

Zee failed.

“He thinks you are small.”

Rez launched the cushion again.

Zee ducked.

For the first time since the surge began, laughter filled the room.

It did not erase the fear.

Nothing could.

Aurelia still watched Zee with new concern.

Lucien still looked at his son as though learning his face again.

Sevran had gone unusually quiet.

The royal witch was already mentally cataloging everything she must not pretend to understand.

And Zee knew something fundamental about himself had changed.

His power had reached beyond blood.

Beyond Mana.

Beyond instinct.

Beyond the boundaries he knew.

But it had not touched the past.

Rez’s seventeen years remained exactly where they belonged.

Behind him.

Real.

Untouched.

Only the living body standing in the room had changed.

By nightfall, Vesper tailors had been summoned under strict secrecy.

Rez acquired clothing that fit.

His armor did not.

His sword required adjustment.

His Blue-White Mana remained precise.

His memories remained seventeen years deep.

His argument with Lucien over whether he could still attend military council lasted nearly an hour and proved, more effectively than any spell, that Rez was still Rez.

And when the palace finally quieted, four figures sat together on the western roof.

Zee.

Ten.

Ner.

Ten.

Zareth.

Ten.

Rez.

Ten to every eye that could see him.

Seventeen in every year he remembered.

Rez looked at the others.

Ner had been smiling at him for several minutes.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You have something.”

“No.”

“Nereus.”

Ner grinned.

“We finally match.”

Rez groaned.

Zee laughed.

Zareth’s amusement rolled warmly through the Link.

Rez looked toward Zee.

The laughter faded into something softer.

“You okay?”

Zee nodded.

Then shook his head.

“Not really.”

“Good.”

Zee frowned.

Rez continued.

“If you said yes, I would worry.”

Zee looked down at his hands.

“What if I cannot control it?”

“Then you learn.”

“What if I change somebody else?”

“Then maybe stop hugging people during magical disasters.”

Zee shoved him.

Rez almost slid off the roof.

His smaller body reacted differently than he expected.

Zee caught him instantly.

Both froze.

Then Rez started laughing.

“That is going to take getting used to.”

Zee held onto his wrist.

“Rez?”

“Yeah?”

“You really staying?”

Rez looked at him.

The question meant more now.

He thought about the wish.

The terrible, loving, careless thought that Zee’s magic had taken literally.

Rez squeezed Zee’s hand once.

“I was staying before you made it weird.”

Zee smiled.

Ner groaned.

“That was disgustingly emotional.”

Rez threw himself at him.

Both ten-year-old bodies crashed across the rooftop.

Ner shouted.

Zee laughed.

Zareth roared encouragement.

Below them, House Vesper continued moving through the night, unaware that one of its princes had entered the day at seventeen and would leave it wearing the body of a ten-year-old.

History had not changed.

The past had not bent.

Nothing already lived had been erased.

But the present had.

And from that night forward, the four who had grown together at different ages now stood physically side by side.

Zee Darkheart.

Nereus Azrion.

Zareth Drakonis.

Rez Vesper.

All ten.

One of them carrying seventeen years behind his eyes.

And none of them yet understood what Zee had truly done.
