Chapter One Hundred Seventeen

The Son Who Knew His Father

Zayrlen learned Zee’s heartbeat before he learned anything else.

That was what Zee decided, anyway.

Ner disagreed.

“He knows mine better.”

Zee looked down at the sleeping newborn between them.

“He reacts when I enter the room.”

“He reacts when anyone enters the room.”

“No.”

Ner stared.

Zee continued.

“Different.”

“You have been a father to him for six days and you are already inventing favoritism.”

“He likes me.”

“He was inside my body for eight months.”

“Historical advantage.”

Ner laughed.

Zayrlen startled.

Both fathers immediately went silent.

The newborn opened his crimson-gold eyes.

Looked between them.

Then began crying.

Ner pointed.

“You woke him.”

“You laughed.”

“You made me laugh.”

Zee picked him up.

Zayrlen stopped crying within seconds.

Zee looked smug.

Ner stared.

“I hate both of you.”

Zayrlen yawned against Zee’s chest.

Zee smiled.

“No, you don’t.”

⸻

Darkheart had become unbearable.

Not because of war.

Because everyone wanted to see the baby.

Gifts continued appearing outside Zee and Ner’s rooms.

Small clothes.

Blankets.

Wooden toys.

Protective charms.

One Wolf family sent a tiny pair of boots despite the fact Zayrlen could not walk.

Rez sent twelve gifts.

Then denied sending eleven of them.

Zareth arrived every other day under increasingly ridiculous excuses.

“I came for diplomatic matters.”

Zee looked toward the bundle in his arms.

“You are holding a carved Dragon.”

“That is unrelated.”

“You carved it.”

“No.”

Teal-and-Gold Mana residue covered the toy.

Zee raised an eyebrow.

Zareth took the Dragon back.

“I can leave.”

Zayrlen made a small noise.

Zareth stopped.

Then looked toward him.

Ner smiled from the bed.

“You can hold him.”

Zareth immediately turned back.

Zee narrowed his eyes.

“You planned that.”

“Of course.”

Zareth took Zayrlen carefully.

Far more carefully than anyone expected from a Dragon who had once torn a siege tower apart because a soldier insulted his scales.

Zayrlen looked up.

Dragon blood recognized Dragon.

But not the same line.

A faint Crimson-and-Gold shimmer moved along the baby’s arms.

Zareth froze.

Through the Link came:

He knows what I am.

Zee answered:

Probably.

He is six days old.

Still.

Zareth stared down at him.

Then quietly said:

“You are going to be dangerous.”

Ner looked toward Zee.

“Apparently that is the family greeting.”

⸻

The first true sign of Zayrlen’s Wolf nature appeared on the ninth night.

The moon was nearly full.

Zee woke because the room smelled different.

Not danger.

Wolf.

He sat upright.

Zayrlen slept in a small cradle beside the bed.

Ner remained asleep.

The newborn’s heartbeat was faster.

His breathing changed.

Then his tiny hands curled.

Claws appeared.

Not long.

Barely more than sharpened nails.

Crimson-gold Witch energy shimmered around them.

Zee moved closer.

The claws vanished.

Then the baby’s body temperature rose.

Dragon.

Three natural foundations reacting to the same lunar pressure.

Still not fully awakened.

Still not active in any controlled sense.

But present.

Complete.

Waiting for growth.

Zee touched the edge of the cradle.

Zayrlen’s eyes opened.

Slit pupils.

For one heartbeat.

Then round again.

Zee smiled.

“Definitely mine.”

Ner’s voice came from behind him.

“He is also mine.”

Zee turned.

“You were awake?”

“For the last minute.”

“Why did you not say anything?”

“I wanted to see how long it took you to claim him.”

Zee looked back at Zayrlen.

“Eight seconds.”

“Embarrassing.”

Zayrlen sneezed.

A spark of Gold hit Zee directly in the face.

Ner started laughing.

Zee stared at his son.

“Betrayal.”

⸻

The kingdom did not stop because a Prince had been born.

Three weeks later, the southern Safe Roads were attacked.

Not by a kingdom.

Not by experimenters.

By slavers.

Ordinary ones.

That almost offended Zee more.

They had heard Darkheart was focused inward after the royal birth.

They assumed fewer patrols.

Less attention.

They assumed correctly.

For six hours.

Then Amara found the missing wagon.

Seven Darkheart citizens had been taken.

Two children.

The slavers were still inside Darkheart territory when Zee heard.

Ner was feeding Zayrlen.

Zee stood by the window.

Did not move.

Ner looked up.

“What?”

“Seven taken.”

His tone changed everything.

Ner glanced toward their son.

Then back at Zee.

“Go.”

Zee looked at him.

“You are sure?”

“Zee.”

“Yes.”

“I am holding your child.”

“Our child.”

“Exactly.”

Ner’s Pure Gold Mana flickered around one hand.

“I am not helpless.”

Zee looked at Zayrlen.

Then at Ner.

The Wolf inside him hated leaving.

The King did it anyway.

Choice.

Trust.

Family did not require possession.

His Phoenix wings opened.

Ner called after him:

“Bring them back.”

Zee looked over his shoulder.

“Yes.”

Then flew.

⸻

The slavers made it twenty-four miles.

Zee found them before sunset.

The King’s Hunt saw through the false wagon trails.

Wolf scent recognized seven Darkheart citizens.

Vampire hearing found restrained heartbeats.

Witch sight found the suppression collars.

Phoenix wings brought him down ahead of the convoy.

The lead rider saw him.

Turned pale.

“Darkheart.”

Zee landed.

Crimson wings opened behind him.

“Yes.”

The slaver raised both hands.

“We surrender.”

Zee looked toward the wagon.

A child cried inside.

“You took my people.”

“We did not kill them.”

“That was not the law.”

The man swallowed.

“We can return them.”

“You already are.”

Zee walked forward.

The man backed away.

“You do not understand.”

“I do.”

“We heard the King had a newborn son.”

Zee stopped.

The slaver realized too late that sentence had made things worse.

“We thought—”

“You thought I was distracted.”

No anger.

No loss of control.

Only certainty.

Zee’s eyes became slit.

“You were right.”

Then he killed him.

⸻

The rest died quickly.

Not every person around the convoy.

One hired driver had not known what the wagon carried.

Zee smelled the truth in his fear.

He survived.

The guards did not.

The organizer did not.

The buyer did not.

The seven Darkheart citizens went home.

The children were returned before nightfall.

Zee came back covered in blood.

Ner was waiting.

So was Zayrlen.

Zee stopped outside the bedroom.

Looked down at himself.

Then turned toward the bathing room.

Ner called:

“Where are you going?”

“I am covered in blood.”

“Yes.”

“I am not holding him like this.”

Ner looked surprised.

Then smiled.

Zee frowned.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“That was something.”

“You have changed.”

“No.”

“You killed an entire slaver band and still stopped at the door because you did not want blood on your son.”

Zee looked at his hands.

Then toward Zayrlen.

“That is not change.”

“What is it?”

Zee thought.

“Priority.”

Ner’s expression softened.

“Close enough.”

⸻

Later that night, Zee held Zayrlen after washing.

The baby slept against his chest.

No crown.

No armor.

No blood.

Just warmth.

Zee listened to the heartbeat.

Tiny.

Strong.

Real.

Ner watched from the bed.

“He knows you.”

Zee looked down.

“Yes.”

That still mattered more than he could explain.

Aidan had known Zee’s face without knowing Father.

Zayrlen would never have to separate the two.

The cruelty of that difference remained.

But Zee no longer let it poison what was good.

He touched Zayrlen’s back.

The baby shifted closer.

Ner smiled.

“You were right.”

Zee looked up immediately.

“About?”

“He does react differently to you.”

Zee smiled.

Ner pointed.

“Do not become unbearable.”

Too late.
