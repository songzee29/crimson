Chapter Twenty-Seven

The Door Beneath Vesper

The bronze door opened inward.

No hand touched it.

No spell forced it.

No Golden Mana reached toward the lock.

It simply moved.

Ancient metal dragged across stone with a sound that rolled through the foundations of House Vesper like something waking beneath the kingdom.

Zee stood at the bottom of the descending stairway with Rez to his right and Ner to his left.

Zareth occupied nearly the entire passage behind them.

At ten years old, the Crown Prince of Drakonis had long since outgrown tunnels built for humans, and his irritation moved clearly through the Dragon Link.

Small. Bad. Break wall.

Zee looked over his shoulder.

“No.”

Zareth sent him an image of the wall collapsing.

Then an image of himself walking comfortably through the new opening.

“That is still no.”

Rez glanced between them.

“What is he complaining about?”

“The tunnel.”

“He is a dragon.”

“That is his argument.”

Rez nodded.

“Strong argument.”

Aurelia Darkheart stepped past them.

Her expression had changed since admitting the truth of Zee’s Dragon blood the previous night.

Not softer.

More exposed.

Now that Zee knew to look, he noticed things he would have missed before.

The way Aurelia’s eyes lingered on old carvings.

How certain scents made her shoulders tighten.

How she automatically positioned herself where she could see both Zee and the nearest exit.

Some habits had nothing to do with being Queen of House Darkheart.

Some were older.

Vale descended behind Aurelia with six Vesper guards.

King Lucien followed.

Caldrin came last, carrying far more knives than anyone had asked him to bring.

The royal witch raised one hand.

Violet Mana formed above her palm.

It illuminated the open doorway.

Beyond it lay darkness.

Ner stopped several feet from the threshold.

His Pure Golden Mana flickered faintly around his fingers.

Zee looked at him.

“You feel something?”

“Yes.”

“What?”

Ner frowned.

“I do not know.”

Rez sighed.

“You two say that constantly.”

Ner looked toward him.

“Would you rather I invent something?”

“No.”

“Then be quiet.”

Zee smiled.

Rez pointed at him.

“See what you created?”

“I did not create Ner.”

“You made him mean.”

Ner looked deeply pleased by the accusation.

Aurelia turned.

“Enough.”

All three stopped.

Zareth did not.

Amusement moved through the Link.

Traitor.

The bronze door waited.

Lucien approached carefully.

“No one crosses until we know whether the threshold is trapped.”

Caldrin stepped beside him.

“I volunteer Rez.”

Rez looked offended.

“You cannot volunteer someone else.”

“I just did.”

Lucien ignored them.

The royal witch extended her hand.

Violet Mana crossed the threshold.

Nothing happened.

A second spell followed.

Still nothing.

Then Ner’s Golden Mana flickered.

The doorway answered.

A thin line of gold appeared along the floor.

Ner stepped backward.

The light faded.

Everyone looked at him.

“I did not do that.”

Zee believed him.

So did Aurelia.

The door was responding to Ner.

Lucien’s expression hardened.

“Nereus stays here.”

Ner stared at him.

“No.”

“Nereus.”

“You brought me because this place reacts to my Mana.”

“I brought you because whatever opened that door may have been coming for you.”

“Then leaving me outside makes no sense.”

Rez whispered to Zee, “He has a point.”

Lucien heard him.

“I am surrounded by insubordination.”

Aurelia looked at Zee.

“I understand.”

Zee frowned.

“I did not say anything.”

“You were about to.”

That was unfairly accurate.

Ner stepped toward the threshold.

Golden light brightened beneath his feet.

Nothing attacked.

Nothing moved.

He looked back.

“If it wanted to hurt me, it already knows where I am.”

Aurelia exchanged a look with Lucien.

Then nodded once.

“Stay between Rez and me.”

Ner smiled faintly.

“Yes, Your Majesty.”

They entered.

The chamber beyond the bronze door was not a room.

It was a road.

A stone avenue stretched downward at an angle too gentle to feel like a staircase and too steep to belong inside any ordinary foundation.

Columns lined both sides.

Each had been carved with hundreds of overlapping symbols.

Witch marks.

Wolf carvings.

Vampiric blood glyphs.

Dragon script.

Others none of them recognized.

Zee slowed.

“These were made by different people.”

The royal witch looked toward him.

“What makes you think that?”

“They do not feel the same.”

Aurelia examined them more carefully.

Zee was right.

Different tools.

Different ages.

Different magical traditions.

Whatever lay beneath Vesper had not belonged to one people.

Others had found it.

Used it.

Changed it.

Left pieces of themselves behind.

Caldrin moved one finger near a damaged carving without touching it.

“Or died.”

Vale looked at him.

“You enjoy that possibility too much.”

“I appreciate preparedness.”

A metallic sound traveled from farther below.

Then silence.

Zareth pushed forward.

The Dragon Link tightened.

Something moved.

Zee felt it too.

Not through blood.

Not through heartbeat.

Not through scent.

Through the strange perception that had developed after the demon beneath Vesper years ago—the ability to sense presence where ordinary predatory senses found nothing.

Something was ahead.

“Stop.”

Everyone did.

Zee pointed toward the left row of columns.

“There.”

Nothing stood there.

Rez’s Blue-White Mana gathered.

Ner raised Golden light.

The empty air distorted.

A figure appeared.

Not demon.

Not human.

Stone and metal covered its body in overlapping plates, but beneath the armor something translucent existed, like spirit trapped inside glass.

It did not attack.

It looked at Ner.

Then Zee.

Then Aurelia.

Its head tilted.

A voice emerged from nowhere Zee could identify.

“Four.”

Everyone froze.

Aurelia’s eyes sharpened.

“Four what?”

The construct ignored her.

Its gaze remained fixed on Zee.

“Three awake.”

Zee’s fingers moved instinctively toward the crimson-black scales along his neck.

Four.

Three awake.

He looked toward Aurelia.

She had gone very still.

Before Zee could ask, she stepped in front of him.

“Enough.”

The construct turned toward her.

Silence held.

Then:

“Two awake.”

Aurelia’s face changed.

Zareth felt it.

The Link tightened.

Mother knows.

The construct moved closer.

Vale raised his sword.

Lucien lifted one hand.

“Do not attack unless it does.”

The figure stopped at the edge of the light.

Its attention returned to Ner.

“Key.”

Ner’s Golden Mana flared.

“I am not a key.”

The construct tilted its head.

“Not object.”

“Then stop calling me that.”

“Function.”

Ner looked offended.

Rez leaned toward Zee.

“I think it insulted him mathematically.”

Ner glared.

The construct extended one hand.

The entire avenue awakened.

Thousands of carvings ignited at once.

Not in one color.

Many.

The stone beneath their feet vibrated.

Then images appeared between the columns.

Not visions of the future.

Records.

Fragments of people who had walked the same avenue centuries earlier.

A vampire king approached alone beneath a torn banner.

The image shifted.

A witch queen appeared with six attendants.

Then a wolf chieftain covered in scars.

Then a Dragon walking in human form.

Different eras.

Different visitors.

Different reasons.

None were Zee.

None were Ner.

None resembled the strange group standing there now.

Zee stared.

“What is this place?”

The construct answered.

“Convergence Vault.”

Aurelia’s expression tightened.

Lucien noticed.

“You know that name?”

“No.”

Too quick.

Zee heard it.

“Mother.”

“I said no.”

“You sounded like yes.”

Aurelia glared.

He stopped.

Mostly.

The construct continued.

“Built by none.”

Caldrin raised an eyebrow.

“Unhelpful.”

“Found by many.”

“Better.”

“Opened by rare resonance.”

Ner looked at his hands.

“Me.”

“Part.”

Ner frowned.

“Part?”

The construct turned toward Zee.

“Other part.”

Silence.

Zee’s crimson eyes narrowed.

“I did not open the door.”

“No.”

“Then why me?”

The translucent core inside the construct brightened.

“Because you carry too many doors.”

Aurelia stepped forward.

“That is enough.”

The construct looked at her.

“Hidden door.”

Aurelia’s scales flashed beneath her skin.

Not illusion.

Not reflected light.

Real scales.

Deep red-brown surfaced briefly along her wrist and neck.

Lucien went completely still.

Vale’s face changed.

The royal witch inhaled sharply.

Zareth surged upright.

The construct spoke.

“Dragon-Witch.”

Lucien looked at Aurelia.

For perhaps the first time in years, King Lucien Vesper had no immediate response.

“You are…”

Aurelia met his eyes.

“Yes.”

“You never told me.”

“No.”

Caldrin folded his arms.

“I suspected.”

Vale turned toward him.

“Of course you did.”

“I suspect everyone.”

“That does not count.”

“It counts when I am right.”

Aurelia ignored them.

Zee stared at his mother.

He had known since the previous night that the Dragon blood came from her.

But hearing something ancient recognize both parts of her made the truth heavier.

Real.

The construct turned away.

“Proceed.”

The avenue illuminated farther down.

Lucien’s expression hardened.

“Why?”

“No compulsion.”

“That was not my question.”

“Choice required.”

That answer mattered.

Nothing was forcing them forward.

They could leave.

Aurelia looked at Zee.

He was already staring down the illuminated path.

“No.”

Zee sighed.

“You did not even ask.”

“I know your face.”

Ner looked toward the deeper passage.

“So do I.”

Rez folded his arms.

“Well, I am seventeen.”

Lucien stared at him.

Rez lifted his chin.

“That argument has to work eventually.”

It did not.

They argued for nearly fifteen minutes.

Then continued anyway.

Together.

The avenue ended inside a circular chamber.

Four stone pedestals surrounded a central pool of perfectly black water.

Nothing rested on them.

No weapons.

No crowns.

No books.

No carved names.

Only smooth stone.

Zee stopped at the entrance.

“That is disappointing.”

Rez looked toward him.

“You wanted treasure?”

“I wanted something.”

Caldrin nodded.

“Reasonable.”

Ner stepped farther inside.

One pedestal illuminated.

Pure gold spread across its surface.

Everyone stopped.

Ner immediately stepped backward.

The gold disappeared.

He stepped forward again.

It returned.

Rez frowned.

“That one is reading you.”

Ner looked toward him.

“Very observant.”

Rez moved toward another pedestal.

Blue-White light awakened beneath his hand.

He withdrew.

The color vanished.

Zareth approached the third.

Teal-and-Gold Dragon Mana rippled across his scales.

The pedestal answered with the same mixed resonance.

Zee looked at the final stone.

Nothing.

Then he let a little Crimson Mana rise from his palm.

The pedestal ignited.

Crimson.

Not only the color of his witchcraft.

Something hotter stirred beneath it.

Crimson Dragon energy moved through the light for a heartbeat.

Zee stared.

Four pedestals.

Four current visitors.

Four present resonances.

Caldrin crouched beside Rez’s pedestal.

He examined the stone.

“No pigment.”

The royal witch nodded.

“No stored color either.”

Ner stepped away again.

His pedestal returned to gray.

Rez did the same.

Gray.

Zareth lowered his Mana.

Gray.

Zee released his.

Gray.

Aurelia exhaled.

“They were not waiting for you.”

Zee looked toward her.

“The room is reading us.”

“Yes.”

Ner’s expression eased.

“Resonance.”

Lucien nodded.

“Not prediction.”

Not destiny.

Not prophecy.

Whatever the chamber was, it had not known their colors before they were born.

It simply knew what stood inside it now.

Zee liked that answer better.

The black water in the center of the chamber moved.

A map rose from it.

No hands touched the surface.

Continents formed in light.

Kingdoms.

Mountain ranges.

Coasts.

Seas.

Some Zee recognized.

Much of it he did not.

Then points appeared.

Dozens.

Then hundreds.

Ner moved closer.

“What are those?”

The construct had followed them.

“Thresholds.”

Lucien looked toward it.

“Like the bronze door?”

“Related.”

Some points existed in witch territory.

Others beneath vampire kingdoms.

Several lay within wolf lands.

A cluster appeared beneath distant mountains.

Others glowed far out at sea.

Several existed beyond the borders of every kingdom shown on modern Vesper maps.

One sat beyond an eastern desert Zee had only heard about.

Another appeared so far north that Caldrin frowned.

“There is nothing there.”

The construct answered.

“Unknown.”

Caldrin smiled faintly.

“I like it less now.”

Zee stared at the distant lights.

The world suddenly felt much larger.

Not built around him.

Not waiting for him.

Full of places that existed whether Zee Darkheart ever found them or not.

Then one threshold turned black.

The point vanished.

Another followed.

Then another.

Ner’s smile disappeared.

“What happened?”

The construct stared at the map.

“Closed.”

“Destroyed?”

“Unknown.”

“Who closed them?”

“Unknown.”

“Why?”

“Unknown.”

Rez looked toward Ner.

“You must love this thing.”

Ner glared.

The water rippled violently.

Every threshold disappeared.

A black line cut across the map.

Moving.

Toward Vesper.

The construct’s translucent core flared.

“Unauthorized breach.”

Aurelia immediately pulled Zee backward.

Lucien turned toward the avenue.

“From where?”

No answer.

The black line moved faster.

Zareth spread his wings as far as the chamber allowed.

Teal-and-Gold Mana ignited.

Vale drew his sword.

Caldrin drew both of his.

Rez moved in front of Ner.

Ner shoved him sideways.

“I can stand by myself.”

“I know.”

“Then stop standing in front of me.”

“I am older.”

“That is becoming your entire personality.”

The wall behind the central pool rippled.

Not cracked.

Rippled.

Like stone becoming water.

Something came through.

Six hooked limbs first.

Then bone armor.

Then smoke.

The creature dragged itself into the chamber without disturbing a single loose stone.

No heartbeat.

No ordinary blood.

No scent.

Zee felt it only through the deeper perception beneath his other senses.

“There.”

The first Vault Predator turned toward him.

Three more emerged behind it.

Then five.

The construct spoke.

“Vault Predators.”

Rez stared.

“You have a name for them?”

“Designation.”

“Can they be killed?”

A pause.

“Eventually.”

Caldrin smiled.

“I have changed my mind. I like it again.”

The predators attacked.

Rez moved first.

Blue-White Mana spread across the floor in a wide arc and struck two predators from the side, changing their direction rather than trying to overpower them.

“Left!”

Vale and Caldrin were already there.

Vale caught one bone limb against his sword.

Caldrin drove a blade between two armored plates.

The knife passed through smoke.

He cursed.

“Less physical than advertised.”

Zee felt where the creature truly existed beneath the haze.

“Lower!”

Caldrin changed angle.

This time steel struck something.

The predator screamed.

Ner raised both hands.

Pure Golden Mana spread between the columns and sealed the space behind three creatures.

They tried slipping into smoke.

The gold compressed around them.

Not destroying.

Limiting.

“Now!”

Zareth launched.

True Dragon strength carried him across the chamber.

His claws struck one predator hard enough to tear bone armor free.

Teal-and-Gold Dragonfire followed.

The creature shrieked.

Aurelia cast from the opposite side.

Witchcraft first.

A sharp violet binding snapped around two limbs.

Then something deeper moved beneath her spell.

Dark crimson-red Dragon energy flashed through it.

Lucien saw.

So did Zee.

The binding strengthened violently.

The creature crashed into the floor.

Lucien raised a Vesper barrier behind the children just as another predator came through the side wall.

Its claws struck the barrier.

The shield bowed.

Lucien gritted his teeth.

“Move!”

Rez redirected it.

Ner closed the opening.

Vale cut low.

Caldrin cut high.

The predator recoiled.

Zee attacked.

Crimson Mana struck it.

The blast passed through its smoke.

Wrong.

He shifted immediately.

Demonic perception.

Presence.

He found the thing underneath its visible body.

Zee lunged where his eyes told him nothing existed.

His hands closed around something solid.

The predator screamed.

Rez’s Blue-White Mana struck the exposed core.

Zareth hit it from the side.

The creature broke apart.

One down.

The others learned.

A predator came for Zee from above.

He sensed it too late.

Hooked claws tore across his shoulder.

Pain burned through him.

He hit the floor.

Crimson scales spread immediately around the wound.

Permanent patches along his shoulder thickened.

New scales raced toward his upper arm.

The predator struck again.

Its claw scraped scales instead of flesh.

Still painful.

Less damage.

Zee rolled.

Another creature caught him around the ribs.

Six limbs wrapped around his torso.

Pressure increased.

His breath vanished.

Bone cracked.

Not death.

Never death.

Pain.

Crushing.

His body fought to survive while the predator tightened.

Zee’s Crimson Mana surged uselessly through part of its smoke.

He stopped wasting it.

Found the true presence.

Then struck.

Once.

Twice.

The predator squeezed harder.

Another rib cracked.

Zee screamed.

Zareth’s roar shook the Vault.

The Dragon Link flooded with rage.

Zee. Pain. Break.

Teal-and-Gold Mana exploded.

“Zareth, wait!”

Too late.

The dragon slammed into the predator.

Its grip loosened.

Zee tore free and rolled across stone.

His ribs had already begun knitting.

Slow enough to hurt.

Fast enough to keep him moving.

No resurrection.

Nothing returned because nothing had ended.

He stood.

Blood ran down his side.

The predator attacked his head.

One hooked limb closed around Zee’s developing horn.

Agony exploded through his skull.

Something answered.

Not Witch.

Not Vampire.

Not Wolf.

Crimson Dragon energy erupted from the horn.

The predator’s limb began burning from the point of contact.

Not Zareth’s fire.

Not Aurelia’s.

Crimson.

Zee’s.

The creature recoiled.

Zee fell to one knee and grabbed his head.

“Do that again,” Rez shouted, “but preferably without letting it grab your horn first!”

Zee glared.

“Helpful!”

Ner trapped another predator inside a shrinking Golden boundary.

“It is more useful than what you are doing!”

“I am bleeding!”

“You do that all the time!”

Rez laughed.

A predator nearly took his head off while he was distracted.

Lucien’s barrier caught it.

“Perhaps fewer jokes!”

“Yes, Father!”

“No promises!” Zee shouted.

Aurelia looked toward him.

“Zee!”

“Sorry!”

Caldrin laughed while stabbing something.

The creatures kept coming.

Not endlessly.

But enough.

Aurelia stopped holding back entirely.

Witch Mana surrounded her hands.

Dark red Dragon energy moved beneath it in thin flashes.

She did not transform.

She did not need to.

Both natures were present.

Both awake.

The construct’s words returned to Zee.

Two awake.

He understood that much.

Aurelia was complete in both parts.

Zee was not.

Four.

Three awake.

His scales did not change that.

Neither did his horns.

Neither did the Dragon energy moving through his blood.

Something inside him was still sleeping by whatever standard the Vault understood.

A predator rushed Ner.

Rez blasted it sideways.

Ner sealed the wall before it could escape.

Zareth attacked.

The creature dissolved into smoke.

Zee felt where it moved.

“Behind Rez!”

Rez ducked.

Vale’s sword passed over him.

Caldrin followed.

The predator became physical for half a heartbeat.

Aurelia bound it.

Lucien reinforced the binding.

Ner closed the space around it.

Zareth tore away the bone armor.

Zee grabbed the exposed core.

Crimson Mana entered through his hands.

The predator convulsed.

Not absorption.

Not blood evolution.

Just pressure focused exactly where its real body existed.

“Now!”

Rez struck.

Blue-White Mana detonated through the core.

The final Vault Predator collapsed.

Silence hit the chamber.

Zee dropped to one knee.

His ribs hurt.

His shoulder burned.

His horn throbbed.

Aurelia was beside him immediately.

“Look at me.”

“I am fine.”

“You are never allowed to say that while bleeding.”

“That seems unfair.”

She touched his ribs carefully.

He winced.

“Cracked.”

“Less cracked now.”

“That is not a medical term.”

Caldrin sheathed one sword.

“It should be.”

The construct flickered.

“Breach ended.”

Lucien stared at it.

“You could have warned us those creatures existed.”

“They did not.”

Caldrin raised an eyebrow.

“Until?”

“Door opened.”

Everyone looked toward Ner.

His eyes widened.

“That is not my fault.”

Zee looked toward him.

“No.”

Ner relaxed.

Zee grinned.

“It is ours.”

Ner shoved him.

Zee winced.

“My ribs!”

“You deserved that.”

Rez pointed between them.

“This is why neither of you gets sole credit.”

Zareth sent through the Link:

Mine.

Zee stared.

“You did not open anything.”

Zareth showed him an image of himself breaking the wall.

Rez laughed.

“He still thinks that would have worked.”

Aurelia closed her eyes.

“We are leaving.”

No one argued.

This time.

The bronze door remained open behind them.

Lucien did not order it destroyed.

He did not know whether it could be.

Instead, he ordered the entire lower approach sealed behind multiple guarded checkpoints.

No one entered alone.

No one touched the Vault without permission.

No one experimented with the thresholds.

Especially not the children.

Rez objected to that description.

Lucien ignored him.

As they reached the upper palace, Lucien stopped beside Aurelia.

“You are Dragon-Witch.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“Yes.”

“You planned to tell me?”

“No.”

Lucien absorbed that.

Caldrin passed behind them.

“I suspected.”

Lucien looked toward him.

“You have mentioned that.”

“I wanted to make sure history records it.”

Vale shoved him forward.

Lucien returned his attention to Aurelia.

“You trusted my kingdom with your son.”

“Yes.”

“But not with this.”

Aurelia’s gaze did not waver.

“They are different secrets.”

Lucien was silent.

Then nodded once.

“I may not like that answer.”

“You do not have to.”

“No.”

He looked exhausted.

“But I have an ancient Vault under my kingdom, disappearing thresholds across the world, creatures walking through stone, and a son who believes seventeen makes him invincible.”

Rez frowned.

“I have never said invincible.”

Lucien looked at him.

“You followed three ten-year-olds into a forbidden underground Vault.”

Rez considered.

“Fair.”

Ner looked toward Zee.

“You called it ours.”

Zee shrugged.

“We both opened it.”

“I did most of it.”

“You were the key.”

Ner stopped walking.

Zee smiled.

Ner punched him in the arm.

Rez laughed.

Zareth sent approval through the Link.

For the first time since entering the Vault, Zee felt light.

Then Aurelia touched his shoulder.

“Tonight.”

He looked up.

“What?”

“We talk.”

His smile disappeared.

Aurelia held his gaze.

“About my blood.”

Zee looked toward the faint scales still visible along her wrist.

House Vesper knew something now.

Lucien knew.

Vale knew.

The royal witch knew.

Caldrin had apparently decided suspicion counted as knowledge.

But Zee understood there was more.

A name.

A history.

A reason his mother had spent decades hiding half of herself.

Zee nodded.

For once, he did not argue.

Because the question waiting for him tonight was not about Victor.

Not about the prophecy.

Not about the Blood Rain.

Not about why the world feared him.

For once, part of the answer to who Zee Darkheart was might begin with his mother.
