Chapter Forty-Four

The Kingdom That Could Not Eat Pride

The grain stopped at sunrise.

Not gradually.

Not with another warning.

Every northern wagon under compact protection turned around at the border.

By breakfast, House Vesper knew.

By noon, the markets knew.

By evening, people who had never heard the name Maelor Renn were already arguing over flour.

Rez spent most of that day in council.

Zee lasted forty minutes.

That was a personal record.

He stood near the rear windows while Vesper ministers moved stones across a trade map.

Blue represented existing supply.

White represented emergency reserves.

Red represented villages that would begin experiencing shortages first.

There were too many red stones.

Councilor Hane tapped the northern road.

“We have enough royal reserve for several weeks.”

Lucien sat at the head of the table.

“That is not the question.”

“No.”

Hane looked toward Rez.

“The question is whether we continue holding Renn while the compact remains suspended.”

Rez’s expression stayed flat.

“We do.”

One of the older nobles sighed.

“Your Highness—”

“Do not.”

The room quieted.

Rez leaned forward.

“If your next sentence is that children being kidnapped matters less than grain prices, improve it before you speak.”

The noble closed his mouth.

Zee smiled.

Rez saw.

“Do not encourage me.”

“I did nothing.”

“You have the face.”

Ner sat beside three ledgers near the opposite wall.

He looked up.

“He does.”

Zee turned toward him.

“You are supposed to support me.”

“I support truth.”

“That sounds suspiciously convenient.”

Ner’s mouth twitched.

Lucien ignored them.

“Western coven routes?”

Lady Merrow pointed toward the lower map.

“Available.”

“Capacity?”

“Insufficient alone.”

“River?”

“Possible.”

“Fast?”

“No.”

Rez looked toward the red stones.

“Which villages run out first?”

Lady Merrow moved three markers.

“These.”

Two sat near Vesper’s northeastern edge.

The third was farther south.

Small.

Agricultural.

Ironically dependent on northern grain because the region grew medicinal plants instead of wheat.

Rez’s jaw tightened.

Zee recognized that expression.

The Crown Prince was no longer discussing politics.

He was seeing people.

Good.

Lucien asked, “What do you want to do?”

Several councilors looked toward him.

Rez looked back at his father.

Not surprised.

Lucien had been doing this increasingly over the last few years.

Handing him real decisions.

Not pretend ones.

Rez touched the western route.

“Buy from the covens.”

Hane frowned.

“We already established it is not enough.”

“I know.”

Then the river.

“Lease civilian barges.”

“Expensive.”

“Yes.”

“Slow.”

“Yes.”

“Dangerous this season.”

“Yes.”

Hane stared.

Rez continued.

“Then reduce palace stores.”

Silence.

A noble blinked.

“Your Highness?”

“Royal reserve feeds the northeastern villages first.”

Lady Merrow looked toward Lucien.

Rez did not.

He was not asking permission yet.

“Vesper city takes ration reduction.”

Hane frowned.

“You cannot cut capital supply without unrest.”

“I can if the palace cuts first.”

Zee stopped smiling.

That was smart.

Rez continued.

“Publish the reductions.”

“Royal kitchens.”

“Barracks.”

“Noble allotments.”

“Mine.”

His finger touched the map.

“We do not ask outer villages to absorb a political punishment aimed at us while the palace eats normally.”

No one spoke.

Then Lucien leaned back.

“Approved.”

Rez looked toward him.

That was it.

No ceremony.

No praise.

Responsibility handed over and accepted.

Zee liked watching him become a ruler.

It was strange.

Rez could be laughing on a roof one night and restructuring Vesper food supply the next morning.

Both were real.

Both were him.

Councilor Hane sighed.

“We still need more grain.”

Ner spoke without looking up.

“The western covens have more.”

Lady Merrow frowned.

“They already stated maximum sale.”

“They stated maximum sale to Vesper.”

Everyone turned.

Ner finally looked up.

“What?”

Rez narrowed his eyes.

“What did you find?”

Ner closed one ledger and opened another.

“The covens are not refusing because they lack grain.”

“They are refusing because they do not want to be seen choosing Vesper against the Northern Council.”

Lucien’s expression sharpened.

“How do you know?”

Ner pointed toward three shipping marks.

“They increased deliveries to two neutral trading Houses last week.”

Lady Merrow approached.

“How much?”

“Enough.”

Rez stood.

“So we buy through the neutral Houses.”

Hane shook his head.

“At double cost.”

“Then negotiate.”

“With whom?”

Ner looked toward Rez.

“The covens.”

Rez frowned.

“They will deny involvement.”

“Probably.”

Zee watched Ner’s face.

There.

That particular little confidence.

He had a plan.

Zee knew it.

Rez knew it.

Lucien probably knew it too.

“What?” Rez asked.

Ner leaned back.

“They invited me.”

The room changed.

Zee’s attention snapped fully toward him.

“Who?”

“The western coven delegates.”

“When?”

“This morning.”

“You did not tell me.”

Ner looked toward Zee.

“They did not invite you.”

“I noticed.”

Rez covered a smile.

Zee ignored him.

Lucien asked, “Why?”

Ner hesitated.

That was enough to make Zee stop being annoyed.

“Because of my Mana.”

Aurelia, who had remained silent until now, looked up.

“Specific request?”

“Yes.”

Ner handed Lucien a sealed letter.

“Their senior ward-master wants to discuss Golden boundary work.”

Zee’s scales warmed faintly.

Not danger.

Protectiveness.

He noticed.

Controlled it.

Ner noticed too.

Their eyes met.

Zee looked away first.

Annoying.

Lucien read the letter.

“They want you to come without royal escort.”

Zee immediately said, “No.”

Ner immediately said, “Yes.”

They stared at each other.

Rez sighed.

“We are doing this now?”

Ner folded his arms.

“I can go to a coven meeting without Zee attached to me.”

“I am not attached to you.”

“You tried answering before Lucien finished reading.”

“That proves nothing.”

“It proves everything.”

Zee stepped closer.

“They asked specifically for no royal escort.”

“Yes.”

“That is suspicious.”

“Or political.”

“Same thing.”

Rez muttered, “Unfortunately true.”

Ner looked toward Lucien.

“I want to go.”

Zee’s jaw tightened.

There.

The actual point.

Not whether Zee approved.

Ner wanted it.

His problem.

His choice.

Zee hated that.

Which meant he needed to learn from it.

He exhaled.

“Fine.”

Ner blinked.

“That easy?”

“No.”

Zee looked at him.

“I just know you will go anyway.”

Ner smiled.

“That sounds more accurate.”

Aurelia watched Zee carefully.

Progress.

Again.

Lucien tapped the letter.

“You will not go alone.”

Ner frowned.

“They explicitly requested—”

“No royal escort.”

Lucien looked toward Caldrin.

Caldrin smiled.

“I am barely royal-adjacent.”

Ner groaned.

Zee laughed.

That helped.

Until Lucien looked toward Rez.

“You go too.”

Rez straightened.

“As Crown Prince?”

“No.”

That surprised everyone.

“As Nereus’s friend.”

Rez looked toward Ner.

Then Lucien.

“Why?”

“Because if this becomes negotiation, I want you there.”

“And if it does not?”

“Then stay quiet.”

Rez looked offended.

Zee laughed harder.

Lucien turned toward Zee.

“You stay.”

Zee stopped laughing.

“What?”

Aurelia’s mouth twitched.

Lucien continued.

“You are the exact opposite of politically invisible.”

“That feels insulting.”

“It is factual.”

Zee looked toward Ner.

Ner tried not to smile.

Failed.

“You seem happy.”

“A little.”

“I hope the covens are terrible.”

Ner laughed.

⸻

Zareth returned from Drakonis that afternoon.

He came down from cloud cover over Vesper’s eastern wall and landed in the training field without damaging anything.

Mostly.

One stone cracked.

Zee decided not to count it.

The Dragon Link hit him the moment Zareth touched ground.

Heat.

Drakonis.

Annoyance.

Three older Dragons arguing.

A ceremonial chamber.

A map with a red border.

Then—

Food.

Zee stared.

“You left for royal council and came back hungry?”

Zareth lowered his head.

The Link delivered offended certainty.

Both possible.

Zee laughed.

He rested one hand against Zareth’s snout.

Five days apart had sharpened how much he missed the weight of the Link nearby.

Not desperation anymore.

Just absence finally filled.

Zareth studied him.

Then Ner.

Then Rez.

Then the map case in Caldrin’s hand.

Question.

Zee sighed.

“They are leaving.”

The Dragon’s attention sharpened.

An image followed.

Zee left behind.

Ner leaving.

Then amusement.

Zee glared.

“You learned too much in Drakonis.”

Zareth’s Teal-and-Gold Mana warmed beneath his scales.

Rez approached.

“Good. You are back.”

Zareth turned toward him.

Rez pointed east.

“I need a Dragon opinion.”

Zee looked at him.

“You are taking him?”

“No.”

Zareth’s head lifted.

Rez continued.

“I need to know whether the northern grain suspension affects Drakonis caravan routes.”

Zareth’s Link shifted toward Zee.

A complicated impression.

Mountain pass.

Dragon-controlled air corridors.

Two neutral road crossings.

No northern authority.

Zee translated.

“Not directly.”

Rez nodded.

“What about Vesper merchants using Drakonis roads?”

Zareth considered.

Then sent:

Possible.

Expensive.

Political consequences.

Dragon Queen approval required.

Rez smiled.

“I knew I liked him.”

Zee frowned.

“He is not solving your grain problem for free.”

Rez stared.

“Did he say that?”

“No.”

Zareth sent approval.

Rez laughed.

Ner arrived wearing a travel coat.

Zee’s eyes moved toward him before he could stop them.

Dark coat.

Gold stitching near the cuffs.

Hair tied back.

He looked—

No.

Zee looked at Zareth.

The Dragon Link instantly returned the emotional equivalent of laughter.

“I hate this bond.”

Ner stopped.

“What did he do?”

“Nothing.”

Rez smiled.

“Zareth has become very observant.”

Zee looked at Rez.

“Do you want to survive fifteen?”

Rez laughed.

Ner shook his head.

Then his expression became serious.

“We leave now.”

Zee hated those words more than expected.

Not because danger was certain.

Because he was not going.

That felt new.

For years the core four had become so practiced together that being excluded from one another’s problems felt unnatural.

But that was exactly why it needed to happen.

Ner stepped toward him.

“I will be fine.”

Zee frowned.

“I did not say you wouldn’t.”

“You have the face.”

“Everyone needs to stop saying that.”

Ner smiled.

Then lowered his voice.

“You really okay?”

Zee looked at him.

Close.

Too close for the courtyard to suddenly feel empty.

“I do not like not being there.”

“I know.”

“That does not mean I think you cannot handle it.”

Ner’s expression softened.

“That is better.”

“Do not sound surprised.”

“I am a little surprised.”

Zee shoved his shoulder.

Ner shoved back.

Their hands brushed again.

Both noticed.

Neither moved immediately.

Rez deliberately turned toward Caldrin.

“Beautiful weather.”

Caldrin looked at the cloudy sky.

“Awful.”

“Yes.”

Ner pulled his hand away first.

“I will come back.”

Zee nodded.

“I know.”

Zareth pressed a faint impression into the Link.

Return.

Same lesson.

Different person.

Zee exhaled.

“Go before I change my mind and become unreasonable.”

Ner grinned.

“You would have to become unreasonable first.”

Zee stared.

Rez grabbed Ner by the shoulder and steered him toward the horses.

“Excellent final words.”

⸻

Zee lasted six hours without them before Aurelia found him training hard enough to crack stone.

Not uncontrolled.

Just angry.

She stood outside the practice ring.

“You are sulking.”

“I am training.”

“You can do both.”

Zee hit the dummy again.

Crimson Mana snapped through the wood.

He stopped before destroying it.

Progress.

Aurelia entered.

“Why are you upset?”

“I am not.”

She looked at the broken post.

Zee sighed.

“Ner wanted to go.”

“Yes.”

“So he went.”

“Yes.”

“That is good.”

“Yes.”

“I hate it.”

Aurelia smiled.

“There.”

Zee glared.

“Do not.”

She approached.

“You are learning something unpleasant.”

“What?”

“People you love will choose risks without your permission.”

He hated the sentence immediately.

“That seems badly designed.”

“Frequently.”

Zee looked down.

His horns had warmed.

He focused.

Not danger.

Not rage.

Fear.

There.

He could name it now.

That mattered.

“What if something happens?”

“Then something happens.”

Zee’s face tightened.

Aurelia continued before he could reject the answer.

“You cannot protect people by reducing their lives until nothing dangerous reaches them.”

Victor lived inside that lesson whether she said his name or not.

Control.

Containment.

Fear disguised as protection.

Zee understood.

He hated understanding.

“I am not Victor.”

“No.”

Aurelia touched his cheek.

“But no one becomes someone terrible all at once.”

Zee looked at her.

“People become what they keep choosing.”

Her old words.

Still true.

He nodded.

Then asked, “Can I at least be annoyed?”

Aurelia smiled.

“Yes.”

“Good.”

He hit the dummy again.

This time she laughed.

⸻

The western covens answered before midnight.

Not through letter.

Through Rez.

A Vesper messenger arrived at full speed carrying a sealed strip marked with Crown Prince authorization.

Lucien opened it.

Zee stood beside him.

Aurelia behind.

Zareth occupied the terrace.

The message was short.

Negotiations accepted. Grain available. Terms changed.

Zee smiled.

Then Lucien read the next line.

The smile vanished.

Ner remains with coven ward-master until morning by his choice.

Zee looked up.

“Why?”

Lucien continued.

Boundary breach discovered beneath western grain storehouses. Golden Mana required to stabilize without destroying reserves. Rez assisting evacuation. No attack confirmed.

Zee’s horns warmed.

Danger.

Real this time.

Zareth felt it.

The Dragon Link tightened.

Aurelia read over Lucien’s shoulder.

“Do not assume.”

Zee looked toward her.

“It says breach.”

“It also says no attack confirmed.”

“Something is under the storehouses.”

“Yes.”

“Ner is staying.”

“By choice.”

That was the part.

Again.

Zee paced once.

Then stopped.

“What can I do?”

Aurelia’s eyebrows rose.

Not Can I go?

What can I do?

Better.

Lucien handed him a second note.

“Rez requested Vesper ward maps.”

Zee took it.

“Then we send them.”

He turned toward the archive corridor.

Aurelia smiled faintly.

The hunted child would have run west.

The fifteen-year-old boy was learning that sometimes protecting someone meant helping from where you were.

That lesson lasted until the second message arrived.

Then everything changed.

The western boundary beneath the grain houses had not been breached from outside.

It had been opened from within.

And the person who opened it had used Pure Golden Mana.
