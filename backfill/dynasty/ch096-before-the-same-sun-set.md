Chapter Ninety-Six

Before the Same Sun Set

Cassel Rhune died before dawn.

Zee remained beside the ruined shrine after the Godslayer’s body went cold.

Blood covered the snow.

Most of it was his.

Black Divinity still burned through the wounds Cassel had opened.

One cut crossed Zee’s face.

Another split his shoulder.

A deeper wound remained beneath his ribs where Cassel’s blade had entered and refused to let Vampire healing close it properly.

His scales were cracked.

His hands were burned.

His heart still carried damage from the blade Cassel had driven through it.

Zee stood anyway.

Zareth watched him in human form.

“You need a healer.”

“I need breakfast.”

“You were stabbed through the heart.”

“I noticed.”

“That was not a joke.”

“It hurt like one.”

Zareth stared at him.

Through the Link came irritation wrapped around concern.

You are barely healing.

Zee looked down at the blackened edge of the wound in his side.

His Crimson biology had learned something during the fight.

Not how to use Dark Divinity.

Not how to absorb it.

Only how to resist being opened quite as deeply by it the next time.

Small.

Incomplete.

The burns still hurt.

The wounds still resisted healing.

Black Divinity remained dangerous.

Good.

Zee preferred dangerous things that stayed dangerous.

Ner arrived as morning light reached the mountains.

He took one look at Zee.

“No.”

Zee frowned.

“No what?”

“Whatever you are about to say.”

“I was going to say hello.”

“You were going to say you are fine.”

Zee smiled.

“I am alive.”

“That was not the question.”

Ner approached and touched the skin near Zee’s ribs without touching the wound itself.

Bloodsong reacted immediately.

Then recoiled.

His expression hardened.

“This is wrong.”

“Godslayer.”

“I know.”

“You asked.”

“I did not.”

Zareth looked between them.

“I am going back to Drakonis before this becomes domestic.”

Zee looked at him.

“You are already involved.”

“That is exactly why I am leaving.”

Through the Link came warmth.

Then Zareth shifted.

True Dragon form exploded across the mountain ridge.

Wings opened.

He launched toward Drakonis.

Ner watched him disappear.

Then looked back at Zee.

“We are going home.”

Zee opened his mouth.

Thunder answered first.

Not ordinary thunder.

The sky turned white.

Then black.

Clouds formed in seconds.

The mountain pressure changed hard enough to make stone groan.

Ner looked upward.

“That is not weather.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“No.”

A figure descended through the storm.

Bare feet never touched the ground.

Gold-white armor.

A spear made from condensed lightning.

Eyes filled with blue divine fire.

The air bent around him.

Not because of magic.

Authority.

The mountain itself seemed to recognize him.

Zee’s smile widened.

Ner sighed.

“Of course.”

The God looked at Cassel’s corpse.

Then Zee.

“So.”

Zee wiped blood from his mouth.

“So?”

“You killed the Godslayer.”

“Yes.”

The God’s gaze moved over Zee’s wounds.

“You are weaker than I expected.”

Zee looked at his own bleeding chest.

“Rude.”

The God ignored that.

“I am Kharos.”

The storm intensified.

“Lord of the Tempest Throne.”

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“Congratulations.”

Ner covered his face briefly.

Kharos’s expression hardened.

“You have killed divine blood before.”

“Yes.”

“You consumed it.”

“Yes.”

“You survived.”

“Yes.”

“And now you have killed one who carried Dark Divinity.”

Zee’s smile disappeared.

Kharos descended another foot.

“Mortals are beginning to mistake survival for authority.”

“I am not mortal.”

“You are not divine either.”

“I know.”

“That distinction will be corrected today.”

Zee glanced toward Cassel’s corpse.

Then back at Kharos.

“You waited until after he cut me apart.”

“I observed an opportunity.”

Zee laughed.

Actually laughed.

Blood ran down his side while he did it.

Kharos frowned.

“You find this amusing?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Zee’s fangs descended.

“Because Cassel already reminded me how much I enjoy fighting things that can hurt me.”

The storm stopped moving.

For one second.

Then Kharos attacked.

⸻

Divine lightning struck first.

Zee moved.

Too slow.

The bolt hit his shoulder.

His entire body locked.

Crimson-black scales cracked.

The old Black Divinity wound reopened.

Zee hit the ground.

Kharos raised one hand.

Gravity changed.

Zee slammed deeper into stone.

Ner’s Gold surged.

Kharos looked toward him.

“Kneel.”

Divine authority struck.

Ner’s knees buckled.

He caught himself with one hand.

Gold shook around him.

Zee’s eyes changed.

Slits.

“Do not.”

Kharos looked back.

“Do not what?”

“Order him.”

“You command everyone around you.”

“No.”

Zee pushed against the divine pressure.

Muscles tore.

Dragon force answered.

He stood.

Slowly.

“They choose whether they stay.”

Kharos drove the spear forward.

Zee caught the shaft.

Lightning exploded through his body.

His hands burned.

Still he held it.

Kharos stared.

Zee smiled.

“You keep expecting pain to make me obedient.”

Then Witchcraft struck.

Silent.

One closed fist.

The stone beneath Kharos collapsed.

Not enough to hurt a God.

Enough to shift his footing.

Assassin lesson.

Position first.

Zee pulled.

Vampire strength.

Dragon force.

Kharos stumbled one step.

Wolf instinct saw the opening before thought finished forming.

Zee struck his knee.

Hard.

Divine bone cracked.

Kharos roared.

The storm answered.

A wall of wind threw Zee across the mountain.

He landed badly.

One rib broke.

Then another.

His healing remained slow.

Cassel’s wounds still interfered.

Kharos flew toward him.

“You should already be dead.”

Zee rolled aside as the spear hit stone.

“I hear that often.”

Kharos backhanded him.

Divine strength broke part of Zee’s jaw.

Zee spat blood.

Then laughed again.

Kharos finally looked disturbed.

Good.

⸻

The fight crossed the mountain.

Kharos controlled the sky.

Zee controlled nothing that easily.

So he stopped fighting the sky.

He went into the forest.

Wolf form.

Four-legged.

Near-black fur.

Crimson-and-gold eyes.

No horns.

No scales.

No Dragon features.

Only predator.

Kharos descended after him.

Mistake.

Lightning destroyed trees.

Zee was never where it landed.

Wolf instinct read pressure shifts.

Air displacement.

Static.

Movement.

Kharos raised the spear.

Wolf Zee changed direction before it moved.

Then changed back to human form behind him.

One hand lifted.

“Down.”

Witch force struck Kharos from above.

Divine authority resisted.

For half a second.

Enough.

Zee hit him.

Fist.

Elbow.

Horn.

Claw.

No wasted motion.

Cassel had wounded Zee.

Khandra Parbat had taught him what to do when power could not solve a fight.

Kharos drove lightning into Zee’s abdomen.

Zee convulsed.

The God grabbed him by the throat.

“You are nothing beside divinity.”

Zee’s eyes became very calm.

Human pupils.

Choice.

“Then why did you wait until I was wounded?”

Kharos froze.

There.

Fear did not arrive yet.

But doubt did.

Zee bit his wrist.

⸻

Divine blood entered Zee’s mouth.

Hot.

Storm-heavy.

Powerful.

His Crimson biology reacted immediately.

Not worship.

Not transformation.

Processing.

The divine blood tried to expand.

Zee’s body crushed it down.

No God nature.

No storm authority.

No divinity.

Only nourishment.

Pressure.

Adaptation.

Kharos screamed and ripped free.

Zee swallowed.

The old Black Divinity wounds burned violently.

For one terrible second, divine blood and anti-divine residue collided inside him.

Then Zee’s body separated them.

Not perfectly.

Enough.

The next time Kharos’s authority crashed into Zee, it still hurt.

But Zee recognized the pressure sooner.

Braced sooner.

Survived cleaner.

Small.

Not immunity.

Just Zee learning.

Kharos saw it.

“You are changing.”

“I always am.”

“That is the abomination.”

Zee wiped divine blood from his mouth.

“No.”

His eyes narrowed into slits again.

“That is the consequence.”

Then he attacked.

⸻

By sunset, the storm was dying.

So was Kharos.

One divine arm hung broken.

His spear was gone.

Zee had thrown it into a ravine after breaking three of his own fingers taking it.

Kharos’s regeneration kept repairing wounds.

Zee kept reopening them.

Witchcraft disrupted position.

Wolf instinct predicted retreat.

Vampire speed closed distance.

Assassin technique targeted the same damaged joints.

Crimson Demonic Dragon force endured impacts that should have shattered him.

Zee himself looked worse.

Blood everywhere.

One eye swollen.

Three black Godslayer wounds still open.

Scales broken.

Breathing heavy.

Still smiling.

Kharos backed toward the edge of the mountain.

The God finally smelled afraid.

Zee stopped.

Enjoyed it.

Fully.

Deliberately.

Kharos understood.

“You could finish this.”

“Yes.”

“Then why are you waiting?”

Zee stepped closer.

“Because you came here believing you were above fear.”

Kharos’s divine fire flickered.

Zee smiled.

“I wanted you to meet it.”

Kharos raised one trembling hand.

“You cannot kill what I am.”

Zee reached him.

“I already have.”

He bit.

Kharos screamed.

Zee fed deeply.

Divine regeneration fought.

Then weakened.

Storm clouds collapsed overhead.

Kharos struck Zee.

Once.

Twice.

The third blow barely moved him.

Zee kept drinking.

The God’s heartbeat slowed.

Divine light dimmed.

Fear became panic.

Zee pulled back before the final beat.

Kharos sagged in his grip.

“Please.”

There.

A God begging.

Zee’s face remained calm.

He could have prolonged it.

Wanted to.

Instead he chose enough.

“Now you understand.”

He broke Kharos’s neck.

Then tore the remaining divine life from him through blood.

The storm stopped.

Completely.

Kharos died.

Permanently.

No escape.

No avatar.

No return.

The Godslayer lay dead before dawn.

The God lay dead before sunset.

Same day.

Zee looked toward the darkening sky.

“Long day.”

Then the river below the mountain rose upward.

⸻

Not flooded.

Rose.

A column of water climbed from the valley against gravity.

Ner moved beside Zee.

Gold ready.

Zee watched.

Water spiraled through the air.

Divine blood from Kharos’s corpse had reached cracks in the mountain.

Underground streams.

The water reacted violently.

Then became still.

Impossible stillness.

A woman stepped from it.

No gasp.

No struggle.

No wet stumble.

She walked through suspended water as if crossing a palace floor.

Dark hair fell down her back.

Pearl-black armor covered her shoulders and waist.

A crown of dark silver rested against her head.

Not decorative.

Royal.

Her eyes were deep blue-black.

Not glowing.

Worse.

Watching.

She looked at Kharos’s corpse.

Then Cassel’s blood still dried across Zee.

Then at Zee himself.

The wounds.

The divine blood.

The broken scales.

She showed no awe.

Only irritation.

“You killed a god.”

Zee looked at Kharos.

“Yes.”

“You killed a Godslayer this morning.”

“Yes.”

Her gaze moved toward the river still climbing behind her.

“And somehow both became my problem.”

Zee smiled.

“Long day.”

She stared at him.

For one second.

Then:

“I already dislike you.”

Zee’s smile widened.

“Good.”

The woman looked toward the water.

“You have made the ocean angry.”

Zee glanced at the dead God.

“Tell it to wait.”

Ner started laughing.

The Queen did not.

Not yet.
