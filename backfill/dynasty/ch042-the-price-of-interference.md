Chapter Forty-Two

The Price of Interference

The first argument waiting for Zee Darkheart at House Vesper wore silk.

He preferred knives.

Knives were honest.

Knives did not sit beneath blue-white Vesper banners drinking expensive wine while explaining why eighteen kidnapped children should have remained somebody else’s problem.

Zee stood against the western wall of the royal council chamber with his arms crossed.

His travel coat was still damp from mountain snow.

A shallow cut beneath his jaw had already closed.

Permanent Crimson scales patterned the sides of his neck, disappeared beneath his collar, then returned along both forearms.

His crimson-black horns made pretending he was an ordinary visiting prince impossible.

That was fine.

He had stopped trying years ago.

Across the chamber, six Vesper nobles argued around the council table.

King Lucien sat at its head.

Rez sat to his right.

Not behind him.

Not watching.

Participating.

The fifteen-year-old Crown Prince had changed clothes after returning from the mountains, but nothing else about him suggested rest. His black hair remained wind-tossed, and Blue-White Mana occasionally flickered around two fingers whenever someone said something especially stupid.

It had happened four times.

Zee was counting.

Councilor Darius Hane struck one palm against the table.

“The Northern Border Council has formally protested.”

Lucien said nothing.

Hane continued.

“Prince Rez entered disputed territory with armed companions, apprehended licensed contractors, seized private documents, and transferred detainees without authorization.”

Zee lifted one eyebrow.

“Licensed kidnappers.”

Every head turned.

Rez closed his eyes.

Lucien looked toward Zee.

“I was handling that.”

“I improved it.”

Ner, seated near the opposite end of the table with three recovered ledgers spread before him, whispered, “You did not.”

Zee looked at him.

Ner did not look back.

That somehow made Zee look longer.

Five years had sharpened Nereus Azrion in ways Zee was still becoming embarrassingly aware of.

His face had lost the roundness of childhood.

His Pure Golden Mana moved with restrained confidence instead of flashing at every irritation.

His hands—

Zee stopped.

Why was he looking at Ner’s hands?

Rez cleared his throat.

Zee immediately faced the table.

Rez’s mouth twitched.

Traitor.

Councilor Hane continued.

“The point remains. Whatever those mercenaries did, Vesper cannot simply cross every border because Zee Darkheart decides someone deserves saving.”

Zee’s jaw tightened.

Lucien spoke before he could.

“They did not enter under Zee’s authority.”

Hane looked toward Rez.

Lucien continued.

“They entered under mine.”

That silenced several nobles.

Rez looked at his father.

Lucien did not look back.

He was letting the statement stand.

The mission had begun with requests transmitted through Vesper.

That made the consequences Vesper’s too.

Another noble, Lady Merrow, leaned forward.

“And now the Northern Council threatens to suspend grain passage through the upper roads.”

Theron Veyrath sat near the rear windows and muttered, “Then perhaps their grain should learn to walk around us.”

Seraphine, beside him, smiled.

“You would be terrible at diplomacy.”

“I survived captivity.”

“That did not make you charming.”

“It should have.”

Aurelia sat between them.

Five years of having both siblings back had not made their arguments less exhausting.

It had made her better at ignoring them.

She looked toward Zee instead.

He recognized the warning.

Do not explode at politicians.

He had received that look many times.

Zee sighed.

Rez leaned over the table.

“What exactly does the Northern Border Council want?”

Hane passed him a sealed letter.

Rez broke the wax.

Read.

His expression changed.

“Return of the prisoners.”

Ner looked up.

“Of course.”

Rez continued.

“Return of all seized property.”

“The ledgers?” Ner asked.

“Yes.”

“No.”

Several nobles looked toward him.

Ner calmly placed one hand over the nearest book.

Pure Golden Mana flickered across the cover.

“No.”

Councilor Hane frowned.

“Nereus, the documents were taken beyond Vesper jurisdiction.”

“They document the sale of children.”

“They allegedly document—”

“They document it.”

Ner looked directly at him.

“I spent six hours reading them.”

Hane’s expression hardened.

“That does not make you a court.”

Ner smiled faintly.

“No.”

Golden Mana formed along the edge of one page.

Hidden writing rose from the parchment.

Names.

Payments.

Village marks.

Transfer dates.

Then vanished when Ner lowered his hand.

“It makes me capable of reading what somebody tried to hide.”

Zee smiled.

Hane did not.

Good.

Rez folded the demand.

“What else?”

Lucien answered.

“The Northern Council is sending a representative.”

“When?”

“Tomorrow.”

Rez frowned.

“They sent the complaint before the children were even back in their villages.”

Silence.

That mattered.

The mountain officials had barely taken custody before the protest arrived.

Too fast.

Zee pushed away from the wall.

“So they already knew.”

Lady Merrow looked toward him.

“Knew what?”

“That the mercenaries would be arrested.”

Rez looked at Zee.

Then the letter.

Ner stopped moving.

Lucien’s eyes sharpened.

Councilor Hane said, “Or they reacted quickly.”

Ner opened the recovered ledger again.

“No.”

His Golden Mana passed over a payment line.

“There were scheduled reporting intervals.”

Rez stood.

He crossed to Ner.

Zee joined them without invitation.

No one bothered protesting.

The four had learned years ago that trying to keep Zee out of a mystery usually resulted in him standing closer.

Ner pointed to several marks near the bottom of the page.

“Every three days.”

Rez frowned.

“These are accounting notations.”

“No.”

Ner traced one symbol.

Gold gathered inside it.

The ink resisted.

His eyes narrowed.

“Mana.”

The royal witch, standing near Lucien, approached.

“Contract binding?”

“Maybe.”

Ner looked closer.

Then corrected himself.

“No.”

Zee smiled faintly.

Ner had learned that from Aurelia.

Never let maybe pretend to be certainty.

Ner continued.

“Something is layered beneath the ink.”

“Can you open it?” Lucien asked.

“Yes.”

Zee looked at him.

Ner’s expression changed.

“Probably.”

“Better.”

Ner rolled his eyes.

He placed both hands against the ledger.

Pure Golden Mana did not flood the room.

It narrowed.

Fine threads slipped between ordinary ink and the spellwork beneath it.

One layer.

Then another.

The council chamber quieted.

Zee watched Ner work.

Really work.

No door.

No ancient Vault.

No convenient Golden key waiting for him.

This was delicate magic.

His magic.

Ner’s fingers separated.

The hidden layer rose.

Blue-black symbols appeared beneath the payment records.

Rez recognized part of the structure first.

“Oath marks.”

The royal witch nodded.

“Silence binding.”

Aurelia stood.

“On whom?”

Ner followed the magical threads.

His face hardened.

“The mercenaries.”

Zee’s Crimson eyes narrowed.

“Meaning?”

“If they identify whoever hired them…”

Ner stopped.

Everyone waited.

He looked at Lucien.

“The spell punishes them.”

“How?”

“I need the prisoner.”

Rez immediately turned toward the guards.

“Bring Kest.”

⸻

Rovan Kest had led the mountain mercenaries.

At fifteen, Zee had met enough hired killers to know several types.

Men who loved money.

Men who loved violence.

Men who loved pretending money made the violence less personal.

Kest belonged somewhere between the first two.

He entered the council chamber with iron around both wrists and resentment in every step.

His eyes found Zee.

“You.”

Zee smiled.

“Me.”

Rez looked toward him.

Zee shrugged.

“He started it.”

Kest sat.

Ner placed the ledger on the table before him.

The mercenary’s face changed.

Tiny.

Enough.

Zee heard the heartbeat betray him.

Ner did too, in another way.

Golden Mana had already begun tracing the oath embedded around Kest’s magical signature.

Ner asked, “Who hired you?”

Kest opened his mouth.

Nothing came out.

His throat seized.

He grabbed his neck.

Zee moved instinctively.

Ner raised one hand.

“Don’t.”

Zee stopped.

Kest convulsed.

Thin black lines appeared beneath the skin of his throat.

Ner cursed.

Pure Golden Mana snapped around Kest’s neck without touching him.

A barrier inside a barrier.

The black lines stopped advancing.

The mercenary dragged in air.

Lucien stood.

“Can you remove it?”

Ner did not answer immediately.

His entire attention had gone inward.

Reading.

Adjusting.

Zee saw sweat form along his temple.

“Ner?”

“Quiet.”

Zee went quiet.

Immediately.

Rez noticed.

So did Seraphine.

Neither commented.

Ner’s Golden Mana divided into dozens of thin threads.

They slipped through the curse pattern.

One by one.

No brute force.

No dramatic explosion.

Precision.

The black lines withdrew.

Kest collapsed forward against the table.

Breathing.

Alive.

Ner lowered his hands.

The room remained silent.

Then Kest looked at him.

“What did you do?”

Ner sat back.

“Stopped it.”

“Can you break it?”

“Eventually.”

Kest laughed bitterly.

“You don’t know what you’re touching.”

Ner stared.

“Neither did the people who tried kidnapping my mother when I was three.”

Kest’s smile vanished.

Ner leaned forward.

“I learned.”

Zee felt something warm and fierce in his chest.

Pride.

Mostly.

Probably.

Rez saw his face.

Again.

Zee glared before he could say anything.

Rez looked innocent.

Lucien turned toward Kest.

“You knew you were bound.”

“Yes.”

“Who did it?”

Kest’s jaw locked.

The black lines twitched.

Ner immediately stabilized them.

Kest looked toward him.

Fear entered his eyes.

Not of Ner.

Of the spell.

Ner spoke calmly.

“Do not answer yet.”

Zee’s gaze moved toward Kest’s hands.

Scars along the fingers.

Old sword calluses.

One missing nail.

He had hurt people.

He had helped cage children.

Zee knew that.

Still, watching a spell squeeze the man’s throat because someone richer wanted silence made something inside Zee turn colder.

Predators could also be owned.

That did not make them innocent.

It made the world uglier.

Rez asked, “Can the oath be traced?”

Ner looked toward the ledger.

“Maybe to whoever anchored it.”

“Without making Kest speak?”

“Yes.”

That answer changed the room.

Lucien looked at his son.

Rez understood.

“We do not need a confession yet.”

Councilor Hane frowned.

“You are proposing magical intrusion into a contract cast outside Vesper.”

Rez looked toward him.

“I am proposing identifying who used magic to prevent testimony about kidnapped children.”

“That distinction will not impress the Northern Council.”

Rez’s expression became calm.

The kind of calm Zee knew meant someone was about to lose an argument.

“Then tomorrow they can explain why.”

Zee smiled.

Lucien’s mouth almost did.

⸻

Zareth left before sunset.

Not because of the council.

Because Drakonis summoned its Crown Prince.

A royal Dragon messenger arrived in true form above Vesper carrying a sealed strip of black-red metal from the Dragon Queen.

Zareth read it.

Then looked toward Zee.

The Dragon Link opened between them.

Mountain peaks.

A circular Drakonis council chamber.

Older Dragons.

Tension.

A border flame.

Responsibility.

Zee understood enough.

“How long?”

Zareth’s answer came as an image of three sunsets.

Then uncertainty.

Zee sighed.

“Go.”

Zareth lowered his head.

Five years ago Zee would have made the word sound wounded.

Now he meant it.

Mostly.

The Link carried warmth.

Then an image of Zee standing beside Ner in the council chamber.

Zee froze.

Zareth sent intense amusement.

Zee’s crimson eyes narrowed.

“I hate you.”

Ner, several yards away, looked over.

“What?”

“Not you.”

Rez walked past.

“He means Zareth.”

Ner nodded.

“That does not explain the face.”

Zee pointed at both of them.

“Neither of you speak.”

Rez smiled.

Zareth launched from the Vesper terrace.

Teal-and-Gold Mana rolled behind his wings.

He climbed through cloud and disappeared east.

The Link thinned.

Did not vanish.

Zee watched only until he was gone.

Then turned back toward the palace.

Progress.

Ner fell into step beside him.

“You are better at that.”

“Everyone keeps saying that.”

“Because you used to become unbearable.”

“I was never unbearable.”

Ner stared.

Zee looked ahead.

“Fine.”

They walked several steps.

Their shoulders brushed.

Neither moved away immediately.

Zee noticed.

Then noticed Ner noticing.

He moved half an inch.

Ner smiled.

Zee hated being fifteen.

⸻

Ner broke the first oath layer shortly before midnight.

Not completely.

Enough.

The magic did not point toward a hidden cult.

It did not reveal an ancient enemy.

It revealed a bureaucrat.

Councilor Maelor Renn.

Senior representative of the Northern Border Council.

The man already traveling toward Vesper to demand the mercenaries’ return.

Rez stared at the revealed magical signature.

Then at Lucien.

“He hired them.”

Lucien remained unreadable.

“Evidence suggests he anchored the contract.”

“That is diplomatic language for yes.”

“It is accurate language.”

Rez exhaled.

Ner sat nearby drinking water.

His hands trembled faintly from the work.

Zee saw.

He crossed the room and placed a cup closer.

Ner looked up.

“I have one.”

“This one is closer.”

Ner stared at him.

Zee immediately regretted speaking.

“Just drink it.”

Ner smiled.

“Yes, Your Highness.”

“Do not.”

Ner drank.

Rez watched both of them.

Zee refused to acknowledge him.

Aurelia entered from the adjoining chamber.

“Renn arrives tomorrow.”

Zee looked toward the glowing oath signature.

“Good.”

Something in his voice changed.

Aurelia heard it.

So did Rez.

Zee looked down at the name.

A man with political authority had helped abduct children.

Had paid mercenaries.

Had silenced those same mercenaries with magic.

And now he was walking voluntarily into Vesper expecting law to protect him.

Zee’s horns warmed.

Not uncontrolled.

Not yet.

He smiled faintly.

That was what bothered Aurelia.

“Zee.”

He looked at her.

“What?”

“You are enjoying this.”

The smile disappeared.

“No.”

Aurelia said nothing.

Zee looked away.

He had not hurt Renn.

Had not even met him.

But the thought of watching the man discover that his position would not save him—

That felt good.

Too good.

Rez folded the evidence.

“Tomorrow is not a hunt.”

Zee looked toward him.

“No?”

“No.”

Rez’s expression sharpened.

“Tomorrow he walks into my father’s court.”

A small smile appeared.

“Which is worse.”

Zee laughed.

Aurelia closed her eyes.

Ner looked between them.

“Both of you are concerning.”

Rez stood.

“Get some sleep.”

Zee stared.

“You are ordering me?”

“I am Crown Prince.”

“You say that when you have no argument.”

“I also say it when I am Crown Prince.”

Ner rose.

“He is right.”

Zee looked betrayed.

“You too?”

Ner passed close enough that his hand brushed Zee’s wrist.

Accidental.

Probably.

Zee’s Dragon scales warmed beneath the contact.

Ner noticed.

His eyes lifted.

For one strange heartbeat neither spoke.

Then Ner continued walking.

Rez waited until he left.

Zee immediately said, “Don’t.”

Rez grinned.

“I said nothing.”

“You have the face.”

“What face?”

Zee threw a cushion at him.

Rez caught it.

Aurelia laughed despite herself.

Tomorrow Councilor Maelor Renn would arrive expecting frightened children, compromised mercenaries, and a foreign king reluctant to risk trade over villagers no powerful House claimed.

Instead he would find Crown Prince Rez Vesper waiting with evidence.

Nereus Azrion waiting with the magic Renn thought could never be opened.

And Zee Darkheart waiting with something far more dangerous than rage.

Choice.

Because at fifteen Zee was beginning to understand that being able to hurt someone was easy.

Deciding what they deserved was harder.

And he was not yet certain which part he enjoyed more.
