Chapter Forty-Three

What Monsters Deserve

Councilor Maelor Renn smiled when he entered Vesper’s royal chamber.

Zee disliked him immediately.

That meant nothing.

Zee had disliked people for terrible reasons before.

Their voices.

Their perfume.

The way they chewed.

One diplomat from the western witch territories had once made Zee furious simply because he breathed loudly through his nose.

Aurelia had informed him that this was not sufficient cause for violence.

Zee still considered the matter undecided.

So he ignored instinct.

Mostly.

Renn wore dark green robes embroidered with the silver lines of the Northern Border Council.

No weapons.

Four attendants.

Two legal scribes.

One witch adviser.

No visible fear.

He bowed to Lucien.

“Your Majesty.”

Then Rez.

“Crown Prince.”

Rez nodded.

Renn looked toward Aurelia.

“Queen Darkheart.”

Aurelia gave him nothing.

Then Zee.

The smile paused.

Only briefly.

“Prince Darkheart.”

Zee leaned against a pillar.

“Councilor.”

Renn’s eyes shifted toward Ner.

Ner sat beside the recovered ledger with one hand resting across its cover.

“Nereus Azrion.”

“Ner.”

The correction came automatically from Zee.

Silence.

Ner looked toward him.

Zee realized what he had done.

Renn’s eyebrow rose.

Rez looked down at his notes with suspicious concentration.

Ner smiled.

“Councilor.”

Zee decided he hated everyone.

Lucien spoke.

“You requested the immediate return of six prisoners currently held under Vesper protection.”

“Seven.”

Rez looked up.

“Six survived the mountain capture.”

Renn’s expression did not change.

“The seventh was not among the group you intercepted.”

Zee’s crimson eyes sharpened.

Rez went very still.

Ner looked toward the ledger.

Renn had just made a mistake.

A small one.

But good mistakes rarely announced themselves.

Rez asked, “How do you know?”

Renn paused.

“Know what?”

“How many were assigned.”

The councilor smiled.

“I read the preliminary report.”

“No.”

Rez folded his hands.

“The report listed six captured.”

Renn’s attendants shifted.

Lucien said nothing.

Rez continued.

“It never listed total company strength.”

Renn’s smile weakened.

“Then I was informed separately.”

“By whom?”

“A border official.”

“Name?”

Renn’s jaw tightened.

“This is becoming tedious.”

Zee smiled.

“Already?”

Aurelia looked toward him.

He stopped.

Rez continued.

“You demanded prisoners, seized documents, and reparations.”

“Yes.”

“You accused Vesper of unlawful intervention.”

“Correct.”

“Then answer the question.”

Renn looked at Lucien.

“Do you routinely permit your son to interrogate foreign representatives?”

Lucien’s expression remained calm.

“When they address matters within his authority.”

Rez’s gaze did not leave Renn.

Zee felt a little burst of pride.

Different from the feeling around Ner.

Much easier to understand.

Brother.

Renn adjusted his robes.

“The Northern Council received private reports.”

Rez nodded.

“Good.”

“Then produce them.”

Renn’s face hardened.

“No.”

“Then we continue.”

He gestured toward the side doors.

Rovan Kest entered.

Renn’s expression did not change.

His heartbeat did.

Zee heard it.

One hard acceleration.

Then controlled.

There.

Kest saw Renn.

Hatred entered his face.

The black oath lines beneath his throat immediately began darkening.

Ner moved.

Pure Golden Mana appeared around Kest before pain could take him.

Renn’s witch adviser stared.

“What is that?”

Ner looked toward her.

“Your Councilor’s work.”

Renn scoffed.

“Absurd.”

Ner raised one hand.

The recovered ledger opened itself.

Pages turned.

The hidden oath structure rose above them in Golden outline.

Not Golden because the original magic was his.

Golden because Ner was exposing its shape.

At the center sat Renn’s magical signature.

The councilor’s face finally changed.

Not fear.

Calculation.

“How theatrical.”

Ner smiled.

“Thank you.”

“It proves nothing.”

“It proves the oath was anchored through your Mana.”

“Forgery.”

Ner tilted his head.

“Would you like to touch it?”

Renn said nothing.

“Thought not.”

Rez stood.

“Rovan Kest, do you recognize Councilor Renn?”

Kest opened his mouth.

The oath tightened.

Ner’s Golden Mana tightened with it.

Not against Kest.

Around the curse.

Holding it apart.

Sweat ran down Ner’s face.

Zee straightened.

This was hurting him.

Not physically.

Straining.

Ner did not need rescue.

Zee knew that.

He still hated watching.

Kest forced one word.

“Yes.”

The oath erupted.

Ner grunted.

Golden Mana flashed violently.

Zee took one step forward.

Ner looked at him.

“No.”

Zee stopped.

The word irritated him.

He obeyed anyway.

Ner separated another layer.

The curse quieted.

Renn’s witch adviser had gone pale.

She looked toward her employer.

“You said those marks were employment restrictions.”

Renn did not look at her.

Rez heard.

“So you knew about them.”

The adviser swallowed.

“I knew there were oaths.”

“Did you know they punished testimony?”

“No.”

Renn snapped, “Enough.”

Rez turned toward him.

“No.”

The single word landed differently from Ner.

Royal.

Cold.

Renn’s face darkened.

“You are fifteen.”

No one moved.

Zee’s expression sharpened.

Rez did not.

“And?”

“You presume to lecture officials who held office before you were born.”

Rez smiled.

“Then you had plenty of time to learn not to kidnap children.”

Zee laughed.

Lucien’s mouth moved once.

Aurelia looked toward the ceiling.

Seraphine, seated among the observers, whispered to Theron:

“I like him.”

Theron nodded.

“Good boy.”

Rez continued.

“Kest. Who hired your company?”

Ner looked toward the mercenary.

“Slowly.”

Kest breathed.

The Golden containment tightened around the oath.

He looked at Renn.

Then said:

“Councilor…”

The curse struck.

Ner caught it.

“…Renn.”

The chamber erupted.

Vesper nobles began speaking at once.

Renn’s attendants moved.

Lucien raised one hand.

Silence returned.

Rez’s eyes remained on Renn.

“Why?”

Kest laughed bitterly.

“Land.”

Renn shouted, “This is coerced testimony.”

Kest turned toward him.

“You promised payment.”

The oath attacked again.

Ner hissed through his teeth.

Zee saw his fingers shake.

His horns warmed.

Aurelia watched him.

Choice.

Zee stayed where he was.

Kest continued.

“Three villages kept fighting over winter pasture.”

“Renn wanted the northern road.”

“He paid us to take children from all three.”

“Leave signs pointing at the others.”

“Keep them angry.”

“Keep the road unstable.”

Rez’s face became disgusted.

“For what?”

“Council purchase.”

Lady Merrow whispered, “He planned to buy the route after the villages collapsed.”

Kest nodded.

Renn moved suddenly.

Not toward Kest.

Toward Ner.

His hand struck the ledger.

Mana flashed.

The hidden oath structure ignited.

Ner shouted.

Golden light buckled.

Kest collapsed.

Black lines raced up his throat.

Renn’s witch adviser screamed.

“I did not—”

Ner dropped to one knee.

Zee moved.

This time nobody stopped him.

He crossed the chamber before Renn’s guards understood he had left the pillar.

One hand closed around the councilor’s wrist.

The other caught his throat.

Renn hit the council table.

Hard.

Crimson Mana shook the wood.

The man’s eyes widened.

Zee smelled fear.

There.

Warm.

Sharp.

Satisfying.

Renn tried casting.

Zee squeezed his wrist.

Bone cracked.

Renn screamed.

And Zee smiled.

He saw it reflected in Renn’s eyes.

That was the part that frightened him.

Not the broken bone.

Not the screaming.

The pleasure.

This man had ordered children taken.

Had turned villages against each other.

Had built magic into hired killers so they could not expose him.

Had just triggered that magic while Ner was holding it apart.

Zee wanted to hurt him again.

Not to stop him.

Not because it was necessary.

Because he deserved it.

At least Zee thought he did.

Renn’s pulse raced beneath Zee’s hand.

Wolf instinct smelled weakness.

Vampire hunger sharpened around blood.

Crimson Dragon heat moved through the scales on Zee’s forearm.

His Witch Mana waited.

Four natures.

Three awake.

Every part of him capable of making Renn’s next moment unbearable.

Zee leaned closer.

“You hurt him.”

Renn struggled.

“He exposed restricted—”

Zee squeezed.

Another scream.

“Wrong answer.”

Aurelia’s voice crossed the chamber.

“Zee.”

He heard her.

Did not stop.

Ner’s voice came next.

“Zee.”

That did.

He looked back.

Ner remained on one knee beside Kest.

Pale.

Angry.

Alive.

Golden Mana still holding the oath apart.

Their eyes met.

Ner shook his head once.

Not frightened of Zee.

Not begging for Renn.

Something simpler.

Not this.

Rez stood beside Ner.

His sword remained sheathed.

“Zee.”

Zee looked back at Renn.

He could crush his throat.

Nothing in the room could stop him before it happened.

That knowledge felt dangerous.

Renn saw it.

For one second the polished official vanished.

Just a terrified man underneath Zee’s hand.

Zee liked that too.

He released him.

Renn collapsed against the table coughing.

The chamber stayed silent.

Zee stared at his own hand.

Then turned away.

“Fix Kest.”

Ner understood.

He returned to the oath.

Zee walked back toward his pillar.

Nobody blocked him.

Nobody thanked him.

Good.

He did not want thanks.

His hand was still tingling from breaking Renn’s wrist.

That bothered him.

Not enough.

That bothered him more.

⸻

Ner dismantled the oath completely twelve minutes later.

Not every mercenary binding in existence.

Only Kest’s.

One person.

One spell.

Careful.

When the final black line disappeared from Kest’s throat, the mercenary inhaled.

Then laughed.

A broken, exhausted sound.

“Gone?”

Ner sat back against the table.

“Yes.”

Kest touched his neck.

“Gone.”

Rez crouched beside him.

“Can you testify freely?”

Kest looked toward Renn.

“Yes.”

Renn sat under Vesper guard with his broken wrist bound.

His diplomatic composure had not returned.

Neither had his authority.

Rez stood.

“Councilor Maelor Renn.”

Renn looked toward him.

“You entered Vesper under diplomatic sanctuary.”

Renn smiled faintly.

“At least one person here remembers law.”

Rez’s expression did not change.

“You then activated lethal coercive magic against a protected witness inside my father’s royal chamber.”

The smile disappeared.

Renn’s legal scribe whispered something urgently.

Too late.

Rez continued.

“Under Vesper covenant law, sanctuary does not protect an envoy actively attempting murder within the king’s court.”

Renn looked toward Lucien.

“This is political theater.”

Lucien finally stood.

“No.”

His voice carried through the chamber.

“This is my kingdom.”

Vesper Mana rolled through the floor.

Dark blue.

Controlled.

Ancient.

“And you tried to kill a witness beneath my roof.”

Renn went pale.

Lucien looked toward the guards.

“Take him.”

Renn stood abruptly.

“If you imprison a Northern Council representative, the grain compact ends.”

Lady Merrow flinched.

Several nobles exchanged looks.

There.

The real consequence.

Not a battle.

Trade.

Winter.

Families.

Politics.

Zee looked toward Rez.

This was his world now.

Not something Zee could punch into simplicity.

Rez looked toward his father.

Lucien did not answer for him.

Interesting.

Rez understood.

His decision.

The Crown Prince turned toward Renn.

“How many villages use that grain compact?”

Renn smiled.

“Enough.”

“How many Northern merchants depend on Vesper salt roads?”

Renn’s smile weakened.

“How many depend on our river wards?”

Silence.

Rez continued.

“How many northern healers buy Vesper frostroot?”

Renn stared.

Rez walked closer.

“You built your plan on frightened villages being too divided to resist you.”

“You are making the same mistake here.”

Renn’s jaw tightened.

Rez looked toward the Vesper nobles.

“If the Northern Council suspends grain passage, Vesper suspends reciprocal river protection and opens emergency trade through the western covens.”

Lady Merrow frowned.

“That will cost us.”

“Yes.”

Rez did not pretend otherwise.

“For a season.”

“Possibly two.”

Hane asked, “And if the Northern Council retaliates further?”

Rez looked toward Renn.

“Then we negotiate with people who did not kidnap children.”

Lucien smiled.

Small.

Proud.

Decision made.

The guards took Renn.

No one stopped them.

The political problem did not vanish.

It became real.

That was different.

By sunset, three affected villages had copies of Kest’s testimony.

The Border Council received formal notice that Maelor Renn was imprisoned for attempted murder under Vesper law.

Two northern councilors immediately denied knowledge of his scheme.

One demanded his release.

Another requested the evidence privately.

The grain compact was suspended before nightfall.

Vesper’s western trade office began opening alternate routes an hour later.

Nothing ended cleanly.

Permanent progress rarely did.

But Renn no longer controlled the children he had weaponized.

His mercenary oath was broken.

His scheme was public.

His protection had limits.

And Rez Vesper had made his first decision of the conflict knowing exactly what it might cost his kingdom.

⸻

Zee found Ner on the western tower after dark.

No guards.

No council.

No Aurelia.

Just wind.

Ner sat on the parapet with one knee raised, Pure Golden Mana turning slowly between his fingers.

Not working.

Playing.

A ribbon of light passed between his knuckles and became a tiny wolf.

Then a bird.

Then something with horns.

Zee frowned.

“Is that me?”

Ner looked at the Golden creature.

“It is supposed to be a goat.”

“That is worse.”

Ner smiled.

The light vanished.

Zee sat beside him.

Not too close.

Then closer than necessary anyway.

For several moments neither spoke.

Vesper stretched beneath them.

Winter fires.

Watchtowers.

Moving guards.

A kingdom accepting another political enemy because its king and prince had refused to hand children back to the people who profited from them.

Zee looked toward Ner’s hands.

“Did it hurt?”

“The oath?”

“Yes.”

“Not exactly.”

Zee waited.

Ner sighed.

“It felt like holding a door shut while somebody stronger kept kicking it.”

Zee’s jaw tightened.

“I should have—”

“No.”

Zee looked over.

Ner met his eyes.

“You did exactly what you were supposed to do.”

“I stood there.”

“Yes.”

“I hated it.”

“I know.”

“I could have helped.”

“You could have made it worse.”

That irritated him because it was true.

Ner continued.

“And when I actually needed you, you moved.”

Zee looked down.

Renn’s wrist breaking beneath his hand returned to him.

The scream.

The fear.

The smile Zee had felt on his own face.

“I liked it.”

Ner went quiet.

Zee had not intended to say that aloud.

Now that he had, he could not take it back.

“When I hurt him.”

Ner watched him.

Zee continued.

“I knew he deserved to be stopped.”

“That is not the same thing.”

“I know.”

He rubbed one thumb over a Crimson scale.

“I liked hearing him scream.”

Ner did not recoil.

That almost made it worse.

“What do you want me to say?”

“I do not know.”

“That you are secretly nice?”

Zee looked offended.

“I am nice.”

Ner stared.

“Sometimes.”

“Rude.”

A small smile passed between them.

Then disappeared.

Ner looked over Vesper.

“Did you want to kill him?”

Zee thought.

“Yes.”

“Did you?”

“No.”

“Why?”

Zee remembered Renn’s throat beneath his hand.

Aurelia’s voice.

Then Ner’s.

“Because you told me to stop.”

Ner looked back.

“That is not enough.”

Zee frowned.

“I stopped.”

“This time.”

That landed.

Ner was not judging him.

That made Zee listen.

“If the only reason you stop is because one of us says your name, what happens when we are not there?”

Zee stared at him.

Wind passed between them.

Somewhere far east, the Dragon Link carried a faint impression from Zareth.

Stone chambers.

Heat.

Drakonis.

Then concern.

Zee had been thinking hard enough for the Link to feel the weight of it.

He let the sensation pass without reaching for Zareth.

This answer belonged to him.

“I do not know.”

Ner nodded.

“Then figure it out before you become stronger.”

Zee almost laughed.

“That sounds threatening.”

“It is.”

“You are threatening me?”

“I am your best friend.”

“That is apparently worse.”

Ner smiled.

Zee looked at him.

Again too long.

Ner noticed.

Again.

This time he did not look away.

Neither did Zee.

The air shifted.

Not magic.

Something much more inconvenient.

Zee became aware that their knees were almost touching.

That Ner’s face was closer than he expected.

That his eyes were warmer in firelight than Pure Golden Mana ever looked.

Zee’s horns heated.

Ner glanced upward.

Then smiled.

Zee immediately looked toward the city.

“Do not.”

“I said nothing.”

“You were going to.”

“I was.”

Zee pushed his shoulder.

Ner pushed back.

Their hands hit the stone between them at the same time.

Fingers brushed.

Neither moved.

For one heartbeat.

Two.

Then footsteps sounded below.

Both pulled away far too quickly.

Rez emerged from the tower stair.

He looked at Zee.

Then Ner.

Then the suspicious distance suddenly placed between them.

His face became unbearable.

Zee pointed.

“No.”

Rez raised both hands.

“I came to tell you dinner is ready.”

Ner stood.

“Good.”

He walked past Rez.

Rez waited until Ner disappeared down the stairs.

Then looked at Zee.

Zee stood.

“Say one word.”

Rez smiled.

“I am proud of your commitment to diplomacy.”

Zee shoved him into the wall.

Rez laughed the entire way downstairs.

⸻

Aurelia was awake when Zee returned to his room.

Of course she was.

She sat beside the window holding a book she had not turned a page of in several minutes.

Zee stopped.

“Were you waiting?”

“Yes.”

“I am fifteen.”

“I noticed.”

“That means you do not have to wait every time.”

“I know.”

She closed the book.

“That does not mean I won’t.”

Zee walked over.

For a moment he considered sitting across from her.

Instead he sat beside her.

Then leaned against her shoulder.

Fifteen did not make that unnecessary.

Aurelia wrapped one arm around him.

Neither spoke at first.

Then:

“I hurt Renn.”

“I heard.”

“I liked it.”

“I know.”

Zee looked up.

“How?”

“You smiled.”

His stomach tightened.

Aurelia had seen.

She did not look frightened.

That mattered too much.

“Does that make me bad?”

Aurelia considered the question instead of answering quickly.

“No.”

Zee frowned.

“That was too easy.”

“I was not finished.”

Of course.

She touched one of the scales along his wrist.

“What you enjoy tells you something about yourself.”

“It does not make every choice for you.”

Zee looked toward the window.

“What if I enjoy it more?”

“Then you had better become very good at knowing why you are hurting someone.”

That answer surprised him.

No demand that he become gentle.

No lie that violence was always wrong.

Aurelia knew their world better than that.

Zee asked, “And if they deserve it?”

“People use that sentence to justify terrible things.”

His face tightened.

“Victor thought I deserved what he did.”

Aurelia went still.

There it was.

The line Zee feared crossing.

Victor had once looked at an infant and convinced himself fear was judgment.

That danger lived in certainty.

Aurelia spoke quietly.

“Then remember him.”

Zee looked at her.

“Not to become him.”

“To remember how easy it is to believe fear makes you righteous.”

Zee thought about Renn.

He had evidence.

Children.

Oaths.

A clear crime.

That had not made breaking the man’s wrist necessary.

The first break stopped his casting.

The second had been because Zee wanted the scream.

He knew the difference.

That was what frightened him.

And perhaps what could protect him too.

“I broke it twice.”

Aurelia understood.

“The first time?”

“To stop him.”

“The second?”

Zee looked down.

“Because I wanted to.”

No excuse.

No monster.

No prophecy.

Just truth.

Aurelia kissed his temple beside one horn.

“Then remember that difference.”

Zee closed his eyes.

Outside, Vesper prepared for a trade dispute.

Rez was probably still arguing with Lucien over alternate grain routes.

Ner was probably pretending the tower moment had not happened.

Zareth was several kingdoms away inside Drakonis, carrying responsibilities that belonged to him alone.

The core four were not children anymore.

Not adults.

Not rulers.

Not legends.

But the world had begun bringing them problems that could not be solved by surviving until morning.

At fifteen, Zee Darkheart was learning that choosing a hunt was easier than choosing what happened when he caught what he hunted.

He could break bones.

He could spill blood.

He could frighten people who deserved fear.

And somewhere beneath the part of him that protected innocents, another part had begun enjoying what monsters sounded like when they realized the monster in front of them was stronger.

Zee did not hate that part.

Not yet.

That was the danger.

He leaned against Aurelia and listened to her heartbeat while he still could.

Then opened his eyes.

Because if Zee Darkheart was going to decide what kind of monster the world deserved—

he would have to make sure the world did not decide it for him first.
