Chapter Twenty-Three

The Hunt Beneath Vesper

The attack did not come from outside the walls.

That was what made it dangerous.

For three years, House Vesper had strengthened its gates, towers, roads, skies, and wards.

So the enemy went beneath them.

The first disappearance seemed meaningless.

A baker’s apprentice failed to return home.

Then a stable boy vanished.

Then a palace laundress.

None had connections to Zee.

None belonged to important families.

None carried unusual magic.

That was why Lucien took the disappearances seriously.

Predators who began with invisible people rarely intended to stop there.

The fourth missing person changed everything.

Nereus’s mother.

She disappeared between the lower palace kitchens and Aurelia’s apartments.

The tray she had been carrying was found in a service corridor.

Nothing else.

No blood.

No struggle.

No broken wards.

Ner waited for her until sunset.

Then until night.

By midnight, his Golden Mana had begun flickering across his skin every time footsteps approached.

Zee sat beside him on the floor of Aurelia’s chambers.

Neither spoke.

Rez paced.

Zareth lay across the doorway.

Aurelia stood beside Lucien near the window while Vale and Caldrin examined a map of the palace.

“She would not leave without Ner,” Aurelia said.

Vale nodded.

“Agreed.”

Lucien looked toward the royal witch.

“Any spell residue?”

“None.”

“Compulsion?”

“Unknown.”

“Teleportation?”

“No sign.”

Caldrin pointed toward the service corridor.

“Then someone walked her out.”

Vale looked at him.

“Past six guarded intersections?”

“Unless they did not use the hall.”

Lucien’s eyes narrowed.

“The old tunnels.”

Rez stopped pacing.

“What tunnels?”

Lucien looked toward him.

“Go back to Ner.”

“I am listening.”

“I noticed.”

Rez crossed his arms.

Lucien sighed.

“Vesper was built over older foundations.”

Caldrin smiled.

“Every castle is.”

“This is not the moment.”

“It is rarely the moment.”

Zee looked toward Ner.

His friend had not moved.

“Your mother is coming back.”

Ner’s face tightened.

“You do not know.”

Zee went still.

It was the first time Ner had ever sounded angry with him.

“You said—”

“I said you do not know.”

Golden Mana flashed.

The room brightened.

Ner looked at his own hands and immediately forced them down.

“I am sorry.”

Zee shook his head.

“No.”

He understood.

Ner was frightened.

Fear made people say things.

He had learned that from adults.

Zee moved closer.

“I will help find her.”

Ner looked at him.

“How?”

Zee thought.

Then looked toward the tray recovered from the corridor.

“Smell.”

Vale turned.

Aurelia’s face hardened.

“No.”

Zee stood.

“I can track.”

“No.”

“Wolves track.”

“You are not going into unknown tunnels after whoever took her.”

Zee looked toward Ner.

Then back at Aurelia.

“They took his mother.”

Aurelia flinched.

Zee continued.

“You would go if someone took me.”

Silence.

Caldrin looked away.

Vale suddenly found the map fascinating.

Lucien closed his eyes.

Aurelia crouched in front of Zee.

“Yes.”

“Then why cannot I help?”

“Because I am your mother.”

“That is not a reason.”

Aurelia stared.

Rez whispered to Ner.

“He really does say that now.”

Despite everything, Ner nearly smiled.

Aurelia spoke carefully.

“You can help.”

Zee brightened.

“From here.”

His face fell.

“You can identify her scent from the tray.”

“And tell Vale where it goes.”

“That is not going.”

“No.”

Zee looked toward Zareth.

The Dragon Link carried a clear image.

Tunnel.

Go.

Zee tried not to smile.

Aurelia noticed.

“Do not even think about it.”

Zee looked innocent.

That expression worried her more than open rebellion.

⸻

The scent led beneath the palace.

Ner’s mother had handled the silver tray for hours.

Her smell remained distinct beneath bread, soap, cooking oil, and the hundreds of palace scents clinging to the corridor.

Zee followed it to an unused storage chamber.

Then stopped beside the rear wall.

Vale watched him.

“There?”

Zee nodded.

Caldrin pressed against the stone.

Nothing.

Lucien summoned dark blue mana.

The wall revealed old inscriptions beneath the plaster.

“A sealed doorway.”

The royal witch frowned.

“This has not been opened in decades.”

Zee sniffed again.

“Today.”

Vale looked toward Lucien.

The king’s expression hardened.

“Open it.”

Magic struck the seal.

Stone shifted.

Cold air escaped.

A staircase descended beneath Vesper.

Lucien immediately turned.

“Zee stays.”

Zee opened his mouth.

“No.”

“But—”

“No.”

Zee looked toward Aurelia.

She pointed toward the corridor.

“Upstairs.”

He scowled.

Zareth growled.

“You too.”

Zareth looked offended.

Lucien almost smiled.

Then Ner spoke from behind them.

“I am going.”

Everyone turned.

His Golden Mana shimmered around his hands.

Aurelia approached.

“No.”

“That is my mother.”

“I know.”

“You said Zee could help because he can smell.”

“Yes.”

“I can feel her.”

Silence.

The royal witch stared.

“What?”

Ner looked toward the stairs.

“My magic.”

Pure gold gathered around his fingers.

A thin thread stretched toward the darkness.

Not physically.

More like light responding to something unseen.

“I feel her.”

Aurelia looked toward Ner.

Then Zee.

Then Lucien.

Lucien spoke first.

“He comes with us.”

Aurelia stared.

“You just told Zee no.”

“Because Zee does not possess whatever this is.”

Ner’s expression tightened.

“I am not staying.”

Aurelia hated the decision.

That did not make it wrong.

She looked toward Zee.

“You remain with Rez.”

Zee looked furious.

Rez stepped forward.

“I am coming too.”

Lucien pointed toward the ceiling.

“No.”

Rez blinked.

“You are taking Ner.”

“Ner can locate the prisoner.”

“I can protect Ner.”

“I can protect Ner.”

Rez’s Blue-White Mana flared.

“Can you protect him and fight whoever took everyone?”

Lucien went silent.

Rez had learned too much from watching adults use logic against one another.

Caldrin rubbed his forehead.

“The children are unionizing.”

Vale almost laughed.

Aurelia did not.

In the end, the group descended with Ner and Rez.

Zee stayed behind.

For seven minutes.

Then Zareth opened an old ventilation passage.

Zee smiled.

“You knew?”

The dragon sent an image.

Found before.

Of course he had.

Zee climbed inside.

Zareth followed.

⸻

Below Vesper, the architecture changed.

The stone became darker.

Older.

No Vesper sigils.

No palace wards.

The tunnel predated the kingdom above it.

Ner led through Golden Mana.

The light responded more strongly the deeper they traveled.

His face grew pale.

Rez remained beside him.

“You can stop.”

“No.”

“You look sick.”

“I am fine.”

“You are lying.”

“So?”

Rez looked surprised.

Ner continued walking.

Vale saw something ahead.

“Bodies.”

Everyone stopped.

Not dead.

Four missing citizens lay unconscious against the wall.

The baker’s apprentice.

Stable boy.

Laundress.

Another man.

Lucien crouched beside them.

“Alive.”

The royal witch examined one.

“Drained.”

“Blood?”

“No.”

“Mana?”

She hesitated.

“Something like it.”

Ner’s Golden Mana brightened.

“Mother.”

He ran.

Aurelia grabbed for him and missed.

“Ner!”

Rez followed.

The tunnel opened into an underground chamber.

Ner’s mother hung suspended inside a circle of black stones.

Alive.

Her eyes opened.

“Nereus?”

Ner stopped.

“Mother!”

A figure stepped between them.

Not a witch.

Not a vampire.

Not human.

Tall.

Thin.

Skin the color of old ash.

Two curved horns emerged behind its temples.

Caldrin drew his blades.

“Demon.”

The creature smiled.

“Eventually someone recognizes quality.”

Lucien’s mana gathered.

“Release her.”

The demon looked toward Ner.

Then his Golden Mana.

“No.”

Its smile widened.

“I waited for that.”

Aurelia’s eyes narrowed.

“You took random people to lure him.”

“Not random.”

The demon gestured toward the unconscious captives.

“Different magical signatures.”

“I was searching.”

“For what?”

The creature looked directly at Ner.

“Gold.”

Ner froze.

The demon continued.

“Pure.”

“Unmixed.”

“Bright enough to reach places ordinary witchcraft cannot.”

Aurelia stepped forward.

“How did you know it existed?”

“I did not.”

The answer surprised everyone.

“I heard rumors.”

“Golden hands beside the Tribrid.”

“Most rumors are worthless.”

“This one was not.”

Good.

Another independent enemy.

Another motive.

The demon did not care about Zee.

Not directly.

It wanted Ner.

That made the danger worse.

“Let my mother go,” Ner said.

The demon looked at him.

“Come closer.”

Rez immediately stepped in front of Ner.

“No.”

The demon smiled.

“Prince Vesper.”

Rez raised both hands.

Blue-White Mana gathered.

“Move.”

The demon laughed.

“Children with titles.”

“Always so brave when adults stand nearby.”

Rez cast.

Blue-White force struck the demon.

The creature disappeared.

Not moved.

Disappeared.

Then reappeared behind Ner.

Aurelia turned.

Too slow.

The demon grabbed him.

Golden Mana erupted.

The creature screamed.

Ner fell.

Rez attacked again.

The royal witch cast violet bindings.

Lucien sealed the exits.

Caldrin and Vale moved from opposite sides.

The demon became smoke.

Every attack passed through.

Then it reformed above them.

“Gold indeed.”

Ner’s mother screamed.

“RUN!”

The black stones around her ignited.

Ner looked toward her.

His fear became rage.

Golden Mana flooded the chamber.

Pure light filled every crack in the ancient stone.

The demon stopped smiling.

Ner raised both hands.

“I said let her go.”

The gold struck the prison circle.

The black stones cracked.

His mother fell.

Aurelia caught her.

The demon stared.

Not at Zee.

Not at prophecy.

At Ner.

Hungry.

“You could open things.”

Ner’s face hardened.

“I opened that.”

He pointed toward the broken circle.

Rez grinned.

“That was actually good.”

The demon lunged.

Something red struck from the ceiling.

Zareth crashed into it.

The creature slammed against the wall.

Everyone stared.

Aurelia’s expression became murderous.

“No.”

Zee dropped from the ventilation shaft.

He landed badly.

Rolled.

Stood.

And smiled.

“Found you.”

Aurelia looked ready to personally invent mortality.

“ZEE DARKHEART.”

He stopped smiling.

The demon looked toward him.

Recognition entered its face.

“So.”

Zee’s expression changed.

“You took Ner’s mother.”

“Yes.”

Wrong answer.

Crimson Mana thickened.

The demon’s eyes widened slightly.

Not because it knew Zee’s limits.

Because instincts worked both ways.

Predator recognized predator.

The demon became smoke.

Zee heard no heartbeat.

Smelled no blood.

Could not track it like a vampire or wolf.

New danger.

Different rules.

The smoke moved toward Ner.

Zee reacted with witch instinct.

His hand lifted.

Crimson Mana spread through the room.

Not binding the demon.

Binding the space.

The smoke slowed.

Lucien saw it.

“Now!”

Blue-White Mana from Rez struck first.

Golden Mana from Ner followed.

Lucien’s dark blue spell closed from behind.

The royal witch sealed the floor.

The demon was forced back into physical form.

Vale struck its leg.

Caldrin cut across its shoulder.

Black blood hit stone.

Zee smelled it.

His vampire instincts surged.

The demon saw that hunger.

Its smile returned despite the wound.

“There you are.”

Zee stared.

The demon rushed him.

Aurelia shouted.

Zee did not move.

The creature struck.

Claws tore across Zee’s chest.

Pain exploded.

His body hit the floor.

But death did not come.

Could not.

The wound began healing before the demon could pull its hand away.

Its expression changed.

Not understanding.

Shock.

Zee grabbed the demon’s wrist.

Crimson Mana entered the wound between them.

The demon screamed.

Its black blood moved.

Zee’s fangs extended.

Aurelia realized what was happening.

“Zee!”

Too late.

He bit.

Demon blood entered him.

The effect was violent.

His body arched.

Crimson Mana darkened around the edges.

Heat flooded his veins.

The demon ripped free and staggered backward.

Zee released it.

He did not copy the creature.

Something inside him rejected most of what entered.

The corruption.

The smoke.

The foreign hunger.

But one property remained.

The ability to sense presence where blood and heartbeat failed.

Not smell.

Not hearing.

Something beneath both.

The demon turned to smoke again.

Zee looked directly at it.

For the first time.

He could feel where it was.

The smoke stopped.

Zee pointed.

“There.”

Rez attacked.

Ner attacked.

Lucien sealed.

The demon was forced back into flesh.

Vale’s sword met it.

Caldrin’s followed.

The creature collapsed.

Still alive.

Barely.

Lucien stood over it.

“Who sent you?”

The demon laughed weakly.

“No one.”

Aurelia looked toward it.

“Why come?”

“Gold.”

Its eyes found Ner.

“Power like his opens old things.”

“What things?”

The demon smiled.

“Ask him when he is older.”

Lucien’s expression hardened.

The demon closed its eyes.

Not dead.

Unconscious.

Vale bound it.

Aurelia immediately crossed the room.

Zee knew that walk.

He backed away.

“Mother—”

“Do not.”

“I helped.”

“You disobeyed.”

“I helped first.”

“Zee.”

Zareth retreated.

Coward.

Zee glared at him.

The Dragon Link carried amusement.

Aurelia grabbed Zee and checked the wound across his chest.

Already closing.

Her anger broke slightly.

Pain remained.

He had still been hurt.

She held him tightly.

Then pulled back.

“You never do that again.”

Zee frowned.

“What?”

“Follow us into an unknown underground chamber.”

“What if someone takes Ner again?”

Ner looked toward him.

Aurelia closed her eyes.

There it was.

The problem no rule could solve.

Zee was becoming protective.

Not because anyone taught him.

Because the world had.

Every time it threatened someone he loved, it carved that instinct deeper.

Aurelia touched his cheek.

“We find another way.”

Zee looked toward Ner’s mother.

Alive.

Ner held onto her.

Rez stood beside them.

Zareth pressed against Zee.

Another way.

Maybe.

But Zee had already learned his own lesson.

When the world took someone he loved…

He followed.

⸻

By sunrise, the missing citizens were safe.

The demon remained imprisoned beneath layers of Vesper wards.

Ner’s mother survived.

The underground tunnels were sealed.

The palace celebrated quietly.

No feast.

Too many questions remained.

But Ner sat beside his mother’s bed holding her hand.

Zee sat on the floor nearby.

Rez leaned against the wall.

Zareth blocked the doorway.

Ner looked toward Zee.

“You came.”

Zee shrugged.

“I said I would.”

“You were told not to.”

“Yes.”

Rez nodded approvingly.

Ner smiled.

Then his Golden Mana appeared faintly around their joined hands.

Zee watched.

“Does it hurt?”

“No.”

“Do you know what it does?”

“No.”

Zee nodded.

“Me neither.”

Rez looked between them.

“That may be the wisest thing either of you has said.”

Zee threw a pillow.

Rez caught it.

Ner laughed.

His mother opened tired eyes.

“Quiet.”

All three boys froze.

Zareth did not.

He climbed onto the end of the bed.

She stared at the dragon.

“Not you too.”

Zareth curled up.

No one had the strength to move him.

Outside, Vesper woke.

And beneath the kingdom, something important had changed.

The demon had not come because of Zee.

It had come because of Ner.

For the first time, Zee confronted an enemy whose story did not begin with the First Tribrid.

The world was larger than his hunt.

His friends would have enemies of their own.

Their powers would create dangers of their own.

Their lives would not exist merely as extensions of his.

Zee did not understand that yet.

But he would.

And when he did, he would face one of the hardest truths of his childhood:

Loving people did not only give his enemies weapons.

Sometimes the people Zee loved carried wars that belonged entirely to them.
