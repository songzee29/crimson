Chapter One Hundred Two

The Child They Chose

They spoke three nights later.

No council.

No ministers.

No crowns.

Just Zee and Nymeris inside a quiet room overlooking Darkheart.

Ner had gone to sleep hours earlier.

Zareth was in Drakonis.

Rez had returned to Vesper.

For once, there was nobody left to interrupt them.

Nymeris stood near the window.

Zee sat across from her.

Neither wasted time.

“I want a child,” Nymeris said.

Zee nodded.

“I know.”

“And not because Nhalaris needs an heir.”

“Good.”

She looked toward him.

“Do you?”

Zee did not pretend he misunderstood.

“Yes.”

The word felt stranger aloud.

Not frightening.

Real.

Nymeris sat across from him.

“I do not want to create someone already indebted to a throne.”

“Neither do I.”

“I do not want ministers choosing what my child means.”

“No.”

“I do not want Nhalaris deciding why he exists.”

“No.”

Zee leaned forward.

“And I do not want Darkheart doing it either.”

Nymeris held his gaze.

“A child first.”

“Heir second.”

“If he wants the throne.”

“If either throne is still relevant by then.”

Nymeris smiled faintly.

“That sounds like something only an immortal King would say.”

“Probably.”

Silence followed.

Not uncertainty.

Weight.

They both understood exactly what they were discussing.

Not politics.

Not obligation.

A person.

A life that did not exist yet.

Nymeris spoke first.

“If we do this, I want him because we chose him.”

“Yes.”

“Not because our bloodlines would be impressive together.”

“Yes.”

“Not because Darkheart and Nhalaris would gain something.”

“Yes.”

“Not because the world expects a continuation.”

Zee looked at her.

“I spent too much of my childhood being told what my existence meant.”

His voice hardened slightly.

“I will never do that to mine.”

Nymeris nodded.

“Then we agree.”

Zee stared at her for a long moment.

“Are we actually doing this?”

“Yes.”

He laughed once.

Not from humor.

Something close to disbelief.

Nymeris smiled.

“You look terrified.”

“I have fought Gods.”

“And?”

“This is worse.”

“Good.”

Zee frowned.

“Why good?”

“Because you understand it matters.”

That ended the last uncertainty.

They chose.

Not King and Queen.

Not Darkheart and Nhalaris.

Zee.

Nymeris.

Two people who wanted a child.

⸻

Zee told Ner before sunrise.

Ner stared at him for exactly three seconds.

Then:

“You and Nymeris.”

“Yes.

“Are having a child.”

“Yes.”

Ner sat back.

Zee narrowed his eyes.

“Say whatever terrible thing you are thinking.”

Ner smiled.

“I was wondering whether the world is prepared for your blood combined with hers.”

Zee stared.

“That was my first thought too.”

“Of course it was.”

Ner became quieter.

“You want this?”

“Yes.”

“Really?”

Zee did not hesitate.

“Yes.”

Ner nodded.

Then smiled differently.

Warm.

“You are going to be unbearable.”

“I already am.”

“You are going to inspect the child every four minutes.”

“No.”

“Every three?”

Zee threw something at him.

Ner caught it.

Then stood and crossed the room.

No jealousy.

No awkwardness.

No competition.

He wrapped his arms around Zee.

“You are going to be a father.”

Zee went still.

That sentence landed harder than expected.

Then he held Ner back.

“Yes.”

Ner whispered against his shoulder:

“That child is going to be loved stupidly.”

Zee smiled.

“Violently.”

“That too.”

⸻

There was nothing romantic in what followed.

Nothing disguised.

Nothing confused.

Zee remained gay.

Nymeris remained his chosen family.

Ner remained the person Zee returned to at night.

The decision to create a child did not alter any of that.

When conception finally happened, it was private.

Intentional.

Briefly handled.

And completely chosen.

No accident.

No prophecy.

No drunken mistake.

No cosmic intervention.

Zee Darkheart and Queen Nymeris Thalryn deliberately created a child together because they wanted him.

Everything after began there.

⸻

The first two months were ordinary.

Mostly.

Nymeris hated being watched.

Zee watched constantly.

“You are staring.”

“I am listening.”

“To what?”

“The heartbeat.”

Nymeris stopped.

Then looked at him.

“You can hear it already?”

“Yes.”

That softened something in her face.

For approximately three seconds.

Then:

“Stop hovering.”

“I am not hovering.”

“You have visited Nhalaris seven times this month.”

“That proves nothing.”

“You live on land.”

“Technicality.”

Nymeris sighed.

Ner, seated nearby, smiled into his drink.

⸻

By the fourth month, the water started moving around Nymeris without permission.

Not violently.

Responding.

A cup trembled when the child shifted.

A shallow basin formed ripples whenever Nymeris rested.

Ocean currents beneath Nhalaris began subtly bending toward the palace.

Nymeris noticed first.

She said nothing for two days.

Then summoned Zee.

He arrived with Ner and Zareth.

Nymeris stood beside an indoor pool.

The water was completely still.

“Watch.”

She placed one hand over her stomach.

The child moved.

The pool rippled.

Zee’s expression sharpened.

Again.

The water moved before Nymeris did anything.

Ner stepped closer.

His Bloodsong hummed.

Then stopped.

He frowned.

“What?”

Zee asked.

Ner listened harder.

“There are two resonances.”

Nymeris looked toward him.

“Two heartbeats?”

“No.”

Ner shook his head.

“One heartbeat.”

Then quieter:

“More than one answer inside it.”

Zee’s face changed.

Zareth moved closer.

Dragon senses focusing.

He stared at Nymeris’s stomach.

Then at Zee.

That is not normal.

Zee answered through the Link.

I know.

Nymeris looked between them.

“You are doing the private conversation.”

“Yes.”

“What did he say?”

Zareth answered aloud.

“Your child feels wrong.”

Nymeris’s eyes narrowed.

Zareth corrected.

“Powerful.”

Zee looked at him.

“Better.”

⸻

By the sixth month, Zee could distinguish patterns.

Not awakened powers.

Foundations.

Something Witch-like responded whenever spellwork happened nearby.

Something predatory answered blood.

Something lupine reacted to the moon and movement.

None fully surfaced.

None behaved like Zee had at birth.

That mattered.

Zee had been born awake.

Everything alive at once.

This child was different.

Compressed.

Deep.

Waiting.

Then there was Nymeris.

Ocean.

Abyss.

Siren resonance.

Not part of the other three.

Separate.

Hereditary.

The unborn child carried that too.

Zee did not name what he was feeling yet.

He did not want to.

Ner did.

One night in Darkheart, he pressed Gold lightly against Nymeris’s wrist.

His expression changed.

“There is something in him that answers me.”

Nymeris looked down.

“Siren?”

“Partly.”

“Bloodsong?”

“No.”

Immediate.

Clear.

Ner shook his head.

“Nothing like mine.”

Good.

Different.

His expression remained uneasy.

“But deep.”

Zee looked toward him.

“How deep?”

Ner met his eyes.

“Enough that I do not think birth is going to be quiet.”

That sentence remained.

⸻

By the seventh month, Zareth stopped joking.

Mostly.

He stood beside Zee on the Nhalaris balcony while deep water rolled against the pressure dome.

“You are terrified.”

Zee looked at him.

“No.”

Through the Link came:

Lie.

Zee sighed.

“I am concerned.”

Zareth smiled.

“That is what Kings call fear.”

Zee looked toward Nymeris in the room behind them.

“She is strong.”

“Yes.”

“The child is stable.”

“Yes.”

“I have checked.”

“Constantly.”

Zee ignored that.

Zareth became serious.

“What are you afraid of?”

Zee listened to the heartbeat.

Steady.

Too steady.

Not Human.

Not ordinary supernatural.

Layered.

“I do not know yet.”

That was worse.

⸻

They chose the name in the eighth month.

No ceremony.

No council.

Nymeris was lying in bed, irritated because Zee had convinced three healers to examine her in one day.

“This is excessive.”

“You said the same thing yesterday.”

“It was true yesterday too.”

Zee sat beside her.

“What about Aidan?”

Nymeris stopped.

Looked at him.

“Aidan.”

“Yes.”

She repeated it softly.

Then smiled.

“Aidan Darkheart.”

Zee looked toward her.

“Thalryn too.”

“By blood.”

“Yes.”

“Prince of Nhalaris.”

“If he wants it.”

“Prince of Darkheart.”

“If he wants that too.”

Nymeris laughed.

“Everything with you becomes conditional on choice.”

“Yes.”

She placed one hand over her stomach.

“Aidan.”

The child moved.

Zee heard the heartbeat accelerate.

Then settle.

He smiled.

There.

Their son had a name.

Not prophecy.

Not destiny.

Aidan.

Chosen because they chose it.

⸻

Labor began during a stormless night.

That was the first wrong thing.

The ocean moved anyway.

Every current around Nhalaris reversed.

Not violently.

All at once.

Nymeris doubled over.

The palace pressure wards activated without command.

Deep bells rang through the city.

Zee stood so quickly his chair shattered behind him.

“What happened?”

Nymeris grabbed the edge of the table.

“He is coming.”

Ner entered seconds later.

His face pale.

“I heard it.”

Zee looked toward him.

“What?”

Ner’s Bloodsong trembled.

“The heartbeat.”

Zee already heard it.

But now—

another rhythm beneath it.

Then another.

Not separate hearts.

Separate structures.

Witch pressure surged through the room.

Every spell ward in Nhalaris lit at once.

Far above the ocean, Darkheart Witches turned toward the same direction without knowing why.

Vampires across distant roads stopped feeding.

For one second, all of them heard an impossible heartbeat beneath their own.

Wolves across three kingdoms went silent.

No howling.

No frenzy.

Silence.

The entire ocean around Nhalaris drew inward.

Nymeris screamed.

Abyssal resonance rolled through the palace.

Zee stood beside her.

And remembered blood raining from the sky.

Witches losing power.

Wolves losing themselves.

Vampires hearing something impossible.

His own birth.

His own beginning.

His own world deciding he was wrong before he could speak.

Zee went cold.

“No.”

Nymeris looked at him through pain.

“What?”

The heartbeat struck again.

Crimson pressure rolled through the room.

Not awakened.

Compressed.

Immense.

Worse because it was waiting.

Zee stared at Nymeris.

At their unborn son.

At the entire supernatural world already beginning to respond.

And understood.

“This is happening again.”

The ocean shook.

Aidan Darkheart was coming.
