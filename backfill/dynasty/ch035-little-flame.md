Chapter Thirty-Five

Little Flame

They left the River Market before sunset.

No banners.

No ceremony.

No return to Vesper.

Lucien received a short message through a Vesper courier:

Khell located east. Following. Rez safe. Everyone difficult.

Caldrin wrote the final sentence.

Aurelia did not remove it.

They rode Blackglass Road through the night.

Zareth remained overhead.

Rez rode beside Zee.

Ner on the opposite side.

Aurelia led with Vale.

The former Khell broker traveled with them under guard.

Her name was Mara.

Zee did not trust her.

Neither did Aurelia.

That made things easier.

No one pretended otherwise.

At dawn they stopped near a ruined watchtower.

Aurelia finally allowed the horses to rest.

Zee did not.

He waited until she walked away from the others.

Then followed.

“Who called you little flame?”

Aurelia stopped.

“You have been waiting all night.”

“Yes.”

“I noticed.”

“Answer.”

She turned.

He was ten.

Dirty from travel.

Hood thrown back.

Crimson-black horns visible.

Scales along his neck faintly glowing from exhaustion.

Still a child.

Still hers.

Aurelia sat on a fallen stone.

Zee joined her.

Zareth landed nearby but remained at a respectful distance.

The Dragon Link stayed quiet.

Aurelia looked east.

“My older brother.”

Zee stared.

“You have a brother?”

“Had.”

“Had?”

“I believed he died.”

“When House Veyrath was hunted?”

“Yes.”

“What was his name?”

Aurelia looked at Zee.

“Theron Veyrath.”

Zee froze.

The name.

Not the same Theron from the old sanctuary.

Different man.

Different history.

The world was large enough to repeat names.

He let that pass.

“What was he?”

“Dragon-Witch.”

“Like you?”

“Yes.”

“Older?”

“Much.”

“How much?”

“Enough to be irritating.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“So like Rez.”

Aurelia almost laughed.

“Very much like Rez.”

From behind them came Rez’s voice.

“I heard that.”

Zee looked back.

“You were supposed to be sleeping.”

“So were you.”

Rez approached with Ner.

Ner carried food.

Good.

That prevented Aurelia from sending them away immediately.

Rez sat beside Zee.

His ten-year-old body made the old brother comparison stranger.

Aurelia looked toward him.

“You really did hear?”

“Vampire hearing is Zee’s problem. I just followed.”

Ner handed Aurelia bread.

“Continue.”

She stared at him.

“You are all terrible.”

Ner nodded.

“We know.”

Aurelia looked toward the east.

“My brother protected me when House Veyrath began breaking apart.”

“From the hunters?”

“Yes.”

“He taught me how to hide Dragon scent.”

“How to suppress transformations.”

“How to live as only a witch when necessary.”

Zee looked toward her.

“He helped you become Aurelia Darkheart.”

Aurelia’s expression shifted.

“He helped me survive long enough to become her.”

That was different.

“Last time you saw him?”

Aurelia was quiet.

“During the final flight from our home territory.”

“What happened?”

“We separated.”

Zee frowned.

“Why?”

“To draw hunters away.”

“Who went which way?”

“He went east.”

Aurelia looked at the road beneath them.

“I went west.”

Silence.

Rez understood first.

“Khell is east.”

Aurelia nodded.

Ner looked toward Mara sleeping under guard near the horses.

“And someone used the name little flame.”

“Yes.”

Zee’s eyes sharpened.

“So he might be alive.”

“Might.”

“Mother.”

“Might.”

She refused certainty.

Even now.

Especially now.

Zee leaned against her shoulder.

Aurelia rested her head briefly against his horns.

“Ow.”

“Sorry.”

“That one is sharp.”

“I know.”

Rez looked toward Ner.

“Family moment.”

Ner nodded.

“Should we leave?”

Neither moved.

Zee looked at them.

“No.”

Rez smiled.

“Good.”

For several minutes they simply sat.

Then Zareth lifted his head.

The Dragon Link changed.

Riders.

Zee stood.

“How many?”

An image.

Six.

Fast.

Behind.

Vale was already moving.

Caldrin woke instantly.

Mara opened her eyes.

Aurelia looked at her.

“Friends?”

Mara listened to the approaching horses.

“No.”

“How do you know?”

“Khell’s people do not ride that formation.”

Vale drew his sword.

“Then whose?”

Mara’s face tightened.

“Ashen Talons.”

Aurelia cursed.

They had been followed from the market.

Different enemy.

Same road.

Zee’s hands closed.

Rez saw.

“Control.”

“I know.”

“No, actually do it.”

Zee glared.

The six riders appeared over the rise.

Gray armor.

Ashen Talon.

The lead hunter raised one hand.

Not attacking yet.

Aurelia stepped forward.

“Keep riding.”

The hunter stopped twenty yards away.

“We want the broker.”

Mara went pale.

Zee looked at her.

“Why?”

The hunter answered.

“She sold Veyrath routes.”

Aurelia’s eyes narrowed.

“She also stopped.”

“That does not erase what happened.”

Mara looked toward the ground.

No denial.

Interesting.

The Talon continued.

“She has names.”

“Locations.”

“Buyers.”

“She belongs to us.”

Caldrin smiled.

“No one belongs to you.”

The hunter’s expression hardened.

“Then this becomes difficult.”

Rez moved beside Zee.

Ner beside him.

Zareth stepped forward.

The hunter looked at the Dragon Prince.

Recognition.

Then at Zee.

The horns.

Scales.

Veyrath blood.

His jaw tightened.

“Move the children.”

Zee’s expression changed.

“I hate that sentence.”

Rez looked toward him.

“It is usually followed by something stupid.”

The hunter raised a crossbow.

Rez sighed.

“There.”

The bolt fired.

Not at Zee.

At Mara.

Vale deflected it.

The fight began.

Two Talons rode straight for Aurelia.

One for Mara.

Three spread wide.

Professional.

Different from ordinary bounty hunters.

They had learned how Aurelia fought.

Black chains came first.

Veyrath suppression.

Aurelia cut one apart with witchcraft before it wrapped her.

Another struck her wrist.

Dark runes activated.

Her Dragon energy dimmed.

Zee felt his own scales react.

Not because the chain touched him.

Because Veyrath blood recognized something built against it.

His horns heated.

Rez grabbed his arm.

“Use it.”

Zee looked at him.

“What?”

“Do not fight the feeling.”

“Last time that went badly.”

“Not the power.”

Rez looked him directly in the eyes.

“The warning.”

Zee understood.

The chain suppressing Aurelia carried a rhythm.

His Dragon nature could feel it.

Not enough to break it automatically.

Enough to identify where the suppression was strongest.

He pointed.

“Third rune!”

Aurelia trusted him.

She struck the third rune with concentrated witchcraft.

The chain shattered.

Rez smiled.

“There.”

Zee smiled back.

Control did not always mean forcing power down.

Sometimes it meant listening correctly.

Ner raised Golden Mana.

A Talon launched a black net.

Ner did not block it.

He opened a narrow gap in his barrier, let the net pass through, then sealed the space around the attacker’s horse instead.

The horse stopped without injury.

The rider did not.

He flew forward into the dirt.

Rez laughed.

“That was mean.”

Ner smiled.

“I learned from you.”

Zareth attacked from above.

Teal-and-Gold Dragonfire struck the ground between two riders and split their formation.

No one rode him.

No one directed him like a mount.

He chose targets himself.

Crown Prince.

Dragon.

His own fighter.

One Talon climbed a boulder and threw a hooked spear toward his wing.

Zee saw.

“Zareth!”

The Link snapped.

Seen.

Zareth twisted midair.

The spear missed.

He caught it in his jaws.

Then threw it back.

The hunter dove aside.

Zee laughed.

The Dragon Link carried intense pride.

The lead Talon reached Mara.

She pulled a knife.

Too slow.

He knocked it away and grabbed her throat.

“You should have stayed useful.”

Mara’s face twisted.

“I got tired of useful meaning evil.”

The Talon tightened his grip.

Zee moved.

Rez caught him.

“Wait.”

“He is hurting her.”

“I know.”

“Rez.”

“Look.”

Mara’s hand had disappeared into the hunter’s coat.

She pulled something free.

A small glass vial.

Then smashed it against his armor.

Red smoke erupted.

The Talon screamed and released her.

Caldrin appeared behind him.

Sword at his throat.

“Useful.”

Mara coughed.

“Shut up.”

The remaining Talons saw the fight turning.

Two fled.

Vale tried following.

Aurelia stopped him.

“No.”

“They will report.”

“Yes.”

“So?”

“We have Khell’s trail.”

She looked east.

“We do not lose it chasing people who already know we exist.”

Good.

Different priority.

Progress.

The captured lead Talon sat bound beneath the watchtower ten minutes later.

Mara cleaned blood from her mouth.

Aurelia stood over him.

“Who hired you?”

“No one.”

Aurelia looked toward Caldrin.

He sighed.

“Apparently that is their favorite sentence.”

The hunter continued.

“We track Veyrath independently.”

“Then why Mara?”

“She knows where blood moved.”

Aurelia’s face hardened.

“So do you.”

“Some.”

“Damaris Khell.”

The hunter’s expression changed.

Tiny.

Enough.

“You know him.”

Silence.

Aurelia crouched.

“Where is he going?”

The hunter looked toward Mara.

She answered.

“Blackglass Crossing.”

His eyes snapped toward her.

There.

Confirmation.

Mara smiled.

“Thank you.”

Zee looked impressed.

“You did that on purpose.”

“Yes.”

Caldrin nodded approvingly.

Aurelia looked toward the hunter.

“What is at Blackglass Crossing?”

He said nothing.

Mara answered.

“A transfer point.”

“For blood?”

“For anything.”

“People?”

“Yes.”

Zee’s stomach tightened.

Aurelia stood.

“We move.”

The hunter laughed.

Everyone looked at him.

“You are late.”

Aurelia’s expression changed.

“How late?”

He smiled.

“Khell moved the Veyrath stock this morning.”

Zee’s horns heated.

Rez touched his shoulder.

Zee breathed.

One sound.

Horse.

Wind.

Rez’s heartbeat.

The heat steadied.

Aurelia did not react outwardly.

“Where?”

The hunter said nothing.

Mara stared at him.

Then suddenly looked toward the east.

“Not farther east.”

Caldrin frowned.

“Why?”

“Blackglass Crossing has only three profitable exits.”

“North goes through witch territory.”

“South reaches vampire roads.”

“East goes to dead country.”

Vale looked toward her.

“Dead?”

“Bad trade.”

“Then?”

Mara pointed southeast.

“The old quarry route.”

Aurelia’s face tightened.

“Where does it lead?”

Mara looked at her.

“To a private holding estate.”

“Whose?”

She swallowed.

“Lady Morcant.”

The witch buyer.

The name from the River Market.

Aurelia understood.

The three Veyrath captives sold years ago.

At least one had gone to Morcant.

Now Khell was moving more cargo toward the same route.

Possibility became pattern.

Zee looked toward his mother.

“We go there.”

Aurelia nodded.

No argument.

The group mounted within minutes.

Rez adjusted his smaller sword.

Ner pulled on his gloves.

Zareth spread his wings.

Mara rode under Vale’s watch.

The captured Talon remained bound for Vesper patrols to collect.

Before Zee mounted, Rez grabbed his sleeve.

“What?”

Rez looked toward his horns.

“Better.”

Zee frowned.

“What?”

“You controlled it.”

“Oh.”

“Do not sound disappointed.”

“I am not.”

Rez smiled.

“You listened before exploding.”

“That is rude.”

“It is accurate.”

Zee looked toward Aurelia.

She was already riding east.

Toward the possibility of a brother she had mourned for years.

Toward Veyrath captives who might still live.

Toward Lady Morcant.

Toward a place where bloodlines were apparently kept as property.

Zee’s expression hardened.

“Come on.”

Rez climbed onto his horse.

Ner followed.

Above them, Zareth rose into the sky.

The four moved together.

Same physical age now.

Different histories.

Different powers.

Different enemies.

But the road ahead finally belonged to something more than survival.

For the first time in Zee Darkheart’s life, they were not merely escaping the people who hunted them.

They were chasing the people who had taken someone else.

And by nightfall, Lady Morcant would learn the difference.
