Chapter Twenty-One

Three Years Later

Zee Darkheart learned to smell poison before he learned to read.

He learned the sound of a crossbow being drawn before he learned the names of every letter.

He could distinguish a vampire heartbeat from a mortal one through two stone walls.

He knew when a wolf was stalking him by the way birds stopped singing.

He knew never to accept sweets from strangers.

He knew windows were dangerous.

He knew locked rooms were worse.

He knew that whenever Captain Vale said stay behind me, something interesting was usually about to happen.

Zee was three years old.

And someone tried to kill him before breakfast.

The assassin came disguised as a kitchen servant.

That was mistake number one.

House Vesper changed household staff around Zee frequently enough that unfamiliar faces were expected.

But Zee remembered scents.

Every person who regularly entered his rooms belonged to a pattern.

Soap.

Herbs.

Steel.

Smoke.

Ink.

Bread.

This man smelled like bitter almonds beneath lavender oil.

Zee stopped walking.

Zareth nearly walked over him.

The dragon had grown enormously during the years since the crownless valley. He was no longer a hatchling small enough to hide beneath blankets. His crimson body now reached nearly Zee’s shoulder at the spine, and his wings could carry him across the Vesper capital without difficulty.

He remained in dragon form.

Always.

Human transformation still belonged to a future neither child had reached.

Their Dragon Link had matured with them.

Images came more clearly now.

Directions.

Faces.

Simple intentions.

Occasionally a wordlike feeling formed between them, though neither could explain where Zee’s thought ended and Zareth’s began.

Zareth smelled the servant.

His head lowered.

Wrong.

Zee nodded.

“I know.”

The servant smiled.

“My Prince.”

Zee hated when strangers called him that.

He looked at the tray.

Honey cakes.

Fruit.

Warm milk.

Aurelia had forbidden anyone from giving Zee honey cakes before his actual breakfast because he would refuse everything else afterward.

Mistake number two.

“Who sent you?”

The servant hesitated.

“The kitchens.”

Zee tilted his head.

“That is not a person.”

Zareth’s Dragon Link carried amusement.

The servant’s smile tightened.

“Queen Aurelia requested breakfast in the eastern garden.”

“My mother says I eat too much sugar.”

The servant stared.

Zee pointed at the cakes.

“She would not send those.”

Mistake number three.

The man dropped the tray.

A blade appeared from beneath it.

Zareth moved.

Fast.

He slammed the assassin into the corridor wall before the man took a full step.

The blade clattered away.

Zee did not flinch.

He looked at the spilled milk instead.

A drop touched the stone.

The surface hissed.

“Poison.”

Zareth growled.

Captain Vale rounded the corridor corner with three guards.

He took in the scene.

Assassin.

Dragon.

Poisoned breakfast.

Zee standing barefoot in his nightclothes.

Vale closed his eyes.

“It is not even sunrise.”

Zee looked up.

“He lied.”

“I gathered that.”

“He said Mother sent honey cakes.”

Vale stared at the unconscious assassin.

“An unforgivable tactical error.”

Zee nodded seriously.

Zareth snorted.

Vale crouched beside the man and searched him.

No death seal.

No bone mask.

No royal insignia.

A small silver coin rested inside his coat.

Vale held it up.

A wolf’s head had been stamped on one side.

Zee leaned closer.

“Wolf?”

“Possibly.”

“Wolf kingdom?”

“Possibly.”

“You do not know?”

Vale looked at him.

“Your mother has trained you too well.”

Zee smiled.

“Mother says people who pretend to know things get kingdoms killed.”

Vale stood.

“She says many cheerful things.”

Zee looked toward the poisoned cakes.

“Can I have real ones?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“You nearly got poisoned.”

“That was fake breakfast.”

Vale stared.

Zareth sent a clear image through the Link.

Cake.

Zee pointed.

“Zareth agrees.”

“I do not care.”

The dragon growled.

Vale walked away.

“I have fought armies less exhausting than you two.”

Zee grinned.

That was how mornings worked now.

⸻

Three years had not ended the hunt.

They had refined it.

Zee had spent those years moving between House Vesper, hidden estates, mountain camps, fortified monasteries, hunting lodges, and once an underground city whose inhabitants tried selling his location before Aurelia had finished unpacking.

No place lasted.

No method repeated for long.

One winter, a witch faction tried binding Zee’s Crimson Mana with twelve layered seals.

He had spent two days sick, furious, and unable to summon more than sparks.

On the third day, the seals began cracking whenever he became angry.

Aurelia had ordered every remaining copy destroyed.

Months later, vampire hunters tried following him through blood scent.

After three nights of pursuit, Zee learned to distort the scent they were tracking by circulating Crimson Mana through his blood.

The vampires lost him in a swamp.

Another faction paid shapeshifters to imitate Vesper soldiers.

Zee recognized the wrong heartbeats.

A demon once entered his dreams.

That attack frightened Aurelia more than any blade.

Zee woke screaming but alive, and afterward his sleeping mind became far harder for the same creature to enter.

No enemy killed him.

Death had already taken its single turn.

But Zee had scars.

Some vanished quickly.

Others remained.

Not always on skin.

He hated cages.

He disliked strangers standing behind him.

He could not sleep if Zareth was too far away.

And whenever Aurelia left a room without telling him where she was going, Zee’s right hand tightened until his small nails cut his palm.

The world had failed to end him.

It had still managed to teach fear.

⸻

House Vesper changed too.

Sanctions had weakened after several kingdoms discovered that refusing Vesper trade harmed their own markets more than Lucien’s.

The Twelve Crowns no longer behaved as twelve.

Old rivalries had returned.

Some still wanted Zee dead.

Some wanted nothing to do with him.

Others had quietly withdrawn from the hunt.

Three had increased their bounties.

Two now wanted Zee captured alive.

One denied ever participating despite possessing banners still stained with Darkheart blood.

Politics had remembered itself.

King Lucien considered that progress.

Aurelia considered it temporary.

Rez considered it boring.

He was ten now.

He had grown taller, faster, and far too confident with Blue-White Mana.

He found Zee in the lower training courtyard shortly after the poisoned breakfast.

“You started without me.”

Zee held a wooden practice blade.

“You were asleep.”

“I was studying.”

Zee stared.

Rez sighed.

“I was asleep.”

Zareth lay beneath the courtyard arch with his wings stretched across warm stone.

Rez looked toward him.

“Lazy.”

One golden eye opened.

“You heard me.”

Zareth closed it again.

Rez turned toward Zee.

“Again.”

Zee raised the practice blade.

Captain Vale watched from the wall.

“No mana.”

Rez groaned.

Zee smiled.

“That means you lose.”

“I am older.”

“That means you lose older.”

Vale coughed to hide a laugh.

Rez attacked.

Zee ducked.

Not elegantly.

Instinctively.

He heard Rez’s foot shift before the blade came.

The wooden sword passed over his head.

Zee struck Rez’s leg.

Rez stumbled.

Zee hit him again.

Rez spun.

Zee was already moving.

Vampire perception.

Wolf instinct.

Three years of surviving people faster, larger, and crueler than him.

Rez finally caught Zee’s blade and knocked it aside.

“Got you.”

Blue-white mana flickered accidentally around his hand.

Zee stared.

“That is cheating.”

“I did not cast.”

“You glowed.”

“That is different.”

Zee dropped the sword.

“I win.”

Rez looked offended.

“How?”

“You cheated first.”

“That means no one wins.”

“That sounds like losing.”

Vale looked toward the sky.

“I understand Caldrin now.”

A voice came from the courtyard entrance.

“That is the nicest thing anyone has ever said about me.”

Caldrin Marr walked through carrying two travel bags and a severed monster horn.

He had disappeared eleven days earlier.

Zee ran toward him.

Caldrin lifted one hand.

“Careful. Something tried eating me.”

Zee stopped.

“Did you win?”

Caldrin looked at the horn.

“No. It gave this to me willingly.”

Zee grinned.

“What was it?”

“Ugly.”

“Monster.”

“Very.”

“Where?”

“South.”

Zee’s expression brightened.

“Can we go?”

Aurelia’s voice came from behind him.

“No.”

Zee turned.

His mother entered the courtyard wearing a dark traveling gown with a sword belted beneath it.

Three years had sharpened Aurelia too.

She no longer looked like the exhausted queen who had fled Castle Darkheart holding a newborn.

The fear remained.

But it had learned discipline.

Zee immediately ran to her.

Aurelia crouched.

He collided with her.

She held him tightly.

For one moment, the First Natural Tribrid was simply a little boy with his face buried against his mother.

Then she pulled back.

“I heard someone brought you breakfast.”

Zee frowned.

“He was rude.”

“He attempted to poison you.”

“That too.”

Aurelia looked toward Vale.

“Alive?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Zee tilted his head.

“Why?”

“Because dead men answer poorly.”

Caldrin nodded approvingly.

Zee looked toward him.

“You answer poorly alive.”

Rez laughed.

Caldrin placed one hand over his heart.

“Betrayal.”

Aurelia almost smiled.

Then another voice entered the courtyard.

“My Queen?”

A woman stood beneath the arch carrying folded linens.

Beside her was a boy Zee had never seen.

He looked roughly Zee’s age.

Dark curls framed his face. He remained partly hidden behind his mother’s skirts, studying the dragon, the prince, the armed captain, and finally Zee.

Aurelia straightened.

“You may come.”

The woman approached and bowed.

“My son arrived from the southern Vesper estates this morning.”

Aurelia looked toward the child.

Zee did too.

The boy did not bow.

His mother gently touched his shoulder.

“Nereus.”

He frowned.

Then remembered.

He bowed badly.

Zee smiled.

The woman looked horrified.

“My apologies.”

Aurelia shook her head.

“He is a child.”

The woman relaxed.

She had served among Aurelia’s household since their return to Vesper, eventually becoming one of the few attendants Aurelia trusted with Zee’s daily care.

Her son had lived elsewhere while the hunts remained at their worst.

Now, with Vesper temporarily stable, she had brought him to court.

“Nereus Azrion,” she said.

The boy looked at Zee.

“Ner.”

His mother sighed.

“Nereus.”

“Ner.”

Zee tilted his head.

The boy looked at him directly.

“You can call me Ner.”

His mother opened her mouth.

Aurelia smiled faintly.

“Apparently the matter is settled.”

Zee stepped closer.

“Ner.”

Something in the boy’s expression changed.

A small smile.

Rez walked over.

“I am Rez.”

Ner looked at him.

“I know.”

Rez blinked.

“Oh.”

Zee laughed.

Then Zareth stood.

Ner’s smile disappeared.

The crimson dragon crossed the courtyard.

Slowly.

His head reached nearly to Ner’s chest now.

Teal-and-gold mana flickered beneath his scales.

Ner did not run.

That surprised everyone.

Zareth sniffed him.

Ner stared at the enormous teeth inches from his face.

“Does he bite?”

Rez answered immediately.

“Yes.”

Aurelia glared.

“Only when provoked.”

Zee looked at Rez.

“He bites you a lot.”

“That proves nothing.”

Ner slowly raised one hand.

Zareth’s eyes narrowed.

Then something happened.

Mana gathered around Ner’s fingers.

Pure gold.

Not yellow.

Not amber.

Not sunlight reflected through another color.

Golden Mana.

Clean.

Bright.

Unique.

Everyone in the courtyard stopped.

The royal witch entered from the corridor at exactly the wrong moment and froze.

Aurelia stared.

Rez’s Blue-White Mana responded unconsciously.

Zee felt the gold before he understood it.

Warm.

Different from Zareth’s teal-and-gold mixture.

Different from every witch he had ever encountered.

Ner looked at his own hand.

Confused.

His mother whispered his name.

“Nereus.”

The gold vanished.

Zareth leaned forward.

Ner held still.

The dragon touched his snout to the boy’s palm.

No growl.

No bite.

The Dragon Link carried a new impression to Zee.

Warm.

Strange.

Good.

Zee stepped closer.

Ner looked at him.

“You have red eyes.”

Zee frowned.

“You have gold hands.”

Ner looked down.

“Only sometimes.”

Rez crossed his arms.

“My mana is better.”

Ner looked toward him.

“Why?”

“It is Blue-White.”

“That does not explain why it is better.”

Zee laughed.

Rez stared at both younger boys.

“I already regret this.”

The warmth lasted less than a minute.

Then an arrow struck the courtyard wall.

Vale moved instantly.

“DOWN!”

Aurelia grabbed Zee.

Rez pulled Ner toward cover.

Zareth spread his wings across all three boys.

A second arrow struck the stone.

Then a third.

Not aimed at Zee.

At Ner.

The golden mana had been seen.

Aurelia realized it immediately.

“Inside!”

Guards flooded the upper walls.

Caldrin looked toward the neighboring rooftop.

“Two shooters!”

Zareth roared.

Zee felt his fury through the Link.

Rez’s Blue-White Mana surged.

Ner stared at the arrow embedded inches from where he had stood.

His golden mana appeared again.

This time violently.

A sphere of pure gold expanded around him.

Not trained.

Not controlled.

Instinctive.

The fourth arrow hit the sphere.

It dissolved.

Everyone stared.

Ner stared hardest.

“I did not do that.”

Zee looked toward him.

“I know.”

Another arrow came.

Zee heard it.

He stepped in front of Ner before Aurelia could stop him.

Crimson Mana caught the projectile.

Golden Mana burned around Ner.

Blue-White gathered around Rez.

Teal-and-gold Dragon Mana rolled across Zareth’s scales.

Four powers filled the courtyard.

Different.

Distinct.

Alive.

Caldrin reached the rooftop.

One shooter escaped.

The other did not.

The attack ended as suddenly as it began.

Silence followed.

Aurelia stared at the children.

Rez.

Zee.

Ner.

Zareth.

Something had shifted.

Not destiny.

Not prophecy.

Not some ancient plan.

Relationships.

The most dangerous thing the world kept accidentally giving Zee.

Ner’s mother rushed to him and pulled him into her arms.

“We are leaving.”

Ner looked toward Zee.

His face tightened.

Zee understood immediately.

Taken.

Gone.

Something old and frightened moved inside him.

“No.”

Aurelia looked toward her son.

Zee walked toward Ner’s mother.

“He can stay.”

The woman stared at him.

Zee was three.

A hunted prince.

Covered in the dust of an assassination attempt.

His crimson eyes remained fixed on Ner.

“He can stay with me.”

The words were simple.

Childish.

But Aurelia heard something beneath them.

Zee had begun choosing people.

Zareth had been given to him by mystery.

Rez had chosen him first.

Ner was different.

Zee was choosing back.

Ner looked toward his mother.

“I want to stay.”

Her face broke with fear.

Aurelia understood that fear better than anyone.

She approached slowly.

“I will not promise you he will be safe near Zee.”

The woman looked at her.

Aurelia continued.

“I will not lie to you.”

“The world comes for my son.”

“Sometimes it comes for the people beside him.”

Ner’s mother looked at the arrow.

Then at her child’s fading Golden Mana.

Aurelia’s expression softened.

“But after what we just witnessed…”

She looked toward the escaped shooter’s path.

“…the world may have found a reason to come for Nereus whether Zee stands beside him or not.”

Silence.

That truth decided more than comfort could.

Ner’s mother pulled him close.

Then closed her eyes.

“He stays.”

Zee smiled.

Ner smiled back.

Rez sighed dramatically.

“I suppose I have two younger idiots now.”

Zee threw the wooden practice sword at him.

Rez ducked.

Ner laughed.

Zareth roared.

And above House Vesper, warning bells began ringing again.

The hunt had come before breakfast.

Then again before noon.

For Zee Darkheart, that was simply childhood.

But on that day, something more important happened than another failed attempt.

The world gave Zee another person to lose.

And Zee, knowing nothing yet of what love would eventually cost him, chose him anyway.
