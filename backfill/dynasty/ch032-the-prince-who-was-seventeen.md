Chapter Thirty-Two

The Prince Who Was Seventeen

Rez discovered the first truly terrible consequence of becoming ten years old again at breakfast.

His chair was too tall.

He stood beside it in the private Vesper dining room, staring at the seat as if it had personally betrayed him.

Ner sat across the table eating fruit.

Zee sat beside him.

Zareth had claimed his usual place outside the open terrace doors because King Lucien had finally banned him from bringing entire animal carcasses into formal dining rooms.

No one spoke.

Rez looked at the chair.

Then at Lucien.

Then back at the chair.

“I used this yesterday.”

Lucien calmly poured tea.

“Yes.”

“My feet touched the floor.”

“Yes.”

“They do not now.”

“No.”

Ner lowered his cup very slowly.

Rez pointed at him.

“Do not.”

Ner immediately looked toward Zee.

Zee was already biting the inside of his cheek.

“Neither of you.”

Zee failed first.

A laugh escaped.

Ner followed.

Rez looked toward the ceiling.

“I hate this kingdom.”

Lucien finally smiled.

“You are its prince.”

“That makes it worse.”

Rez climbed into the chair with as much dignity as possible.

His newly tailored clothes fit properly now. Dark Vesper trousers. Blue tunic. A smaller coat embroidered with silver near the collar.

The clothing helped.

Everything else did not.

His arms were shorter.

His stride had changed.

His center of balance was wrong.

His appetite was strangely enormous.

His body became tired sooner than his seventeen-year-old instincts expected.

And every reflective surface in the palace continued showing him a face he remembered seeing seven years earlier.

His face.

Nothing false about it.

Nothing illusionary.

Just younger.

Zee had barely spoken since sitting down.

Rez noticed.

“You are doing it again.”

Zee looked over.

“Doing what?”

“Looking at me like you expect my head to fall off.”

“I am not.”

“You checked me four times since breakfast arrived.”

Aurelia entered.

“Five.”

Zee glared at his mother.

Rez pointed toward her.

“Thank you.”

Aurelia sat beside Lucien.

She had changed the bandage beneath her clothes, but Zee could still smell the fading wound from the Veyrath attack.

That scent bothered him.

Everything bothered him lately.

Rez being ten.

Aurelia being injured.

Sevran downstairs.

Ashen Talon prisoners beneath Vesper.

His Dragon nature pushing against something unfinished.

The Convergence Vault calling him four and only three awake.

And now another impossible power existed inside him.

One capable of touching another living person without asking permission.

Zee looked down at his breakfast.

“I should not have done it.”

Rez sighed.

“We discussed this.”

“I know.”

“No, you heard us discuss it. That is different.”

Zee looked up.

Rez leaned forward.

“You did not choose to make me ten.”

“I still did it.”

“Yes.”

The answer surprised him.

Rez continued.

“That matters.”

Lucien watched his son carefully.

Rez had spent the previous night being examined by three healers and two royal witches.

Every result agreed.

His body was ten.

His bones.

His organs.

His blood.

His living biological state.

Ten.

But nothing in Rez’s memories had changed.

Nothing in his magical training had disappeared.

And nothing in his history had moved.

Lucien remembered holding him as a newborn seventeen years earlier.

The palace records still contained his birth.

Servants remembered watching him grow.

His old clothing still existed.

His seventeen-year-old measurements remained written in the royal tailor’s ledger from two weeks earlier.

History refused to cooperate with his current appearance.

Good.

Aurelia had spent half the night testing that fact.

Not because she doubted Rez.

Because she needed Zee to understand the boundary.

“Nothing behind Rez changed,” Aurelia said.

Zee nodded.

“I know.”

“Say it.”

He frowned.

“Why?”

“Because you are frightened enough to imagine more than happened.”

Zee looked at Rez.

Then reluctantly:

“Rez was born seventeen years ago.”

Lucien nodded.

“He lived seventeen years.”

“Yes.”

“He was seven when I was born.”

Rez smiled faintly.

“And unbearably handsome.”

Ner snorted.

Zee continued.

“He grew up.”

“Everything still happened.”

“My magic did not go backward.”

Aurelia held his gaze.

“No.”

Zee looked at Rez’s younger hands.

“It only changed him now.”

“That is what the evidence shows.”

“Can I change him back?”

“No one knows.”

Rez immediately raised a hand.

“And we are not experimenting on me until someone knows significantly more.”

Ner chewed thoughtfully.

“That is surprisingly sensible.”

Rez looked toward him.

“I still have seventeen years of intelligence.”

Ner tilted his head.

“Where?”

Zee laughed.

Rez threw a piece of bread at him.

Ner caught it.

Zareth’s amusement entered the Dragon Link.

Small Rez angry.

Zee immediately looked down.

Rez narrowed his eyes.

“What did he say?”

“Nothing.”

“That means something.”

Zareth pushed his head through the terrace doorway.

Rez stared at him.

“You have been enjoying this far too much.”

The Dragon Prince showed teeth.

Ner nodded.

“He definitely understands you.”

Rez looked at Lucien.

“Can we send him home?”

Zareth growled.

Zee answered immediately.

“No.”

“Worth asking.”

The moment lightened.

Only briefly.

Lucien set down his tea.

“There is another problem.”

Rez looked at him.

“My chair?”

“Your legal position.”

Rez’s expression changed.

Zee and Ner stopped smiling.

Lucien continued.

“You are Crown Prince of Vesper.”

Rez nodded.

“Still.”

“Yes.”

“But the moment court learns what happened, questions begin.”

“About whether I am still heir.”

“Among other things.”

Rez leaned back.

His feet swung several inches above the floor.

He noticed.

Stopped them immediately.

“What other things?”

“Whether you have been replaced.”

“I have not.”

“Whether you are enchanted.”

“I am.”

Lucien gave him a look.

Rez corrected himself.

“Changed.”

“Whether you remain capable of royal duties.”

Rez’s face hardened.

“I do.”

“I know.”

“Then that is the answer.”

Lucien’s expression softened.

“To me.”

Rez understood.

Politics had never cared what was emotionally convenient.

A seventeen-year-old heir becoming physically ten overnight would create panic.

Rivals.

Marriage questions.

Succession challenges.

Foreign speculation.

Claims that Zee had attacked House Vesper.

Claims that Lucien had allowed Darkheart magic to corrupt his son.

Claims that Rez was no longer the same person.

The absurdity faded.

Ner looked toward Zee.

Zee had gone still.

“This is because of me.”

Lucien answered before anyone else.

“This situation began because your magic changed him.”

Zee flinched.

Lucien continued.

“That does not make every decision other people make afterward your responsibility.”

Zee looked at him.

“You cannot control what frightened nobles decide to believe.”

Aurelia glanced at Lucien.

The lesson sounded familiar.

Lucien noticed.

“So,” Rez said, “we hide it.”

“For now.”

Rez frowned.

“For how long?”

“Until I know which Vesper nobles will respond with loyalty and which will respond with ambition.”

Rez smiled without humor.

“So we are testing them.”

Lucien’s expression became equally dry.

“We are observing them.”

Ner looked toward Zee.

“That is king language for testing them.”

“I know.”

Rez folded his arms.

“What am I supposed to do? Hide in my room?”

Lucien stared.

Rez groaned.

“You are considering it.”

“No.”

“Good.”

“You are moving into the western family wing.”

Rez blinked.

Zee smiled.

Ner smiled wider.

“That is where we live.”

Rez looked horrified.

Lucien continued.

“Until the palace adjusts.”

“I am seventeen.”

Ner immediately said, “Historically.”

Rez threw another piece of bread.

Sevran had been waiting in the council chamber for nearly an hour when Aurelia finally arrived.

He looked toward Zee.

Then Rez.

Then Rez again.

“What happened to you?”

Rez pointed toward Zee.

“His magic has poor listening comprehension.”

Sevran stared.

Zee crossed his arms.

“We are not discussing it.”

Sevran looked toward Aurelia.

She shook her head.

“Good.”

For once, Sevran obeyed.

Three captured Ashen Talon hunters remained beneath Vesper.

Two had refused to speak.

The third had begun asking for terms.

That was why they were here.

Lucien entered moments later with Vale and Caldrin.

Ner remained too.

Not because the Ashen Talons concerned him directly.

Because he had threatened to listen from the ventilation shafts if excluded.

Lucien had decided allowing him inside was less exhausting.

The prisoner was brought in wearing iron restraints.

A middle-aged woman.

Gray armor removed.

Dark hair cut short.

A burn crossed one side of her throat where Zareth’s Dragonfire had nearly reached her.

She looked at Aurelia first.

Then Sevran.

Then Zee.

Her eyes stayed on Zee longer than he liked.

Aurelia noticed.

“Eyes on me.”

The woman smiled.

“Protective.”

Sevran muttered, “Do not.”

The prisoner looked toward him.

“Still alive, Sevran.”

“Disappointed?”

“Surprised.”

Aurelia leaned forward.

“Who are the Ashen Talons working for?”

The woman laughed.

“No one.”

Caldrin sighed.

“Organizations always say that.”

“We take contracts.”

“From whom?”

“Anyone who pays.”

Sevran shook his head.

“Lie.”

The woman looked at him.

“You know better.”

“I know the Talons existed before most of your current buyers.”

“History changes.”

“Purpose does not.”

The woman’s smile vanished.

Aurelia noticed.

“What purpose?”

Silence.

Sevran answered.

“Control.”

The prisoner looked at him sharply.

Sevran continued.

“The old Talons believed supernatural bloodlines became dangerous when they crossed boundaries.”

Ner’s Golden Mana flickered.

“Like Dragon and Witch.”

“Yes.”

“Like me?”

The woman looked toward him.

“No.”

That was almost reassuring.

Then she added:

“Not yet.”

Ner’s face hardened.

Rez moved slightly closer.

Zee noticed.

Older-brother instinct surviving a younger body.

Aurelia’s voice became cold.

“You came for Veyrath.”

“Yes.”

“Why now?”

The prisoner stared at her.

“You transformed.”

Aurelia’s jaw tightened.

“We had not confirmed you lived.”

“But Sevran led you.”

“Sevran led us toward something.”

She looked directly at Aurelia.

“Your Dragon signature gave us certainty.”

Zee felt guilt again.

Aurelia did not allow it time to grow.

“My decision.”

She did not look at him.

She did not need to.

The prisoner continued.

“There are buyers who have been searching for Veyrath blood for years.”

“Which buyers?”

“I do not know all of them.”

“Names you do know.”

The woman hesitated.

Caldrin smiled.

“There we are.”

She glared.

Then:

“Two eastern covens.”

“A private Dragon collector south of the Black Ridge.”

“A blood broker operating between vampire territories.”

Sevran’s expression changed at the last one.

Aurelia saw.

“You know him.”

“I know of him.”

“Name.”

“Damaris Khell.”

The prisoner smiled.

“Still pretending you do not know where Veyrath survivors went?”

Sevran went still.

Aurelia’s eyes narrowed.

“What does that mean?”

The prisoner looked at Sevran.

“Tell her.”

Sevran did not.

The prisoner leaned back.

“Some Veyrath blood is still alive because Khell sells it alive.”

Silence.

Zee’s horns heated.

Rez saw immediately.

“Zee.”

“I am fine.”

“No.”

Zee looked toward him.

Rez stood from his chair.

His smaller body made the movement less imposing than yesterday.

His expression did not.

“Out.”

“What?”

“You heard me.”

“You are not my father.”

“Thank the gods.”

Zee glared.

Rez walked toward him.

“Your scales are spreading.”

Zee looked down.

Crimson scales had reached across the backs of both hands.

No danger.

Only anger.

Again.

His control was still unstable.

Aurelia stood.

“Rez is right.”

Zee looked betrayed.

“You are agreeing with him?”

“Frequently.”

“That seems unhealthy.”

Even the prisoner almost smiled.

Aurelia touched Zee’s shoulder.

“Go with Rez and Ner.”

“I want to hear this.”

“You will.”

“When?”

“When your Mana stops reacting to every sentence.”

Zee hated that she was right.

He hated more that Rez knew she was right.

“Fine.”

He walked toward the door.

Rez followed.

Ner slipped from the table.

Zareth waited in the corridor.

Before Zee exited, the prisoner called after him.

“Darkheart.”

Zee stopped.

Aurelia’s face hardened.

The woman continued.

“If Khell hears what you carry, he will not come to kill you.”

Zee turned.

“He will come to buy you.”

Crimson Mana slammed through the room.

Every candle died.

Aurelia moved.

Rez grabbed Zee’s arm.

Ner’s Golden barrier rose instinctively between Zee and the prisoner.

Zareth roared from the corridor.

For one heartbeat, the same unnatural stillness from the previous night returned.

Dust hung.

The prisoner’s hair lifted as though submerged in water.

Rez tightened his grip.

“Zee.”

Zee looked at him.

Same face height now.

Same apparent age.

Different history.

Rez shook his head.

“Not here.”

Zee breathed.

Once.

Twice.

The stillness released.

Dust fell.

Aurelia stared.

Lucien looked deeply unsettled.

Sevran looked worse.

Zee pulled his arm from Rez.

“I controlled it.”

Rez nodded.

“Eventually.”

“That counts.”

“It does.”

Aurelia held Zee’s gaze.

It did count.

Small progress.

Necessary progress.

Zee left.

Behind him, Aurelia turned back toward the prisoner.

“Tell me everything you know about Damaris Khell.”

The Veyrath hunt had finally produced a direction.

Not a complete answer.

A name.

A buyer.

And the possibility that people carrying Aurelia’s blood were still alive somewhere in the world.

That changed everything.

That night, Rez attempted sword training.

It went badly.

His old weapon had been shortened temporarily, but his body did not move like seventeen anymore.

His mind commanded a longer reach.

His arm ended early.

He overextended.

Zee knocked the sword away.

Rez stared at the weapon on the floor.

“No.”

Zee lowered his practice blade.

“You want to stop?”

“No.”

Rez picked it up.

“Again.”

Ner sat on a wall nearby.

“You fell three times.”

Rez looked toward him.

“I remember being better than you.”

“You still remember it.”

Zee laughed.

Rez attacked.

This time he adjusted.

Shorter step.

Different weight.

Earlier turn.

Zee blocked.

Rez changed again.

His experience remained.

His body simply demanded new mathematics.

By the seventh exchange, he landed a clean strike against Zee’s shoulder.

Rez smiled.

“There.”

Zee rubbed his arm.

“That was good.”

“I know.”

Ner frowned.

“It took seven attempts.”

Rez pointed his sword toward him.

“Get down here.”

“No.”

“Coward.”

“Historically older people should know better than to bully children.”

Rez dropped the sword.

“I am going to kill him.”

Zee grabbed him around the waist before he reached the wall.

Rez struggled.

“Release me!”

Zee laughed.

“I am stronger now.”

“You were stronger yesterday!”

“Yes, but now it is funny.”

Zareth’s amusement flooded the Link.

Rez stopped fighting.

For a moment he simply stood there.

Then laughed too.

It was strange.

Terrible.

Embarrassing.

Permanent, at least for now.

But not the end of him.

Rez picked up the practice sword again.

“Tomorrow morning.”

Zee looked at him.

“What?”

“We train.”

“You hate mornings.”

“I need to rebuild everything.”

The words quieted Zee.

Rez saw the guilt returning.

He pointed the sword at him.

“Do not.”

“I did not say anything.”

“You have the face.”

Zee smiled faintly.

Rez lowered the weapon.

“You changed my age.”

“Yes.”

“You did not erase me.”

Zee looked at him.

“No.”

“Good.”

Rez returned the sword to guard position.

“Again.”

Zee raised his own.

This time neither smiled.

Blue-White Mana moved cleanly around Rez.

Crimson Mana answered from Zee.

The seventeen years inside Rez had not disappeared.

Now he would learn how to fit them inside a ten-year-old body.

And Zee would have to learn something harder.

How to live with power that could change people he loved.

Without letting fear become the thing that controlled him.
