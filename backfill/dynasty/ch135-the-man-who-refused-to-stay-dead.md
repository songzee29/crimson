Chapter One Hundred Thirty-Five

The Man Who Refused to Stay Dead

Victor was dead for eleven minutes.

Then the fire came back.

Black-crimson first.

Scarlet beneath.

A single ember appeared inside the hole where Zee had destroyed his heart.

Then another.

Then hundreds.

The body ignited.

Not cremation.

Reconstruction.

Bones pulled back into alignment.

Blood returned from ash.

Flesh rebuilt.

The heart formed last.

One beat.

Then another.

Victor gasped.

His eyes opened.

He screamed.

Again.

The Abyssal Titan Phoenix Sovereign refused death.

Again.

⸻

Victor lay in the ruined field staring at the sky.

For several minutes, he did not move.

He understood everything immediately.

Zee killed him.

Again.

Not metaphorically.

Not almost.

Real death.

Then resurrection.

Again.

Victor slowly sat up.

His clothes were mostly gone.

His body remained covered in black-crimson fire that gradually faded.

He looked down at his chest.

No wound.

No hole.

Nothing.

He laughed once.

Then stopped.

Not because it was funny.

Because the alternative was screaming again.

“My son killed me twice.”

The empty ruins offered no opinion.

Victor looked toward the place Zee had stood.

Then remembered the transformation.

Massive black-crimson body.

Scarlet blood-fire.

Crimson-Gold eyes.

Fangs.

Witch lightning.

One coherent supernatural organism.

Abysscin.

Not an Abyssal Titan Phoenix Sovereign.

Not a Tribrid with extra blood layered on top.

Something new.

Victor whispered:

“First Natural Abysscin.”

The name felt correct.

Uncomfortably correct.

Zee’s historical birth remained what it always had been.

First Natural Tribrid.

But what Zee had become now existed beyond that birth classification.

Victor looked toward his own hand.

Black-crimson flame answered.

His.

Ancient.

Unchanged.

Then he thought of Zee.

The Crimson Black Abyss Phoenix.

Unique.

Impossible.

Of course.

Zee had never known how to inherit anything normally.

⸻

Victor stood.

Immediately regretted it.

Resurrection restored the body.

Not dignity.

His head hurt.

His pride hurt more.

He started walking.

Then stopped.

Because instinct suggested one obvious thing.

Leave.

Very far.

Possibly forever.

His son had already killed him twice.

The second time had involved becoming a colossal Phoenix and punching reality with talons.

Victor considered this.

Then continued toward Darkheart.

Not directly.

Not today.

But generally.

Because Victor Darkheart had many flaws.

Learning the correct lesson quickly had never been one of his strengths.

⸻

He reached a roadside inn by nightfall.

Nobody recognized him.

Good.

A merchant near the fire was discussing Darkheart.

Again.

Victor began to suspect the world had developed no other topic.

“King’s changing,” one traveler said.

“How?”

“More dangerous.”

Victor sat with his back to them.

Interesting.

“After that ambush?”

“Yes.”

“Someone really beat him?”

“Supposedly.”

Victor’s face changed.

That part still mattered.

Separate.

Not him.

Whoever had ambushed Zee had done something Victor himself had not.

Actually overwhelmed him through coordinated planning.

Victor did not like that.

Then remembered he had no right to be protective.

Then disliked them anyway.

Complicated.

Appropriate.

Another traveler said:

“Darkheart still hunting the people behind it.”

“Merek Voss, right?”

Victor listened.

Name.

No connection to himself.

Good.

That thread remained elsewhere.

Victor had enough crimes without borrowing someone else’s.

⸻

At Darkheart, Zee returned in Human form.

Eventually.

The transformation had not been easy to reverse.

For several minutes after Victor’s death, the Crimson Black Abyss Phoenix had resisted compression.

Not because it had another mind.

Because it was Zee’s body now.

A true form.

Returning to Human required deliberate control.

Witch discipline.

Crimson Sovereign precision.

Wolf grounding.

Vampire focus.

Dragon endurance.

Phoenix instinct.

All unified.

Eventually bones changed back.

Mass collapsed.

Wings returned to their familiar shape.

Mostly.

Not familiar anymore.

Black-crimson feathers now dominated much of them.

Scarlet blood-fire moved persistently through the shafts when power rose.

His eyes remained Crimson-Gold.

His body felt different.

Larger even when it was not.

Like the Phoenix existed folded underneath.

Waiting.

Zee hated not understanding it.

He hated something else more.

Victor.

⸻

Ner met him before he reached the nursery.

One look at Zee’s face ended every casual question.

“What happened?”

Zee stopped.

Ner waited.

“My father is alive.”

Silence.

Ner stared.

No memory complication.

No hidden Aidan issue.

Just shock.

“Victor.”

“Yes.”

“You killed Victor.”

“Yes.”

“I know.”

“I killed him again.”

Ner stared harder.

“Again.”

“Yes.”

Silence.

Then:

“What?”

Fair.

Zee rubbed his face.

“He came back from the first death.”

Ner’s Pure Gold Mana flickered.

“And he was the attacker.”

“Yes.”

“The one who attacked Darkheart.”

“Yes.”

“The one who made you think Zayrlen was targeted.”

“Yes.”

Ner’s expression became terrifyingly blank.

Zee knew that face.

“He said it was a test.”

Ner looked toward the nursery.

Then back at Zee.

“A test.”

“Yes.”

“To see what kind of father you are.”

“Yes.”

Ner stared for another long moment.

Then said:

“I hope you killed him painfully.”

Zee almost smiled.

“I did.”

Good.

⸻

Zayrlen was awake.

Of course.

Zee entered.

The baby’s Crimson-Gold eyes found him immediately.

No knowledge of Victor.

No Phoenix inheritance.

No impossible connection.

Just father.

Zee lifted him.

Zayrlen settled against his chest.

Tiny.

Warm.

Safe.

Ner stood beside them.

Zee looked down.

For one second, he remembered Victor watching.

The idea made his jaw tighten.

Zayrlen grabbed one of Zee’s fingers.

That helped.

Ner noticed.

“You are thinking about him.”

“Yes.”

“Do not let him take this moment too.”

Zee looked at his son.

Then exhaled.

Correct.

Zayrlen yawned.

Daddy won again.

⸻

Rez arrived soon after.

Then Zareth.

Elias.

Tessa.

Kael.

Amara.

Rhyven.

The explanation took less time than expected.

Mostly because everyone already knew Victor had once been terrible.

The resurrection was the surprising part.

Rez stared.

“He came back.”

“Yes.”

“From Phoenix blood.”

“Yes.”

“And you killed him again.”

“Yes.”

Rez thought.

“Do you think he can come back again?”

Zee looked at him.

“No.”

That answer would age badly.

Zareth’s Dragon Soul Link carried Zee’s certainty.

And underneath it—

uncertainty.

The transformation had changed too much too quickly.

Zareth looked toward the wings.

“Show us.”

Zee frowned.

“No.”

“Zee.”

“No.”

“You became something large enough that the Link nearly tore my mind open.”

“That sounds personal.”

“Show us.”

Ner smiled faintly.

“Later.”

Zareth looked betrayed.

Again.

Family was healing.

Slowly.

⸻

Elias studied Zee’s altered blood.

His face changed almost immediately.

“This is not just inheritance anymore.”

Zee looked toward him.

“I know.”

“Your entire biology is integrating it.”

“Yes.”

“Victor named it?”

Zee’s expression darkened.

“Abysscin.”

Elias repeated carefully.

“Abysscin.”

Exact.

Then looked toward Zee.

“Species?”

“Yes.”

“Form?”

“Crimson Black Abyss Phoenix.”

Silence.

Rhyven stared.

“That is different from Abysscin.”

“Yes.”

Good.

Clarity.

Elias nodded.

“Then we keep them separate.”

Zee appreciated that more than he said.

Zareth looked toward him.

“And First Natural Tribrid?”

“Still mine.”

“Historical birth title.”

“Yes.”

No replacement.

No contradiction.

Zee was born the First Natural Tribrid.

He had become the First Natural Abysscin.

His true Phoenix form was the Crimson Black Abyss Phoenix.

Crimson Sovereign remained what Witch, Vampire, and Wolf became in perfect synchronization.

Crimson Demonic Dragon remained his evolved Dragon nature.

Nothing erased.

Everything integrated.

⸻

Far away, Victor stood outside the roadside inn staring toward Darkheart.

He should have turned away.

Again.

He did not.

He knew better now.

That was not the same as behaving better.

His son had killed him twice.

His grandson did not know he existed.

Ner probably wanted him burned.

Rez probably wanted answers.

Zareth would absolutely attack first.

None of that changed one thought.

He wanted to see Zee again.

Not to test.

That excuse was dead now.

Zee had destroyed it.

Victor knew exactly what kind of father his son was.

Better than him.

That truth remained unpleasant.

Useful.

Unavoidable.

Victor looked toward his own black-crimson fire.

Then laughed quietly.

“What exactly are you going to do?”

No answer.

Good.

He started walking again.

⸻

The darkly funny problem began there.

Victor could die.

Zee could kill him.

Victor could return.

Neither man yet understood how exhausting that would become.

For now, Zee believed the second death had solved the problem.

Victor knew otherwise.

That imbalance would not last.

⸻

Before sleeping, Zee stood at the nursery window holding Zayrlen.

Ner already asleep.

The baby remained awake.

Naturally.

Zee looked down.

“You need better habits.”

Zayrlen blinked.

“You are not listening.”

The baby grabbed Zee’s shirt.

Zee sighed.

“Apparently hereditary.”

Scarlet-black fire moved once across Zee’s wings.

Zayrlen glanced toward it.

No fear.

Then back at Zee.

Zee smiled faintly.

His father had returned from death.

His species had changed.

His true Phoenix form had awakened.

Merek Voss remained somewhere beyond Darkheart, tied to a completely separate enemy who had already proven Zee could lose.

The Devouring Choir still moved somewhere beyond the stars.

None of that disappeared.

But tonight—

his son was safe.

That remained the center.

Far beyond the nursery, a resurrected Victor kept walking.

He was ashamed.

Proud.

Intrusive.

Complicated.

Alive.

Again.

And whether Zee Darkheart liked it or not—

his father had officially become a problem death could no longer solve.
