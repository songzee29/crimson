Chapter Ninety-Four

The Man Who Killed Gods

The first warning was a corpse.

Not Human.

Not Witch.

Not Vampire.

A god.

Darkheart scouts found the body three miles beyond the northern boundary.

It had fallen from the sky.

No heartbeat.

No divine regeneration.

No return.

One wound crossed its chest.

Black.

Still burning.

Zee crouched beside it.

Ner stood behind him.

“This is not like the one you killed.”

“No.”

Zee touched the earth beside the wound.

The grass had died.

Not burned.

Denied.

Even the dead god’s residual power refused to heal around the injury.

Zee’s expression sharpened.

“Something made sure this stayed dead.”

A voice answered from the trees.

“I did.”

Zee stood.

A man walked into the clearing.

Human-shaped.

Tall.

Dark coat.

No crown.

No armor.

One black blade strapped across his back.

His heartbeat was slow.

His blood smelled Human.

Mostly.

But something underneath it made Zee’s Vampire senses recoil.

Not Demon.

Not God.

Not Witch.

Darkness with structure.

Ner’s Gold rose.

The stranger looked at it.

“Beautiful.”

Ner’s expression cooled.

“Do not.”

The man smiled.

Then looked at Zee.

“Zee Darkheart.”

“Unfortunately.”

“You drained a god.”

“Yes.”

“You survived divine blood.”

“Yes.”

“You corrupted what remained.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“No.”

The man tilted his head.

“No?”

“My blood changed it.”

“That distinction matters to you?”

“Yes.”

The stranger smiled faintly.

“Good.”

Zee looked at the dead god.

“Name.”

“Cassel Rhune.”

“And what are you?”

Cassel pulled the black blade from his back.

The dead god’s remaining aura recoiled from it.

Ner whispered:

“Zee.”

He felt it too.

Cassel answered himself.

“Godslayer.”

Silence.

Zee looked at him.

Then at the dead god.

“Dramatic profession.”

Cassel laughed.

“I expected more fear.”

“You came to my border alone.”

“Yes.”

“That usually means confidence or stupidity.”

“Which am I?”

Zee’s pupils narrowed into slits.

“I have not decided.”

Cassel smiled wider.

“Then decide while fighting.”

He moved.

⸻

Fast.

Not Vampire fast.

Different.

Efficient.

The black blade struck Zee’s forearm.

Crimson-black scales caught it.

For half a second.

Then split.

Zee’s eyes widened.

The blade cut through scale and flesh.

Black fire entered the wound.

Zee jumped backward.

Healing began.

Stopped.

That was new.

Cassel saw his expression.

“There.”

Zee looked at his arm.

The wound remained open.

Dark Divinity burned along the edges.

His Vampire regeneration pushed.

Wolf vitality followed.

Dragon durability resisted.

Crimson biology searched for an answer.

Nothing immediate.

Cassel attacked again.

Zee smiled.

“Oh.”

Ner knew that smile.

“You are enjoying this.”

“Yes.”

“That is concerning.”

Cassel’s blade came for Zee’s throat.

Zee ducked.

Assassin movement.

Palm into elbow.

Knee.

Turn.

Cassel absorbed the strike and drove his forehead into Zee’s face.

Horn met skull.

Cassel staggered.

Zee did not.

“Human,” Zee said.

Cassel wiped blood from his forehead.

“Very.”

Then black force erupted from his free hand.

It hit Zee like a collapsing cathedral.

He flew through three trees.

Ner’s Gold struck Cassel from the side.

Cassel raised the blade.

Gold touched Dark Divinity.

Cracked.

Ner stared.

Cassel looked at him.

“I did not come for you.”

“Then stop hitting my boyfriend.”

Zee climbed from the broken trees.

Blood ran down his arm.

“I had it handled.”

Ner looked at the three destroyed trees.

“Clearly.”

Cassel watched them.

“You love each other.”

Zee’s face cooled.

Cassel continued.

“That creates weaknesses.”

“No.”

“It always does.”

Zee disappeared.

Vampire speed.

Cassel turned late.

Zee caught him by the throat and drove him into the ground.

The clearing cratered.

Cassel stabbed upward.

The blade entered Zee’s side.

Deep.

Zee snarled.

Cassel twisted.

The wound burned.

Healing died around it.

Then Zee smiled down at him.

“You think pain makes me release people?”

Cassel’s eyes changed.

First uncertainty.

Good.

Zee grabbed the blade with one hand.

Silver hurt.

Magic hurt.

This—

this hurt differently.

Black Divinity burned directly through supernatural resistance.

Zee pulled himself farther onto the blade.

Closer.

Cassel stared.

“That should stop you.”

“It did.”

Zee’s fangs descended.

“From healing.”

Then he bit Cassel.

⸻

The blood was wrong.

Not poisonous.

Worse.

Dark Divinity moved inside it like something with intention.

It entered Zee’s mouth.

Touched Crimson blood.

And attacked.

Zee released Cassel immediately.

Black veins spread across his jaw.

Ner moved.

“Zee!”

Zee dropped to one knee.

His body tried processing it.

Demon.

No.

Divine.

No.

Corruption.

No.

Dark Divinity refused every category.

It did not want to become part of him.

Crimson biology pushed back.

Hard.

The foreign force burned out through Zee’s mouth in black smoke.

Zee spat blood.

Cassel stood slowly.

Now genuinely interested.

“You rejected it.”

Zee wiped his mouth.

“It rejected me first.”

“No.”

Cassel stared at him.

“Most things touched by Dark Divinity either submit or rot.”

Zee stood.

His wound still open.

“I do neither.”

Cassel’s expression darkened.

“That is exactly why you are dangerous.”

Zee laughed once.

“You killed a god and came to lecture me about danger?”

“I kill gods because gods become untouchable.”

“I am not a god.”

“You consumed one.”

“And remained me.”

“For now.”

There.

Motive.

Zee understood.

Cassel did not want his blood.

Did not want hybrids.

Did not care about Darkheart.

He believed Zee might someday become divine.

And Cassel killed divinity before it became permanent.

Zee’s expression cooled.

“You came to kill what I might become.”

Cassel did not deny it.

The similarity to Mara was immediate.

Not exact.

But close enough to irritate him.

“That is a dangerous argument around me.”

Cassel lifted the blade.

“I have killed nine gods.”

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“Then you should know better than to mistake one.”

⸻

They collided again.

Witchcraft shattered the ground beneath Cassel.

Dark Divinity broke the spell before it closed.

Zee shifted Wolf.

Near-black.

Four-legged.

He vanished into the trees.

Cassel turned.

Listened.

Wrong sense.

Zee came from behind.

Jaws caught Cassel’s sword arm.

Cassel drove black force through Zee’s ribs.

Wolf Zee released.

Rolled.

Changed human mid-motion.

Claws struck.

Cassel blocked two.

Third opened his chest.

Blood.

Human blood laced with darkness.

Zee smelled fear now.

Tiny.

Beautiful.

Cassel smiled despite it.

“You like that.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

He cut Zee across the face.

The wound stayed.

Zee touched it.

Blood.

Then smiled.

“You do too.”

Cassel said nothing.

That answer was enough.

Two dark men.

Different rules.

Cassel killed gods because he believed unchecked divinity inevitably became tyranny.

Zee killed threats because he believed choice mattered more than blood.

Neither considered himself clean.

Good.

Clean people were exhausting.

Then Cassel raised the blade toward Darkheart.

“Your kingdom follows you like worshippers.”

Zee’s smile vanished.

“They are citizens.”

“Kings always say that before temples.”

“You know nothing about them.”

“I know they kneel.”

“Some do.”

“They obey.”

“Sometimes.”

“They fear you.”

Zee remembered the quarry.

That one landed.

Cassel saw it.

Mistake.

Zee crossed the distance.

One fist hit Cassel’s jaw.

Then another.

Dragon force.

Vampire strength.

Wolf brutality.

Assassin placement.

The third strike broke bone.

Cassel hit the ground.

Zee stepped on his sword arm.

Cassel looked up.

“You are proving—”

Zee broke the arm.

Cassel screamed.

Zee liked it.

Deliberately.

Choice intact.

“That,” Zee said quietly, “was for using my people to explain me.”

Then black force exploded.

Zee flew backward.

Cassel escaped into the forest.

Not running blindly.

Strategic retreat.

Zee started after him.

Ner caught his wrist.

Zee turned.

Ner did not tell him not to kill.

He asked one thing.

“Are you choosing?”

Zee looked toward the blood trail.

Human pupils returned.

“Yes.”

Ner released him.

“Then go.”

Zee smiled.

And hunted.
