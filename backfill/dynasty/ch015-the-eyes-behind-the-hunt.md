Chapter Fifteen

The Eyes Behind the Hunt

They did not return by the same road.

Lucien changed the route twice before sunset.

No fires.

No visible camp.

No repeated trails.

The prisoner from the sanctuary remained unconscious across one Vesper soldier’s horse while the royal witch worked periodically to stabilize him.

Aurelia rode in silence.

Zee slept.

The crimson hatchling did not.

Something about the rider on the ridge had changed him.

Through the Dragon Link, he kept sending Zee the same crude image.

Black.

Far.

Watching.

Zee could not understand a spy.

He understood the feeling behind it.

Seen.

Aurelia noticed the hatchling looking backward every few minutes.

“So do I.”

Lucien glanced toward her.

“What?”

“The feeling.”

She looked behind them.

“No matter where we go, someone is watching.”

Lucien did not offer reassurance.

He had stopped offering reassurance he could not prove.

“We assume they are.”

Vale nodded.

“That changes travel.”

Caldrin looked back.

“It changes everything.”

The royal witch spoke from beside the prisoner.

“He is waking.”

They stopped beneath a low ridge.

The man coughed violently.

Lucien dismounted.

“What is your name?”

The prisoner stared at him.

“Theron.”

“Family name?”

“None worth using.”

Caldrin smiled faintly.

“I like him already.”

Aurelia remained mounted.

“Theron.”

He looked at her.

“The people who held you.”

His face tightened.

“What did they call themselves?”

“Nothing.”

“Never?”

“They used numbers.”

Lucien frowned.

“For rank?”

“I think.”

“What numbers?”

“First Hand. Second Hand. Third Hand.”

Caldrin’s expression changed.

“That is not a coven structure I know.”

The royal witch shook her head.

“Nor I.”

Aurelia asked, “Did they worship anything?”

“No.”

“Serve a king?”

“Not openly.”

“A god?”

“I never heard prayer.”

“Demons?”

Theron looked uncertain.

“No.”

Good.

No premature answer.

Lucien crouched.

“What did they want from you?”

Theron laughed weakly.

“My blood.”

“What are you?”

“A witch.”

“Only?”

Theron’s smile vanished.

“My mother was wolf-blooded.”

Vale looked toward the others.

Another mixed line.

Theron continued.

“I never transformed.”

“Did they care?”

“No.”

“They cared that the blood was there.”

Aurelia looked down at Zee.

Three complete natures.

Born together.

No wonder anyone obsessed with mixed blood would want him.

Not prophecy.

Not destiny.

Opportunity.

Lucien stood.

“What did they do?”

Theron looked away.

“They exposed us to things.”

“What things?”

“Blood.”

“Whose?”

“Vampire.”

“Monster.”

“Demon.”

The royal witch’s expression tightened.

“Trying to force adaptation.”

Theron looked at her.

“Yes.”

Aurelia’s voice hardened.

“Did it work?”

“Sometimes.”

Silence.

Caldrin’s eyes narrowed.

“What does sometimes mean?”

Theron looked toward the ground.

“Long enough to regret it.”

No one asked more.

Not yet.

The road continued.

⸻

They reached the Vesper border after midnight.

No attack came.

That made everyone more nervous.

At dawn, the capital appeared.

The gates opened.

Rez was waiting.

Lucien saw him from fifty yards away.

His expression changed.

“Why is he outside the walls?”

A Vesper captain rode forward.

“My King.”

“Why is my son outside the walls?”

“He refused to wait inside.”

Rez heard.

“I am standing behind the first gate.”

Lucien pointed toward the city.

“Inside.”

Rez crossed his arms.

“No.”

Aurelia looked at Lucien.

“Good luck.”

He glared at her.

“This is not funny.”

“It is a little.”

Rez ignored his father and looked toward Zee.

The crimson hatchling immediately recognized him.

The Dragon Link carried something new.

Known.

Not family.

Not yet.

But known.

Zee opened his crimson eyes.

Rez smiled.

“You came back.”

Lucien dismounted.

“We need to speak.”

“I know.”

“No, you do not.”

Rez looked toward the unconscious prisoner.

“Who is that?”

Lucien closed his eyes.

Aurelia laughed quietly.

“Definitely your son.”

⸻

The palace council met before noon.

Lucien.

Aurelia.

Vale.

Caldrin.

The royal witch.

Three Vesper ministers.

Theron, once he was strong enough to sit.

Rez was excluded.

He listened outside the door.

Lucien knew.

He allowed it.

The map on the table had changed.

Red markers represented known bounty routes.

Black represented assassination attempts.

Silver represented coalition kingdoms.

Now Lucien added another marker.

White.

Unknown.

He placed it over the sanctuary.

“Someone used the abandoned sanctuary for experiments involving mixed supernatural blood.”

One minister frowned.

“For how long?”

“Unknown.”

“Before Zee was born?”

“Yes.”

Aurelia leaned forward.

“That matters.”

The minister looked toward her.

“Because they were not preparing specifically for him.”

“Exactly.”

Caldrin nodded.

“They were preparing for possibility.”

Theron sat wrapped in blankets near the fire.

“They believed nature could be forced.”

The royal witch asked, “Forced into what?”

“No one told us.”

“Did they succeed with anyone?”

Theron’s face became distant.

“One girl survived longer than the others.”

Aurelia looked toward him.

“What was she?”

“Witch and wolf.”

The witch’s eyes narrowed.

“What happened?”

“They gave her vampire blood.”

Silence.

Lucien looked toward Theron.

“And?”

“She changed.”

Aurelia’s expression sharpened.

“Into what?”

“I do not know.”

Not a Tribrid.

Not certainty.

Theron continued.

“She became violent.”

“Stronger.”

“Faster.”

“Then she began dying.”

“From the vampire blood?”

“Maybe.”

“From the wolf blood?”

“I do not know.”

“Magic rejection?”

“I do not know.”

He slammed one weak fist against the chair.

“I do not know.”

The room went quiet.

Aurelia softened slightly.

“Enough.”

Theron nodded.

Lucien looked toward the map.

“Whoever did this may now know Zee exists.”

“They do,” Caldrin said.

Aurelia turned.

“You sound certain.”

“They sent the assassins.”

“Possible.”

“They burned the sanctuary.”

“Possible.”

“They watched us leave.”

“Likely.”

Caldrin sighed.

“You have become difficult.”

“Fear nearly killed my son.”

Her voice stayed calm.

“I prefer difficult.”

Lucien looked between them.

“She is right.”

He tapped the map.

“We separate fact from assumption.”

He began listing.

“Fact: someone experimented on mixed bloodlines.”

“Fact: the sanctuary was used.”

“Fact: a black-robed rider observed us.”

“Fact: assassins using witch death seals attacked the palace.”

“Not fact: same organization.”

Caldrin nodded reluctantly.

“Fine.”

“Not fact: they predicted Zee.”

“Not fact: they understand what he is.”

“Not fact: they know about his resurrection.”

Vale looked toward him.

“Rumors already spread.”

“Yes.”

“Then assume they heard.”

“Assume.”

Lucien’s finger moved toward the black markers.

“Do not build strategy on certainty we do not possess.”

Aurelia looked toward Zee sleeping in a guarded chamber above.

For the first time, the people around him were learning the same lesson his enemies refused to learn.

Observe.

Do not invent.

Survive what is real.

⸻

Later that afternoon, Zee fed.

Then slept.

Then woke hungry again.

For all the wars surrounding him, he remained a newborn.

That contrast had begun affecting Aurelia strangely.

One hour, she discussed spies and blood experiments with kings.

The next, she cleaned milk from Zee’s chin.

The hatchling watched every feeding with unbearable interest.

“No.”

Aurelia moved the bottle away from him.

The dragon chirped.

“No.”

Another chirp.

“You eat meat.”

The hatchling stared at the milk.

Zee felt the hunger through the Link.

The infant turned toward the dragon.

Hungry.

The hatchling answered.

Yes.

Aurelia looked between them.

“You are conspiring.”

Rez sat nearby.

“He wants some.”

“Thank you.”

“I am helping.”

“You are not.”

Rez took a second small cup from the tray.

Aurelia stared.

“Do not.”

Rez poured a little milk onto a saucer.

The hatchling attacked it.

Milk went everywhere.

Rez laughed.

Aurelia covered her face.

“This is a prince?”

“We do not know that.”

Rez looked at the hatchling.

“He acts like one.”

“How?”

“Entitled.”

The dragon sneezed milk onto him.

Rez stopped laughing.

Aurelia did not.

The sound startled Zee.

Then he made one too.

Small.

Barely a laugh.

But real.

Everyone froze.

Aurelia stared.

Rez stared.

The hatchling licked milk from his snout.

Zee made the sound again.

Aurelia’s eyes filled with tears.

Not sadness.

Something rarer now.

Joy.

For a few minutes, no one hunted him.

No prophecies.

No armies.

No experiments.

Only a child laughing because a dragon had sneezed on a prince.

Then the door opened.

Lucien entered.

His face erased the moment.

Aurelia stood.

“What?”

“A message.”

“From whom?”

“We do not know.”

Rez frowned.

“That happens a lot now.”

Lucien ignored him.

He handed Aurelia a small piece of parchment.

No seal.

No signature.

Only seven words.

WE DO NOT WANT HIM DEAD YET.

Aurelia read it twice.

Her expression turned cold.

Caldrin appeared behind Lucien.

“There is another line.”

Aurelia turned the parchment over.

Three more words.

LET HIM GROW.

Silence.

Rez’s blue-white mana flickered.

Lucien took the parchment back.

“They are studying him.”

Aurelia’s eyes hardened.

“Or trying to frighten us.”

“Yes.”

“Or both.”

“Yes.”

She looked toward Zee.

The infant was still smiling faintly.

The hatchling leaned against him.

The message changed nothing about what Zee knew.

Nothing about what he felt.

But it changed the adults around him.

Some enemies wanted him dead.

Others wanted him alive.

That distinction was not comforting.

Sometimes being wanted alive was worse.

⸻

Far beyond House Vesper, the woman in black received a report.

“The sanctuary burned.”

She nodded.

“Good.”

“One prisoner escaped.”

Her expression changed.

“Which?”

“Theron.”

“Did he reach Vesper?”

“We believe so.”

The woman looked toward a series of glass containers lining the chamber wall.

Inside each rested small samples of blood labeled only by number.

Some dark.

Some pale.

Some nearly black.

None crimson.

A man beside her asked, “Should we recover him?”

“No.”

“He knows too much.”

“He knows fragments.”

“That may be enough.”

She smiled faintly.

“Let him speak.”

“Why?”

“Because frightened kings are useful when they believe they have discovered something themselves.”

The man looked uneasy.

“And the child?”

“Do not touch him.”

“For how long?”

The woman looked toward a fresh report.

Subject survived army siege.

Subject survived confirmed cardiac death — unverified witness chain.

Subject displayed reactive force manifestation.

Subject demonstrated abnormal predatory perception.

Subject potentially absorbed foreign essence.

She tapped the final line.

“Until we know whether every attempt to destroy him teaches us more about him…”

She paused.

“…or teaches him more about us.”

The man lowered his head.

“And if he keeps evolving?”

The woman looked toward the empty crimson vial prepared on the table.

“Then eventually…”

She smiled.

“…we stop observing from a distance.”

⸻

That night, Zee dreamed.

Nothing complicated.

Red light.

Warmth.

Aurelia’s voice.

The hatchling beside him.

Then something else.

A shape.

Black.

Watching from far away.

The image had not come from Zee.

It came through the Dragon Link.

The hatchling was awake.

He remembered the rider.

Zee felt his fear.

He responded instinctively.

Not with language.

Not with reassurance.

With presence.

Here.

The hatchling moved closer.

Together.

Their heartbeats settled.

Outside the room, Vale stood guard.

Beyond him, Vesper soldiers patrolled halls layered with wards.

Farther still, the Twelve Crowns tightened sanctions.

Bounty hunters studied new reports.

Witch factions argued.

Vampire courts listened for Zee’s heartbeat.

Wolf packs continued changing beneath instinct they did not understand.

And somewhere beyond all of them, unseen hands began collecting every story about the First Natural Tribrid.

Zee had survived the world trying to kill him.

Now he had entered a more dangerous stage.

The world had begun trying to learn him.
