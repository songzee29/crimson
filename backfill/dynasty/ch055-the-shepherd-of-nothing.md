Chapter Fifty-Five

The Shepherd of Nothing

They followed the migration until night.

Not the black thing.

That would have been impossible.

It moved too slowly to judge distance and too strangely to judge speed.

Sometimes it appeared miles away.

Sometimes it seemed close enough to touch.

Then hills passed in front of it that should have been behind.

Ner hated it.

Zee could tell because Ner had gone silent.

That was always serious.

Rez noticed too.

“How bad?”

Ner looked toward him.

“I cannot decide whether it is large.”

Zee frowned.

“It is obviously large.”

“No.”

Ner pointed toward the horizon.

“You are assuming apparent size corresponds to distance.”

Rez looked again.

Then stopped smiling.

Ner continued.

“It may be closer and smaller.”

“Farther and larger.”

“Or…”

He hesitated.

Zee looked over.

“Or?”

“I do not know whether all of it occupies the same distance.”

Zee stared.

“That sentence was terrible.”

“I hated saying it.”

Zareth flew lower.

The Link carried discomfort.

The black shape crossed beneath a moon that momentarily disappeared behind it.

Then the moon reappeared inside the shape.

Not through a hole.

Inside.

Zee stopped walking.

“No.”

Rez looked toward him.

“That is your analysis?”

“Yes.”

“Strong.”

The migration continued around them.

Animals moved with strange calm.

None appeared exhausted.

None stopped to feed.

That finally bothered Zee enough to test something.

He approached a gray wolf traveling near the edge of the herd.

Ordinary wolf.

Mostly.

It looked at him.

No fear.

Zee crouched.

His own Wolf nature reached instinctively.

Not command.

Recognition.

Pack language without words.

The wolf’s mind felt—

Far away.

Not controlled.

Focused.

One need overwhelmed everything else.

West.

Not go west.

More like:

home is west now.

Zee recoiled.

Rez stepped closer.

“What?”

“They think they belong there.”

“Do they?”

“I do not know.”

Ner crouched beside the wolf without touching.

Golden Mana formed around one eye.

Not entering the animal.

Reading the magical boundary around it.

“There is no compulsion spell.”

“Sure?”

“Yes.”

“Curse?”

“No.”

“Possession?”

“No.”

Rez looked toward the herd.

“So nothing is forcing them.”

Ner frowned.

“Not magically.”

Zee stood.

“Instinct.”

“Changed instinct?”

“Maybe.”

Rez sighed.

Ner looked at him.

“You hate uncertainty.”

“I hate plans made from uncertainty.”

“That is different.”

“Barely.”

The wolf continued.

Zee let it.

⸻

They camped on a ridge.

Not because any of them wanted sleep.

Because moving in unknown territory at night while sound, distance, and animal behavior were all unreliable was stupid.

Rez said that.

Zee agreed reluctantly.

Zareth remained awake.

Of course.

His huge body curled around one side of the camp without enclosing it completely.

Not possession.

Protection.

The Dragon Link remained tense.

Zee sat near the fire.

Ner beside him.

Rez slept lightly several feet away with one hand near his sword.

The black shape remained visible.

It had not moved much.

Or had moved tremendously.

Impossible to tell.

Zee looked toward Ner.

“You are thinking too loudly.”

Ner smiled faintly.

“You cannot hear thoughts.”

“I know.”

“Then?”

“You have the face.”

Ner laughed.

“Everyone says that to you.”

“I am stealing it.”

“That is growth.”

Zee looked toward the distant shape.

“What are you thinking?”

Ner took his time.

“That the deep wild had rules we did not understand.”

“Yes.”

“This feels different.”

“How?”

“The deep wild reacted to boundaries.”

“To injury.”

“To intrusion.”

Ner looked toward the migration.

“This does not feel reactive.”

Zee understood.

“It is doing something.”

“Yes.”

“On purpose?”

“I do not know.”

A pause.

Then Ner looked toward Zee.

“You are not smiling.”

Zee frowned.

“Should I be?”

“You usually smile when you are scared.”

“That is not true.”

“It is.”

Zee thought.

Annoyingly, yes.

Fear often made him curious.

Alive.

Ready.

This felt different.

Not predator fear.

Not fight fear.

Smallness.

He did not like that.

Ner’s shoulder touched his.

Deliberate this time.

Zee noticed.

Did not move.

“You okay?” Ner asked.

“No.”

Ner smiled.

“Good.”

Zee looked at him.

“That is my line.”

“It is Rez’s.”

“Everything good apparently belongs to Rez.”

Rez, eyes still closed, muttered:

“Correct.”

Zee stared.

“You were awake?”

“No.”

Ner laughed.

Zee threw a pebble.

Rez caught it without opening his eyes.

Annoying.

Zareth’s Link stirred.

Then sharpened violently.

Zee stood.

“What?”

The Dragon rose.

Every animal in the valley stopped.

Thousands.

At once.

No sound.

No motion.

The black shape stopped too.

Then turned.

Toward them.

Zee’s body went cold.

Not metaphorically.

His skin temperature dropped.

His Crimson Dragon energy retreated beneath his scales as if instinct wanted to become smaller.

Zareth spread his wings.

Teal-and-Gold Mana surged.

Not attack.

Readiness.

Rez was standing now.

Sword in hand.

Ner’s Gold lit the ridge.

The black shape bent.

The horizon bent with it.

Slowly, something descended from cloud.

Not a head.

A structure resembling one only because Zee’s mind needed somewhere to put eyes.

There were none.

Instead, long vertical openings appeared in the darkness.

Inside them—

stars.

Not the sky behind it.

Different stars.

Zee knew because one cluster formed a shape he had never seen from Earth.

His breath stopped.

Life beyond the stars existed.

He knew that as possibility in the world.

But this was not confirmation of where the thing came from.

Could not be.

Not yet.

Ner whispered:

“Those are not holes.”

Rez looked toward him.

“You know?”

“No.”

Good.

Terrible.

The creature—if creature was even right—lowered further.

The migration began again.

Every animal moved toward the valley below the shape.

Then disappeared.

Not dying.

Not swallowed.

One deer crossed an invisible line and simply ceased to occupy the ridge.

A wolf followed.

Birds.

Fish moving over wet ground.

Thousands.

Zee’s Crimson fold perception activated.

Lines filled the valley.

But these were not like the deep wild.

Those folds bent space.

These lines—

Moved with the animals.

Zee’s eyes widened.

“They are carrying the folds.”

Ner looked toward him.

“What?”

“The animals.”

He pointed.

“Each one has a line.”

Ner’s Gold sharpened.

He tried to see.

Could not.

“Where do they go?”

Zee followed one.

The line disappeared into the black shape.

Then farther.

Not inside.

Beyond.

His head began hurting.

He stopped.

Rez looked at the migration.

“Do we stop them?”

Zee opened his mouth.

No answer.

Ner asked the better question.

“Why?”

Rez looked toward him.

“If they are being taken—”

“Are they?”

Silence.

Zee looked at the ordinary wolf from before.

It approached the invisible line.

Its mind still held one certainty.

home is west now.

Not fear.

Not coercion.

Need.

Choice?

Instinct?

Something between.

The wolf crossed.

Gone.

Zee’s darkness offered nothing.

No villain.

No deserving target.

No one to punish.

He hated how useless violence felt.

The black structure lowered again.

Zareth roared.

The Link carried warning.

Something was coming toward them.

Not the giant.

Smaller.

From beneath it.

A shape descended through the air without wings.

Humanoid.

Mostly.

Rez moved in front instinctively.

Zee stepped beside him.

Ner beside Zee.

Zareth behind all three.

Core four.

Automatic.

The figure landed twenty yards away.

No impact.

No sound.

Tall.

Thin.

Wrapped in layers of pale material that looked grown rather than woven.

Its face was smooth except for three vertical slits where eyes might belong.

No heartbeat.

No blood scent Zee understood.

No visible Mana.

It looked at the four.

Then at the migrating animals.

Then back.

No hostility.

Rez spoke first.

“Can you understand us?”

No response.

Ner lifted one hand.

Not casting.

Showing empty palm.

The being copied him.

Slowly.

Zee frowned.

“Maybe.”

The figure tilted its head.

Then touched its own chest.

A sound came.

Not speech.

A layered vibration.

Zee’s bones felt it more than his ears.

Zareth reacted.

The Link sent recognition of nothing.

That was becoming common.

The figure touched the ground.

A pale line spread through the grass.

Ner immediately crouched.

Golden Mana hovered above it.

“Structure.”

Rez looked down.

“What kind?”

“I do not know.”

The line split.

Four branches.

One toward each of them.

Zee’s Crimson perception saw something else.

The lines did not carry magic.

They carried relation.

Like paths.

The figure pointed toward the giant shape.

Then toward the migrating animals.

Then touched its chest again.

Rez frowned.

“It is responsible.”

Ner shook his head.

“Maybe connected.”

Zee watched.

The figure repeated.

Chest.

Animals.

Giant.

Then opened both hands.

The migration continued.

Zareth’s Link offered a concept.

Shepherd?

Zee looked toward him.

Maybe.

He pointed toward the figure.

“Shepherd?”

The being became still.

Then touched its chest.

Same vibration.

No translation.

No confirmation.

But not rejection either.

Rez lowered his sword fully.

Good.

The figure looked toward him.

Then at the blade.

Then mimicked the motion of putting something away.

Rez stared.

Slowly sheathed it.

Zee smiled faintly.

“Smart.”

Rez whispered:

“Do not make me regret it.”

The being turned toward Zee.

Its three facial slits narrowed.

Then it approached.

Zareth moved.

Zee lifted one hand.

“Wait.”

The Dragon Link filled with immediate rejection.

Zee sent:

No threat yet.

Zareth stopped.

Barely.

The figure came within arm’s reach.

It looked at Zee’s horns.

Scales.

Then touched the air near his shoulder ridges without touching him.

His Dragon nature erupted.

Not pain.

Not awakening.

A violent sensation of being recognized incorrectly.

As though the being had looked at four blood natures and found something fifth-shaped that was not actually there.

Zee staggered.

Ner caught his elbow.

The figure withdrew immediately.

Interesting.

It learned.

Zee breathed.

“What did you do?”

No answer.

The being looked toward Zareth.

Then Aurelia—

No.

Not Aurelia.

An image appeared in Zee’s mind.

Not telepathy.

Not thought inserted.

Memory-like impression carried through whatever interaction had just happened.

Aurelia in Dragon form.

Veyrath.

Zareth.

Drakonis.

Zee.

Four lines converging.

Then one line continuing into darkness.

Zee ripped himself back.

“No.”

The impression vanished.

Ner’s grip tightened.

“What?”

“It saw my blood.”

“Can it do that?”

“I do not know.”

The figure tilted its head.

Then pointed toward Zee.

Toward the giant shape.

Then west.

Rez’s expression changed.

“It wants him to go with it.”

Zareth roared.

The figure did not flinch.

Zee stared.

“No.”

The figure remained still.

No force.

No threat.

It pointed again.

Zee shook his head.

“No.”

This time the being lowered its hand.

Accepted.

Just like that.

That was almost more disturbing.

Then it turned toward the migration.

Walked away.

No attempt to take Zee.

No prophecy.

No special claim.

No declaration that he belonged somewhere else.

Invitation refused.

Done.

Ner exhaled.

“That was easy.”

Rez looked toward the giant shape.

“Nothing about this is easy.”

The figure stopped near the invisible migration line.

Looked back once.

Then crossed.

Gone.

The giant shape began rising.

Animals continued disappearing beneath it.

Thousands.

No panic.

No blood.

No death they could detect.

Then the last creature crossed.

The valley emptied.

Completely.

The giant shape drifted upward.

As it rose, the strange stars inside its vertical openings vanished one by one.

The night sky returned to normal.

Then the thing was gone.

Not moved away.

Gone.

The world remained.

The grass bent in the wind.

Sound returned properly.

Zee heard Rez breathe.

Ner’s heartbeat.

Zareth’s wings shifting.

Normal.

Almost.

Then something small moved near Zee’s boot.

A black fish.

One of the migrating silver-black fish from Vesper.

It had not crossed.

It flopped once.

Zee crouched.

The narrow walking fins were disappearing.

Not rotting.

Receding into ordinary anatomy.

Ner stared.

“It changed for the journey.”

Zee looked toward him.

“Adaptation?”

“Maybe.”

Zee smiled.

Ner immediately frowned.

“Not yours.”

“I know.”

Important.

The fish had changed itself—or been changed by whatever migration demanded.

Not Zee.

The world had its own adaptive miracles.

That realization made Zee strangely happy.

He picked up the fish carefully.

A nearby stream ran downhill now.

Normal.

He placed it in the water.

It swam east.

Back toward Vesper.

Rez watched.

“So not every animal wanted to go?”

Ner shook his head.

“Or not every animal completed whatever instinct changed.”

Zee looked west.

Nothing remained.

No giant.

No shepherd.

No explanation.

Zareth’s Link carried unease.

Then wonder.

Same as Zee.

Rez exhaled.

“We report what we actually know.”

Zee smiled.

“That will be short.”

“Very.”

Ner began counting on his fingers.

“Mass migration.”

“Temporary biological changes in at least one known species.”

“No standard compulsion magic.”

“Unknown nonhuman entity.”

“Another unknown larger entity or phenomenon.”

“Possible transport across nonlocal boundaries.”

Rez looked at him.

“Stop.”

“I am organizing.”

“You are making it worse.”

Zee laughed.

Then looked toward the place where the figure had invited him.

Why?

Because of his Dragon blood?

His four natures?

His strange adaptations?

Something else?

He did not know.

For once, he did not chase the answer.

That surprised him.

The invitation had been real.

So had his refusal.

Maybe one day the road west would matter again.

Not tonight.

Tonight they had learned something larger.

The deep wild was not the edge of the strange world.

It was only one region.

One kind of impossible.

There were beings that migrated through distances kingdoms could not name.

Creatures capable of changing biology for a journey without Zee’s blood.

Entities that used no magic Ner recognized.

Things Drakonis had never recorded.

The world beyond crowns was not one mystery.

It was countless.

Rez looked toward the empty valley.

“We are going home.”

Zee nodded.

No argument.

Ner smiled.

“Who are you and what did you do with Zee?”

Zee shoved him.

Ner laughed.

Their shoulders collided.

Stayed close.

Zareth’s amusement rolled through the Link.

Rez started walking east.

“Come on.”

Zee followed.

Behind them, the western horizon remained empty.

Ahead lay kingdoms.

Vesper.

Aurelia.

Lucien.

Theron.

Seraphine.

Politics.

Food.

Arguments.

Home.

All of it suddenly seemed smaller than it had yesterday.

Not less important.

Smaller.

And Zee Darkheart finally understood that there could be entire lives, migrations, powers, and purposes moving through the world that had nothing to do with him.

He loved that.

Because for the first time, infinity did not feel like something hunting him.

It felt like somewhere to go.
