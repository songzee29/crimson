Chapter One Hundred Forty-Two

Father and Son

The alarm reached Darkheart before sunrise.

Not bells.

Blood.

A wounded Wolf rider collapsed through the eastern gate with half his armor missing and another man’s blood dried across his throat.

Zee smelled it before the guards finished carrying him inside.

He entered the lower hall already knowing someone had crossed the line.

Tessa knelt beside the rider.

Ner stood nearby.

Rez had a map open across the nearest table.

Zayrlen arrived seconds later.

Older now.

Teenage.

Tall enough that the difference between boy and young man had become impossible to ignore.

Still Zee’s son.

Always.

The rider tried to sit up.

Tessa pushed him back down.

“Do that again and I will personally knock you unconscious.”

He obeyed.

Zee looked at him.

“What happened?”

The Wolf swallowed.

“Ridge settlement east of Vaelor Pass.”

Darkheart protected territory.

Families.

Supply roads.

People who had sworn to Zee because Zee had promised something simple in return.

Safety.

The rider continued.

“Raiders came before dawn. Mixed group. Mercenaries. Wolves. Vampires. Witches.”

“How many?”

“Forty, maybe fifty.”

Rez looked at the map.

“Too organized for ordinary bandits.”

The rider nodded.

“They knew the patrol rotation.”

Zee’s expression cooled.

“Dead?”

“Three guards.”

Silence.

“Civilians?”

“Two wounded when I left. They were trying to drag people out of the houses.”

That was enough.

Zee turned.

“Rez.”

“Already sending reinforcements.”

“Ner.”

“I’ll keep Darkheart sealed until you return.”

Zee nodded.

Then he looked toward the door.

Zayrlen stood there.

Waiting.

Neither spoke for several seconds.

The conversation from the rooftop lived between them.

Will you ever let me fight with you?

Yes.

When you earn it.

Zayrlen stepped forward.

“I am coming.”

Ner looked at Zee.

Not Zayrlen.

This decision belonged to both fathers.

Zee studied his son.

Really studied him.

Not the Dragon.

Not the Wolf.

Not the Mana.

Him.

Then:

“Why?”

Zayrlen frowned.

“Because they’re attacking our people.”

“Not enough.”

Zayrlen understood immediately.

He thought.

Then answered:

“Because you need fighters who can move between the ridge and the forest quickly. The road is narrow. Dragon form gives me air control, Wolf form gives me ground pursuit, and Witchcraft lets me hold routes without destroying the settlement.”

Zee’s expression did not change.

“Better.”

Zayrlen held his gaze.

“And because I asked you to trust my judgment.”

There.

Zee nodded once.

“Get your gear.”

Ner exhaled quietly.

Zayrlen did not move.

“That’s a yes?”

Zee stared.

“Do not make me change it.”

Zayrlen disappeared.

Rez smiled.

“That was almost sweet.”

Zee looked at him.

“No.”

⸻

Ner caught Zayrlen before he reached the armory.

Not to stop him.

To look at him.

Zayrlen slowed.

Ner adjusted the strap across his shoulder.

The same way he had once adjusted blankets around a baby.

Years changed scale.

Not instinct.

“You listen to your father.”

Zayrlen nodded.

“But?”

Ner touched his chest.

“You also listen to yourself.”

Zayrlen’s expression softened.

“I know.”

“You are not there to impress him.”

“I know.”

“You are not there to prove you are the strongest.”

“I know.”

“And you come home.”

Zayrlen smiled faintly.

“That one sounded less like advice.”

“It was not.”

Zee appeared at the end of the hall.

“Ready.”

Ner looked toward him.

Then back to Zayrlen.

“Go.”

Zayrlen did.

⸻

They rode only partway.

The ridge became too broken for horses.

Zee dismounted first.

Zayrlen followed.

Kael came with a strike group behind them.

Amara took the southern tree line.

Rhyven ranged ahead through the woods.

No grand army.

No ceremony.

A response.

Fast.

Direct.

The smell of smoke reached them before the settlement came into view.

Zayrlen’s Wolf senses opened.

Blood.

Fear.

Metal.

Pitch.

Magic.

Too much movement.

He looked toward Zee.

“Three groups.”

Zee glanced at him.

Zayrlen continued.

“One near the houses. One behind the grain store. One higher on the ridge.”

“How sure?”

“Very.”

“Good.”

Zee drew no weapon.

He rarely needed one.

Zayrlen’s Crimson-and-Gold Mana formed around his hands.

Zee looked at him once.

“Remember why you are here.”

Zayrlen nodded.

“Judgment.”

“Good.”

Then Zee vanished.

The battle began.

⸻

The first mercenary died before he understood Zee had arrived.

Vampire speed.

One hand through the chest.

Zee dropped the body and kept moving.

Two Witches turned.

“Hold.”

Both froze mid-cast.

Zee broke one neck.

The second tried screaming.

“Silence.”

Nothing came out.

Crimson Sovereign rolled through him with perfect control.

Wolf awareness.

Vampire speed.

Witch precision.

The Crimson Demonic Dragon burned beneath his skin.

Enemies felt him before they saw him.

Some ran.

Wrong.

Zayrlen entered differently.

He lifted both hands.

Crimson-and-Gold rings formed across the road.

Not one.

Seven.

Moving.

Rotating.

They snapped into position around the settlement entrances.

A Vampire tried crossing one.

The ring collapsed inward.

Crushed his momentum.

Threw him into a wall.

Zayrlen shifted left.

Mana threads spread between buildings like luminous wire.

Not traps for civilians.

Paths.

Channels.

Enemy movement redirected.

Friendly movement opened.

He was shaping the battlefield.

Not copying Zee.

Not trying.

A mercenary Wolf lunged at him.

Zayrlen dropped low.

Human one second.

Crimson-and-Gold Wolf the next.

Four-legged.

Fast.

He slipped beneath the strike.

Teeth caught the attacker’s forearm.

Twisted.

Released.

Kept moving.

No waste.

No flourish.

The Wolf disappeared behind a wagon.

A Human Zayrlen came out the other side with a Crimson-and-Gold spear already formed.

It struck a Witch through the shoulder and pinned her to the ground.

Zee saw it.

Did not comment.

Kept killing.

⸻

The attackers had prepared for resistance.

Not for them.

A group on the ridge released enchanted bolts downward.

Zayrlen looked up.

Dragon senses sharpened.

Air pressure.

Heat.

Trajectory.

He raised both arms.

A broad Crimson-and-Gold barrier unfolded over three houses.

Bolts struck.

The shield bent.

Held.

One bolt punched through the edge and sliced his upper arm.

Blood ran.

Zayrlen hissed.

Did not stop.

Zee smelled it immediately.

His head turned.

Only once.

Zayrlen saw the look.

“I’m fine!”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

Then another attacker came at him.

Zee killed the man instead of answering.

Good.

He was not babysitting.

Not during the battle.

⸻

The commander appeared ten minutes in.

At least Zayrlen assumed he was the commander.

Black armor.

Red sash.

Two curved blades.

Protected by four fighters.

He had been directing movement from the ridge.

Now the line had collapsed.

He turned.

Ran.

Directly toward the eastern cut.

Zayrlen saw him.

Everything inside the Wolf surged.

Target.

Responsible.

Fleeing.

Perfect.

He started after him.

One step.

Two.

Then stopped.

Something was wrong.

Not emotion.

Pattern.

The commander smelled afraid.

But not enough.

His stride was too even.

Too deliberate.

He kept looking forward.

Never back.

Someone actually fleeing Zee Darkheart checked behind them.

Always.

Zayrlen’s ears sharpened.

He listened.

The eastern cut should have held panicked movement.

Instead—

quiet.

Too quiet.

Then Dragon perception caught heat behind stone.

Six bodies.

Hidden.

Waiting.

And Witch Mana.

Buried beneath the path.

A constructed spell.

Triggered by weight.

Zayrlen’s own kind of battlefield magic.

Only cruder.

The commander wanted pursuit.

He wanted someone fast.

Someone angry.

Someone important.

Zayrlen almost smiled.

Bait.

⸻

He did not follow.

The commander’s scent changed.

Confusion.

Then irritation.

Good.

Zayrlen turned away.

That was when he saw the real threat.

Three attackers had slipped around the grain store.

Not toward Zee.

Toward the wounded.

Darkheart guards had moved injured civilians behind a stone wall.

The hidden team had waited for the battlefield to split.

One carried a Witch charge.

Enough force to collapse the entire structure.

Zayrlen moved.

No warning.

No shout.

No order from Zee.

He became Wolf.

Crimson-and-Gold tore across the ground.

One hidden attacker turned too late.

Zayrlen hit him at full speed.

Bones broke.

He shifted Human during the roll.

Mana threads snapped from both hands.

One wrapped the Witch charge.

Another wrapped the attacker’s wrist.

A third anchored to the stone wall.

The charge ignited.

Zayrlen’s eyes widened.

Not enough time to disarm.

So he changed the geometry.

Five Crimson-and-Gold rings formed around the device.

One inside another.

Each rotated opposite the last.

The explosion hit.

The rings folded inward.

Compressed the blast.

Directed it up.

Fire punched into the sky instead of through the wounded civilians.

The force still threw Zayrlen backward.

He hit the ground hard.

Ribs screamed.

One ear rang.

He rolled.

Got up.

The remaining two attackers charged.

Zayrlen did not shift Dragon.

Too large.

Too close to civilians.

Wrong tool.

Wolf again.

Fast.

Low.

First attacker lost a knee.

Human.

Mana spear through the second man’s thigh.

He dropped.

Zayrlen kicked the weapon away.

Then smelled another presence behind him.

He spun.

Too late.

A blade cut across his ribs.

Deep enough to bleed.

Not deep enough to stop him.

He caught the attacker’s wrist.

Headbutted him.

Then Crimson-and-Gold threads wrapped the man’s throat and arms.

He pulled.

The attacker slammed face-first into stone.

Done.

⸻

Across the battlefield, Zee saw everything.

The fleeing commander.

The eastern cut.

Zayrlen stopping.

Turning.

Choosing.

The explosion redirected into the sky.

The wounded civilians still alive.

The hidden attackers down.

Zee understood immediately.

His son had seen the bait.

Rejected it.

Found the real threat.

No order.

No warning.

No help.

Something inside Zee changed.

Not relief.

Not exactly.

Trust.

The thing he had refused to give cheaply.

The thing Zayrlen had wanted more than permission.

Zee met his son’s eyes across the smoke.

No smile.

No praise.

Just recognition.

Zayrlen understood.

Then Zee deliberately turned his back on him.

There were still enemies alive.

Still danger.

And Zee turned away.

Not carelessness.

Trust.

My son has this side.

Zayrlen felt it hit harder than any compliment could have.

He straightened.

Then went back to work.

⸻

The commander realized the bait had failed.

He ran harder.

Zee appeared in front of him.

The man stopped so suddenly his boots dragged through mud.

Zee looked at him.

“You were leaving.”

The commander raised both blades.

Zee smiled slightly.

“That was rude.”

They fought for less than twenty seconds.

The commander was good.

Very good.

Silver edges.

Anti-Vampire coating.

Witch resistance charms.

Wolf irritant oil.

Someone who had prepared for monsters.

Not enough.

Zee let one blade glance off Dragon scales.

Caught the wrist.

Broke it.

The second blade came up.

“Down.”

The man’s knees hit earth.

Zee’s hand closed around his throat.

The commander spat blood.

“You protect animals and mixed-blood filth.”

There it was.

Reason.

Verified.

Zee’s expression emptied.

“They are mine.”

Then he crushed the man’s throat.

No speech.

No mercy.

Done.

⸻

The remaining attackers broke.

Some tried the forest.

That belonged to Zayrlen.

Zee did not even look.

He trusted him now.

The young Prince shifted into his Crimson-and-Gold Wolf form and disappeared between the trees.

Ground pursuit.

Scent.

Speed.

Three fleeing mercenaries split.

Zayrlen tracked the one carrying blood from a wounded Darkheart guard.

Priority.

The others were moving away from civilians.

He chose the dangerous one.

Correct.

The mercenary doubled back suddenly.

Zayrlen anticipated it.

Shifted Human beneath the swing.

Mana ring closed around the man’s weapon hand.

Another around the ankle.

Pulled opposite directions.

The attacker hit the ground.

Zayrlen stood over him.

The man reached for a hidden knife.

Zayrlen smelled the movement before he saw it.

Crimson-and-Gold spear.

Straight through the chest.

Then silence.

⸻

Above the ridge, two enemy Witches escaped onto a rock shelf.

Zayrlen looked up.

Different problem.

Wolf would take too long.

He shifted.

Dragon.

Crimson-and-Gold power erupted through the trees.

Wings opened.

Massive.

True.

No Phoenix.

No demonic corruption.

His Dragon form launched upward.

The surviving attackers looked back.

Regretted everything.

Zayrlen circled once.

Not to show off.

To see.

Wind.

Terrain.

Friendly positions.

Then Crimson-and-Gold Witch rings formed around his wings.

He drove downward.

One barrier blocked the enemy’s escape path.

Three Mana spears forced them apart.

A wingbeat knocked one off balance.

The second fired a spell.

Zayrlen took it across the shoulder.

Scales cracked.

Pain.

He roared.

Did not lose himself.

He slammed one claw into the shelf.

Stone fractured.

Not beneath the civilians.

Not beneath allies.

Only under the attackers.

They fell into the lower clearing.

Zee was waiting.

One landed at his feet.

Zee looked down.

Then up at the Dragon.

Zayrlen understood.

Your problem now.

Zee killed the man.

The second tried crawling.

Kael ended that one.

⸻

Then father and son finally moved together.

Not planned.

Natural.

Zee tore through the center.

Zayrlen controlled the edges.

Crimson one-word laws cut openings.

Crimson-and-Gold structures turned those openings into cages.

“Break.”

An enemy barrier shattered.

Zayrlen’s rings flowed through the gap before the fragments hit ground.

Three mercenaries trapped.

Zee entered.

Finished them.

A Witch launched a chained curse toward Zee’s blind side.

Zayrlen saw it from above.

Mana thread caught the spell.

Pulled its path two feet wide.

That was enough.

Zee turned.

“Silence.”

The Witch’s follow-up died in her throat.

Zayrlen landed behind her in Human form.

One spear.

Done.

Another group rushed from the road.

Zee stepped toward them.

Zayrlen shifted Wolf and came around the flank.

They never coordinated verbally.

Did not need to.

Zee forced the center.

Zayrlen broke the edges.

One monster.

One young hunter.

Different.

Complementary.

Terrifying.

For a brief moment the enemy understood exactly what Darkheart had become.

Not one impossible King.

A bloodline capable of standing beside him.

⸻

The final attacker dropped his weapon.

Too late.

He had spent the morning dragging Darkheart civilians from homes.

Zee knew.

Zayrlen knew.

The man looked at the teenager first.

Maybe hoping youth meant softness.

“Please.”

Zayrlen looked toward Zee.

Not for permission.

For understanding.

Zee said nothing.

Zayrlen looked back at the man.

“You had people tied inside that house.”

The attacker swallowed.

“Yes.”

“You set it on fire.”

Silence.

Zayrlen’s expression hardened.

The man backed up.

Zee moved.

One hand.

One strike.

The attacker dropped.

Dead.

Zayrlen watched.

No flinch.

No horror.

He knew his father.

Dad at home.

King here.

Monster to the people who harmed theirs.

Protector to everyone behind him.

The contradiction had stopped being a contradiction years ago.

⸻

The battlefield went quiet.

Smoke.

Blood.

Broken stone.

Wounded people alive because Zayrlen had chosen correctly.

Zee scanned the area once.

Threats gone.

Then battle-Zee vanished.

Completely.

He turned.

Found Zayrlen.

Walked straight toward him.

Zayrlen saw the expression and immediately knew what was coming.

“Oh, no.”

Zee reached him.

“Where are you hurt?”

“I am not.”

Zee grabbed his chin.

“That was not the question.”

“Dad.”

“Zayrlen.”

“I am fine.”

“You are bleeding.”

“It is barely—”

Zee lifted his shirt.

Zayrlen slapped his hand down.

“Can you not do this in front of everyone?”

“No.”

Kael turned away to hide a smile.

Amara did not bother.

Zee inspected the cut across Zayrlen’s ribs.

Then the upper arm.

Then the cracked scale line along the shoulder from Dragon form.

Zayrlen groaned.

“I fought an entire battle.”

“Yes.”

“I became a Dragon.”

“I noticed.”

“I stopped an explosive.”

“I noticed.”

“I killed people.”

“I noticed.”

“And now you’re checking my ribs.”

“Yes.”

Zayrlen stared.

Zee stared back.

Teddy Bear Dad had returned with absolute authority.

⸻

Ner arrived with the second reinforcement group before Zee finished checking him.

He dismounted.

Saw Zayrlen standing.

Alive.

Bloody.

Then looked at Zee.

“Well?”

Zee said:

“He is hurt.”

Zayrlen said:

“I am fine.”

Ner walked over.

Looked at the rib wound.

Then at Zayrlen.

“You are hurt.”

Zayrlen looked betrayed.

“Not you too.”

Ner touched his face once.

Just once.

Then stepped back.

“Did he do well?”

Zee looked toward his son.

The question changed the air.

Zayrlen went quiet.

No jokes now.

No irritation.

No pride.

Just waiting.

The same look, somehow, as a child curled against Zee after his first Wolf transformation.

Did I do good?

Years disappeared for one second.

Zayrlen finally asked:

“Did I prove it?”

Zee did not tease him.

Did not make him wait.

“Yes.”

Clean.

Certain.

Zayrlen’s face changed.

Zee continued.

“You saw the commander.”

“I did.”

“You wanted him.”

“Yes.”

“You knew he was bait.”

“Yes.”

“And you left him.”

Zayrlen nodded.

“There were people behind the grain store.”

“I know.”

“I thought if I chased him—”

“You made the right call.”

Zayrlen went still.

Zee stepped closer.

“You protected the battlefield instead of feeding your ego.”

A pause.

“You made the decision without me.”

Another.

“And when I turned my back—”

Zayrlen smiled faintly.

“I knew.”

Zee nodded.

“I know.”

That was the answer.

The blanket refusal ended there.

Not every mission.

Not every war.

Not every danger.

But no longer:

You are not ready.

Zayrlen had earned his place.

⸻

Later, as Darkheart soldiers secured the road, Zee and Zayrlen stood on the ridge together.

Zayrlen looked down at the settlement.

Smoke still rose.

People were alive.

That mattered more now.

He understood why Zee had spoken so much about judgment.

Power had been the easiest part.

The harder thing had been knowing where not to use it.

Zee stood beside him.

Zayrlen glanced over.

“So I can come next time?”

Zee looked at him.

“Appropriate missions.”

Zayrlen sighed.

“There it is.”

“I am still your father.”

“I know.”

“And King.”

“I know.”

“And if I say no—”

“I know.”

Zee narrowed his eyes.

Zayrlen smiled.

“But sometimes you’ll say yes.”

Zee looked back over the valley.

“Yes.”

That was enough.

⸻

Zayrlen leaned against the stone railing.

Older.

Stronger.

Two true forms.

His own Witchcraft.

His own instincts.

His own judgment.

Zee looked at him.

And saw everything at once.

The baby gripping his horn.

The toddler running toward him instead of Ner.

The little boy with a scraped knee.

The Crimson-and-Gold Wolf lost in the rain.

The frightened teenager saying:

Dad—I can’t stop it.

The enormous Dragon lowering its head so Zee could touch its snout.

And now—

the fighter who had stood beside him.

Not behind him.

Not protected from the world.

Beside him.

Zee had spent most of his life becoming impossible to kill.

Learning how to destroy threats.

Learning how to protect what belonged to him.

This had required something harder.

Letting go enough to trust.

Zayrlen looked over.

“What?”

Zee shook his head.

“Nothing.”

“You are staring.”

“I can stare at my son.”

“You do it weird.”

“Disrespectful.”

Zayrlen laughed.

Zee reached over.

Pulled him closer by the back of the neck.

Zayrlen immediately protested.

“Dad.”

“What?”

“I just fought beside you.”

“Yes.”

“I am covered in enemy blood.”

“Yes.”

“I became a Dragon over a battlefield.”

“Yes.”

“You cannot keep doing this.”

Zee looked at him.

“Watch me.”

Zayrlen groaned.

Ner laughed behind them.

And Zee smiled.

The warrior had earned his trust.

The son had earned his place.

But the child—

the one Zee had carried, protected, spoiled, taught, feared for, and loved from his first breath—

never disappeared.

That was the final truth.

Zayrlen Keal Darkheart had become strong enough and wise enough to stand beside his father in battle.

And Zee Darkheart had finally become strong enough as a father to let him.

The monster trusted his son.

The son proved that trust deserved to exist.

And when they went home—

Zee was still going to make Tessa check his ribs.
