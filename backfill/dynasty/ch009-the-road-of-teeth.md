Chapter Nine

The Road of Teeth

The western road had been abandoned before Aurelia ever reached it.

That was the first thing Captain Vale noticed.

No merchants.

No pilgrims.

No wagons.

No patrols from nearby villages.

Only wheel ruts hardened in old mud and a line of overturned carts left beneath a stand of black pines.

Caldrin crouched beside one of them.

“Too clean.”

Aurelia shifted Zee higher against her shoulder.

“What does that mean?”

“No bodies.”

The royal witch scanned the tree line.

“Maybe whoever attacked them took the dead.”

Caldrin stood.

“Maybe.”

The crimson hatchling pressed against Zee beneath Aurelia’s cloak.

The two had grown quieter since leaving Castle Darkheart.

Not calmer.

More aware.

The Dragon Link had begun carrying new flashes between them.

Not words.

Shapes.

Cold.

Movement.

The direction of fear.

Zee turned his head toward the forest.

The hatchling did the same.

Vale saw it.

“You both felt something?”

The hatchling’s scales tightened along his spine.

Zee’s Crimson Mana stirred.

Caldrin drew his sword.

That was answer enough.

Something moved between the trees.

Fast.

Low.

Then another shape crossed behind it.

Aurelia stopped.

“How many?”

Vale whispered, “Pack.”

The first wolf stepped onto the road.

It was enormous.

Larger than any natural wolf.

Its fur was gray-black and soaked with old blood. One ear had been torn away. Its yellow eyes fixed on Zee before moving to the dragon hatchling.

Then came the second.

Third.

Sixth.

Tenth.

The pack spread across the road and tree line until retreat vanished.

The royal witch raised both hands.

Violet mana gathered around her wrists.

“Werewolves?”

Vale studied them.

“No.”

Caldrin frowned.

“Then what?”

Vale’s expression darkened.

“Wildbloods.”

The alpha growled.

A sound answered from deeper in the forest.

Dozens more.

Aurelia tightened her hold around Zee.

“Explain quickly.”

“Wolves born too close to old magic,” Vale said. “Not shifters. Not beasts anymore either.”

The alpha took one step forward.

Then stopped.

Its nostrils flared.

It smelled Zee.

Something passed through the entire pack.

Confusion.

Hunger.

Fear.

The wolves lowered their bodies.

Not submission.

Preparation.

The attack came all at once.

Vale met the first wolf with his sword.

Steel cut across its shoulder.

The wound barely slowed it.

Caldrin drove his blade through another’s jaw.

The witch released a violet shockwave that threw three wolves into the trees.

More replaced them.

Aurelia backed toward the overturned carts.

The hatchling climbed from beneath her cloak.

“No.”

He ignored her.

Tiny crimson wings spread.

Teal-and-gold mana crawled along his scales.

Zee felt the intention.

Protect.

He reached toward the hatchling.

The Dragon Link answered with something simple.

Together.

A wolf leaped.

The hatchling screamed.

Teal-and-gold fire burst from his mouth.

Not large.

Not controlled.

But hot enough to strike the wolf across the face.

It crashed into the road, smoking.

The pack recoiled.

For one moment.

Then the alpha charged.

Vale moved to intercept.

Too slow.

The wolf hit him like a battering ram and threw him into a tree.

Aurelia turned.

The alpha came for Zee.

Zee stared at it.

His crimson eyes widened.

The wolf scent entered him.

Predator.

Pack.

Territory.

Threat.

His own wolf nature answered.

Not with transformation.

With instinct.

A low growl came from the newborn’s chest.

Every Wildblood stopped.

The sound was too small to matter.

Yet it did.

The alpha’s ears flattened.

Zee growled again.

His Crimson Mana spread into the air.

The pack began whining.

Aurelia stared.

The alpha fought whatever instinct had seized it.

Then lunged anyway.

The hatchling attacked first.

He slammed into the wolf’s muzzle and bit down.

The alpha shook violently.

The hatchling flew sideways.

Zee screamed.

The Dragon Link flooded with pain.

The world around him narrowed.

He could feel the hatchling’s fear.

His anger answered before thought.

Crimson Mana erupted from Zee’s body.

Not as a wave.

As pressure.

The alpha collapsed mid-step.

Every wolf on the road dropped with it.

No bones broke.

No blood spilled.

They simply flattened against the earth as though the air itself had become too heavy to resist.

The witch stared.

Vale pushed himself upright.

Caldrin stopped fighting.

Aurelia looked down at her son.

Zee was crying now.

Furious.

The hatchling crawled back toward him.

The moment their bodies touched, the pressure vanished.

The wolves gasped.

Several immediately fled.

Others limped backward.

The alpha remained.

Its eyes fixed on Zee.

Not hunger anymore.

Recognition.

Something in the newborn’s scent had changed.

Or perhaps the wolf had simply noticed what it had ignored before.

Zee was not prey.

The alpha lowered its head.

Then turned away.

The rest of the pack followed.

Within moments, the road was empty again.

Caldrin stared after them.

“I preferred the dead men in the crypt.”

Vale coughed.

“You say that now.”

Aurelia ignored both of them.

She looked at Zee.

“What did you do?”

The child hiccuped.

The hatchling nudged his cheek.

Safe.

Zee relaxed.

The royal witch approached carefully.

“My Queen…”

Aurelia glanced at her.

The woman corrected herself immediately.

“I do not know.”

“Good.”

The witch almost smiled.

Almost.

Vale walked toward the place where the alpha had fallen.

He crouched and touched the earth.

No spell mark.

No scorch.

Nothing.

Only deep impressions where claws had dug into the road.

“He did not cast.”

Caldrin looked at Zee.

“No.”

Vale stood.

“He commanded.”

Aurelia’s expression hardened.

“You do not know that either.”

“No.”

Vale sheathed his sword.

“But the wolves believed he did.”

That mattered.

Perhaps too much.

⸻

The road grew worse by afternoon.

They left it.

Caldrin led them into the foothills where ancient hunting paths wound between dark trees and limestone ridges.

Aurelia’s body began failing before sunset.

She refused to admit it.

The royal witch noticed anyway.

“You need rest.”

“We need distance.”

“You gave birth days ago.”

“I remember.”

“You were wounded.”

“I remember that too.”

“You cannot keep walking.”

Aurelia stopped.

Her eyes hardened.

“Then carry me when I fall.”

The witch said nothing after that.

Zee slept.

The hatchling did not.

He rode against Aurelia’s shoulder, staring constantly into the woods.

Something had changed after the Wildblood attack.

His protectiveness had become sharper.

More deliberate.

He had felt Zee die once.

Now every danger seemed to carry the memory of absence.

The Dragon Link repeated one instinct again and again.

Stay close.

Zee answered even while sleeping.

Warmth.

Near.

The bond quieted.

Caldrin stopped suddenly.

Everyone froze.

Vale whispered, “What?”

Caldrin pointed toward the ground.

A footprint.

Human.

Then another.

Fresh.

Vale followed the trail with his eyes.

“Not ours.”

The witch lowered her voice.

“Soldiers?”

Caldrin shook his head.

“Too light.”

“Scouts?”

“No armor marks.”

Aurelia looked toward the trees.

“Assassins.”

Caldrin smiled without humor.

“Probably.”

The first arrow came from above.

It struck the witch’s barrier.

Violet light flashed.

“Down!”

Vale pulled Aurelia behind a rock.

Three more arrows hit the barrier.

The fourth passed through.

Not because the shield failed.

Because the arrowhead carried a seal etched into black metal.

It disrupted the spell on contact.

The witch stared.

“Anti-magic iron.”

Caldrin cursed.

Figures appeared along the ridge.

Six.

No banners.

No armor.

Mercenaries.

One raised a crossbow.

“Alive if possible!”

Another called back.

“The queen too?”

“Not required!”

Aurelia’s face went cold.

The bounty had already traveled.

Faster than expected.

Vale drew his sword.

“They are not soldiers.”

Caldrin unsheathed both blades.

“That makes them worse.”

The mercenaries descended.

They knew what they were doing.

One targeted the witch.

Two went for Vale.

Two spread wide to cut off Caldrin.

The last stayed behind with the crossbow.

No one charged Aurelia directly.

Not yet.

They wanted the defenders separated first.

The witch cast.

Violet force struck one mercenary.

He threw a black metal disk into the ground.

Her spell collapsed around it.

Caldrin saw.

“Runes!”

“They prepared for witches!”

“Of course they did.”

The world was already learning.

That frightened Aurelia more than the attack itself.

These men had heard reports.

They had adapted.

The next hunters would know even more.

Vale blocked a sword strike.

Caldrin buried a blade beneath another mercenary’s ribs.

The witch tried a different spell.

The black disk disrupted that too.

The crossbowman aimed.

At Zee.

Aurelia saw it.

“No!”

The bolt fired.

The hatchling reacted.

Teal-and-gold mana flashed.

He spread his wings in front of Zee.

The bolt struck him.

The tiny dragon screamed.

Aurelia screamed with him.

The projectile had pierced one wing.

The Dragon Link flooded Zee with pain.

Sharp.

Hot.

Wrong.

Zee woke instantly.

His crimson eyes opened.

The hatchling fell from Aurelia’s shoulder.

Blood stained his crimson scales.

Zee stared.

The mercenary reloaded.

Something inside Zee shifted.

Not power arriving from nowhere.

Instinct finding a path.

His vampire senses sharpened.

He heard the crossbow string tighten.

He heard the man’s heartbeat.

He smelled blood from the wound in Zareth’s wing.

He smelled every living body around him.

Six rhythms.

Five.

Four.

His perception narrowed until the forest seemed suddenly too slow.

Crimson Mana gathered around his eyes.

The crossbowman fired again.

Zee turned his head.

The bolt stopped inches from his face.

Not suspended by force this time.

Caught.

Aurelia stared.

Zee’s tiny hand was wrapped around the shaft.

No newborn should have possessed the reflexes.

No newborn should have possessed the grip.

The mercenary stared too.

That hesitation killed him.

Caldrin’s throwing knife struck his throat.

He fell.

The remaining mercenaries broke.

One ran.

Vale caught him.

Another surrendered.

Caldrin did not care.

The witch turned toward the hatchling.

“Let me see him.”

Zee began crying.

The sound was different.

Not fear.

Rage.

The hatchling’s blood touched Zee’s hand.

Crimson Mana reacted immediately.

It crawled toward the wound.

The witch stopped.

“My Queen.”

Aurelia looked.

Teal-and-gold Dragon Mana flickered around the damaged wing.

Zee’s Crimson Mana touched it.

For a moment, the two powers met.

They did not merge.

They did not become the same.

The teal-and-gold remained Zareth’s.

The crimson remained Zee’s.

But Zee reacted to what he felt through the Link.

Pain.

Torn flesh.

Heal.

His mana tightened around his own fingers.

Something inside his blood seemed to study the injury without understanding it.

Then the hatchling’s wound began closing faster.

Not instantly.

Not completely.

But faster than before.

The witch stared.

Aurelia looked at Zee.

He had not healed Zareth.

Not exactly.

Something about their bond had pushed his own dragon vitality harder.

The hatchling’s breathing steadied.

Zee stopped crying.

Safe?

The hatchling nudged his hand.

Safe.

Caldrin approached with blood on both swords.

“You should see this.”

Aurelia looked toward him.

One mercenary remained alive.

Vale held him on his knees.

The man was young.

Younger than Aurelia expected.

Terrified.

Caldrin pulled a folded parchment from his coat.

A bounty notice.

A rough sketch of Zee covered the center.

Crimson eyes.

Fangs.

A dragon beside him.

Below it:

THE DARKHEART TRIBRID

TWENTY THOUSAND SILVER CROWNS ALIVE

TEN THOUSAND DEAD

ADDITIONAL PAYMENT FOR BLOOD, TEETH, OR CONFIRMED MAGICAL REMAINS

Aurelia stopped breathing.

Vale read over her shoulder.

His face changed.

“Who issued this?”

The mercenary spat blood.

“Does it matter?”

Caldrin pressed a blade beneath his chin.

“Yes.”

The man laughed nervously.

“Half the continent will issue one by winter.”

Aurelia stepped forward.

“Who paid you?”

The mercenary looked at Zee.

Fear crossed his face.

Not because Zee threatened him.

Because the child existed.

“A broker in Red Hollow.”

“Working for whom?”

“I do not know.”

Caldrin increased pressure.

“You expect us to believe that?”

“I swear.”

The man swallowed.

“Contracts are being sold everywhere.”

“Kings?”

“Some.”

“Witches?”

“Yes.”

“Vampires?”

“Yes.”

“Wolves?”

The mercenary hesitated.

“Some packs are paying for information.”

Vale’s expression darkened.

Aurelia looked at the parchment again.

Three days old.

Zee was three days old.

And the world had already turned his body into currency.

She tore the bounty in half.

Then again.

And again.

The pieces fell into the dirt.

“Let him go.”

Caldrin looked at her.

“My Queen?”

“Let him go.”

Vale frowned.

“He knows our direction.”

“Exactly.”

Aurelia stepped toward the mercenary.

The man recoiled.

She looked down at him.

“You will go back.”

“To whom?”

“Everyone.”

Her voice remained calm.

“You will tell every broker, king, witch, vampire, hunter, and bastard who thinks my son is worth silver that they failed.”

The mercenary stared.

Aurelia continued.

“And tell them something else.”

Her eyes hardened.

“The price is too low.”

Caldrin smiled.

Vale released the man.

He ran.

No one stopped him.

The story would travel.

Not accurately.

Stories never did.

But it would travel.

The Tribrid lived.

The dragon lived.

The first bounty had failed.

And whoever came next would come prepared for what the mercenaries had learned.

⸻

That night, they made camp inside the ruins of an abandoned shrine.

No fire.

No songs.

No guards standing openly.

Only darkness.

The witch slept first.

Vale watched the eastern approach.

Caldrin watched the west.

Aurelia sat against a broken statue with Zee in her arms and the hatchling curled across her lap.

The injury in his wing had nearly closed.

Too quickly.

Aurelia touched the repaired membrane gently.

The hatchling opened one eye.

“You almost died for him.”

The tiny dragon blinked.

“You are both insane.”

He chirped.

Zee stirred.

Aurelia smiled despite herself.

For a few breaths, there was peace.

Then Zee’s eyes opened.

He looked toward the darkness outside the shrine.

His vampire hearing caught something distant.

A heartbeat.

Then another.

Then another.

Not human.

Too slow.

Too deep.

He did not understand what he heard.

But the wolf inside him recognized the scent carried by the wind.

Predator.

The hatchling lifted his head.

The Dragon Link tightened.

Danger.

Aurelia saw both infants react.

Her smile vanished.

“Vale.”

The captain stood immediately.

“What?”

Caldrin rose too.

The royal witch woke.

Aurelia looked toward the tree line.

“We are not alone.”

A growl answered from the darkness.

Not wolf.

Not Wildblood.

Something larger.

Two pale eyes opened between the trees.

Then four more.

Then eight.

Caldrin drew his sword.

“I suppose sleeping was too ambitious.”

Vale stepped in front of Aurelia.

The creatures began moving toward the shrine.

Zee watched them come.

Three days old.

Already hunted by kingdoms.

Already hunted by mercenaries.

Now hunted by something with no interest in money at all.

And beneath his skin, the First Natural Tribrid remembered every danger that had touched him.

The world had learned to hunt differently.

So had Zee.
