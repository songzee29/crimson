Chapter Fifty-Eight

The Empty Place

Aurelia’s body remained where she had fallen until Zee moved it himself.

No servant touched her.

No healer suggested another examination.

No witch whispered about impossible rituals.

Everyone in the shattered western hall understood the truth.

Queen Aurelia Darkheart was dead.

Zee knelt beside her in silence while Vesper burned around him.

Not the kingdom.

Only pieces.

A tower.

Two corridors.

The western courtyard where Zareth had fought the Dragon killer.

Stone still smoked beneath Teal-and-Gold fire.

Blood covered the floor in six separate trails that all ended the same way.

Empty bodies.

The Human.

The Vampire.

The Witch.

The Wolf.

The Dragon.

The Demon.

Zee had drained them all.

Their blood remained inside him.

Not quietly.

Human blood had already disappeared into hunger.

Normal nourishment.

The others had not.

Vampire blood moved like cold pressure beneath his ribs.

Wolf blood sharpened every scent in the palace until Zee could separate fear from grief several rooms away.

Witch blood made Crimson Mana pulse strangely around his veins, as though magic had begun recognizing his blood as something more important than a vessel carrying it.

Dragon blood burned deepest.

Not awakening.

Not completion.

But his horns had remained hot for hours.

The ridges beneath his shoulder blades ached continuously.

Demon blood was worse.

It had settled nowhere.

Zee felt it whenever shadows moved incorrectly.

Whenever something supernatural occupied space without blood or heartbeat.

His body had taken what entered it and begun rewriting.

No powers copied.

No чужой magic sitting ready to use.

Only Zee changing around what he had survived.

Normally he would have been fascinated.

Tonight he did not care.

He lifted Aurelia.

Carefully.

One arm beneath her knees.

One beneath her shoulders.

At fifteen, Zee could carry enormous weight.

His mother felt impossibly heavy.

Rez stood near the ruined doorway.

His sword remained bloody.

He said nothing.

Ner sat against a pillar beneath blankets, alive enough to make Zee keep checking his heartbeat every few seconds.

Every time.

Beat.

Beat.

Beat.

Still there.

Zareth crouched outside the broken wall.

The Dragon Link had become a continuous ache.

No language.

No images.

Only grief passing between them until Zee no longer knew which parts belonged to whom.

Theron reached the hall first.

Then Seraphine.

Theron stopped.

His face emptied.

Seraphine made one sound.

Small.

Then crossed the room.

“No.”

Zee held Aurelia tighter.

Seraphine looked at her sister.

At the bolt still buried beneath the ribs because removing it no longer mattered.

At Aurelia’s face.

Then at Zee.

“What happened?”

Zee answered without emotion.

“Seven came.”

Theron’s Dragon scales appeared across both hands.

“Who?”

“Six are dead.”

“And the seventh?”

Zee looked toward the open sky.

“Gone.”

Something in his voice made Rez glance at him.

Not rage.

Promise.

Theron stepped toward Aurelia.

His hand trembled before touching her hair.

For five years he had lived with the miracle of having his sister back.

Five years.

Not enough.

Never enough.

Seraphine’s face crumpled.

She turned away.

Then back.

Then struck the nearest broken table so hard her hand split.

No one stopped her.

Zee understood.

He wanted to hit something too.

Instead he carried Aurelia from the room.

That frightened Rez more.

⸻

The funeral happened the next morning.

Not because grief had finished.

Because Aurelia would have hated being left beneath preservation spells while kingdoms debated proper ceremony.

Seraphine said that.

Theron agreed.

Zee made the decision.

No foreign crowns were invited.

No delegations.

No political spectacle.

House Vesper gathered.

House Veyrath gathered.

House Darkheart was informed.

Whether Victor came was not Zee’s concern.

He did not.

Zee noticed.

Nothing more.

Aurelia was placed beneath an open sky in Vesper’s oldest garden.

Not buried yet.

Veyrath tradition required flame first.

Dragon-Witch flame.

Theron stood on one side.

Seraphine on the other.

Zareth beyond them.

Zee stood closest.

The sky was clear.

Too clear.

He hated it.

People gathered behind him.

Lucien.

Rez.

Ner, despite every healer telling him to remain inside.

Vale.

Caldrin.

Veyrath survivors.

Vesper witches.

Servants who had known Aurelia since Zee was small.

People Zee did not remember seeing before.

Aurelia had apparently belonged to more lives than his.

That felt unfair.

Zee had always known she was more than his mother.

Knowing and accepting were different.

Theron’s Dragon energy appeared first.

Dark red.

Seraphine joined.

Her Veyrath power burned lighter, almost crimson-violet.

Zareth lowered his head.

Teal-and-Gold Dragonfire gathered behind his teeth.

Then everyone looked at Zee.

He did not have Dragonfire.

Not yet.

No wings.

No full Dragon form.

No completed fourth awakening.

For one bitter heartbeat he hated his unfinished nature.

Then Crimson Mana rose around his hands.

Witch.

His.

Vampire blood pulsed beneath it.

Wolf instinct held him steady.

Dragon heat answered from within.

Four natures.

Three awake.

One still becoming.

Zee placed his palm against the stone beneath Aurelia.

Crimson energy entered it.

Not Dragonfire.

Not pretending.

Just him.

“Do it.”

Three flames came.

Theron.

Seraphine.

Zareth.

The bier ignited.

Zee did not look away.

He smelled everything.

Wood.

Her clothes.

Dragon blood.

Witchcraft.

Smoke.

Death.

His Vampire nature cataloged details he would have given anything not to know.

He forced himself to remain.

Aurelia had asked him not to be angry with her.

He was.

Furious.

Not because she saved him.

Because she had been allowed to die at all.

He understood the difference.

He did not forgive the world for it.

Ner moved beside him.

Not touching at first.

Then his fingers brushed Zee’s.

Zee looked down.

Ner’s hand was cold.

Too cold.

His pulse—

Different.

Not dead.

Different.

Vampire.

Zee smelled it now.

His own blood inside Ner had changed.

Not remained Zee’s.

Changed.

Golden Witch Mana lived beside something darker in Ner’s veins.

And underneath both—

another scent.

Saltwater without ocean.

Song without sound.

Something old.

Zee stared.

Ner noticed.

“What?”

“Your blood.”

Ner’s face tightened.

“Bad?”

“I do not know.”

“Excellent.”

Zee almost smiled.

Almost.

Then Aurelia’s fire cracked.

The smile died.

Ner squeezed his hand once.

Not replacing anyone.

Never.

Simply there.

Zee allowed it.

⸻

By evening Vesper had already begun moving again.

That was the cruelest part of death.

Servants still needed food.

Walls still needed repairs.

Lucien still had decisions.

Rez still had reports.

Zareth still had Drakonis.

Ner still had whatever Zee had accidentally made him.

The world refused to stop because Zee’s mother had.

Zee sat inside Aurelia’s private solar alone.

No one had entered since her death.

Her book remained beside the chair.

A half-finished letter sat at the desk.

One hairpin near the mirror.

A cloak over the back of another chair.

Everything still expected her.

Zee looked at the letter.

Did not touch it.

He could read it later.

Or never.

Both were allowed.

A soft step entered behind him.

Too soft for an ordinary person.

Zee drew a knife.

Stopped it against Ner’s throat.

Again.

This time Ner did not flinch.

Dark-red flashed beneath his golden irises.

“Seriously?”

Zee lowered the blade.

“You are quieter.”

“So are you.”

Ner entered.

Zee watched automatically.

Weight distribution.

Hands.

Distance.

Throat.

Heart.

Weak points.

Then hated that his mind did it to Ner.

Not enough to make it stop.

Nanda Parbat had changed his instincts permanently.

Ner sat across from him.

For several seconds neither spoke.

Then Ner said:

“I can hear blood.”

Zee looked up.

“What?”

“I know how that sounds.”

“Bad.”

“Yes.”

Ner placed one hand over his chest.

“Heartbeats are different now.”

“Vampire hearing.”

“Not only.”

His expression tightened.

“When someone bleeds…”

He hesitated.

Zee waited.

Ner had earned that.

“It feels loud.”

“How?”

“I do not know.”

Good.

Ner continued.

“Like the blood is carrying something.”

“Emotion?”

“Maybe.”

“Magic?”

“Sometimes.”

Zee leaned forward.

“Your voice?”

Ner looked at him.

“You saw.”

“Everyone saw.”

Ner closed his eyes.

“When I made that sound…”

“The blood moved.”

“Yes.”

“Can you do it again?”

Ner opened his eyes.

“No.”

Immediate.

Zee stopped.

Ner looked relieved he did not push.

“I do not know what I am.”

“You are Ner.”

Ner stared.

Then laughed once.

“Your mother said something like that to you.”

Zee went still.

The room changed.

Ner regretted it immediately.

“Zee—”

“No.”

Zee looked toward Aurelia’s empty chair.

“She did.”

Silence.

Then he looked back.

“And she was right.”

Ner’s expression softened.

Zee continued.

“You are still you.”

“Something happened.”

“Yes.”

“Because of your blood.”

“Yes.”

“Do you know what?”

“No.”

That word again.

Zee was beginning to appreciate it.

Not knowing prevented stupid certainty.

Ner looked down.

“I have fangs.”

Zee’s mouth twitched.

“I noticed.”

“I was hoping nobody would.”

“They are difficult to miss.”

“They are not always there.”

“Neither are mine.”

Ner looked offended.

“You are enjoying this.”

“A little.”

There.

A tiny piece of himself.

Still alive.

Ner smiled.

Then something sharpened in his face.

“Hunger.”

Zee stopped smiling.

“Yes.”

Ner whispered:

“I hate it.”

Zee knew.

Not the same way.

But enough.

“Blood?”

Ner nodded.

Zee looked toward his own wrist.

Ner saw.

“No.”

“I was not offering.”

“You looked.”

“I look at everything now.”

“That is creepy.”

“Yes.”

No shame.

Ner noticed that too.

Zee stood.

“We find another way.”

Ner looked up.

“What way?”

“I do not know.”

“There it is again.”

Zee smiled faintly.

Then opened the door.

“Come.”

“Where?”

“Hunting.”

Ner stared.

“What?”

“Not people.”

“That clarification came too late.”

Zee almost laughed.

⸻

They entered Vesper’s western forest under moonlight.

Rez followed because he discovered them leaving.

He complained for ten minutes.

Then stopped when he realized Ner’s hunger was worsening.

Zareth followed through the Link and arrived from the outer field without being asked.

No Aurelia.

The absence walked with them anyway.

Zee tracked a deer.

Wolf instinct first.

Scent.

Broken fern.

Hoof mark.

Heat on bark where fur had brushed.

Then Vampire blood sense found the pulse.

He approached silently.

Nanda Parbat removed his footsteps.

The deer never saw him.

Zee could have killed it.

He did not.

Ner stood behind him breathing too fast.

“Can you feed without killing?”

Ner looked horrified.

“I do not know.”

Rez folded his arms.

“Then we find out carefully.”

Zee caught the deer.

Not with magic.

Vampire speed.

Wolf grip.

Controlled strength.

He held it while Ner approached.

Ner’s fangs descended.

His face changed.

Hunger.

Fear.

Disgust.

Zee understood all three.

“Small.”

Ner nodded.

Bit.

The deer struggled.

Zee held it steady.

Ner drank.

One swallow.

Then two.

His Pure Golden Mana flashed.

The forest changed.

Every nearby heartbeat echoed.

Zee heard something else with it.

A low note beneath Ner’s breathing.

Blood in the deer responded.

Flowing toward the bite more easily.

Not compelled exactly.

Resonating.

Ner jerked away.

“Enough.”

Zee released the deer.

It ran.

Alive.

Ner wiped his mouth.

His eyes glowed Gold and dark-red together.

Rez stared.

“That is new.”

Ner glared.

“Useful.”

“I did not say bad.”

Zareth lowered his head.

The Link carried fascination.

Zee looked at Ner’s blood scent again.

Witch.

Vampire.

Something singing underneath.

No name.

Not yet.

But real.

Ner breathed.

“I feel better.”

Good.

Zee nodded.

Then smelled something else.

Blood.

Not deer.

Human.

Fresh.

Farther west.

His head turned automatically.

Rez saw.

“What?”

“Someone is hurt.”

“How far?”

“Half a mile.”

Wolf instinct sharpened.

Three scents.

Human.

Two supernatural.

Predators.

Zee’s expression changed.

Ner noticed.

“You are smiling.”

Zee touched the knife at his hip.

This time he did not deny it.

“Yes.”

The attackers were feeding on a traveler.

Two feral Vampires.

No allegiance.

No kingdom.

No deeper mystery.

They had cornered a middle-aged merchant beside an overturned cart and were taking turns drinking while keeping him alive.

Zee watched from the trees.

Assassin instinct cataloged.

First Vampire favored left side.

Second had an old knee injury.

Both armed.

Neither had noticed the four.

Rez whispered:

“We can take them alive.”

Zee looked at him.

“Why?”

Rez hesitated.

Good question.

Not disagreement for its own sake.

Ner looked toward the bleeding merchant.

“Can they tell us anything useful?”

“No,” Zee said.

“How do you know?”

Wolf scent.

Vampire bloodline recognition.

Behavior.

“They are unaffiliated ferals.”

Rez looked again.

One Vampire laughed as the merchant begged.

Then bit deeper solely to make him scream.

Rez’s face hardened.

“Fine.”

Zee smiled.

Not large.

Not excited.

Just decision.

“I take one.”

Rez looked toward him.

“We take both.”

“No.”

Zee’s eyes stayed on the predators.

“I want one.”

Something in his voice made Ner look at him.

Zee felt his concern.

Did not apologize.

The first Vampire never knew Zee entered the clearing.

One moment feeding.

The next Zee’s knife was through the nerve beneath his jaw.

The Vampire’s body locked.

Zee pulled him backward into darkness.

Silent.

Precise.

The second turned.

Rez hit him with Blue-White Mana before he could move.

Ner sealed the merchant behind a Golden barrier.

Zareth stayed beyond the trees where his size would not crush the wounded man.

Zee’s Vampire struggled.

Stronger than expected.

Good.

Zee enjoyed that.

The predator tried compulsion.

Zee laughed.

Then his own crimson eyes darkened.

“Kneel.”

The Vampire resisted.

Zee felt the mind push back.

He pressed harder.

No Witchcraft.

Vampire domination.

“Kneel.”

The man’s knees struck dirt.

His face filled with horror.

Zee felt something evolve in the pressure.

Eye contact remained.

But the command seemed to settle deeper than ordinary compulsion.

Like blood itself recognized hierarchy.

Interesting.

Later.

The Vampire whispered:

“What are you?”

Zee crouched.

“You were hurting someone who could not stop you.”

The Vampire spat.

“So?”

There.

Line crossed.

No guilt.

Zee pulled his head sideways.

Fangs descended.

Ner said:

“Zee.”

Not stop.

Just name.

Zee looked toward him.

Ner did not ask him to spare the Vampire.

Important.

Zee smiled faintly.

Then bit.

The feeding was slower than necessary.

Zee knew that.

The Vampire knew too.

Fear entered the blood.

Zee tasted it.

Liked it.

His Vampire nature surged.

The supernatural blood entered him.

Cold.

Predatory.

His own Crimson blood began rewriting around it again.

Regeneration tightened.

Senses sharpened.

Something about his predatory presence deepened.

Not copied.

Evolved.

The Vampire’s heartbeat weakened.

Zee could have stopped.

Did not.

He drained him completely.

The body fell.

Zee stood with blood on his mouth.

No apology.

No guilt.

Only awareness.

Rez looked at him.

“You enjoyed that.”

Zee wiped his mouth.

“Yes.”

Silence.

Then he looked toward the second Vampire.

Rez had him pinned.

Alive.

Zee’s hunger answered.

Stronger now.

Ner stepped between them.

Not as conscience.

As himself.

“That one surrendered.”

Zee looked at the Vampire.

Hands open.

Terrified.

No longer attacking.

Different.

Zee’s darkness wanted the blood anyway.

That mattered.

He stared long enough that the prisoner began trembling.

Zee enjoyed that too.

Then he looked at Ner.

“He lives.”

Ner nodded.

Nothing sentimental.

No praise.

Just decision.

Zee approached the wounded merchant instead.

He crouched.

“Look at me.”

The man did.

Terrified.

Zee softened the predatory pressure deliberately.

“Who are you?”

“Joren.”

“Can you stand?”

“I think.”

“Good.”

Zee rose.

The merchant stared at the drained body.

Then Zee.

“You killed him.”

“Yes.”

No excuse.

“And him?”

“Rez decides where he goes.”

Rez looked offended.

“Why me?”

“You caught him.”

“That is not how law works.”

Zee smiled.

“There you are.”

Rez sighed.

The merchant looked between them.

Confused.

Alive.

That was enough.

Zee turned toward the forest.

His senses were different.

Not dramatically.

But Vampire blood had done something.

He could smell Ner’s new blood more clearly now.

Golden Witch.

Vampire.

And that hidden singing current.

He looked at him.

Ner noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You are staring again.”

“For a different reason.”

Ner’s eyebrows rose.

Zee immediately realized how that sounded.

Rez laughed.

Even Zareth’s amusement entered the Link.

Zee sighed.

Perhaps some things survived grief whether he wanted them to or not.

He looked toward the dead Vampire.

Then Aurelia’s words returned.

Do not make this ugly.

Zee no longer believed ugliness meant blood.

Sometimes blood saved people.

Sometimes killing ended cruelty.

Sometimes mercy mattered.

Sometimes it did not.

Aurelia was not there to decide.

No one else would do it for him.

That empty place remained empty.

Zee licked the last trace of Vampire blood from one fang.

Then looked toward the surviving predator.

“Tell every feral nest west of Vesper what happened here.”

The Vampire stared.

Rez frowned.

“You are releasing him?”

Zee smiled.

“After you get whatever information you need.”

Understanding reached Rez.

Reputation.

Weapon.

Ner understood too.

Zee stepped closer to the prisoner.

His predatory presence spread.

Not a spell.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Assassin stillness.

Crimson eyes.

The man began shaking.

Zee spoke softly.

“Tell them there are easier roads than mine.”

The Vampire swallowed.

“And if they come anyway?”

Zee’s smile disappeared.

“Then I feed.”

No threat needed beyond that.

The prisoner believed him.

Good.

Zee was beginning to understand something.

Fear invented by strangers had once made him furious.

Fear he deliberately earned?

That felt useful.

And he was done pretending he did not like useful things simply because they were dark.
