Chapter One Hundred Forty-One

The Dragon in His Blood

The castle found out twenty minutes later.

Mostly because Zayrlen’s room no longer had a wall.

That made secrecy difficult.

Rez arrived first.

Stopped outside.

Looked at the destruction.

Then at Zee.

Then at Zayrlen.

“What happened?”

Zayrlen answered:

“I became a Dragon.”

Rez looked at the missing wall.

“I gathered that.”

Ner arrived next.

He saw Zayrlen.

Ignored the room.

Crossed directly to his son.

“You okay?”

“Yes.”

Ner hugged him.

Zayrlen accepted that one with less complaining.

Then Ner pulled back.

“Did it hurt?”

“A lot.”

Zee’s expression changed.

Ner noticed.

“Did you know what to do?”

Zee answered before Zayrlen could.

“No.”

Zayrlen looked toward him.

“But he stayed.”

Ner softened.

“That sounds like him.”

Zee looked away.

Uncomfortable.

Good.

⸻

Victor arrived.

Stopped at the doorway.

Looked at the broken room.

Then at Zayrlen.

Then at Zee.

“You let him destroy an entire chamber.”

Zee stared.

“He transformed into a Dragon.”

“Yes.”

“First time.”

“Yes.”

“What did you want me to do? Ask him to be smaller?”

Victor opened his mouth.

Closed it.

Fair.

Zayrlen smiled.

Victor noticed.

“You find this amusing.”

“Yes.”

Victor looked toward Zee.

“He definitely gets that from you.”

Zee folded his arms.

“Careful.”

⸻

Zareth arrived later.

He did not pretend to be calm.

The moment he saw Zayrlen, his Teal-and-Gold Dragon Mana reacted.

He stopped.

Then smiled.

Wide.

“You did it.”

Zayrlen nodded.

“Apparently.”

Zareth looked toward Zee.

“You saw?”

“Yes.”

“First?”

“Yes.”

Only.

Zareth understood immediately.

No jealousy.

No offense.

Good.

“That was yours.”

Zee nodded.

Zayrlen looked between them.

“You are being weird.”

Zareth laughed.

Then crouched slightly.

“Show me.”

Zayrlen’s expression changed.

“No.”

Zareth blinked.

“What?”

“I cannot just do it again.”

“Why not?”

“Because I do not know how.”

Good.

Reality.

Awakening was not mastery.

Zareth’s expression softened.

“Then do not.”

No pressure.

No scheduled transformation.

No ritual.

The Dragon would become controllable with time.

⸻

That time came gradually.

Not instantly.

The next shift happened three weeks later.

By accident again.

Zayrlen sneezed.

Nobody ever let him forget that.

He was standing in the eastern training yard with Amara when Dragon pressure surged.

He tried stopping it.

Remembered Zee’s words.

Did not fight.

Shifted halfway.

Then lost it.

Human again.

Amara stared.

Zayrlen stared back.

“If you tell anyone—”

Rhyven was already laughing from across the yard.

Too late.

⸻

The third transformation worked.

Mostly.

He chose it.

No panic.

No tearing through a bedroom.

The true Crimson-and-Gold Dragon emerged in an open field beyond the castle.

This time everyone saw.

Zee stood with Ner.

Zareth beside them.

Rez.

Victor.

Elias.

Kael.

Tessa.

Amara.

Rhyven.

Family.

The Dragon opened his wings.

Enormous.

Young.

Crimson and Gold burning beneath sunlight.

Darkheart soldiers along the wall stopped what they were doing.

Some stared.

Some cheered.

Zee said nothing.

Ner looked toward him.

“You are doing the face.”

“What face?”

“The proud one.”

“I have no proud face.”

Victor said:

“You absolutely do.”

Zee looked at him.

Victor smiled.

“Do not kill me. We already established how pointless that is.”

Zee sighed.

Progress.

Unfortunately.

⸻

Zayrlen landed badly.

Very badly.

The ground shook.

One wing hit dirt.

His tail took out a training post.

Silence.

Then Rez started laughing.

Zayrlen shifted back immediately.

His face burned.

“Not a word.”

Rez could barely breathe.

Zee looked toward him.

“Enough.”

Rez pointed.

“He destroyed the post with his ass.”

Zayrlen looked horrified.

Ner lost the fight and laughed too.

Zee tried not to.

Failed.

Zayrlen stared at his father.

“You too?”

Zee covered his mouth.

“No.”

“You are literally laughing.”

“No.”

“I hate this family.”

Victor nodded.

“Finally something we agree on.”

Zayrlen pointed at him.

“You are part of the problem.”

Victor looked almost pleased.

That was new.

⸻

The Dragon changed how Zayrlen understood himself.

Not because it replaced the Wolf.

It did not.

The Wolf remained his first true form.

Crimson and Gold.

Fast.

Grounded.

Predatory.

Instinctive.

The Dragon was different.

Massive.

Aerial.

Powerful.

Harder to hide.

Harder to control.

Where the Wolf sharpened the world, the Dragon expanded it.

He could feel distance differently in the air.

Could sense heat beneath stone.

Could hear the pressure of wind around his wings.

Could feel his own body as something built to dominate space.

That frightened him at first.

Then fascinated him.

⸻

His Witchcraft began adapting around both true forms.

Crimson-and-Gold Mana threaded through Wolf movement.

Not transforming the Wolf into anything else.

Simply his Witch nature working while Wolf.

In Dragon form, Witch structures became enormous.

Barriers large enough to shield buildings.

Mana rings orbiting wings.

Crimson-and-Gold spears forming in the air around him.

Still unstable.

Still young.

Still learning.

But distinctly Zayrlen.

Not Zee.

Not Zareth.

Him.

⸻

Zee noticed that first.

During one field exercise, Zayrlen shifted into Wolf form instead of Dragon.

Amara tried forcing him toward open terrain.

Zayrlen refused.

He used Wolf speed to disappear between trees.

Then Crimson-and-Gold Witch rings formed around Amara from three different directions.

She broke two.

The third trapped her ankle.

Zayrlen hit from behind.

Human again before impact.

Shoulder into ribs.

Amara rolled.

Recovered.

Smiled.

“Good.”

Zee watched from the ridge.

Rez stood beside him.

“He fights like neither of you.”

“Yes.”

“That bothers you?”

“No.”

Immediate.

Rez looked over.

Zee’s expression held something almost fierce.

Pride.

“He should not fight like me.”

That mattered.

Zayrlen needed his own identity.

Zee understood that now.

⸻

The first real argument about battle came not long after.

Zayrlen stood in Zee’s council chamber.

Older.

Stronger.

Still teenage.

“I am ready.”

Zee looked up from a map.

“No.”

Zayrlen’s jaw tightened.

Ner, nearby, immediately knew where this was going.

“For what?” Zee asked.

“Field deployment.”

“No.”

“You did not even think.”

“I did.”

“For half a second.”

“That was enough.”

Zayrlen’s Dragon heat rose.

“Dad.”

“No.”

“I fight better than half the outer guard.”

“Probably.”

“I can shift.”

“Yes.”

“I can use Witchcraft in both forms.”

“Yes.”

“I have done live exercises.”

“Yes.”

“Then what exactly am I waiting for?”

Zee looked at him.

That was harder.

Because the answer could not be:

Until I stop seeing you as my baby.

That would never happen.

So Zee said:

“Until I trust your judgment more than your power.”

Zayrlen went quiet.

Ner looked toward Zee.

Good answer.

Zee continued.

“You are strong enough.”

Zayrlen listened.

“You are fast enough.”

Another beat.

“You are dangerous enough.”

Zayrlen’s eyes sharpened.

“But battle is not proving what you can survive.”

Zee leaned back.

“It is knowing when not to chase.”

Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Eight lived underneath that sentence.

“When to retreat.”

Another.

“When the most dangerous target is bait.”

Another.

“When someone is using you to move the rest of the battlefield.”

Zayrlen looked at his father differently now.

Zee continued.

“If you come with me, I cannot spend the entire fight deciding for you.”

There.

The real standard.

“I need to know that if I turn my back, you will make your own correct choice.”

Zayrlen’s anger cooled.

Not gone.

Understood.

“Okay.”

Zee blinked.

That had been easier than expected.

Then Zayrlen said:

“So how do I prove it?”

There he was.

Zee smiled faintly.

“By living long enough to annoy me.”

“I already do that.”

“Excellent progress.”

⸻

That night, Zee found Zayrlen on the highest western roof.

Human form.

Legs hanging over the edge.

Crimson-and-Gold Mana moving lazily around one hand.

Zee sat beside him.

No King.

No lesson.

Zayrlen looked over.

“You still think I am a baby.”

“Yes.”

Zayrlen sighed.

“I am serious.”

“So am I.”

“I can become a Dragon.”

“You are still my baby.”

“I can become a Wolf.”

“Still.”

“I can break castle wards.”

“Still.”

“I am almost as tall as you.”

Zee looked at him.

“Dangerous argument.”

Zayrlen laughed.

Then went quiet.

“Will you ever let me fight with you?”

Zee looked out over Darkheart.

Eventually.

He knew that now.

Zayrlen was not asking to play at violence.

He was growing into someone capable of carrying it.

That terrified some part of Zee.

Not because of what Zayrlen was.

Because Zee knew exactly what battle cost.

He looked at his son.

“Yes.”

Zayrlen turned.

“Really?”

“When you earn it.”

“I will.”

“I know.”

That answer surprised Zayrlen.

Zee looked away before the emotion showed too clearly.

Too late.

Zayrlen smiled.

“Proud face.”

Zee groaned.

“Victor needs to die again.”

Zayrlen laughed.

“No, he was right.”

“That makes it worse.”

⸻

Below them, Darkheart lived.

Victor argued with Rez somewhere.

Ner was probably looking for both of them.

Zareth had returned to Drakonis.

Rhyven trained near the southern wall.

Elias still worked too late.

Kael cheated at cards.

Amara pretended not to notice.

Tessa threatened everyone equally.

The world kept moving.

The Devouring Choir remained somewhere beyond the stars.

Merek Voss remained unresolved somewhere beyond Darkheart’s borders.

Victor remained irritatingly alive.

And Zayrlen Keal Darkheart had become something new without ceasing to be himself.

Witch.

Wolf.

Dragon.

Crimson and Gold.

Still learning.

Still growing.

Not ready for war yet.

But close enough that Zee could finally see the day coming.

And when that day arrived—

Zee would have to learn the hardest thing Teddy Bear Dad had faced yet.

Not how to protect his son from every battle.

How to trust him inside one.
