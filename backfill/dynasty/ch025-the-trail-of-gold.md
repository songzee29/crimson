Chapter Twenty-Five

The Trail of Gold

Aurelia did not allow Zee to go.

Zee went anyway.

The difference was that, at ten years old, he had finally become clever enough not to announce it first.

The receiving hall remained filled with broken stone, shattered constructs, frightened diplomats, Vesper soldiers, and three southern witches loudly insisting that the attack had nothing to do with them.

Ner stood beside the hole in the floor.

The final thread of Pure Golden Mana remained wrapped around two of his fingers.

It descended into darkness.

Moving.

Fading.

But present.

“They are getting farther away.”

Aurelia looked toward Lucien.

“Send soldiers.”

Lucien was already issuing orders.

“Vale takes the first unit. Caldrin takes the second. Seal every known exit beneath the southern quarter.”

Rez folded his arms.

“And us?”

“No.”

Zee looked at him.

“That sounded familiar.”

Rez ignored him.

“I am seventeen.”

Lucien stared at his son.

“You say that as though seventeen is an ancient and respected age.”

“It is compared to them.”

Zee and Ner both glared.

Rez pointed between them.

“See?”

Aurelia crouched in front of Zee.

“You are staying here.”

“They attacked Ner.”

“Yes.”

“They are escaping.”

“Yes.”

“Ner can track them.”

“Yes.”

“So—”

“No.”

Zee frowned.

Aurelia continued before he could argue.

“You have broken ribs that are still healing.”

“They are almost done.”

“That sentence does not help you.”

Zee touched his side.

The worst of the pain had already faded.

His Natural Tribrid body repaired damage rapidly now, but healing did not make injury pleasant. Every breath still pulled against tender bone.

Aurelia touched one of the crimson-black scales along his neck.

“And whatever happened to these during the fight is not something I am willing to ignore.”

Zee’s expression sharpened.

“You said you would tell me.”

“I will.”

“When?”

“When there are not attackers beneath the palace.”

“That is always when people say later.”

Aurelia’s face softened.

“I know.”

Zee hated when she answered calmly.

It made rebellion less satisfying.

Behind him, Zareth sent a thought-fragment through the Dragon Link.

Other way.

Zee’s eyes shifted toward the dragon.

An image followed.

Old service shaft.

Western wall.

Down.

Zareth had found it years earlier.

Of course he had.

The dragon spent his childhood exploring every place adults told him not to enter.

Zee looked toward Ner.

Ner noticed.

Rez noticed Ner noticing.

Then Rez noticed Zee trying not to look guilty.

“Oh, no.”

Aurelia turned.

“What?”

Rez smiled immediately.

“Nothing.”

Zee stared at him.

Traitor.

Rez’s expression answered clearly.

You owe me.

Lucien called from across the chamber.

“Rez.”

“Yes?”

“You remain with them.”

Rez’s face collapsed.

“What?”

“You wanted responsibility.”

“I wanted to go.”

“You now have responsibility.”

“That is not the same thing.”

Lucien walked away.

Caldrin passed Rez on his way toward the underground passage.

“Character building.”

Rez looked murderous.

Caldrin smiled.

Then vanished below.

Aurelia kissed Zee’s forehead.

“Stay.”

Zee nodded.

He waited until she left.

Then turned toward Rez.

“No.”

Zee blinked.

“I did not say anything.”

“You have the face.”

“What face?”

“The one you used when you were six and decided stealing a vampire hunter’s horse was ‘reconnaissance.’”

“That worked.”

“You rode into a swamp.”

“The horse chose badly.”

Ner looked between them.

“We are wasting time.”

Rez pointed at him.

“You are supposed to be the sensible one.”

“I never agreed to that.”

The golden thread around Ner’s fingers dimmed.

His face changed.

“They are going to break the connection.”

Zee looked toward Zareth.

The Dragon Link flashed.

Now.

Zee ran.

Ner followed.

Zareth crashed through the side passage.

Rez stared after them.

For one long second, he considered doing the responsible thing.

Then cursed and followed.

The hidden shaft dropped steeply beneath the southern palace.

Zareth went first.

His wings scraped stone as he descended, teal-and-gold mana illuminating the passage in brief flashes.

Zee followed using handholds carved into the wall.

Ner climbed below him.

Rez came last.

“This is idiotic.”

Zee looked up.

“You followed.”

“So you would not die.”

“I cannot.”

Rez stopped.

The words carried more weight now that Zee was old enough to understand them.

Absolute immortality was not something Zee announced often.

Even among the people closest to him, it sat strangely.

Rez looked down.

“You know what I mean.”

Zee did.

He continued climbing.

The shaft opened into an ancient aqueduct beneath Vesper.

Water moved through narrow channels cut into black stone.

Ner landed beside Zee.

The golden thread brightened.

“This way.”

They ran.

The architecture became older the deeper they traveled.

Vesper stonework disappeared behind rough foundations belonging to whatever civilization had occupied the valley before House Vesper ever claimed it.

Zee’s vampire hearing stretched ahead.

Water.

Rats.

Three heartbeats.

Then five.

Then—

Nothing.

He stopped.

Rez nearly hit him.

“What?”

Zee listened again.

“The people ahead disappeared.”

Ner frowned.

“Teleportation?”

“No.”

Zee smelled the air.

Dust.

Water.

Metal.

Magic.

And something else.

Clay.

He remembered the constructs.

“More stone bodies.”

Zareth growled.

The Dragon Link carried warning.

Walls.

Zee looked around.

The tunnel walls were covered in carved human faces.

He had thought they were decoration.

One opened its eyes.

“Move!”

The wall exploded.

Stone hands reached from both sides.

Rez shoved Ner forward as a construct tore free behind them.

Blue-White Mana erupted from his palm.

The blast froze half the creature’s body.

Zee struck the frozen section.

Crimson scales spread across his knuckles.

Stone shattered.

Another construct emerged from the floor.

Then four more.

Ner raised both hands.

Pure Golden Mana expanded in a ring.

The constructs stopped.

Not immobilized.

Revealed.

Golden lines appeared beneath their stone shells.

“Threads again.”

Rez looked around.

“Can you follow them?”

Ner gritted his teeth.

“They split.”

Zee ducked beneath a stone fist.

“Then find the real one!”

“That would be easier if you stopped shouting!”

“I am helping!”

“You are loud!”

Zareth slammed into two constructs and sent both through the wall.

Rez pointed.

“He is helping.”

Zee glared.

Then one construct caught him.

A stone hand closed around Zee’s throat and lifted him.

Pain.

Pressure.

His feet left the ground.

His body did not die.

Could not.

But the grip crushed against muscle and bone hard enough to make his vision blur.

Crimson Mana erupted.

The construct absorbed it.

Its runes brightened.

Zee remembered what happened upstairs.

The harder he fed the construct, the more it stored.

So he stopped pushing.

Changed.

His wolf instinct took over.

Movement.

Balance.

Weakness.

The construct’s left knee shifted before every turn.

Zee kicked it.

Nothing.

Again.

A crack.

The construct tightened its grip.

Zee’s dragon nature responded.

Crimson-black scales spread across his throat beneath the stone fingers.

Protection.

Not full armor.

Enough.

Zee inhaled.

Then drove both heels into the damaged knee.

It shattered.

The construct fell.

Zee rolled free.

His neck already healed beneath the scales.

Rez struck the broken construct with Blue-White Mana.

It exploded.

“Still think this was a good idea?”

Zee coughed.

“Yes.”

Rez laughed despite himself.

“That is what I was afraid of.”

Ner suddenly shouted.

“There!”

Golden Mana raced along the floor.

One control line brightened more than the others.

It ran east.

Zareth tore through the remaining constructs.

They followed.

⸻

The tunnel ended at a bronze door.

No Vesper crest.

No known witch sigil.

Only a circular depression in the center.

Ner stopped.

His Golden Mana pulled toward it.

Rez looked at the door.

“They wanted you to open this.”

Ner’s face tightened.

“That is why they attacked the hall.”

Zee touched the bronze.

“Then why run here?”

Rez understood first.

“They did not fail.”

Silence.

Ner looked at him.

“They wanted me to follow.”

A slow clap came from the darkness behind them.

Everyone turned.

A man stepped from an alcove.

Middle-aged.

Gray robes.

No mask.

No royal colors.

Seven others appeared around him.

Witches.

Humans.

One vampire.

Different backgrounds.

Different weapons.

Mercenaries working under one contract.

The gray-robed man smiled.

“Prince Rez.”

Rez raised his sword.

“Nereus Azrion.”

Golden Mana gathered.

“Crown Prince Zareth.”

The dragon bared his teeth.

Then the man looked at Zee.

“Darkheart.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“You built the stone things.”

“I designed them.”

Ner stepped forward.

“For me.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

The man looked toward the bronze door.

“Because I cannot open that.”

“What is inside?”

“I do not know.”

Zee blinked.

The man laughed.

“You expected something grander?”

“No.”

Zee looked at Ner.

“He sounds like Caldrin.”

Rez snorted.

The man’s smile disappeared.

“This chamber predates Vesper. Every magical lock responds to structure. Every structure can be solved.”

He looked toward Ner’s hands.

“Except this one.”

Ner said nothing.

“It rejects ordinary witchcraft.”

“Dragon Mana.”

“Blood magic.”

“Vampire compulsion.”

“Demonic contracts.”

“Everything.”

Rez looked at the bronze door.

“So you decided to use a child.”

“I decided to use the correct key.”

Ner’s Golden Mana flared.

“I am not a key.”

The man smiled.

“That depends on the lock.”

Zee’s expression changed.

Zareth felt it.

Anger.

The Dragon Link warned him.

Scales.

Zee looked down.

Crimson-black scales had begun spreading along his forearms again.

The gray-robed man noticed.

His eyes sharpened.

“So the reports were accurate.”

Zee looked at him.

“What reports?”

No answer.

The man raised one hand.

The mercenaries attacked.

Rez met the vampire first.

Blue-White Mana flashed against supernatural speed.

Zareth took the left side, wings filling the tunnel as teal-and-gold Dragon Mana blasted two witches backward.

Ner raised a Golden barrier.

Three spells struck.

The barrier held.

Zee went for the gray-robed man.

The man had prepared.

A black sigil struck Zee’s chest.

His Crimson Mana vanished.

Not gone.

Suppressed.

His scales dimmed.

Zee stumbled.

The man smiled.

“Ten years of reports.”

“Enough observations.”

“Enough failures.”

“We finally built something that interferes with you.”

Zee tried summoning Crimson Mana.

Nothing answered.

Pain shot through his chest.

The suppression seal tightened.

The man stepped closer.

“You see?”

“You are not incomprehensible.”

“Only complicated.”

Zee looked frightened.

The man mistook that for victory.

Zareth did not.

Through the Dragon Link came Zee’s actual feeling.

Wait.

Zareth understood.

He kept fighting.

The man crouched in front of Zee.

“Victor failed because he tried to destroy what he feared.”

Zee’s crimson eyes hardened.

The man continued.

“I prefer measurement.”

Zee punched him.

No Mana.

No spell.

Just a ten-year-old fist backed by vampire strength and wolf muscle.

The man flew backward.

Zee stood.

“You measured wrong.”

Rez laughed from across the chamber.

“That was good!”

The gray-robed man rose with blood running from his mouth.

“You—”

Zee attacked.

The suppression stopped his Mana.

It did not stop his blood.

It did not stop his vampire speed.

His wolf reflexes.

His Dragon-toughened bones.

He had spent his entire life being attacked by enemies who wanted one part of him neutralized.

The mistake was thinking Zee had only one part.

He ducked a spell.

Closed distance.

Hit the man again.

The artificer tried retreating.

Zee smelled fear.

Zareth cut off the exit.

Ner trapped the mercenaries behind a Golden barrier.

Rez disarmed the vampire and kicked his weapon away.

The fight ended.

The gray-robed man sat against the bronze door breathing hard.

Zee stood before him.

“Who paid you?”

The man smiled through blood.

“No king.”

“Who?”

“A collector.”

“Name.”

“I never received one.”

Rez searched his coat.

“Broker tokens.”

He held up three silver discs.

Different markings.

“Multiple clients.”

Zee looked toward the door.

“You attacked Ner because of this.”

“Yes.”

Ner approached.

“What happens if I open it?”

The man’s expression changed.

“I do not know.”

Ner stared at him.

“You were willing to attack an entire palace because you were curious?”

The man smiled faintly.

“Curiosity built kingdoms.”

Ner’s Golden Mana brightened.

“And ruins.”

He closed his fist.

The remaining control threads vanished.

All surviving constructs went still.

Permanently.

The gray-robed man watched him.

Something close to fear entered his face.

Ner looked toward Lucien’s approaching soldiers echoing through the tunnels.

“You wanted to know what I can open.”

He stepped away from the bronze door.

“Now you can spend the rest of your life wondering.”

That hurt the man more than defeat.

Zee smiled.

“I like that.”

Ner smiled back.

Then Aurelia entered the chamber.

Zee stopped smiling.

She looked at him.

Then Ner.

Then Rez.

Then Zareth.

Then the unconscious mercenaries.

Then the bronze door.

Her voice was terrifyingly calm.

“Seven minutes.”

Rez looked confused.

“What?”

“I left you alone for seven minutes.”

Zee pointed toward the prisoners.

“We caught them.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“You disobeyed me.”

“Yes.”

“You entered an unknown underground structure.”

“Yes.”

“You fought mercenaries.”

“Yes.”

“You were magically suppressed.”

Zee hesitated.

“Yes.”

Aurelia closed her eyes.

Ner whispered, “Should we run?”

Rez shook his head.

“Too late.”

Zareth began backing away.

Zee stared at him.

Traitor.

The Dragon Link carried one emotion.

Survive.

⸻

The bronze door remained closed.

Lucien ordered three guard stations built around it before sunrise.

No one would touch the lock without his permission.

Especially Ner.

The gray-robed artificer was imprisoned.

His broker tokens proved exactly what Aurelia had feared.

The attack had not belonged to the black-robed organization.

Nor the Twelve Crowns.

Nor Victor.

Several private collectors had funded it independently.

One wanted information about Golden Mana.

Another wanted whatever lay behind the bronze door.

A third had paid merely for observations of Zee’s developing scales.

Different enemies.

Different interests.

Same children caught between them.

But the hunt had changed in one important way.

This time, Zee had gone toward it.

Aurelia understood that.

And that frightened her more than she wanted to admit.

Because the infant she once carried between kingdoms was gone.

Zee was ten.

Curious.

Angry.

Loyal.

And increasingly unwilling to let adults fight every battle for him.

That night, Aurelia stood outside his room.

Zee sat on the floor beside Zareth.

He knew why she had come.

“You said you would tell me.”

Aurelia entered.

She closed the door.

“Yes.”

Zee looked toward the crimson-black scales along his arm.

“About this?”

Aurelia nodded.

Zareth lifted his head.

The Dragon Link became intensely attentive.

Aurelia sat opposite her son.

For several heartbeats, she simply looked at him.

Then she said:

“Your Dragon blood did not come from Zareth.”

Zee went completely still.

And somewhere inside him, something ancient stirred.
