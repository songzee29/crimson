Chapter Twenty-Nine

The Ghost of Veyrath

Zee’s Dragon nature had not awakened all at once.

It had begun around his second year.

Before then, the fourth blood nature inside him had remained quiet—present from birth, inherited naturally from Aurelia, but buried beneath the three supernatural natures that had opened their eyes with him.

Witch.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Zareth had been beside him from the beginning.

The Dragon Link, the constant presence of Teal-and-Gold Dragon Mana, their shared emotions, their fights, their closeness—all of it had quietly pressed against something already sleeping inside Zee.

At two, his body had begun answering.

First came abnormal heat.

Then temporary Crimson scales.

Durability that exceeded even what his other three natures should have given him.

Later, some scales stopped disappearing.

Horn buds followed.

Then Crimson Dragon energy.

Zee’s body had always adapted too quickly.

What might have taken decades in another bloodline had spent eight years growing inside a Natural Tribrid whose existence refused to remain still.

Yet the Convergence Vault had made one truth painfully clear.

Four.

Three awake.

Whatever Dragon was becoming inside Zee…

it had not finished waking.

Now, beneath the moonlit cliffs outside House Vesper, one of the dead had apparently come looking for it.

Not Zee’s dead.

Aurelia’s.

⸻

“You should be dead.”

Aurelia’s voice did not shake.

That frightened Zee more than if it had.

The silver-haired stranger remained several yards away beneath the trees, dark red eyes fixed upon her.

“So should our bloodline.”

Zareth moved beside Zee.

Teal-and-Gold Dragon Mana threaded between his scales.

The Dragon Link carried a sharp command.

Behind.

Zee frowned.

“No.”

Zareth turned his head.

Danger. Behind.

“I heard you.”

He stayed exactly where he was.

Zareth sent an image of biting him.

Zee would normally have argued.

Not tonight.

The stranger finally looked toward the Crown Prince of Drakonis.

Recognition touched his face.

“Drakonis.”

Zareth growled.

The man looked back at Aurelia.

“You keep unusual company.”

Aurelia’s Witch Mana gathered around both hands.

“And you still speak too much.”

The stranger smiled.

That smile carried familiarity.

Not affection.

History.

Zee saw it immediately.

“You know him.”

Aurelia did not look away.

“Yes.”

“Who is he?”

The stranger answered for himself.

“Someone your mother once trusted.”

Aurelia’s magic intensified.

“Do not speak to him.”

Zee looked between them.

That answered more than either realized.

The man noticed the scales along Zee’s temples again.

His gaze lingered on the crimson-black horns.

Curiosity.

Then confusion.

Not understanding.

Good.

He did not know what Zee was.

Not completely.

“Those are Veyrath traits,” he said.

Aurelia moved one step sideways, blocking more of Zee from view.

“They are his.”

The stranger’s expression shifted.

“Protective distinction.”

“Necessary distinction.”

Zee looked up at his mother.

He understood what she meant.

Not Veyrath scales copied onto him.

Not Zareth’s Dragon nature given to him.

His.

Everything inside Zee became Zee eventually.

The stranger looked toward the remnants of Crimson Dragon energy flickering around Zee’s horns.

“That is not how ours looked.”

Aurelia’s eyes narrowed.

“Ours?”

For the first time, the man’s confidence weakened.

Aurelia noticed.

“You have been gone too long to call anything ours.”

The stranger’s jaw tightened.

Zee watched carefully.

“You really thought he was dead.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because I saw enough to believe it.”

The stranger laughed quietly.

“You saw what they wanted survivors to see.”

Aurelia went still.

Zee hated that sentence.

“Who are they?”

Neither answered him.

Zee crossed his arms.

“I am standing right here.”

Aurelia glanced down.

“Zee.”

“You promised truth.”

“I promised truth about your blood.”

“This seems related.”

The stranger smiled.

“He has your persistence.”

“He has nothing from you.”

The smile vanished.

Zee caught that too.

Interesting.

“Mother.”

Aurelia exhaled slowly.

Then spoke without lowering her magic.

“His name is Sevran Veyrath.”

Zee looked at him.

Sevran.

The name stirred nothing.

No inherited Dragon memory.

No sudden recognition.

No magical revelation.

Just a man from a family Zee had only learned existed hours ago.

“How are you related?”

Aurelia answered.

“Cousin.”

Sevran’s expression hardened.

“Was.”

Zee frowned.

“You stop being cousins?”

Caldrin would have enjoyed the question.

Aurelia almost did.

“Blood does not stop.”

Sevran replied coldly.

“Families do.”

Zareth shifted.

The Dragon Link carried a memory-fragment from minutes earlier.

Aurelia in true Dragon form.

Then Sevran.

Then the smell shared between them.

Similar.

Not same.

Veyrath.

Zee understood.

“You are a Dragon too.”

Sevran looked at him.

“Yes.”

“A witch?”

A pause.

“No.”

That interested Zee.

“So not everyone in House Veyrath was Dragon-Witch.”

“No.”

Aurelia’s voice remained guarded.

“Compatibility did not mean every Veyrath carried both natures.”

Sevran looked at her sharply.

“You told him.”

“Tonight.”

“How much?”

“What belongs to him.”

“Then he knows very little.”

Zee immediately disliked him.

“I know enough to know Mother does not want you here.”

Sevran’s dark red eyes returned to him.

“You speak boldly for a child.”

Zee frowned.

“I am a child.”

Sevran blinked.

Apparently that answer had not occurred to him.

Zee continued.

“People keep saying that like I am supposed to be embarrassed.”

Zareth sent amusement.

Aurelia’s mouth almost moved.

Almost.

Sevran studied Zee longer.

Not as a legendary Tribrid.

Not yet.

As Aurelia’s son.

That somehow felt more invasive.

“What did Victor father?”

The cliff changed.

Aurelia’s magic erupted.

Zee barely saw her move.

One moment she stood beside him.

The next her hand was around Sevran’s throat.

She drove him into a tree hard enough to split the trunk.

Dark red Dragon energy flashed beneath her skin.

Sevran’s eyes widened.

Zareth surged forward.

Zee did too.

Aurelia snarled.

“Speak about my son like that again.”

Sevran grabbed her wrist.

His own Dragon strength surfaced.

The ground cracked beneath his boots.

“I did not come to insult him.”

“You came uninvited.”

“Because every other Veyrath path is watched.”

“By whom?”

Sevran looked toward Zee.

Aurelia tightened her grip.

“Eyes on me.”

He obeyed.

“Release me.”

“Answer.”

“Aurelia.”

“Answer.”

Something in Sevran’s face broke.

Not physically.

Emotionally.

“You still think the hunt ended.”

Aurelia froze.

Zee saw it.

“The Veyrath hunt?”

Sevran looked toward him despite Aurelia’s warning.

“Yes.”

Aurelia released him.

Sevran dropped to one knee, coughing.

She stepped back.

Zee came immediately beside her.

Aurelia placed one arm across his chest without looking.

Not pushing him away.

Keeping him close.

“Explain,” she said.

Sevran stood slowly.

“No.”

Zee stared.

Aurelia’s expression became dangerous.

Sevran continued.

“Not here.”

“You came here.”

“I followed your Dragon signature after it appeared tonight.”

Aurelia’s face changed.

Her transformation.

Zee understood immediately.

“You felt Mother become a Dragon.”

“Yes.”

“How far away?”

Sevran looked toward the eastern mountains.

“Far enough.”

Aurelia cursed quietly.

For decades she had hidden her true form.

Tonight she revealed it for Zee.

And someone had felt it.

Zee’s excitement from earlier curdled into guilt.

“If I had not asked—”

“No.”

Aurelia turned toward him immediately.

“This is not yours.”

“But—”

“No.”

Her voice softened.

“I chose to show you.”

Sevran watched them.

Something unreadable crossed his expression.

Then he looked toward Zareth.

“And his Mana helped amplify it.”

Zareth growled.

Sevran raised one hand.

“I am not blaming him.”

“Good,” Zee said.

Sevran looked at him.

“Protective.”

Zee shrugged.

“He is mine.”

Zareth’s head snapped toward him.

Zee corrected himself quickly.

“My friend.”

The Link carried deep offense.

Prince.

“My very annoying prince friend.”

Zareth settled.

Sevran stared at them.

“The Link is real.”

Aurelia’s expression hardened again.

“You know nothing about it.”

“No.”

That answer surprised her.

Sevran looked between the two ten-year-olds.

“I felt something when I approached.”

“What?”

“I do not know.”

Zee almost smiled.

Finally.

Someone else willing to say it.

Sevran continued.

“But your son’s Dragon nature reacts to the Drakonis prince.”

Aurelia stepped closer.

“We already know.”

“You know it reacts.”

Sevran’s gaze dropped to Zee’s horns.

“You do not know what happens when it finishes.”

Neither did he.

Zee heard that immediately.

“You do not either.”

Sevran looked at him.

“No.”

Aurelia’s shoulders eased by the smallest amount.

Sevran was not here with answers.

Only danger.

Those were different problems.

“Why did you come?” she asked.

Sevran looked toward the forest.

“To warn you.”

“About House Veyrath?”

“About what remains of it.”

Aurelia’s face tightened.

Zee listened.

Sevran continued.

“The survivors did not stay together.”

“Some hid.”

“Some were captured.”

“Some joined the people who hunted us.”

Aurelia’s eyes went cold.

“Names.”

“Not here.”

“You keep saying that.”

“Because we have been standing in the open for too long.”

Zareth suddenly turned.

The Dragon Link flashed.

Movement.

Zee heard it next.

Heartbeats.

Several.

Far below.

Not Vesper patrol rhythm.

Different.

Aurelia saw Zee’s face.

“How many?”

He listened.

“Seven.”

Then another.

“Nine.”

Sevran cursed.

“They followed me.”

Aurelia’s hand closed around his coat and yanked him forward.

“You brought them to my son?”

“I did not know they were still on me.”

“Who?”

Sevran looked toward the forest.

“I do not know which group.”

Zee frowned.

“That is not helpful.”

“No.”

Aurelia’s Witch Mana spread.

Dark violet lines disappeared through the earth.

Searching.

Zee felt Zareth’s Mana rising beside him.

Sevran began shifting.

Scales appeared along his jaw.

Dark red.

Different from Aurelia’s.

Different from Zee’s.

Aurelia grabbed his shoulder.

“No full transformation.”

Sevran looked at her.

“If they found me—”

“If you become a Dragon here, everyone within miles sees you.”

He stopped.

Aurelia looked toward Zee.

“Back to Vesper.”

“No.”

“Zee.”

“They followed him here.”

“Yes.”

“So they will follow us there.”

Aurelia paused.

He was right.

Zee continued.

“If we run straight home, we show them where.”

“Vesper is not hidden.”

“No, but why help them?”

Sevran stared at him.

“He thinks tactically.”

“He has had practice,” Aurelia said.

That sentence carried no pride.

Only grief.

Zee heard it.

Then one of the approaching heartbeats stopped.

Not death.

Stillness.

Hidden.

Another stopped.

Then another.

“They know I can hear them.”

Aurelia’s expression sharpened.

That meant preparation.

Whoever followed Sevran had information.

Maybe about Veyrath.

Maybe Zee.

Maybe neither.

Zee reached for the presence sense beneath his ordinary hearing.

There.

Nine figures spreading through the forest.

One was strange.

No normal heartbeat.

Not demon either.

Zee frowned.

“One is different.”

Sevran looked at him.

“How?”

“I do not know.”

“What do you sense?”

“Something alive.”

“That narrows it down terribly.”

Zee glared.

Aurelia almost smiled despite everything.

Then the first bolt came from the trees.

Not at Zee.

At Sevran.

He moved.

The projectile struck stone.

A black chain exploded from it.

Sevran’s face changed.

“Down!”

More bolts followed.

Zareth spread one wing in front of Zee.

Aurelia threw up a violet barrier.

The black chains hit it.

They did not break through.

They wrapped around the spell.

Then began tightening.

Aurelia’s face hardened.

“They adapted.”

Sevran looked at her.

“To Veyrath magic.”

Not Zee.

Not Vesper.

Not Ner.

Not Rez.

This hunt had its own history.

The barrier cracked.

Aurelia dropped it deliberately before the chains could collapse around them.

“Move!”

They scattered.

Figures entered the moonlight.

Seven wore gray armor without crests.

Two wore dark robes.

None carried the symbols of Zee’s known enemies.

The nearest raised a curved weapon.

“Sevran Veyrath.”

Sevran’s jaw tightened.

“You found me.”

“We never lost you.”

Aurelia glanced toward him.

He looked away.

That answer would require discussion later.

The hunter’s attention moved toward Aurelia.

He stopped.

For the first time, genuine surprise appeared.

“Aurelia.”

Not Darkheart.

Not Queen.

Just Aurelia.

The others shifted.

Recognition spread.

One whispered:

“She lives.”

Zee felt his mother become very still beside him.

The hunter smiled.

“This night improves.”

Then he noticed Zee.

The smile weakened.

Not because he knew what Zee was.

Because he saw the horns.

The scales.

A child standing beside Aurelia Veyrath.

“What is that?”

Zee’s expression changed.

Zareth snarled.

Aurelia stepped forward.

“My son.”

The hunter stared.

Understanding came only that far.

Aurelia.

Son.

Veyrath blood.

Nothing more.

Then greed entered his face.

“Take all three.”

Zee counted.

Three.

Aurelia.

Sevran.

Him.

Not Zareth.

The Dragon Prince was collateral.

Zee almost laughed.

Someone had finally arrived underestimating the wrong Dragon.

Zareth felt it.

The Link carried delighted violence.

Now bite?

Zee’s fangs showed.

“Now.”

Zareth attacked.

Teal-and-Gold Dragonfire tore across the cliff.

The hunters scattered.

Aurelia cast simultaneously.

Her Witch Mana struck the black chains and pulled them apart before they could reform.

Sevran slammed into the nearest armored man with enough force to drive him through a boulder.

Zee ran straight toward the second.

The hunter saw a ten-year-old boy.

That mistake lasted less than a second.

Zee ducked under the curved blade.

Wolf instinct read the man’s balance.

Vampire speed closed distance.

His fist struck armor.

Crimson Mana followed.

The chest plate dented inward.

The hunter flew backward.

Zee looked at his hand.

His scales had expanded.

The Dragon nature stirred.

Not fully awake.

Still useful.

Another hunter came from behind.

Aurelia shouted.

Zee turned too late.

The black chain wrapped around his torso.

It tightened.

Mana suppression followed.

Crimson light dimmed around him.

Pain crushed into his ribs.

But Zee remained standing.

The hunter pulled.

“Got you.”

Zee looked down at the chain.

Then at him.

“No.”

The man pulled harder.

Zee’s ribs compressed.

Pain sharpened.

His body adapted beneath it.

Crimson scales spread under the chain.

The suppression disrupted his Mana.

So Zee stopped relying on Mana.

He grabbed the chain with both hands.

Vampire strength.

Wolf muscle.

Dragon durability beginning beneath both.

He pulled back.

The hunter stumbled.

Zee yanked again.

The man flew toward him.

Zee headbutted him.

His juvenile horn struck the helmet.

Metal cracked.

Zee immediately grabbed his forehead.

“Ow!”

Zareth sent laughter through the Link while fighting two hunters.

“Shut up!”

Aurelia heard him.

“Focus!”

“I am!”

The strange heartbeat-less attacker moved.

Zee sensed it near his mother.

“Behind you!”

Aurelia turned.

Too late.

A gray blade cut across her side.

Blood hit stone.

Zee stopped.

Everything inside him changed.

“Mother.”

Aurelia staggered.

Not dying.

Wounded.

But blood was enough.

The hunter raised the blade again.

Zee moved.

Not with careful thought.

Not training.

Rage.

Crimson Mana exploded from him.

His horns burned.

Scales raced across his shoulders and neck.

The faint ridges beneath his shoulder blades tightened painfully.

His Dragon energy surged beside the three natures that had protected him since birth.

The black chain around him shattered.

Zee hit the attacker hard enough to carry them both across the cliff.

They crashed.

The hunter’s weapon skidded away.

Zee landed on top.

His fist rose.

Then Aurelia’s voice reached him.

“Zee.”

His hand stopped.

The hunter beneath him breathed rapidly.

Fear.

Zee wanted to hit him again.

Aurelia’s blood was still in the air.

“Zee.”

He looked back.

She stood.

One hand pressed to her side.

Alive.

Watching him.

Not afraid of him.

Never afraid of him.

“Come here.”

Zee’s fist remained raised.

Zareth’s Link pressed against his mind.

Mother. Alive. Zee. Stop.

Slowly, Zee lowered his hand.

He stood.

The hunter tried reaching for a hidden blade.

Zee kicked it away.

“Do not make me regret listening to my mother.”

Caldrin would have been proud.

The battle ended moments later.

Two hunters escaped into the forest.

Three lay unconscious.

Four were wounded badly enough that Aurelia’s bindings held them.

None carried identifying crests.

Sevran stared after the escapees.

His face had gone pale.

Aurelia noticed.

“You know them.”

“Yes.”

“Who?”

Sevran looked toward the prisoners.

“Not here.”

Zee groaned.

“I hate him.”

Aurelia almost laughed despite the blood on her side.

Sevran looked offended.

“I just helped save you.”

“You brought them.”

“Fair.”

Zareth moved beside Zee.

The Dragon Link carried immediate concern toward Aurelia.

Zee ran to his mother.

Her wound was already being wrapped with cloth from Sevran’s cloak.

Zee touched her arm.

“Are you okay?”

“Yes.”

“Truth.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“It hurts.”

“Bad?”

“Enough.”

Zee’s face tightened.

He hated that answer.

Because it was honest.

He looked down at the blood on her clothes.

The world could not kill him.

But it could still do this.

The lesson was old.

Tonight it felt new again.

Aurelia touched one of his horns.

“You stopped.”

Zee looked away.

“Because you told me.”

“No.”

He looked back.

“Because you chose to.”

Zee did not answer.

Sevran watched them.

Then something in his expression changed.

Not warmth.

Recognition.

Perhaps grief.

“They would have wanted to know you.”

Aurelia’s face hardened.

“Do not.”

Sevran stopped.

Zee looked between them.

“Who?”

Neither answered.

Zee sighed loudly.

“Everyone in this family is annoying.”

Aurelia laughed.

Actually laughed.

Even Sevran smiled.

For three breaths, they were not fugitives or survivors or hidden blood.

Then distant horns sounded from Vesper.

Lucien’s patrols.

The fight had been heard.

Sevran’s expression turned serious again.

“I cannot go inside those walls.”

Aurelia looked at him.

“You are going exactly inside those walls.”

“No.”

“Yes.”

“You have no idea what follows me.”

Aurelia stepped closer.

“You have no idea what follows my son.”

Sevran looked toward Zee.

Zee smiled sweetly.

Zareth bared his teeth.

Sevran considered.

“Fair.”

Aurelia looked toward the captured hunters.

“You came to warn me.”

“Yes.”

“Then you will explain everything you actually know.”

Sevran’s face tightened.

“Not everything belongs to—”

Aurelia’s Witch Mana flashed.

“Try that sentence.”

He stopped.

Zee grinned.

“That is what she does to me.”

“I understand why.”

Aurelia began walking toward Vesper.

Zee stayed beside her.

Zareth followed.

Sevran remained behind for one moment, staring toward the dark forest.

At the people who had found him.

At the bloodline he thought scattered.

At the cousin he thought gone.

Then at the ten-year-old boy carrying Veyrath Dragon blood alongside three other natural supernatural natures Sevran had not even begun to understand.

He followed.

Zee looked back once.

This was not his hunt.

Not originally.

That realization mattered.

For ten years, enemies had crossed kingdoms because of Zee Darkheart.

Tonight, Zee was walking into a war that had started before he existed.

Aurelia’s war.

House Veyrath’s war.

A history buried beneath the name Darkheart.

And for the first time, the hunted prince was not asking how to escape it.

He was wondering who had hurt his mother badly enough to make her hide half of herself from the world.
