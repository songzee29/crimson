Chapter Seventy-Five

Seven Knives

The attacks landed within three hours.

None looked the same.

That was what made them dangerous.

Vesper’s was sabotage.

Drakonis faced a direct strike.

One Veyrath enclave received poisoned rain.

A mixed-blood settlement east of Darkheart was attacked by hired Wolves.

A coastal Bloodsong rumor-cell was targeted by Vampire killers who thought Ner had created more like himself.

Wrong.

There was only Ner.

Another strike hit a Demon-Witch refuge under neutral protection.

The last came for Zee.

That one waited until he returned to Darkheart.

Of course.

⸻

Rez’s message arrived first.

Aqueduct secure. Four dead. Nine captured. Father furious. I am enjoying it.

Zee smiled.

Ner read over his shoulder.

“He wrote that?”

“Yes.”

“That is concerning.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Lucien furious is apparently entertaining.”

Ner looked unconvinced.

Then his own Bloodsong pulsed.

He stopped.

“Veyrath.”

Zee’s face changed.

“What?”

“Someone is bleeding.”

“Who?”

Ner closed his eyes.

His Bloodsong had learned distance imperfectly over the years.

Not names.

Not thoughts.

Resonance.

Blood he had encountered before was easier.

Theron.

Zee smelled Ner’s fear before he spoke.

“Theron.”

Zee’s hand closed.

Then the Link hit.

Zareth.

Full sentence.

Drakonis is under control. Do not come here. Go to Theron.

Beneath it Zee felt torn wing membrane, smoke, two broken talons, and Zareth lying about how fine he was.

Zee answered privately.

You are bleeding.

I said under control, not comfortable.

Idiot.

Go.

Zee went.

⸻

Theron had already killed three attackers when Zee arrived.

Seraphine killed the fourth.

Good.

No helpless family.

Theron leaned against a stone wall with poison spreading black beneath one Dragon scale patch.

Seraphine stood over a dead Witch.

“You are late.”

Zee crouched beside Theron.

“You are alive.”

“Barely.”

“Dramatic.”

Theron smiled weakly.

“Family trait.”

Ner arrived seconds later and dropped beside him.

Pure Gold formed.

Bloodsong followed.

The poison reacted badly.

Ner frowned.

“This was designed for Veyrath.”

Zee smelled it.

Dragonbane.

Witch suppression.

Blood clotting agent.

Specialized.

His body recognized pieces.

Not whole.

He opened his palm with one claw.

Seraphine immediately said:

“No.”

Zee looked at her.

“One drop.”

“You do not know what that does.”

“Neither do you.”

“That is why I said no.”

Theron coughed.

“I vote yes.”

Seraphine glared.

“You are poisoned.”

“Exactly. Motivated reasoning.”

Zee almost laughed.

Then seriousness returned.

He placed one drop of Crimson blood against the poison near Theron’s wound.

Ner isolated it with Gold.

The reaction was immediate.

The black spread stopped.

Not cured.

Stopped.

Zee’s accumulated resistance interfered with the poison’s structure.

Ner used Bloodsong to push the broken toxin away from Theron’s heart.

Seraphine added Witchcraft.

Three powers.

Not one solution.

Theron breathed easier.

Zee looked at Ner.

“Can you finish?”

“Yes.”

Good.

Zee stood.

One attacker remained alive.

Seraphine had kept him that way deliberately.

The man knelt against a pillar.

Human.

Terrified.

Zee approached.

The attacker whispered:

“Please.”

Zee crouched.

“Who gave you the poison?”

“Caldus.”

“Directly?”

“Yes.”

Truth.

“Anyone else involved?”

“No.”

Truth.

“Did you know who lived here?”

“Yes.”

“Did you know there were children?”

The man froze.

Seraphine’s face hardened.

Zee smelled the answer.

“Yes.”

He killed him.

Quick.

No speech.

No guilt.

Then turned back to Theron.

Different.

Gentler.

Both Zee.

Theron watched him.

“Aurelia would worry about you.”

Zee stopped.

Not because the name was forbidden.

Because Theron had earned the right to use it.

“I know.”

“She would also be glad I am alive.”

“Yes.”

“Then focus on that.”

Zee looked at him.

For one second, human pupils.

Then he nodded.

No moral reset.

Just family.

⸻

The mixed-blood settlement survived because Darkheart riders arrived before Zee.

That mattered.

His people were learning.

The coastal Vampire strike failed because Ner sent a Bloodsong warning through three connected blood samples before the killers reached shore.

That frightened him.

Not because the power was bad.

Because it was growing.

He told Zee later.

“I could feel them before they arrived.”

“How far?”

“Too far.”

“That is not a measurement.”

“I know.”

Zee smiled.

Ner did not.

Zee touched his face.

“What?”

Ner looked at him.

“If I can reach blood from that far away eventually…”

“Yes.”

“What stops me from touching everyone?”

Zee’s expression softened.

“You.”

Ner stared.

Not because Zee was calling him good.

Because the answer placed responsibility where it belonged.

Ner nodded.

“Fair.”

Then kissed him.

Brief.

Needed.

The moment ended when a Darkheart horn sounded.

Attack seven.

Home.

⸻

The killers came through the throne hall.

Four.

Not an army.

Professionals.

They knew Zee’s patrol schedule.

They knew the hidden stair.

They knew the old Victor-era passages.

One had been a Darkheart servant years ago.

Betrayal.

Different category.

Zee entered silently behind them.

The first assassin never knew.

Knife.

Down.

Second turned.

Vampire.

He saw Zee’s eyes.

Slits.

Tried compulsion.

Zee smiled.

“Really?”

The command broke against him.

Zee answered.

“Kneel.”

The Vampire resisted.

Strong.

Older than Zee.

Did not matter enough.

Vampire dominance pressed through voice and blood.

The attacker’s knees hit stone.

Third and fourth moved together.

Witch and Wolf.

Good coordination.

Zee barely lifted one hand.

The Witch’s spell veered left.

The Wolf adjusted.

Exactly what Zee expected.

Nanda Parbat.

He stepped into the new line.

One elbow.

One broken wrist.

Wolf claws caught his shoulder.

Crimson-black scales took most of it.

Zee grabbed the Wolf.

Headbutt.

Horn edge opened blood.

Then claws.

Wolf Zee without transforming.

Natural.

Complete.

He drove the attacker down.

The Witch tried another spell.

Zee closed his fist.

Her spell structure collapsed.

“Silence.”

Her mouth stopped.

Not compulsion.

Witchcraft.

Precise.

The kneeling Vampire struggled against Zee’s command.

Interesting.

Zee looked at him.

“Still.”

The command deepened.

No eye contact.

The Vampire froze completely.

The Wolf on the floor smelled terrified.

Zee liked that.

Then the former Darkheart servant—the first assassin he thought was down—moved.

Not dead.

Feigning.

Good technique.

Bad luck.

Zee heard the heartbeat change.

He turned before the hidden blade reached him.

Caught the hand.

The servant stared.

“You remember me.”

“Yes.”

That surprised him.

Zee remembered everyone who had worked near Aurelia.

This man had served wine at two dinners.

Opened doors.

Carried letters.

Then disappeared after Victor died.

“Why?”

The man’s face twisted.

“You destroyed the House.”

Zee looked around.

Darkheart banners.

Living guards.

People choosing loyalty instead of inherited fear.

“No.”

“You killed the King.”

“Yes.”

“My King.”

There.

Personal.

Not Caldus.

Not bloodline philosophy.

Grief.

Zee understood more than he wanted to.

The servant continued.

“You killed my brother when you took the throne.”

Zee searched memory.

Fight.

Victor loyalists.

One man attacked Ner after surrender was offered.

Zee killed him.

“Yes.”

“You remember.”

“Yes.”

The servant’s eyes filled.

“I hate you.”

Zee’s grip loosened slightly.

Not mercy yet.

Recognition.

“That is fair.”

The man froze.

Zee continued.

“You still came here to kill me.”

“Yes.”

“And them?”

The assassin glanced toward the hall.

Darkheart guards.

Servants.

Ner entering behind Zee.

His answer came too slowly.

Zee smelled it.

He had planned collateral.

There went the sympathy.

Zee broke the wrist.

The man screamed.

“Your grief is yours.”

Zee’s expression went cold.

“What you do with it is also yours.”

Then Zee killed him.

Personal.

Fast.

No feeding.

The remaining three surrendered after that.

Zee spared two.

The Vampire tried to break compulsion and reach a hidden blade.

Zee drained him.

Fully.

Because he wanted the blood.

No apology.

⸻

By midnight, all seven attacks had failed.

Not without losses.

Nine dead across four territories.

Twenty-three wounded.

Theron alive.

Zareth alive.

Lucien alive.

Veyrath survivors still standing.

Mixed-blood settlements damaged but not destroyed.

The world had thrown seven knives.

None reached the heart.

Zee sat on the Darkheart throne after the reports ended.

Rez appeared through a communication mirror from Vesper.

His hair was wet.

His coat burned.

He looked exhausted.

“Still alive?”

Zee smiled.

“Unfortunately.”

Rez nodded.

“Same.”

Zareth spoke privately through the Link from Drakonis.

My wing is fine.

Zee felt the pain immediately.

Liar.

Minor lie.

Then aloud, through a Drakonis mirror, Zareth said:

“Stop telling everyone I am injured.”

Rez stared at the mirror.

“You can hear him from there?”

Zee smiled.

“Yes.”

Zareth replied:

“And he can feel that I am considering throwing something at him.”

Ner laughed.

The room felt almost normal.

Almost.

Then Lord Merek entered.

“Majesty.”

Zee looked up.

“What?”

“One of Caldus’s strike teams surrendered before attacking.”

Silence.

Rez’s face changed in the mirror.

Ner looked toward Merek.

“Why?”

“They heard Caldus was dead.”

Zee leaned back.

“And?”

Merek hesitated.

“They said they believed if they carried out the order, you would hunt every surviving member personally.”

Zee’s face went still.

Fear.

Reputation.

Prevention.

No blood spilled because people feared what Zee would do afterward.

That was new.

And Zee liked it.

Perhaps too much.

Ner saw.

Did not say anything.

Zareth felt it through the Link.

His mental voice came quietly.

You like that they were afraid.

Zee answered honestly.

Yes.

Good.

Zee blinked.

Good?

Zareth sent heat, exhaustion, and one clear thought.

They did not kill anyone. Fear did what an army could not. Use what works. Just know when you are using it.

Zee smiled faintly.

Dragon morality.

Different.

Useful.

Rez’s voice came through the mirror.

“You are both having a private conversation, aren’t you?”

Zee looked at him.

“Yes.”

“That is rude.”

Zareth said aloud:

“We were discussing you.”

Rez narrowed his eyes.

“Liar.”

“Completely.”

Ner laughed again.

Zee looked around.

His people.

His lover.

His brother.

His bonded Dragon prince.

No Aurelia.

Never again.

Still, life.

Still war.

Still blood.

The Covenant’s Five were dead or neutralized.

Seven attacks had failed.

The world had learned something.

Not that Zee could stop everything.

He could not.

Not that his people were untouchable.

They were not.

Something more dangerous.

Attacking King Zee Darkheart no longer meant dealing only with Zee.

It meant Darkheart.

Vesper.

Bloodsong.

Drakonis.

Veyrath.

Wolves.

Witches.

Vampires.

People who had chosen him.

And now, for the first time, one group had decided not to attack because Zee’s reputation reached them before his claws did.

Zee rested one hand against the throne.

Crimson-and-gold eyes calm.

Human pupils.

For now.

He had spent his childhood hating fear.

At twenty-one, he was beginning to understand its most useful form.

Not panic.

Not cruelty.

Deterrence.

And King Zee Darkheart had every intention of learning how far it could reach.
