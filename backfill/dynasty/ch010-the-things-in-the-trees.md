Chapter Ten

The Things in the Trees

The growl came again.

Low.

Wet.

Too deep to belong to any wolf Aurelia had ever heard.

Captain Vale moved in front of the ruined shrine entrance with his sword raised while Caldrin circled toward the broken western wall. The royal witch stood behind them, both hands already glowing with violet mana.

Aurelia remained near the fallen statue with Zee held close.

The crimson hatchling climbed onto her shoulder and spread his wings.

Teal-and-gold mana moved beneath his scales in thin currents.

The eyes beyond the trees multiplied.

Pale.

Unblinking.

Watching.

Caldrin whispered, “Not Wildbloods.”

Vale answered, “No.”

“Good.”

“That was not reassurance.”

Something stepped into the moonlight.

It looked almost like a wolf.

Almost.

Its front legs were too long.

Its ribs pushed visibly beneath hairless gray skin.

Its jaw split farther back than any natural animal’s should have, exposing rows of narrow teeth slick with black saliva.

Two more emerged behind it.

Then five.

Then twelve.

The royal witch’s expression tightened.

“Gravehounds.”

Aurelia looked toward her.

“You know these?”

“I know the stories.”

Caldrin glanced back.

“I am rapidly developing a hatred for stories.”

The witch ignored him.

“They hunt burial grounds. Battlefields. Plague pits. Anywhere death gathers long enough.”

Vale studied the pack.

“Then why are they here?”

The nearest Gravehound lowered its head.

Its nostrils flared.

Toward Zee.

Aurelia’s stomach tightened.

Caldrin answered quietly.

“They smelled someone who died.”

Silence.

The hatchling growled.

The Gravehound pack erupted forward.

Vale met the first one with steel.

His blade struck the creature’s shoulder and cut nearly to bone.

It did not slow.

The hound twisted and snapped at his throat.

Vale barely blocked with his forearm guard before kicking it away.

Caldrin drove both swords into another creature’s ribs.

Black blood spilled.

The animal screamed.

The sound made every other hound more violent.

The royal witch cast.

Violet chains burst from the ground and wrapped around three Gravehounds.

They tore through the first layer.

Not the second.

She clenched both hands.

The chains tightened and dragged the creatures down.

“Move!” she shouted.

Aurelia ran toward the rear of the shrine.

No exit.

Only broken stone and a collapsed wall.

The hatchling suddenly hissed.

Danger.

Left.

Aurelia turned.

A Gravehound had climbed over the ruined roof.

It dropped.

The hatchling launched himself from her shoulder.

Teal-and-gold fire struck the creature across the face.

The Gravehound hit the floor beside Aurelia and rolled.

Still alive.

Still moving.

Zee woke.

His crimson eyes opened.

The smell hit him first.

Rot.

Blood.

Something old in the Gravehound’s flesh.

His vampire instincts reacted.

His wolf nature reacted faster.

Threat.

Pack hunter.

Predator.

But there was something else now.

A memory.

Not a thought.

A bodily memory.

Falling stone.

The Hollow creature.

Pain through the Dragon Link.

The mercenary’s crossbow.

His body recognized danger before his newborn mind could.

The Gravehound lunged.

Aurelia turned to shield Zee.

The creature never reached them.

Zee’s Crimson Mana slammed into it.

The hound stopped in midair and crashed sideways into the broken wall hard enough to crack stone.

Aurelia stared.

The Gravehound rose again.

Its body bent wrong.

Bones shifted beneath its skin.

Then it came back.

Caldrin shouted from across the shrine.

“They do not stay down!”

Vale cut the throat of another.

It kept biting.

The witch changed spells.

Instead of striking the creatures, she sealed the doorway with violet fire.

Two hounds ran directly through it.

Their skin burned.

They did not care.

Caldrin cursed.

“What kills them?”

The witch shouted back, “Enough damage!”

“Excellent!”

A Gravehound slammed into him.

He disappeared beneath it.

Vale rushed forward.

Aurelia saw blood.

The hatchling saw it too.

His fear flashed through the Link.

Zee felt it.

Pain.

Friend.

Danger.

His heartbeat accelerated.

The sounds around him sharpened.

Every Gravehound heartbeat.

Every breath.

Every claw scraping stone.

Too many.

His body had learned the rhythm of predators.

The Wildblood attack had taught him pressure.

The mercenary ambush had sharpened his reflexes.

Now something else began forming.

Recognition of movement.

Not intelligence.

Instinct.

The closest Gravehound shifted its weight before attacking.

Zee reacted before it moved.

Crimson Mana snapped around its legs.

The creature crashed face-first into stone.

The second changed direction.

Zee’s head turned before the hound did.

A burst of force struck it through the side.

The third hesitated.

That hesitation mattered.

The crimson hatchling attacked.

He bit into the Gravehound’s neck and released teal-and-gold fire directly into the wound.

The creature screamed.

This time…

It stayed down.

The witch saw.

“Inside!”

Vale looked toward her.

“What?”

“Burn them from inside!”

Caldrin emerged from beneath the hound pinning him.

Blood ran from his forehead.

“Would have appreciated that earlier.”

He stabbed through the open mouth of the next creature.

The witch cast into the wound.

Violet fire erupted beneath its skin.

The Gravehound collapsed.

Now they had a method.

The fight changed.

Vale drove steel deep enough to create openings.

Caldrin forced jaws wide.

The witch burned through flesh from within.

The hatchling struck where the creatures were already wounded.

Aurelia stayed moving.

Always keeping Zee away from the nearest threat.

But the pack kept coming.

Then the largest Gravehound entered.

The alpha.

It did not rush.

It walked.

Its skin was almost black.

Old scars covered its body.

One eye had been replaced by a pale milky membrane.

Its jaw opened.

Human teeth rested among the animal ones.

Caldrin looked at it.

“I hate that.”

Vale breathed hard.

“Agreed.”

The alpha ignored them.

It stared at Zee.

Then lowered itself.

Not in submission.

In hunger.

The scent of death clung to Zee.

Not because he was dead.

Because death had touched him and failed.

The alpha could smell the contradiction.

It charged.

The witch cast first.

The alpha tore through her binding.

Vale attacked.

The creature struck him aside.

Caldrin went low.

Its claw ripped across his shoulder.

The hatchling launched himself.

The alpha caught him in its jaws.

Zee screamed.

The Dragon Link exploded.

Pain.

Terror.

Darkness.

The hatchling’s wing bent under the pressure.

Teal-and-gold mana flashed wildly.

Zee’s Crimson Mana surged.

The alpha tightened its bite.

Something inside Zee changed.

His attention narrowed entirely onto the creature.

The world around him disappeared.

Only the heartbeat remained.

Slow.

Heavy.

There.

Zee reached.

Not with hands.

With instinct.

The alpha froze.

Its heartbeat stumbled.

Aurelia felt Zee’s small body tense.

The witch stared.

The Gravehound’s chest convulsed.

Its blood shifted beneath the skin.

Not controlled.

Pulled.

The alpha released the hatchling.

Black blood burst from its mouth.

The creature collapsed.

The hatchling fell.

Aurelia caught him with one arm while still holding Zee with the other.

The remaining Gravehounds stopped.

Their alpha lay dead.

Not burned.

Not cut apart.

Something inside it had simply failed.

The pack backed away.

One by one, they disappeared into the trees.

No one chased them.

The shrine fell silent.

Caldrin sat against a broken column.

“Was that him?”

The witch looked at Zee.

“I do not know.”

Vale coughed.

“You say that often.”

“I have learned.”

Aurelia looked down at her son.

His crimson eyes were still fixed on the dead alpha.

Something black moved beneath the Gravehound’s skin.

Then its blood began leaking toward Zee.

Slowly.

Thin threads.

Aurelia stepped back.

“No.”

The blood stopped.

For a moment.

Then Zee’s Crimson Mana reached toward it.

Not deliberately.

Automatically.

The essence entered him.

His body stiffened.

The smell of grave soil and death filled the shrine.

The hatchling whimpered.

Zee’s fangs lengthened slightly.

His heartbeat changed.

Then the blackness inside the absorbed essence met the Crimson Mana.

It began burning away.

Not disappearing.

Changing.

The witch watched with wide eyes.

She said nothing.

The dark threads beneath Zee’s skin turned crimson-black.

Then settled.

The transformation ended.

Zee exhaled.

The dead alpha began decomposing.

Fast.

Within moments, only bones remained.

Caldrin stared.

“Well.”

Vale looked toward him.

“That is all you have?”

“I am injured.”

Aurelia ignored them.

She touched Zee’s cheek.

“What did you take?”

He blinked.

He did not know.

Neither did she.

The hatchling pressed close.

The Dragon Link carried one simple thing.

Alive.

Zee answered.

Alive.

⸻

They left the shrine before dawn.

No one wanted to remain near the bones.

Caldrin’s shoulder required binding.

Vale limped.

The royal witch had burned through too much mana and needed rest.

Aurelia was exhausted beyond words.

Zee slept.

The hatchling did not.

The road west continued.

But something followed them now.

Not physically.

A story.

A merchant caravan found the Gravehound bones by midday.

They found black blood on the shrine walls.

They found signs of witchfire.

They found a dead alpha stripped down to bone in a way no hunter could explain.

By sunset, the nearest village heard that the Tribrid child had killed a Gravehound pack.

By midnight, the story changed.

The child commanded death-hounds.

By morning, it changed again.

The child ate monsters and stole what made them strong.

None of the witnesses had actually seen Zee.

That did not matter.

The bounty increased.

So did the fear.

And somewhere far ahead, beyond three mountain passes and a river dividing kingdoms, House Vesper received its first reliable report that Queen Aurelia Darkheart was traveling west.

Not alone.

King Lucien Vesper read the message twice.

Then handed it to the boy standing beside him.

Prince Rez Vesper.

Blue-white mana shimmered faintly around the young prince’s fingers.

Rez read the final line.

The Darkheart child lives.

He looked up.

“What do we do?”

King Lucien turned toward the western gates.

“We open them.”
