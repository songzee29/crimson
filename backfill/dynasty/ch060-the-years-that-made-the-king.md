Chapter Sixty

The Years That Made the King

Sixteen

One year after Aurelia died, Zee stopped visiting her grave every morning.

Not because he missed her less.

Because grief had changed shape.

At fifteen, he had woken expecting pain and found it waiting.

At sixteen, pain woke with him.

It had become part of the architecture.

He carried it into hunts.

Into meals.

Into sleep.

Into the spaces where laughter still occasionally found him and immediately made him remember the person whose laugh he wanted most.

He no longer spoke to Aurelia’s grave every day.

Sometimes he simply sat beside it.

Sometimes he brought blood on his mouth.

That would have bothered the boy he had been.

It no longer bothered the boy becoming a man.

Tonight, he arrived after killing three hunters who had spent months taking supernatural children from isolated villages and selling their locations to kingdoms that wanted rare bloodlines contained.

Zee had followed them for nine days.

Not because he could not catch them sooner.

Because he wanted everyone attached to the network.

Nanda Parbat had changed the way he hunted.

He did not attack the first man.

He followed him.

Learned where he slept.

Who he paid.

Who he feared.

Which hand reached for a knife.

Which tavern door lacked a guard during rain.

Which accomplice always checked behind himself twice and therefore never noticed the person already ahead.

The first hunter died without seeing Zee.

One knife beneath the ribs.

Upward.

Heart.

Silent.

The second realized what was happening only when Zee appeared inside his locked room.

The man reached for a bell.

Zee’s Vampire speed could have crossed the distance.

He did not need it.

He had already cut the bell cord.

The hunter looked toward the empty rope.

Then Zee.

Fear.

Zee liked that moment now.

The realization.

The mind trying to recalculate around a predator already inside the plan.

“Please.”

Zee studied him.

Blood scent.

Human.

No compulsion marks.

No magical coercion.

No evidence someone else had forced him into this.

“You knew what the children were for?”

The man swallowed.

“Yes.”

“Did you care?”

Silence.

Enough.

Zee killed him.

No speech.

No anger.

No guilt afterward.

The third hunter was a Wolf.

He ran.

That was a mistake.

Zee let him.

For nearly an hour.

Through wet forest.

Across a stream.

Up stone where the hunter deliberately cut his own palm and threw blood east to confuse a Vampire tracker.

Zee smiled when he found it.

Clever.

Not enough.

Wolf Zee took over.

The trail was not blood.

It was crushed fern.

Changed bird calls.

A fox refusing one patch of ground.

Mud compressed beneath one heel harder than the other.

The hunter had an old hip injury.

Zee knew which side he would favor when tired.

He reached the destination first.

Waited inside the abandoned watchtower.

The Wolf entered gasping.

Saw Zee sitting on the windowsill.

Stopped.

“You…”

Zee stood.

“No more running?”

The Wolf attacked.

Claws.

Strength.

Fury.

Zee met him without Witchcraft.

Wolf against Wolf.

His own claws extended.

His stance lowered.

His body read aggression before conscious thought.

Shoulder.

Hip.

Weight.

Predatory instinct.

The enemy lunged.

Zee slipped outside the attack, hooked one arm, drove his knee into the old hip, and broke the joint.

The Wolf screamed.

Zee caught his throat before he fell.

Fangs descended.

The Wolf smelled what was coming.

“No.”

Zee’s crimson eyes darkened.

“You sold children to people who cut them open.”

“I can tell you names.”

“I already have them.”

“Please.”

Zee thought about mercy.

Actually thought.

Then decided no.

He fed.

Wolf blood burned hotter than Human.

By sixteen, Zee could tell far more from supernatural blood than species.

Age.

Health.

Poison.

Recent spell exposure.

Foreign blood in the body.

Old curse residue.

The Wolf had traces of silver poisoning from years earlier.

His body had adapted around it imperfectly.

Zee drank.

His own Crimson blood processed everything differently.

Nothing copied.

Nothing stolen whole.

The silver history entered an organism that had already survived suppression, dragonbane, demonic essence, strange folded spaces, and the blood of six killers.

Hours later, silver touched Zee’s skin and burned less deeply than before.

Not immunity.

Accumulation.

Crimson adaptation.

One more thing that would have killed an ordinary predator becoming less effective against him.

Zee returned to Aurelia’s grave after midnight.

Rez was already there.

Of course.

He sat against the opposite side of the memorial stone with a cup in one hand.

“You smell terrible.”

Zee sat beside him.

“Wolf.”

“I guessed.”

“Then why comment?”

“Brotherly obligation.”

Zee took the cup.

“Mine?”

“It was.”

Zee drank.

Rez stared.

“You are unbelievable.”

“You gave it to me.”

“I set it down.”

“Near me.”

“That is not law.”

“It should be.”

For a moment they were sixteen-year-old idiots beside a grave.

Zee laughed.

Quiet.

Real.

Then footsteps approached.

Ner.

His eyes had changed over the year.

Pure Gold remained.

Always.

But dark-red sometimes moved beneath it when hunger sharpened or Bloodsong rose close to the surface.

His voice had changed too.

Not deeper simply from age.

Richer.

Layered when power entered it.

He could feel injured blood before wounds appeared in sight.

Could sometimes calm a panicked heart with a single tone.

Could make spilled blood reveal who it had belonged to through resonance.

He hated when people treated that as a trick.

Zee had learned not to.

Ner sat on Zee’s other side.

“Three?”

Zee looked over.

“How do you know?”

“I can hear it.”

“Their blood?”

Ner nodded toward Zee’s chest.

“Inside you.”

Zee went still.

That was new.

“What does it sound like?”

“Mostly like you ate badly.”

Rez laughed.

Zee shoved him.

Ner smiled.

Then his expression softened.

“You all right?”

Zee looked at Aurelia’s name.

No one replaced her.

No hand did what hers had done.

No voice reached the same place.

“No.”

Ner nodded.

No attempt to repair the answer.

His shoulder leaned against Zee’s.

Rez remained on the other side.

Far above, the Dragon Link opened faintly.

Zareth was in Drakonis.

Mountain wind.

Heat.

A council chamber.

Then warmth.

The four remained connected even when not together.

But the empty place beside Aurelia’s grave stayed empty.

Zee had stopped expecting otherwise.

That year, the stories changed.

The Blood Hunter stopped meaning a reckless prince who happened to kill predators.

It began meaning something else.

If Zee Darkheart accepted your hunt—

he usually finished it.

⸻

Seventeen

At seventeen, Zee compelled thirty-two Vampires without looking at half of them.

That was not the plan.

The plan had been negotiation.

Rez blamed Zee.

Zee blamed the Vampires.

Ner blamed everyone.

Zareth found the entire situation extremely entertaining until someone shot him with a ballista.

Then he became less charitable.

The Crimson Marsh settlement had spent six months raiding villages beneath Vesper protection, taking blood from humans and witches under the claim that ancient feeding rights predated Lucien’s border agreements.

Rez arrived with signed treaties.

The Vampire lord laughed at them.

“Paper.”

Rez remained seated across the long stone table.

“Law.”

“Your law.”

“Your signature is on it.”

“My grandfather’s.”

“Then your House inherited the benefits and obligations.”

The lord smiled.

“You sound like your father.”

Rez smiled back.

“Thank you.”

Zee stood behind him in black, silent enough that three Vampires repeatedly forgot he had changed position.

Nanda Parbat habits had become instinct.

He watched wrists.

Throats.

Weapons.

Heartbeats.

Exit angles.

The Vampire lord looked toward him.

“The Blood Hunter.”

Zee’s expression did not change.

“Apparently.”

“I expected more.”

“People usually do.”

Ner sat near the wall reading the treaty annotations.

He looked up.

“Your grandfather also agreed that feeding on protected settlements would terminate your marsh rights.”

The lord’s eyes narrowed.

“You are the singing Witch.”

Ner closed the document.

“Nereus.”

Zee’s jaw tightened.

Ner glanced toward him.

He did not need rescuing from a bad title.

Zee let it go.

Progress.

The Vampire lord leaned back.

“We are done.”

Rez nodded.

“Then you stop feeding beyond your territory.”

“No.”

Silence.

Rez stood.

“Then you understood the negotiation incorrectly.”

Every Vampire in the chamber moved.

Thirty-two.

Some old.

Some young.

All armed.

Zee inhaled.

Bloodlines opened to him.

Family relations.

Sired lines.

Age differences.

Three recently fed.

One poisoned.

Two carrying enchanted blood from witches they had bitten.

He knew which one would move first.

Before she did.

Zee whispered:

“Sit.”

The first Vampire stopped.

Only her.

Everyone stared.

Her knees bent.

She sat.

The lord’s face changed.

“Compulsion.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“No.”

He looked across the room.

His predatory presence changed.

Vampire Zee.

No Witchcraft.

No Crimson spell.

His will pressed outward through sound, blood, fear, and the instincts every Vampire understood when a stronger predator entered territory.

“Everyone.”

Twenty heads turned.

Twelve did not.

Zee did not raise his voice.

“Sit down.”

Twenty obeyed.

The others resisted.

Zee felt every resistance separately.

Old minds.

Wards.

Training.

One wore a charm blocking eye-based domination.

Zee noticed.

Interesting.

His eyes shifted toward the Vampire with the charm.

The man deliberately stared at the floor.

Zee spoke.

“You too.”

The Vampire’s body jerked.

He remained standing.

Barely.

Zee felt the command catch somewhere beyond eyes.

Voice.

Blood.

Predatory hierarchy.

Not perfected.

Not effortless.

But there.

The Vampire’s knees trembled.

Then hit stone.

The remaining eleven reacted.

Weapons came free.

Rez sighed.

“You could have waited.”

“They were going to attack.”

“I know.”

“Then why complain?”

“Tradition.”

The chamber erupted.

Zee moved.

Assassin technique inside Vampire speed.

One throat struck.

One elbow broken.

A pressure point beneath one jaw shut down another attacker’s balance before supernatural strength finished the problem.

Rez turned the room into geometry.

“Left!”

Zee moved left before asking.

Blue-White Mana froze two floor sections, controlling movement.

“Ner, upper balcony!”

Pure Gold split into six narrow boundaries, turning the balcony into a cage.

Zareth crashed through the outer wall because the ballista crew had made the poor decision of involving him.

Stone exploded.

Rez shouted:

“There was a door!”

The Dragon Link returned absolute indifference.

Zee laughed while disarming a Vampire.

Then the Vampire lord tried escaping.

Zee smelled it before he moved.

Fear.

Blood.

Intent.

Wolf pursuit joined Vampire blood sense.

Zee disappeared.

He caught the lord outside the settlement half a mile away.

No magic.

The lord stared.

“How?”

Zee smiled.

“You ran where frightened things run.”

The lord attacked.

Zee ended it in six movements.

He could have killed him.

Instead he compelled him.

The lord resisted.

Hard.

Zee pushed.

Blood ran from one nostril.

Not the lord’s.

Zee’s.

The domination strained something inside his Vampire nature.

He enjoyed the contest.

“Look at me.”

The lord squeezed his eyes shut.

Zee’s smile deepened.

“No.”

He stepped close.

“You do not get to hide from me that easily.”

The command struck again.

The Vampire’s eyes opened against his will.

There.

A threshold.

Not ordinary compulsion anymore.

Still imperfect.

Still needing focus.

But evolving.

“Kneel.”

The lord knelt.

“Release every captive.”

“Yes.”

“Honor the treaty.”

“Yes.”

“Remember exactly how helpless this felt.”

The lord’s face twisted.

“Yes.”

Zee released him.

He could have drained him.

Wanted to.

Did not.

Not mercy.

Utility.

Fear would travel faster if the lord survived.

By winter, three settlements surrendered captives before Zee ever crossed their borders.

Reputation had become a weapon.

Zee began using it deliberately.

⸻

The relationship with Ner changed that same year.

Not during a battle.

Not while someone was bleeding.

Not under moonlight with some ridiculous speech.

It happened in Vesper’s western kitchen at two in the morning.

Ner was stealing cake.

Zee caught him.

“You said you were going to sleep.”

Ner froze with half a slice in his hand.

“You were not supposed to be here.”

“I live here.”

“So do I.”

“That weakens your argument.”

Ner took a bite.

Zee stared.

“You are eating my cake.”

“Lucien’s cake.”

“Everything in Vesper eventually becomes mine through Rez.”

“That is not how inheritance works.”

“You sure?”

“Yes.”

Zee stepped closer.

Ner did not move.

Seventeen years of friendship sat between them.

Childhood.

Blood.

Death.

Aurelia.

Ner’s second heartbeat beginning where none should have.

Countless almost-moments.

Looks.

Touches.

Things everyone except them had stopped pretending not to understand.

Zee reached for the cake.

Ner moved it away.

Their bodies ended up closer.

Neither laughed.

Zee looked at his mouth.

Ner noticed.

“Zee.”

“What?”

“You are doing it again.”

“Looking?”

“Yes.”

“I know.”

Ner stopped breathing for half a heartbeat.

“That is new.”

Zee’s crimson eyes lifted.

“I got tired of pretending.”

Ner’s expression softened.

“Took you long enough.”

Zee frowned.

“You knew?”

“Everyone knew.”

“I hate everyone.”

“I know.”

Ner set the cake down.

Then kissed him.

Simple.

Brief.

Enough to silence Zee Darkheart completely.

Ner pulled back.

Zee stared.

“You—”

“Yes.”

“You did that.”

“Very observant.”

Zee kissed him back.

Longer.

Still seventeen.

Awkward enough that their noses bumped and Ner laughed against his mouth.

Zee laughed too.

No grand declaration.

They already loved each other.

That had been the problem.

Now they stopped lying about what kind of love had grown beside friendship.

Later, Rez found them sitting on the kitchen floor sharing the stolen cake.

He looked at their joined hands.

Then at both of them.

“No.”

Zee smiled.

“What?”

“I waited years.”

“For?”

“This.”

Ner covered his face.

Rez pointed.

“I deserve recognition.”

“You deserve to leave,” Zee said.

Rez stole the last piece of cake and ran.

Zee chased him through the palace.

Ner followed laughing.

For one night, grief did not disappear.

It simply made room.

⸻

Eighteen

At eighteen, Zee stopped living at Vesper full-time.

He did not leave because Lucien asked.

Lucien never did.

He did not leave because Rez wanted distance.

Rez hated the decision.

He did not leave because Ner or Zareth had become less important.

The opposite.

He left because he finally understood the difference between being loved somewhere and belonging beneath someone else’s roof forever.

Vesper was home.

It was not his kingdom.

Darkheart was his blood.

It was not yet his rule.

Drakonis loved Zareth.

Not Zee.

Good.

Zee needed something that existed because people chose him.

The first place was ugly.

An abandoned fortress on no kingdom’s favored border.

Half the northern wall had collapsed.

The well tasted metallic.

The towers leaned.

There were rats large enough that Zee briefly considered recruiting them as guards.

Rez stared at the ruin.

“This?”

Zee stood inside the broken courtyard.

“Yes.”

“There are better forts.”

“They belong to people.”

“This one belongs to someone.”

“Who?”

Rez checked the parchment.

“A dead baron whose heirs abandoned it sixty years ago.”

Zee smiled.

“Exactly.”

Ner walked through a doorway that immediately collapsed behind him.

He looked back.

“No.”

Zee laughed.

Zareth landed on a nearby hill because the courtyard could not hold him.

The Link carried disgust.

“This is temporary.”

The Dragon remained unconvinced.

Zee looked around.

No banners.

No throne.

No inherited expectation.

Perfect.

People arrived anyway.

First, a Witch family driven from a southern kingdom because one daughter had demon ancestry.

Zee allowed them inside.

Then three Wolves who refused a clan lord’s order to kill mixed-blood infants.

Then two Vampires who had broken from a nest feeding on unwilling humans.

Then mercenaries.

Not saints.

People.

Some needed protection.

Some wanted employment.

Some wanted Zee.

That distinction mattered.

He made rules.

Few.

Simple.

No slavery.

No trafficking.

No forced feeding on people under his protection.

No hunting children.

No killing inside the fortress unless defense demanded it or Zee authorized judgment.

No one belonged to him merely because he protected them.

And if someone brought danger through the gates knowingly—

they answered for it.

Rez read the first version.

“This is not enough law.”

Zee smiled.

“It is enough for forty people.”

“It will not be enough for four hundred.”

“Then I will write more.”

Rez stared.

Zee looked toward him.

“What?”

“You are planning for four hundred.”

Zee glanced across the courtyard.

People repaired stone.

A Witch reinforced the well.

A Vampire and a Wolf argued over roof beams.

Ner stood teaching two younger witches how not to walk directly through a Golden boundary while he repaired a collapsed arch.

Zareth slept outside while three children dared each other to touch his tail.

The Dragon knew.

He pretended not to.

Zee’s face softened.

“Maybe.”

Rez smiled.

“There he is.”

“Who?”

“The king.”

Zee looked at him.

“No.”

“Not yet.”

Zee’s expression darkened.

“Do not call me that.”

Rez nodded.

He understood why.

Victor.

Crown.

Control.

Not yet.

Still, people began coming.

Not because law said Zee owned them.

Because his name meant something.

To predators, threat.

To people no crown wanted, possibility.

He accepted both uses.

By eighteen, Zee could enter a settlement without guards and still change the entire mood.

His horns had matured farther, curving back in larger crimson-black arcs.

Permanent scales covered portions of his shoulders, ribs, forearms, and upper spine.

His body had filled into adult shape without being finished—taller, broader, built by supernatural movement rather than ornamental training.

His Dragon durability had increased enough that an ordinary sword shattered against one scale patch during a border fight.

His fourth nature still did not wake completely.

No wings.

No full Dragon.

But the shoulder structures beneath his back had become impossible to mistake.

Zareth noticed every change.

Through the Link came pride.

And impatience.

Zee sent back:

I know.

No spoken telepathy.

Just shared understanding.

That year, Zee took no crown.

But eighty-seven people began calling the ruined fortress home.

Not Darkheart subjects.

Not Vesper refugees.

His people.

That frightened Victor more than any battle had.

⸻

Nineteen

At nineteen, Zee killed twelve people before breakfast and slept perfectly well that night.

They were not innocent.

That distinction remained.

They belonged to a supernatural raiding company that had burned two villages after the villagers refused to provide bodies for blood sport.

Zee did not arrive with an army.

Armies were loud.

He arrived alone.

Rez hated the plan.

Ner hated that he did not hate it.

Zareth thought it was efficient.

The raid camp contained thirty-seven fighters.

Zee killed twelve.

Compelled nine to disarm each other.

Drove six into fleeing routes where allied Wolves captured them.

Left seven unconscious through pressure points and poisoned needles.

Three surrendered before he touched them.

He spared those three.

Choice.

The first death happened in darkness.

Knife.

Carotid.

Zee caught the body before it hit dirt.

The second was a Witch.

One precise force spell shifted her casting hand into the path of her own binding sigil.

Her magic locked her arm.

Zee broke her neck.

No flourish.

Third.

Wolf.

Tracked by scent after he fled beneath a suppression field.

Wolf Zee moved low through underbrush, silent and relentless.

When the raider turned to fight, Zee met him with claws.

Not Witchcraft.

Not Vampire speed.

He wanted the hunt.

He enjoyed it.

The raider realized that halfway through.

Fear changed his scent.

Zee smiled.

The fourth and fifth were Vampires.

He compelled one to freeze.

The other carried an anti-compulsion charm.

Zee smelled Witch blood woven into the enchantment.

He cut the cord without touching the Vampire’s body.

Then compelled him too.

“Do not move.”

The Vampire’s muscles locked.

Zee circled.

“You thought that would stop me?”

The man whispered:

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Zee fed.

Not completely.

Enough to weaken.

Enough to taste bloodline.

The Vampire had consumed demon blood recently.

Zee recognized the foreign layer instantly now.

His blood sense had become frighteningly precise through years of feeding.

He could distinguish possession residue from voluntary ingestion.

Curse from inheritance.

Poison from disease.

Foreign magic carried in blood from magic produced by the body itself.

The Vampire lied.

Zee smelled it.

Killed him.

Fed fully.

Crimson adaptation moved through Zee’s regeneration.

Years of Vampire blood had changed how fast his body recovered after severe injury.

Not ordinary healing.

His blood now prioritized structural damage almost intelligently.

Bones first if movement mattered.

Blood volume first if feeding had depleted him.

Poison isolation before tissue repair.

His body was learning survival around him.

Sixth.

Demon.

It hid inside another man’s shadow.

That trick would have fooled Zee at fifteen.

At nineteen, demon blood history had sharpened Zee’s presence perception.

Shadow wrong.

No heartbeat.

No scent.

Still there.

He stabbed it before it emerged.

The Demon begged when Zee pinned it.

Actually begged.

Once, begging would have complicated everything.

Now Zee listened for information.

There was none.

“Please.”

Zee looked at the burned village behind them.

“No.”

He fed.

Demon blood remained unpleasant.

His body remained fascinated.

Years of consuming supernatural enemies had made him harder to suppress, harder to poison, harder to bind, and increasingly resistant to hostile environments that depended on corrupting the body’s supernatural balance.

No borrowed demonic tricks.

No copied spell.

Only Zee becoming more difficult to affect the same way twice.

Seven through twelve died through combinations.

Assassin silence carrying Vampire speed.

Wolf instinct identifying an ambush before Witch sight confirmed it.

Crimson Mana redirecting one arrow into another.

Dragon durability allowing him to deliberately accept a blade so he could trap the attacker’s arm against his own scales.

Then fangs.

Always eventually blood.

When the surviving commander surrendered, Zee stood over him covered in red.

“Mercy.”

Zee crouched.

“Why?”

The commander stared.

“What?”

“Give me a reason.”

“I surrendered.”

“That is a reason not to kill you during the fight.”

The man swallowed.

“What else do you want?”

“Something that makes you useful alive.”

Silence.

Zee smiled faintly.

There.

The begging changed.

Not moral appeal.

Information.

Names.

Routes.

Employers.

One employer had ordered the village burned.

Zee listened.

Then spared the commander.

Not because mercy was good.

Because a living witness would destroy the employer more efficiently than a corpse.

Rez met him outside the camp.

“You kept him alive.”

“Yes.”

“I am proud.”

Zee stared.

“Do not make it weird.”

Rez laughed.

Ner arrived minutes later, Pure Gold tracing through the prisoners’ blood to identify which among them had actually participated in the killings and which had been forced into the company under blood-contract coercion.

His Bloodsong had grown frightening too.

He could hear lies in circulation sometimes—not thoughts, not magical truth detection, but changes in blood resonance when emotion and intent sharpened.

He made mistakes.

Corrected them.

Never pretended certainty.

One prisoner had been compelled into service.

Ner proved it.

Zee spared him immediately.

Another had volunteered.

Killed children personally.

Zee learned that through evidence, not instinct alone.

He executed him.

Ner watched.

Did not stop him.

Did not approve either.

Later, under canvas while rain struck the camp, Ner washed blood from Zee’s hands.

Not because Zee needed help.

Because he wanted to.

Zee looked at him.

“You hate when I kill slowly.”

Ner continued cleaning one knuckle.

“Yes.”

“You did not say anything.”

“You already knew.”

Zee’s expression changed.

Ner looked up.

“I am not here to issue permission.”

Good.

That was why their love survived Zee’s descent.

Ner did not exist to fix him.

Zee did not need Ner to pretend every choice was right.

They kissed with blood still drying on Zee’s throat.

Gentle.

Then Zee went outside and ordered an execution.

Both were him.

That year, some kingdoms stopped calling Zee a prince entirely.

They called him lord.

Hunter.

Monster.

Protector.

Blood King, though he had no crown.

Zee corrected none of them.

Names were useful only when people acted differently after hearing them.

Increasingly, they did.

⸻

Twenty

At twenty, Victor finally understood that Zee had become impossible to contain without touching him.

And Victor had promised himself, years ago, never to touch that kind of violence again.

Not after the infant.

Not after watching death fail.

So he used House Darkheart.

Again.

Only now the House resisted.

The first open fracture came when Lord Merek Darkheart refused Victor’s order to expel three families who had sworn protection to Zee’s growing fortress.

Victor called it treason.

Merek called it prudence.

Two more nobles sided with Merek.

Then five.

Then twelve.

Not all loved Zee.

Some feared him more than Victor.

Some simply believed the future had shifted.

Victor saw every vote as another line of an ancient prophecy tightening around his throat.

He would sometimes wake hearing the words.

Every blade raised against him shall sharpen what it seeks to destroy.

The first blade had been Victor’s.

That memory never left.

Then reports came.

Zee survived poisons that once would have crippled him.

Zee broke supernatural compulsions faster.

Zee fed from things ordinary Vampires avoided.

Zee’s hunters crossed borders where Darkheart soldiers no longer could.

Mixed supernatural families traveled voluntarily toward his fortress.

Dragons spoke his name without contempt.

Wolves called him predator and meant respect.

Witches whispered that Crimson Mana responded differently now.

And Victor remembered another fragment.

He shall not inherit the world as it was given.

He stopped sleeping.

No one knew why.

Not his council.

Not Darkheart witches.

Not even the priests who thought the King had simply grown paranoid with age.

Only Victor knew the entire prophecy.

Only Victor understood why every report felt like a countdown.

Zee knew none of it.

He spent his twentieth year doing exactly what Victor feared without ever knowing fear had predicted the shape.

He expanded the fortress.

Not into a kingdom.

Not yet.

Into territory.

People patrolled under Zee’s mark.

Not a crown.

A simple Crimson cut across black cloth.

No ancient symbolism.

No prophecy.

Just something visible at night.

Rez mocked the design.

“It looks like somebody spilled wine on a funeral banner.”

Zee stared.

“You helped choose it.”

“I was seventeen.”

“You were eighteen.”

“Even worse.”

Ner liked it.

Zareth tried burning one and discovered the material Zee’s witches used was more fire-resistant than expected.

The Link carried offense.

Zee laughed for several minutes.

At twenty, laughter happened less often.

That made everyone notice when it came.

He was fully dangerous now.

Not because he had reached his final strength.

Far from it.

Because experience had caught up to power.

Zee no longer entered fights to discover what he could do.

He entered knowing.

Blood tracking.

Wolf pursuit.

Mass compulsion.

Precise Witchcraft.

Dragon durability.

Assassin skill.

Crimson adaptation.

He used whichever nature solved the problem cleanest.

Sometimes none required combination.

Vampire Zee could dismantle a room alone.

Wolf Zee could hunt for days.

Witch Zee could control a battlefield with movements small enough enemies did not realize spells had been cast.

Dragon Zee could stand through force that broke stone and answer with Crimson heat that made older Dragons take him seriously.

Still no wings.

Still no completed awakening.

Still unfinished.

That fact annoyed him less than it once had.

He had learned unfinished did not mean weak.

Then Victor made the decision that ended everything.

He declared every Darkheart noble, soldier, witch, servant, or blood-relative who swore direct loyalty to Zee guilty of treason.

He ordered their lands seized.

Their titles stripped.

Their children removed from succession.

And those already inside Castle Darkheart who had publicly supported Zee—

imprisoned.

Not killed.

Victor had learned some lessons.

Containment.

Always containment.

Forty-three people disappeared into Darkheart cells in one day.

Lord Merek among them.

The order reached Zee at sunset.

He read it once.

Rez stood opposite the table.

Ner beside him.

Zareth outside, the Dragon Link already darkening with anger.

Theron and Seraphine waited near the wall.

Zee placed the decree down.

No shouting.

No broken furniture.

That version of him was years gone.

“What do you want to do?” Rez asked.

Zee looked toward Castle Darkheart on the map.

Victor had spent twenty years deciding what happened to people because they stood too close to his son.

Aurelia was gone.

No one stood between them now.

Zee touched the seal.

“Bring everyone home.”

Ner watched him.

“Darkheart?”

Zee nodded.

Theron’s expression sharpened.

Seraphine went still.

Rez asked the obvious question.

“How?”

Zee looked up.

His crimson eyes held no hesitation.

“I am going to my father.”

“Negotiation?”

“No.”

Silence.

Zee folded the decree.

He did not smile.

Did not threaten.

Did not need to.

“The old reign ends.”

Outside, Zareth roared.

The sound rolled across the unfinished fortress and through the people who had chosen Zee without a crown.

Some looked toward him.

Some bowed.

Zee hated that once.

At twenty, he let them.

Far away in Castle Darkheart, Victor woke from a dream with the final words of the prophecy echoing through his chamber.

The world shall break before it teaches him how to die.

He sat alone beneath the crown and understood that the future was no longer approaching.

His son was.

And this time Aurelia would not be there to save either of them.
