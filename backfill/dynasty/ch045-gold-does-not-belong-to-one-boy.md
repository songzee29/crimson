Chapter Forty-Five

Gold Does Not Belong to One Boy

Zee read the line twice.

Then a third time.

Pure Golden Mana confirmed at breach site.

“No.”

Lucien looked toward him.

Zee shook his head.

“Ner did not do it.”

Aurelia stood beside the council table.

“No one said he did.”

“They will.”

That was the problem.

Pure Golden Mana belonged to Nereus.

Everyone who mattered knew that.

At least everyone they trusted.

If another Golden signature appeared beneath western coven grain stores, then either the report was wrong—

or something more complicated had happened.

Zee’s first instinct was to leave immediately.

He felt it.

Named it.

Then did not obey it.

“Who sent this?”

Lucien handed him the seal.

“Rez.”

Zee checked it himself.

Vesper mark.

Blue-White authentication line.

Rez.

Real.

“What else?”

“Nothing yet.”

Zareth’s Link tightened.

Question.

Zee sent the concept.

Ner.

Gold.

Wrong.

Zareth’s Mana flared once.

Concern.

Then an image.

Fly west.

Zee almost agreed.

Aurelia saw.

“No.”

He looked at her.

“Zareth can reach them before morning.”

“And arrive over a witch coven with a Dragon Crown Prince while they are already suspicious of Nereus?”

Zee stopped.

Bad.

Very bad.

Zareth understood too.

His irritation rolled through the Link.

Zee sighed.

“Fine.”

Lucien looked toward him.

“Go sleep.”

Zee stared.

“No.”

“Then sit.”

“I can do that.”

“Without pacing.”

Zee looked toward the floor.

He had already worn a line into the rug.

Annoying.

He sat.

For three hours.

The longest three hours of his life.

Then Rez came through the council doors just before dawn.

Alone.

Zee stood so fast the chair fell backward.

“Where is Ner?”

“Alive.”

“Where?”

“Still west.”

“Why?”

Rez crossed to the water table and drank before answering.

That alone told Zee this was bad.

“Ner found the breach.”

“I know.”

“He stabilized it.”

“I know.”

“The coven ward-master accused him of creating it.”

Zee’s jaw tightened.

Rez continued.

“I stopped that from becoming an arrest.”

“How?”

“Politics.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It is at four in the morning.”

Zee glared.

Rez sighed.

“The covens need Vesper grain purchases.”

“We need their supply.”

“They cannot openly imprison Lucien’s protected guest without evidence.”

“And?”

“And Ner volunteered to stay until they determine what happened.”

Zee stared.

“He volunteered?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because if he leaves, it looks like guilt.”

“He does not owe them—”

“I know.”

Rez’s voice sharpened.

“He knows too.”

That stopped Zee.

Rez looked exhausted.

Not frightened.

Angry.

Different.

“The problem is not that the signature resembles Ner.”

Zee’s stomach tightened.

“What?”

“It is Ner.”

Silence.

Aurelia stepped forward.

“What exactly does that mean?”

Rez pulled a folded ward sheet from his coat.

“The Golden Mana in the breach matches his.”

Zee shook his head.

“That is impossible.”

Ner was unique.

Pure Gold belonged to him.

Rez looked toward Aurelia.

“The ward-master says the breach was opened approximately two days before Ner arrived.”

Zee froze.

Two days.

Impossible.

Not by Ner.

He had been in Vesper.

Everyone knew.

Aurelia asked, “Stored Mana?”

Rez nodded slowly.

“That is their working theory.”

Zee looked toward him.

“Someone took his magic.”

“Not all of it.”

“Copied?”

Rez frowned.

“Ner says no.”

Good.

That matched everything Zee knew about rare power.

No direct copying assumptions.

Rez continued.

“He thinks someone preserved a piece of Golden Mana from an older spell.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“When?”

“That is the problem.”

Ner had used Golden Mana publicly for years.

Barriers.

Seals.

Combat.

Rescues.

Political meetings.

Any number of people could have collected residue if they understood how.

His uniqueness had become reputation.

Reputation created vulnerability.

For once, the threat belonged to Ner.

Not Zee.

Zee’s horns warmed.

Anger.

Focused.

“Who benefits?”

Rez smiled without humor.

“Now you sound like Crown Council.”

“Answer.”

“The western covens lose grain.”

“Vesper loses access.”

“Ner gets blamed.”

“Trade collapses.”

Aurelia looked toward the map.

“Which benefits the Northern Council.”

Rez nodded.

“Possibly.”

Zee frowned.

“Renn is imprisoned.”

“Renn is one councilor.”

There.

Larger world.

Not one mastermind.

Not one villain controlling every thread.

People sharing interests without sharing loyalty.

Aurelia said, “Do we have evidence?”

“No.”

Zee hated that.

Because evidence was what separated justice from whatever his anger wanted.

Rez continued.

“Ner asked for you.”

Zee looked up.

That changed everything.

“When?”

“Now.”

⸻

They traveled west without Zareth.

The Dragon Prince had his own problem.

A Drakonis messenger arrived before they left carrying an urgent summons tied to the same northern trade disruption. Dragon-controlled mountain roads were suddenly being pressured by merchants seeking alternate passage.

Zareth could have ignored it.

He did not.

He looked toward Zee.

The Link carried conflict.

Friend.

Duty.

Both.

Zee touched his snout.

“Go.”

Zareth hesitated.

Zee smiled faintly.

“I mean it.”

An image came.

Ner.

Danger.

Zee.

Then trust.

Zareth launched east.

Zee traveled west with Aurelia, Rez, Caldrin, and six Vesper guards.

No Dragon.

No full core four.

Different problem.

Good.

The western covens occupied a series of fortified hill settlements surrounded by cultivated fields and old stone ward towers.

Zee had visited twice before.

Neither visit ended comfortably.

Today was worse.

The coven gates opened for Rez.

Not Zee.

That was deliberate.

Zee noticed.

Did not care.

Much.

Ner stood inside the main ward hall with three senior witches around him.

He looked tired.

Golden Mana circled both wrists in thin restraint bands.

Zee stopped.

Everything inside him sharpened.

Ner saw.

“Don’t.”

Zee stared at the bindings.

“They restrained you.”

“I agreed.”

“You agreed badly.”

Ner almost smiled.

One of the senior witches stepped forward.

“Prince Darkheart—”

Zee looked at her.

She stopped.

Aurelia touched Zee’s arm.

Not restraint.

Reminder.

He breathed.

Anger.

Not command.

“Why is he bound?”

The witch answered carefully.

“Because until we understand how his Mana signature opened the lower ward, we cannot allow unrestricted casting.”

Ner lifted both hands.

“They are symbolic.”

Zee looked closer.

They were.

Weak enough Ner could break them instantly.

That mattered.

He had chosen to wear them.

Zee still hated them.

Ner looked toward Rez.

“You took forever.”

“It is far.”

“You are a witch.”

“I cannot teleport kingdoms.”

Ner sighed.

“Disappointing.”

Then his gaze shifted to Zee.

Their eyes held.

Something inside Zee unclenched.

“You okay?”

“Yes.”

“Truth.”

Ner looked down at the Golden restraints.

“Angry.”

“Good.”

One coven elder frowned.

“Good?”

Zee looked at her.

“He tells the truth when he is angry.”

Ner smiled faintly.

“Usually.”

Aurelia stepped toward the breach map.

“Show us.”

⸻

The grain storehouse sat above an old boundary chamber.

No prophecy.

No ancient destiny.

Just infrastructure.

Witch kingdoms built wards around food stores for obvious reasons.

Fire.

Poison.

Theft.

Rot.

The lower chamber contained six stone anchors.

One had been opened.

Golden residue remained inside.

Ner stood several feet away.

“It is mine.”

Zee looked toward him.

“You are sure?”

“Yes.”

No hesitation.

That bothered Zee more than denial would have.

Ner crouched.

“The structure came from one of my older barrier patterns.”

“How old?”

“Years.”

Rez looked around.

“Can you tell which one?”

Ner closed his eyes.

Pure Golden Mana rose from his hands despite the restraint bands.

The coven elders tensed.

Ner ignored them.

A thin line extended toward the residue.

Touched.

His expression changed.

“Oh.”

Zee moved closer.

“What?”

“I know this.”

“From where?”

Ner looked toward him.

“Vesper.”

Everyone went still.

Rez frowned.

“When?”

“Three years ago.”

He touched the residue again.

“South wall reconstruction.”

Zee remembered.

A storm had broken part of Vesper’s outer ward network.

Ner had spent three days helping rebuild it.

Dozens of workers.

Witches.

Contract laborers.

Foreign masons.

Officials.

Not secret.

Not guarded like a unique magical ritual because at thirteen they had not yet understood how valuable magical residue could become.

Ner stood.

“Someone harvested leftover Mana.”

A coven elder looked horrified.

“Can that be done?”

Ner looked at her.

“Apparently.”

Zee frowned.

“You sound annoyed.”

“I am.”

“This is your power.”

“Yes.”

“And someone used it.”

“Yes.”

His voice hardened.

“Which means I decide what happens next.”

Zee looked at him.

There it was.

Not Zee’s hunt.

Ner’s.

Good.

Hard.

Necessary.

“What do you want?”

Ner looked toward the six anchors.

“Find the carrier.”

“How?”

Ner smiled.

The kind of smile Zee liked for dangerous reasons.

“They preserved my Mana.”

He lifted one hand.

“So they preserved my mistakes too.”

Rez’s eyebrows rose.

“That sounds useful.”

“It is.”

Ner touched the residue.

Pure Gold spread into it.

Not overpowering.

Comparing.

A tiny flicker of pale green appeared inside the Golden signature.

Zee saw.

“What is that?”

“Copper.”

Caldrin frowned.

“Metal?”

“Dust.”

Ner looked toward the floor.

“South wall reconstruction used copper binding powder.”

Rez understood.

“Meaning whoever stored the Mana took it directly from that work.”

“Yes.”

Aurelia looked toward the coven elders.

“Who had access to both Vesper reconstruction crews and northern trade interests?”

Silence.

One elder went pale.

Rez saw.

“Who?”

She hesitated.

Zee smelled fear.

Not guilt necessarily.

Fear.

Important difference.

The elder answered.

“The Brannic Trade House.”

Lucien’s records had mentioned them before.

Neutral merchants.

Not villains.

Not yet.

Rez frowned.

“They supplied stone to Vesper.”

“And transport grain for the north,” the elder said.

There.

Connection.

Not proof.

Ner stood.

“I want their depot.”

The elder stared.

“You cannot simply enter a neutral merchant depot.”

Zee smiled.

Ner looked toward him.

“No.”

Zee’s smile widened.

“We can ask.”

Rez laughed.

“That was not what I expected.”

Zee shrugged.

“I am growing.”

Aurelia looked suspicious.

Ner knew him too well.

“What is the second half of that sentence?”

Zee’s smile sharpened.

“If they say no, we know more.”

⸻

Brannic Trade House said yes.

That complicated everything.

Their depot manager welcomed inspection.

Opened records.

Allowed Ner to test storage rooms.

Provided employee lists.

Offered tea.

Zee distrusted him for being cooperative.

Ner told him that was irrational.

Zee agreed.

Then kept distrusting him.

Three hours produced nothing.

No Golden residue.

No hidden containers.

No suspicious correspondence.

Rez found ordinary tax fraud.

The depot manager looked deeply embarrassed.

Rez looked delighted.

“That is not our problem today.”

“Thank you.”

“Vesper’s revenue office may feel differently.”

The man stopped smiling.

Ner searched the final storage hall.

Nothing.

Zee leaned against a crate.

“So they are innocent.”

Rez answered.

“Of this.”

Ner frowned.

“Maybe.”

Zee looked toward him.

Ner was staring at the floor.

“What?”

“Copper.”

A faint dust line ran beneath one crate.

Normal.

Except Ner followed it.

To a loading door.

Then outside.

Toward wagon tracks.

He crouched.

Gold moved across the earth.

One wheel rut responded.

Not with Golden Mana.

With residue from a preservation vessel.

Ner stood.

“Transport.”

Rez looked down the road.

“Where?”

The depot manager checked records.

“That wagon left yesterday.”

“For?”

He swallowed.

“Northern Council diplomatic stores.”

Zee’s face changed.

There.

Not one person.

A route.

A mechanism.

Evidence.

Ner looked toward him.

“Do not smile like that.”

“I am not.”

“You are.”

Rez sighed.

“Both of you can argue while riding.”

The diplomatic wagon was half a day ahead.

They caught it before sunset.

No dramatic chase.

No army.

One wagon.

Four guards.

One clerk.

The guards surrendered immediately when Rez identified himself.

The clerk did not.

He ran.

Zee caught him in twelve seconds.

The man tripped across a dry field.

Zee landed in front of him.

The clerk stumbled backward.

“No.”

Zee tilted his head.

People had been saying that a lot lately.

“Why did you run?”

“I panicked.”

“Badly.”

The clerk reached for a knife.

Zee sighed.

“Worse.”

He caught the wrist.

Twisted.

Stopped before bone broke.

That was deliberate.

The man stared.

Zee stared back.

He could feel how easy it would be.

One movement.

One scream.

That dark satisfaction waited.

Interested.

Zee released the wrist.

“Do not make me regret that.”

The clerk dropped the knife.

Rez arrived moments later.

Ner behind him.

Ner opened the wagon.

Inside were six preservation jars.

Five empty.

One still containing a trace of Pure Golden Mana.

Ner went completely still.

No joke.

No smile.

Someone had bottled pieces of him.

Zee looked toward the clerk.

Anger became something colder.

“Who?”

The clerk shook his head.

“I only transport.”

Rez approached.

“Names.”

“I do not know.”

Ner touched one jar.

His face changed.

“Truth.”

Zee looked at him.

“You can tell?”

“The jar was sealed against handler contamination.”

Ner studied the clerk.

“He probably never touched the contents.”

That saved the man’s wrist.

Maybe more.

Rez opened the paperwork.

No buyer name.

Only a council storage mark.

Then a second seal.

The same office Renn had used.

Not Renn personally.

His administrative network.

Rez exhaled.

“This is bigger than him.”

Aurelia, arriving with Caldrin and the guards, looked at the jars.

“Not necessarily bigger.”

Rez looked toward her.

“Wider.”

Better word.

One corrupt councilor could be removed.

A bureaucracy that had learned to profit from his methods was harder.

Zee looked toward Ner.

“What do you want to do?”

Ner stared at the jar.

For once he looked fifteen.

Not powerful.

Not rare.

Not Golden.

Fifteen.

Angry that someone had taken something of his without asking.

“Break them.”

Zee nodded.

Ner lifted the first jar.

Pure Golden Mana entered it.

The preserved residue responded.

Then folded inward.

Not released.

Not reused.

Destroyed.

One jar.

Then another.

Then another.

The final trace vanished.

Ner exhaled.

Gone.

No one else would use those pieces of him.

The clerk looked terrified.

Zee turned.

“Take him.”

Rez frowned.

“To Vesper?”

“No.”

Everyone looked at Zee.

He pointed toward the western coven guards approaching from the road.

“The breach happened in their territory.”

Rez’s expression changed.

Approval.

Different moral code.

Not Zee decides everything.

Not Vesper owns every consequence.

Jurisdiction mattered when it protected people instead of shielding predators.

The clerk sagged with relief.

Zee noticed.

That irritated him.

“You look happy.”

The man froze.

Zee stepped closer.

“You helped move stolen magic.”

“I did not know.”

“Maybe.”

Zee’s horns warmed.

He could hurt him.

Would enjoy it if the man proved complicit.

That was exactly why he did not decide yet.

“Let them find out.”

The clerk stared.

Zee turned away.

Ner watched him.

No smile.

Something quieter.

Proud, maybe.

Zee looked toward him.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You have the face.”

Ner laughed.

“Your face.”

Zee smiled.

The western covens took custody of the clerk and wagon.

The grain agreement survived.

More than survived.

By nightfall, the covens agreed to sell directly to Vesper under emergency humanitarian terms, publicly separating the trade from Northern Council pressure.

That mattered.

Rez sent the agreement home under royal seal.

Vesper villages would eat.

The Northern Council had lost one weapon.

And Nereus Azrion had discovered something ugly about reputation.

Once the world knew your power was rare enough to matter, people did not always need to possess you.

Sometimes they only needed what you left behind.

That night Ner sat beside the road fire turning a tiny sphere of Golden Mana between his fingers.

Zee sat beside him.

Not touching.

Close.

“You okay?”

“No.”

Zee nodded.

Ner looked over.

“That is all?”

“You hate when people tell you what you should feel.”

Ner smiled faintly.

“True.”

“So be angry.”

“I am.”

“Good.”

Ner looked at the Gold in his hand.

“I used to think being the only one meant no one could take it.”

Zee understood that feeling too well.

“They did not take you.”

Ner looked at him.

Zee continued.

“They stole residue.”

“Used it badly.”

“Blamed you.”

“Yes.”

Zee’s face hardened.

“Still not you.”

Ner was quiet.

Then:

“You are getting less terrible at this.”

Zee looked offended.

“I am excellent at this.”

“No.”

“Rude.”

Ner smiled.

Their shoulders touched.

This time neither moved.

Across the fire, Rez saw.

He looked toward Caldrin.

Caldrin followed his gaze.

Rez immediately whispered, “Do not.”

Caldrin smiled.

“I said nothing.”

Rez groaned.

Zee heard.

“Stop talking about us.”

Ner looked toward him.

“Us?”

Zee froze.

Rez covered his face.

Caldrin turned away to laugh.

Zee’s horns heated.

Ner looked up at them.

Then back at him.

A slow smile appeared.

Zee stared into the fire.

“I hate all of you.”

Ner leaned his shoulder more firmly against Zee’s.

“No, you don’t.”

Zee did not answer.

Because that was becoming the problem.

He did not.

Not even close.

And somewhere far east, the Dragon Link stirred with Zareth’s distant amusement as if he had somehow felt exactly enough to become unbearable about it later.
