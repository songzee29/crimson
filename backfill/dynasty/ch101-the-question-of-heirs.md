Chapter One Hundred One

The Question of Heirs

The conversation began because Rez insulted everyone.

Which was normal.

Darkheart hosted its first formal summit with Nhalaris that winter.

Vesper attended.

Drakonis attended.

No alliances merged.

No crowns surrendered authority.

The entire point was cooperation without ownership.

Rez called it:

“A room full of powerful people agreeing not to become one country.”

Nymeris looked at him.

“That may be the first useful description anyone has given.”

Zee frowned.

“I explained it better.”

“No,” Ner said.

Zareth nodded.

“You absolutely did not.”

Zee looked around the table.

“I hate this family.”

Rez smiled.

“Family?”

Zee realized too late.

Nymeris looked down at her wine.

Ner smiled.

Zareth sent laughter through the Link.

Zee decided murder remained socially inappropriate.

For now.

⸻

The summit lasted four days.

Trade.

Safe Roads.

Coastal protections.

Refugee passage.

Monster boundaries.

Shared warnings about divine disturbances.

Nothing world-ending.

That was important.

Kingdoms also survived through boring agreements.

Zee hated those most.

On the final night, the subject changed.

Succession.

A Nhalaris minister asked Nymeris whether she intended to name an heir.

Silence.

Nymeris placed her cup down.

“Not tonight.”

The minister bowed.

But the question remained.

Later, Zee found her alone beside Darkheart’s inner lake.

She stood several feet from the water.

Interesting.

Usually Nymeris moved toward water instinctively.

Tonight she watched it.

Zee approached.

“You scared your minister.”

“He will recover.”

“Unfortunate.”

Nymeris glanced toward him.

“You are avoiding your own council.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“They are discussing grain.”

She nodded sympathetically.

“Tragic.”

Zee sat on the stone wall.

Nymeris remained standing.

After a moment, he asked:

“Why did that question bother you?”

She looked toward him.

“It did not.”

“Lie.”

“You are irritating.”

“Yes.”

Nymeris exhaled.

“My House expects continuity.”

“Heir.”

“Yes.”

“You do not want one?”

Her expression changed.

“That is not the same question.”

Zee understood immediately.

Good.

He waited.

Nymeris looked toward the lake.

“I want children.”

Zee said nothing.

“I do not want a child because ministers are worried about succession.”

“Fair.”

“I do not want someone born carrying a kingdom before they learn their own name.”

That hit something inside him.

Zee’s childhood.

Crown.

Prophecy.

Fear.

Victor.

He looked away.

Nymeris noticed.

“You understand.”

“Yes.”

“Of course you do.”

Silence settled.

Then she asked:

“What about you?”

Zee frowned.

“What about me?”

“Children.”

He almost laughed.

Then realized she was serious.

“I have not thought about it.”

Nymeris looked at him.

“That is a lie.”

Zee narrowed his eyes.

“You are becoming annoying.”

“I learned from you.”

Fair.

He looked toward the lake.

Had he thought about children?

Yes.

Not often.

Never safely.

For years, survival had been the only future he trusted.

Then kingship.

Darkheart.

Ner.

His people.

Now twenty-five.

Immortal.

A kingdom growing around him.

Zee finally answered.

“I think about what they would inherit.”

Nymeris did not mistake the meaning.

“Power.”

“Enemies.”

“Your name.”

“My blood.”

“Your history.”

“Yes.”

Nymeris came closer.

“And what would they inherit that you actually want them to have?”

That question was worse.

Zee thought.

“A home.”

Nymeris waited.

“People who love them.”

Another pause.

“The right to choose who they become.”

There.

Nymeris smiled faintly.

“Then perhaps you have thought about it.”

Zee looked toward her.

“Do not become smug.”

“Too late.”

⸻

Ner joined him later that night.

Zee was already in bed.

Not sleeping.

Ner knew.

“What happened?”

“Nymeris asked if I want children.”

Ner stopped removing his coat.

Then looked at Zee.

“And?”

“I threatened her.”

Ner stared.

“With what?”

“Being annoying.”

“That is not a threat. That is your personality.”

Zee threw a pillow.

Ner caught it.

Then sat beside him.

“So?”

Zee looked at the ceiling.

“I do.”

Ner did not react dramatically.

That made Zee look toward him.

“You knew?”

“I suspected.”

“How?”

“You stare at every child in Darkheart like you are checking whether the world is bothering them.”

“That proves nothing.”

“It proves several things.”

Zee rolled his eyes.

Ner became quieter.

“Do you want one now?”

“No.”

Immediate.

Clear.

Ner nodded.

“Good.”

Zee frowned.

“Good?”

“You answered without fear.”

That made Zee think.

He had not said no because children frightened him.

He said no because he was not ready.

Different.

Ner leaned against him.

“Someday?”

“Maybe.”

“Then someday.”

Simple.

No jealousy.

No permission.

Partner.

Zee wrapped an arm around him.

“What if my child is like me?”

Ner smiled faintly.

“Impossible?”

“Yes.”

“Annoying?”

“Ner.”

“Fine.”

Ner became serious.

“If your child is powerful, then your child is powerful.”

“That sounds simple.”

“It is not.”

Zee looked at him.

Ner continued.

“But power is not a crime.”

No.

Zee knew that better than anyone.

⸻

Nymeris returned to Nhalaris after the summit.

For the next year, the question of children remained exactly that.

A question.

No decision.

No plan.

Life continued.

Darkheart expanded another Safe Road.

Nhalaris opened its first permanent surface embassy.

Rez strengthened Vesper’s independence while still coordinating with both kingdoms.

Zareth took greater responsibility inside Drakonis.

Kael became one of Darkheart’s most respected field commanders.

Tessa expanded battlefield medicine into three permanent clinics.

Amara trained mixed patrols through the southern forests.

Ner traveled between Darkheart and Nhalaris twice, learning more about ancient Siren resonance without becoming one of them.

Zee kept ruling.

Kept hunting.

Kept killing enemies he consciously chose to kill.

Kept feeding.

Kept enjoying fear.

Kept learning where choice ended and impulse began.

And Nymeris became normal inside his life.

That may have been the strangest part.

She appeared for council without announcement.

He visited Nhalaris without complaining every single time.

Sometimes they worked.

Sometimes they argued.

Sometimes they sat in silence and understood the same burden without explaining it.

Years of trust did not arrive through one dramatic oath.

It accumulated.

One decision at a time.

⸻

Near the end of Zee’s twenty-sixth year, Nymeris came to Darkheart alone.

No royal escort.

No diplomatic vessel.

She found Zee in the training courtyard with a twelve-year-old Wolf boy hanging from his back.

The child was trying to choke him.

Poorly.

Zee continued walking.

“You are too high.”

The boy tightened his arm.

“I almost have you.”

“No.”

Nymeris watched.

Zee noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You are staring.”

“I am looking.”

Zee narrowed his eyes.

“That is mine.”

“I improved it.”

The boy finally released Zee and dropped to the ground.

“Again?”

“Tomorrow.”

The child ran off.

Nymeris watched him leave.

Then looked at Zee.

“You would be good at it.”

Zee’s expression changed.

“Do not.”

“I said one sentence.”

“You knew what you were doing.”

“Yes.”

He sighed.

Nymeris smiled.

Then became serious.

“I have been thinking.”

“Dangerous.”

“Zee.”

He stopped.

Her tone was different.

Not Queen.

Not joking.

Something personal.

“What?”

Nymeris looked toward the empty courtyard.

“About heirs.”

Zee said nothing.

“About children.”

Still nothing.

“And about whether bloodlines should continue because kingdoms demand it…”

She looked at him.

“…or because the people carrying them actually choose to.”

Zee’s eyes sharpened slightly.

There it was.

Not a decision.

Not yet.

But the question had changed.

No longer:

Do either of them want children?

Now:

What would it mean to choose one?

Nymeris held his gaze.

“We should talk.”

Zee glanced toward the castle.

“Now?”

“No.”

That surprised him.

“When?”

“When neither of us is speaking as King or Queen.”

Zee understood.

No councils.

No succession.

No kingdoms deciding for them.

Personal.

Choice.

He nodded.

“Then we talk.”

Nymeris turned to leave.

Zee called after her.

“Nymeris.”

She looked back.

“This does not mean anything yet.”

“I know.”

“No decisions.”

“I know.”

“No ministers.”

“Absolutely.”

“No prophecy.”

Her expression darkened.

“Never.”

Good.

Zee watched her walk toward the keep.

For years, the future had been something that happened to him.

Now he was beginning to consider making one deliberately.

Not an heir.

Not a weapon.

Not a prophecy.

A child.

But not yet.

Not until the choice belonged completely to the people making it.
