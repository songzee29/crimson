Chapter Forty-Seven

Burn the Records

The Northern Trade Registry was already on fire when Zee arrived.

Not metaphorically.

Actual fire.

Three floors of old stone and timber burned beneath a night sky stained orange.

Witches flooded the courtyard.

Some fought the flames.

Others fought each other.

That was less helpful.

Zee hit the outer wall at full speed, ran three steps vertically with Crimson Mana beneath his boots, caught the balcony edge, and pulled himself over.

A registry guard stared.

“Darkheart?”

Zee kept moving.

“Where is Rez?”

“Inside!”

“Ner?”

“Inside!”

“Of course.”

He ran.

Behind him, a Dragon roar shook the district.

Zareth had arrived.

People screamed.

Zee reached through the Link.

No fire.

A burst of offended restraint returned.

Good.

He entered smoke.

⸻

Rez had commandeered the central records hall.

That was the only reason half the building still existed.

Blue-White Mana covered the support beams in controlled frost, slowing combustion without freezing the stone hard enough to fracture it.

At fifteen Rez no longer looked like a boy pretending to command adults.

He pointed.

People moved.

“East shelves out first!”

Two registry witches obeyed.

“Do not carry sealed asylum ledgers into the public courtyard!”

A clerk changed direction.

“Where?”

“Inner stone annex!”

“Your Highness, that annex is full.”

“Then make room!”

Zee found him halfway up the stairs.

Rez looked over.

Relief flashed across his face.

Then vanished under work.

“You took forever.”

“I beat Zareth.”

A roar hit the roof.

Rez looked upward.

“No, you didn’t.”

Zee smiled.

“Where is Ner?”

“Lower archive.”

Zee stopped smiling.

“Why?”

“Because the fire began there.”

“Who is with him?”

“Caldrin.”

“Good.”

Rez grabbed Zee’s sleeve before he moved.

“Listen.”

Zee looked at him.

“This is not random.”

“I assumed.”

“Someone activated internal fire wards backward.”

“Meaning?”

“They turned suppression wards into accelerants.”

Zee’s expression hardened.

“Who can do that?”

“Senior registry access.”

“Names?”

“Five.”

“Where are they?”

Rez looked toward the burning lower level.

“Three accounted for.”

“Fourth fled.”

“Fifth is missing.”

Zee understood.

“Ner thinks one is below.”

“Yes.”

Rez released him.

“Do not destroy the archive.”

Zee frowned.

“That feels specifically directed at me.”

“It is.”

“Rude.”

“Accurate.”

Zee ran.

⸻

The lower archive was worse.

No open flames.

Heat.

Smoke.

Magical fire moving behind walls.

Ner stood inside a ring of Pure Golden Mana with both hands raised.

The entire corridor around him glowed.

Not because his power was opening anything.

Because he was holding six magical boundaries apart at once.

Caldrin stood beside a collapsed clerk.

Alive.

Barely conscious.

Zee entered.

Ner did not look back.

“You came.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Zee stopped beside him.

“What do you need?”

That question made Ner glance over.

Only for a heartbeat.

Then back to the wards.

“Three anchor rooms.”

“Which one?”

“All.”

Zee frowned.

“I cannot be in three places.”

Ner’s mouth twitched.

“Disappointing.”

“Still joking?”

“Barely.”

Golden Mana trembled.

Zee saw the strain.

“What happens if you let go?”

“The fire crosses the archive boundaries.”

“And?”

“Everything burns.”

Zee looked around.

Records.

Protected asylum identities.

Trade histories.

Criminal ledgers.

Veyrath routes.

Witch coven treaties.

Centuries of things some people needed hidden and others needed exposed.

A mess.

“What do I do?”

Ner nodded toward the far passage.

“Find the person feeding it.”

“Heartbeat?”

“Maybe.”

“Maybe?”

“Half the building is full of terrified people.”

Fair.

Zee closed his eyes.

Listened.

Heartbeats everywhere.

Fast.

Uneven.

Smoke coughs.

Running.

Then one pattern stood out.

Slow.

Too slow.

Someone calm in a burning archive.

Zee opened his eyes.

“Found one.”

Caldrin looked toward him.

“Could be unconscious.”

“Walking.”

“Better.”

Zee ran.

⸻

Senior Registrar Elian Voss was destroying files by hand.

Not escaping.

Burning specific pages.

Zee stopped in the doorway.

Voss looked up.

Older man.

Gray hair.

Northern robes.

No armor.

No weapon.

One hand glowing with dull green Witch Mana.

A ledger burned between his fingers.

He dropped it.

Zee crossed the room and crushed the flame beneath his boot.

Voss stared.

“You.”

“Everyone keeps saying that.”

“You should not be here.”

Zee looked around.

“No.”

He smiled faintly.

“I definitely should.”

Voss cast.

Zee moved aside.

Green fire struck a shelf.

Zee swore.

“Rez specifically told me not to let you do that.”

Voss attacked again.

Zee closed distance.

One hand caught the wrist.

He twisted.

Not breaking.

Yet.

“Stop.”

Voss laughed.

“You think this is about children.”

Zee’s expression hardened.

“What?”

“You think Renn was the rot.”

“No.”

That surprised him.

Zee continued.

“I think Renn was one man.”

Voss’s eyes narrowed.

Good.

Zee was learning.

Voss tried pulling away.

Zee held him.

“What are you burning?”

“Things that should have remained buried.”

“Like Ner’s stolen Mana?”

Voss’s face changed.

There.

Zee’s horns warmed.

Anger.

Controlled.

“Why?”

Voss smiled.

“Because rare power belongs in hands capable of using it properly.”

Zee’s grip tightened.

“Wrong answer.”

Voss winced.

“You children stumble into forces kingdoms spent centuries controlling.”

“Gold.”

“Dragon blood.”

“Mixed blood.”

“Ancient wards.”

“You break structures because nobody taught you why they exist.”

Zee leaned closer.

“Sometimes they exist because people like you built them.”

Voss laughed.

“And sometimes monsters need boundaries.”

There it was.

Monster.

Old word.

Less power over Zee now.

Not none.

Voss continued.

“You think the world fears you because it is ignorant?”

“No.”

Zee’s answer came easily.

“Sometimes I am frightening.”

Voss stopped.

Zee smiled without humor.

“But you used kidnapped children to protect grain routes.”

“I did not.”

“Renn did.”

“Yes.”

“And you helped cover him.”

Silence.

“You stole Ner’s Mana.”

“It was residue.”

“It was his.”

“Magic has no owner once released.”

That made Zee’s expression change.

He thought of blood brokers.

Khell.

Morcant.

People reducing living parts of others into commodities.

Something inside him darkened.

Voss saw.

He smiled.

“Go on.”

Zee frowned.

“What?”

“Hurt me.”

The invitation was almost eager.

“You want to.”

Zee hated that Voss was right.

The man’s wrist rested inside his grip.

One turn.

One crack.

Easy.

Voss continued.

“Everyone knows what you are becoming.”

Zee’s Dragon energy warmed beneath his scales.

“Do they?”

“The hunted child grew teeth.”

Zee’s fangs showed.

“He always had teeth.”

Voss laughed.

Then whispered:

“Prove them right.”

Zee almost did.

That was the danger.

Not because Voss manipulated him well.

Because Zee wanted an excuse.

He could break him.

Hear the scream.

Feel that satisfaction Renn had given him.

Maybe more.

This man had stolen from Ner.

Helped bury crimes.

Was currently burning evidence that might protect people.

Zee’s hand tightened.

Then Ner’s words returned.

If the only reason you stop is because one of us says your name, what happens when we are not there?

Ner was not here.

Aurelia was not here.

Rez was upstairs.

Zareth was outside.

No one said his name.

Zee let go.

Voss stumbled backward.

Confusion crossed his face.

Zee looked toward the burning ledger.

“Put your hands on the wall.”

Voss stared.

“What?”

“You heard me.”

“You think restraint makes you better?”

“No.”

Zee stepped closer.

“I think I need you alive.”

That answer mattered more.

Voss tried casting again.

This time Zee hit him.

One strike.

Enough to drop him.

No pleasure.

Not because it was painless.

Because Zee chose the amount before he moved.

Voss hit the floor conscious.

Zee kicked his wand away.

“Hands.”

Voss laughed through blood.

“You are afraid of yourself.”

Zee looked at him.

“Yes.”

The honesty shut him up.

Zee bound his wrists with the man’s own belt.

Then grabbed the rescued ledgers.

“Good.”

Voss stared.

Zee smiled faintly.

“Fear can be useful.”

⸻

The internal fire wards shut down when Voss’s access seal was removed.

Ner did that personally.

He emerged from the lower archive pale, furious, and coated in soot.

Zee met him in the courtyard.

Ner looked toward Voss under guard.

“You found him.”

“Yes.”

“You hurt him?”

“A little.”

Ner stared.

Zee shrugged.

“He cast twice.”

“That was not my question.”

Zee looked at him.

“I wanted to.”

Ner waited.

Zee continued.

“I did not do more.”

Something in Ner’s face softened.

“Without us there?”

“Yes.”

Ner smiled.

Small.

Real.

“Good.”

Zee hated how much that smile affected him.

His horns warmed.

Ner looked up.

Zee immediately said, “Different reason.”

Ner’s smile widened.

“Oh?”

Zee froze.

Caldrin walked past carrying three rescued ledgers.

“Terrible time to flirt.”

Zee’s head snapped around.

“We are not—”

Ner laughed.

Actually laughed.

Zee stared at him.

That made it worse.

Rez appeared from the building.

“What did I miss?”

“Nothing,” Zee said.

Caldrin answered, “Everything.”

Zee closed his eyes.

Zareth landed in the street beyond the courtyard.

The Link instantly flooded Zee with amused curiosity.

“No.”

The Dragon had not even done anything yet.

That did not matter.

He would.

⸻

By dawn the registry fire was contained.

One archive wing was destroyed.

Two damaged.

Most protected asylum records survived.

The stolen Golden Mana records survived too.

So did payment logs connecting Renn’s administrative office to preservation vessels, false grain disruptions, mercenary contracts, and three other officials.

Not an entire kingdom.

Not every Northern Council member.

Three.

Specific.

Important.

The Council suspended them before noon.

Not from morality.

From self-preservation.

Rez knew that.

He accepted it anyway.

Political consequences rarely required pure hearts.

The grain compact reopened under emergency review.

Vesper kept its alternate western routes.

That was permanent too.

Rez had no intention of allowing one neighboring council to control Vesper food supply so completely again.

He stood on the registry steps after sunrise while northern officials argued around him.

Fifteen.

Tired.

Soot on one sleeve.

Absolutely in his element.

Zee approached.

“You enjoy this.”

Rez looked over.

“What?”

“Watching politicians realize they lost.”

Rez smiled.

“Maybe.”

Zee nodded.

“Good.”

Rez laughed.

“Your morality is deteriorating.”

“Probably.”

Then Rez became serious.

“You did well.”

Zee looked toward Voss being transported under guard.

“I almost didn’t.”

“But you did.”

“He wanted me to hurt him.”

“People will.”

Zee looked over.

Rez continued.

“They will provoke you because they fear you.”

“Or because they want proof.”

“Exactly.”

Zee thought about Victor.

Again.

Fear becoming judgment.

Judgment becoming violence.

He understood the trap better now.

That did not mean he would always avoid it.

He was fifteen.

Angry.

Powerful.

And sometimes violence felt very, very good.

That truth remained.

But another truth existed beside it now.

He could choose how much.

Sometimes.

That was enough progress for one night.

Ner descended the steps behind them.

Zee looked automatically.

Rez saw.

Zee saw Rez seeing.

“Do not.”

Rez smiled.

“I am leaving.”

“Good.”

Rez walked away.

Too easy.

Zee narrowed his eyes.

He turned back toward Ner.

Ner stopped beside him.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You have the face.”

“Everyone needs new material.”

Ner looked toward the rising sun.

Then toward Zee.

“You stayed.”

Zee frowned.

“Where?”

“With Voss.”

“You could have killed him.”

“Yes.”

“But you didn’t.”

“No.”

Ner studied him.

Zee suddenly became very aware of how close they were again.

“I remembered what you said.”

Ner’s expression changed.

“What part?”

“The part about figuring it out when none of you are there.”

A quiet moment passed.

Ner smiled.

“I am glad.”

Zee looked at him.

Too long.

Again.

This time Ner looked too long too.

Neither pretended otherwise.

No confession.

No grand declaration.

Just something changing.

Slowly.

Dangerously.

Fifteen.

Ner looked away first.

“Come on.”

“Where?”

“Food.”

Zee smiled.

“Finally, something important.”

They started down the steps together.

Behind them the Northern Trade Registry still smoked.

Ahead, Rez was already arguing with a council secretary.

Zareth waited beyond the walls.

Aurelia would want the full truth when they returned.

And somewhere inside Zee Darkheart, the darker thing growing beside his mercy had learned its first real boundary.

Not every monster needed to die.

Some needed to live long enough to answer for what they had done.

And Zee was beginning to discover that restraint could be its own kind of cruelty.

He was not sure whether that realization made him better.

He was very sure he liked it.
