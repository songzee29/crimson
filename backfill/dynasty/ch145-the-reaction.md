Chapter One Hundred Forty-Five

The Reaction

The first version said Selk Varren had burned alive inside his own fortress.

The second said Zee Darkheart had become immune to suppression.

The third said an enormous black Phoenix had destroyed half the estate.

That one was wrong.

Zee had never fully transformed.

The fourth said Selk had vanished.

The fifth said the King of Darkheart was killing everyone connected to an old contract.

That one was close enough.

By the second day—

the remaining seven had heard something.

And each reacted differently.

⸻

Brannic Dor heard while eating breakfast.

He was older now.

Gray threaded through his beard.

The former Wolf hunter sat outside a mountain lodge while two younger hunters argued over tracks.

A messenger whispered into his ear.

Brannic stopped chewing.

“Selk?”

“Yes.”

“Confirmed?”

“Yes.”

Brannic looked toward the mountains.

“How?”

“Black fire.”

Brannic frowned.

“Witch?”

“No.”

“Dragon?”

“No.”

“Then what?”

The messenger hesitated.

“Wings.”

Brannic slowly placed his fork down.

He remembered Zee Darkheart’s Wolf senses screaming beneath compounds Brannic had personally chosen.

Remembered reading the field report afterward.

Remembered satisfaction.

Not joy.

Professional satisfaction.

The compound had worked.

Brannic stood.

“Burn the old stores.”

The messenger blinked.

“All of them?”

“All.”

“What about the Wolfbane?”

“Especially that.”

Brannic looked toward the distant road.

“If he’s coming as something else, I don’t want to die holding the wrong weapon.”

⸻

Lord Vaeren Thoss laughed.

Actually laughed.

“Selk got careless.”

His guests did not join him.

The coastal noble poured another glass of blood-wine.

A Vampire.

Old.

Wealthy.

Comfortable.

His estate overlooked the sea.

“Zee Darkheart has been terrifying people for decades.”

One guest shifted.

“Selk’s defenses were extensive.”

“Selk trusted machines.”

Thoss drank.

“I trust money.”

Another guest looked toward the armed guards surrounding the room.

Dozens.

Thoss smiled.

“Kings bleed.”

His eyes reddened.

“We proved that once.”

Then he raised his glass.

“To Selk.”

Nobody joined the toast.

⸻

Kaldrek Vane did not laugh.

He closed his forge.

Immediately.

The Wolf smith pulled the iron shutters himself.

His apprentices stared.

“Master?”

“Go home.”

“But the commission—”

“Go.”

They obeyed.

Kaldrek waited until the forge was empty.

Then he walked beneath the main furnace.

Opened a hidden compartment.

Inside rested an old Dragonbane barb.

Not the one used on Zee.

A prototype.

Kaldrek picked it up.

Stared.

Years ago, he had been proud of this design.

Proud that his craftsmanship could penetrate something people called unbreakable.

Now?

He threw it into the furnace.

The metal glowed.

Kaldrek watched it burn.

Then whispered—

“He finally came.”

⸻

Neris Pell destroyed records.

Not all.

Only specific ones.

The information broker moved through her archive with ruthless efficiency.

Zee’s travel patterns.

Old maps.

Darkheart access routes.

Public sightings.

Family connections.

Battle observations.

She burned them.

Her assistant stared.

“You said information is never destroyed.”

“I lied.”

“Where are we going?”

Neris stopped.

“We?”

The assistant’s face changed.

Neris handed him a bag of coin.

“You never worked for me.”

“What?”

“You have never heard the name Zee Darkheart.”

“Neris—”

“Go.”

He went.

Neris waited until the door shut.

Then removed a single sealed envelope from beneath the floor.

Black wax.

Old.

Three vertical cuts.

One horizontal line.

She stared at it.

Did not open it.

She had never opened it.

She had only delivered information where she was paid to deliver it.

“It wasn’t personal,” she whispered.

Then she looked at the smoke rising from her burning records.

For the first time in years—

she wondered whether Zee would care.

⸻

Drovak Senn packed.

Efficiently.

Three shirts.

Coin.

Weapons.

Documents.

No sentimental objects.

The Human logistics controller had survived wars because he understood one rule better than most:

Know when the road is closing.

Selk dead meant the road was closing.

He opened a map.

North?

Too obvious.

East?

Darkheart influence.

West?

Ports.

South?

Neutral kingdoms.

He chose none.

Instead, he took out the extraction map from Chapter 128.

Old.

Folded.

Bloodstained.

Drovak studied it.

Then smiled grimly.

“If I were you…”

His finger moved across the map.

Not toward escape.

Toward someone else.

Jorren.

⸻

Sera Quill did not run.

She opened a book.

The Dragon woman sat alone inside a stone chamber lit by one hanging flame.

Before her rested thirteen years of observations.

Zee Darkheart.

Changes.

Adaptations.

Kingdom expansion.

Known battles.

Behavioral evolution.

New tactics.

His shift after Chapter 128 had fascinated her most.

False openings.

Misdirection.

Delayed responses.

Changing priorities.

He had become less predictable.

She had noticed before most.

Selk’s death report lay beside her.

BLACK-CRIMSON FIRE.

PHOENIX WINGS.

UNKNOWN EXPRESSION.

NO OBSERVED WITCH COMBAT.

NO OBSERVED VAMPIRE COMBAT.

NO OBSERVED WOLF COMBAT.

NO OBSERVED DRAGON COMBAT.

Sera stared at the words.

Then opened a fresh page.

At the top she wrote:

ABYSSCIN?

She had heard the term.

Rumors from Darkheart.

Very little reliable information.

Below it:

UNKNOWN PHOENIX EXPRESSION.

Then:

DO NOT ASSUME OLD COUNTERS APPLY.

Sera leaned back.

For the first time in years—

she smiled.

Not because Selk was dead.

Because the problem had changed.

“Show me,” she whispered.

⸻

Jorren Vale received the news at night.

He read the message once.

Then again.

No visible reaction.

He was sharpening a blade.

He continued.

A younger mercenary waited.

“Should we move?”

“No.”

“Fortify?”

“No.”

“Contact Quill?”

Jorren stopped sharpening.

The room became quiet.

He remembered Zee Darkheart differently from the others.

Not from charts.

Not from supply lists.

Not from secondhand reports.

He remembered Zee breathing hard.

Bleeding.

Trying to rise.

He remembered calling rotations.

Remembered watching each team force another answer.

Remembered Zee adapting anyway.

Again.

Again.

Again.

Until adaptation could no longer keep pace.

He remembered the moment the King finally went down.

He remembered standing near him.

Close enough to see the hatred in Crimson-Gold eyes.

Close enough to know Zee was memorizing faces.

Jorren had given the order.

“Confirmation.”

Then—

“Withdraw.”

He had walked away.

Years passed.

Now Selk was dead.

Jorren placed the blade on the table.

“Send nothing to Sera.”

The mercenary hesitated.

“She contacted you.”

“I know.”

“What do we do?”

Jorren looked toward the dark window.

“Wait.”

“For what?”

Jorren’s face went still.

“For him.”

⸻

Back in Darkheart—

Merek Voss had learned to hate footsteps.

Every set made him look toward the door.

Guards brought food.

Water.

Nothing else.

Zee had not tortured him constantly.

That was somehow worse.

Merek understood the arrangement.

Useful.

Alive.

Useless—

uncertain.

The door opened.

Merek looked up.

Zee entered.

No blood on him.

No visible wounds.

No armor.

Merek swallowed.

“Selk?”

“Dead.”

Merek closed his eyes.

Not grief.

Calculation.

One down.

Seven.

Zee placed correspondence on the table.

Merek looked.

Recognized the handwriting.

“Sera.”

“She kept studying me.”

Merek’s face changed.

“What?”

“For years.”

“That wasn’t part of the contract.”

“I know.”

Merek leaned forward.

“Let me see.”

Zee didn’t move.

Merek remembered who controlled this room.

“Please.”

Zee slid one page across.

Merek read.

His brow furrowed.

Then deepened.

“This is recent.”

“Yes.”

“She’s still working with Jorren.”

“Apparently.”

Merek looked up.

“That is bad.”

Zee smiled.

“For them.”

⸻

Rez waited outside.

Ner beside him.

Zayrlen leaned against the wall farther down the corridor.

Older now.

Powerful enough to have fought beside his father.

Wise enough not to pretend every war belonged to him.

Zee emerged.

Rez looked at the correspondence.

“Selk?”

“Finished.”

“Anything useful?”

“Sera never stopped watching.”

Rez’s expression hardened.

“Darkheart?”

“Yes.”

Ner immediately lost whatever casualness remained in his posture.

“How much?”

“Enough.”

“Zayrlen?”

Zee looked toward his son.

“Reports. Public information. Nothing suggesting she got close.”

Zayrlen pushed away from the wall.

“She studied me?”

“Possibly.”

His Crimson-and-Gold eyes sharpened.

“I’m coming when you find her.”

“No.”

Zayrlen’s jaw tightened.

Then he caught himself.

This was not Chapter 142.

Not his test.

Not his debt.

“Fine.”

Zee looked almost suspicious.

“That easy?”

“No.”

“Good.”

Zayrlen folded his arms.

“I said fine. I didn’t say I liked it.”

Ner smiled faintly.

“That is unfortunately your child.”

Zee looked offended.

“He gets that from you.”

“He gets the attitude from you.”

“I don’t have an attitude.”

Rez laughed.

Zee looked at him.

Rez immediately stopped.

Mostly.

⸻

Elias examined a scorched fragment Zee had brought back from Selk’s containment system.

Not because Zee needed a healer.

Because Elias liked understanding what Zee’s body was doing.

“The heat never spread beyond what you intended?”

“No.”

“Even underground?”

“No.”

Elias looked impressed.

Years ago, Zee’s Phoenix power had been enormous.

Now it was disciplined.

Not simply hotter.

Better.

“You could have destroyed the estate.”

“Yes.”

“But you didn’t.”

“There were people there who had nothing to do with it.”

Elias nodded.

That distinction mattered.

Zee’s darkness had never required stupidity.

“Any trouble from the suppression?”

“No.”

“Dragonbane?”

“Didn’t use Dragon.”

“Wolf irritants?”

“Didn’t use Wolf.”

“Silver?”

“Didn’t use Vampire.”

Elias smiled.

“And Witch suppression?”

Zee looked at him.

Elias laughed.

“Right.”

Zee leaned against the table.

“They built a cage for someone who didn’t show up.”

“That someone still exists.”

“I know.”

Elias understood.

Zee had not lost Witch.

Vampire.

Wolf.

Dragon.

Crimson Sovereign.

Crimson Demonic Dragon.

All remained.

He had simply decided these eight did not deserve to see them.

They had studied those answers.

They were getting another one.

⸻

Victor entered without knocking.

Zee looked toward him.

“No.”

Victor stopped.

“I haven’t said anything.”

“I know.”

“I was going to ask how it went.”

“Now you don’t have to.”

Victor stared.

“You become more pleasant with age.”

“You keep resurrecting. We all suffer.”

Victor’s mouth twitched.

Zayrlen laughed from the doorway.

Zee looked toward his son.

“Don’t encourage him.”

Victor looked at Zayrlen.

“I like him.”

“You are still on probation.”

“It has been years.”

“And?”

Victor sighed.

Ner walked past.

“Your son is right.”

Victor looked at him.

“I was not speaking to you.”

Ner stopped.

Victor reconsidered.

“Good evening, Ner.”

“Better.”

Zee almost smiled.

Almost.

Then Rez entered with a message.

The humor disappeared.

⸻

“Movement.”

Zee straightened.

“Who?”

“Three of them.”

Rez laid reports across the table.

“Neris Pell has begun destroying archives.”

Zee nodded.

“Expected.”

“Drovak Senn vanished from his known residence.”

“Running?”

“Maybe.”

Rez touched another report.

“Brannic Dor dismissed most of his people and burned several stores.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“Smart.”

“And Thoss?”

“Still hosting parties.”

Zee looked disappointed.

“That might be the dumbest one.”

Rez continued.

“Kaldrek closed his forge.”

“Sera?”

“Nothing.”

“Jorren?”

Rez paused.

“Nothing.”

Zee looked at him.

That interested him more.

Seven people.

Seven reactions.

Fear.

Pride.

Preparation.

Flight.

Silence.

And somewhere between them—

someone still possessed the original research.

Someone had continued studying him.

Someone had contacted the man who once stood over Zee while he bled.

The game had changed.

Good.

⸻

Far from Darkheart, Sera Quill turned another page.

She had already revised three old conclusions.

Zee no longer responded to being studied the way he once had.

He deliberately fed false information.

Changed patterns.

Used expectation as a weapon.

Chapter 128 had taught him.

Sera knew because she had helped write the lesson.

She stared at Selk’s final report.

Black fire.

Scarlet beneath.

Wings.

No old powers observed.

Then she wrote one final line.

He is choosing what we see.

Her pen stopped.

That realization disturbed her more than the fire.

Because if Zee was choosing what they saw—

then the hunt had already become tactical.

Sera closed the book.

Across another country, Brannic watched his old compounds burn.

Thoss raised another glass.

Kaldrek stood alone in a dark forge.

Neris held a sealed envelope.

Drovak disappeared down an unmarked road.

Jorren sat beside the blade he had sharpened.

And inside Darkheart—

Zee stood before seven names.

Selk Varren had been crossed out.

Not ceremonially.

One line.

Done.

Zee’s finger moved down the list.

Brannic Dor.

Vaeren Thoss.

Kaldrek Vane.

Sera Quill.

Jorren Vale.

Neris Pell.

Drovak Senn.

Seven.

He remembered what Selk had said.

We did beat you.

Zee had answered truthfully.

They had.

That victory belonged to them.

He would never steal it.

Never rewrite it.

Never pretend he had secretly been stronger.

They chose the battlefield.

They chose the timing.

They studied his habits.

They rotated the counters.

They put him down.

Chapter 128 belonged to them.

Zee’s black-crimson Phoenix wings slowly opened.

Scarlet burned through every feather.

The First Natural Abysscin looked at the remaining names.

Their night was over.

This one belonged to him.
