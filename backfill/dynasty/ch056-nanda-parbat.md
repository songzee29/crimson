Chapter Fifty-Six

Nanda Parbat

Zee left Vesper before sunrise.

No note.

No guard.

No explanation.

Aurelia would have stopped him.

Rez would have demanded a plan.

Ner would have asked enough questions to make lying irritating.

Zareth would have felt the direction through the Link eventually and followed.

So Zee did something he had become increasingly interested in learning how to do properly.

He disappeared.

Not with magic.

That distinction mattered.

His Witch nature could conceal him.

His Vampire speed could carry him beyond watchtowers before guards finished blinking.

His Wolf senses could find every patrol route.

His developing Dragon durability could get him through terrain ordinary travelers would avoid.

But Zee wanted something none of those powers gave automatically.

Skill without supernatural advantage.

He had spent fifteen years surviving people who came for him with armies, spells, blood, claws, politics, monsters, and fear.

He could overpower most ordinary fighters.

That was becoming a problem.

Strength taught bad habits when strength always worked.

Renn had taught him that.

Voss had taught him more.

The deep wild taught him the rest.

There were things power could not simply crush.

There were moments when knowing how to kill mattered more than being strong enough to do it.

And somewhere in the mountains, according to whispers from killers who refused to say the name twice, there existed a place where people devoted their lives to exactly that lesson.

Nanda Parbat.

The League of Assassins.

Zee wanted what they knew.

Nothing more.

No ancient power.

No hidden bloodline.

No artifact.

Skill.

So he followed rumors east and north until roads stopped.

Then tracks.

Then smoke.

Then nothing.

That was when he knew he was close.

Someone had erased their presence too carefully.

Wolf instinct found the first mistake.

Not a footprint.

The absence of one.

A mountain hare had crossed fresh snow, then altered direction for no visible reason.

Something had occupied the space.

Something the animal sensed.

Zee crouched.

His Vampire hearing found no heartbeat nearby.

Good.

His Witch perception found a suppression veil.

Better.

His Dragon instincts warned that eyes watched from above.

Best.

Zee smiled.

“Found you.”

A blade touched the side of his throat.

He stopped smiling.

A voice behind him said, “No.”

Zee’s crimson eyes shifted sideways.

Black clothing.

Human heartbeat.

Steady.

He had not heard the assassin approach.

Interesting.

The assassin pressed the blade closer.

“We found you.”

Zee disappeared.

Vampire speed.

The man turned.

Too slow.

Zee caught his wrist.

Then stopped.

That was the point.

He had already cheated.

The assassin smiled beneath his mask.

“You came to learn and used magic before the first lesson.”

Zee frowned.

“How do you know why I came?”

“Because if you came to kill us, you would have tried already.”

Fair.

The man pulled free with a joint movement Zee did not recognize.

Zee’s grip broke.

Not through superior strength.

Angle.

Leverage.

Technique.

The assassin swept Zee’s knee.

Zee hit the snow.

A knife stopped above his heart.

Again.

Two failures.

Zee’s smile returned.

“Oh, I like this place.”

⸻

The League did not welcome him.

That made Zee like it more.

He was blindfolded.

Disarmed.

Searched.

His knives removed.

Then his spare knife.

Then the knife hidden in his boot.

Then the sliver of sharpened metal sewn into his sleeve.

The assassin searching him paused.

“How many weapons?”

Zee smiled beneath the blindfold.

“That depends what you count as a weapon.”

They removed his belt too.

Rude.

He was taken through mountain corridors by sound alone.

He counted turns.

Seven left.

Four right.

One descent.

Two climbs.

Twenty-three steps through colder air.

A waterfall somewhere behind stone.

Three doors.

One open courtyard.

Then someone hit him.

Zee blocked too late.

Wood cracked against his ribs.

He ripped off the blindfold.

A tall man stood across from him with a practice staff.

Older.

Gray at the temples.

Nothing supernatural in Zee’s senses.

Human.

Relaxed.

Dangerous.

The others bowed.

Master Assassin.

No name offered.

Zee did not ask.

The Master swung again.

Zee caught the staff.

The Master let go.

Zee’s balance shifted.

A knife handle struck the nerve beneath his elbow.

His arm went dead.

The Master swept his ankle.

Zee hit the stone.

Again.

Zee growled.

The Master looked down at him.

“You are powerful.”

“I know.”

“That is why you are easy to read.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

The Master continued.

“You enter every movement believing failure remains survivable.”

Zee went still.

Accurate.

“You rely on speed because few can punish a mistake.”

“Strength because few can resist.”

“Magic because it solves distance.”

“Instinct because it warns you faster than thought.”

The Master crouched.

“Take those away.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“That is why I came.”

For the first time, the Master looked interested.

“Good.”

Then he punched Zee in the throat.

Training began.

⸻

The first lesson was walking.

Zee hated it.

He could cross a room without sound using Vampire control.

The Master called that cheating.

“Again.”

Zee crossed.

One board whispered beneath his foot.

“Dead.”

Again.

This time no board.

His coat brushed stone.

“Dead.”

Again.

His breathing changed near the door.

“Dead.”

Again.

His shadow crossed a candle before his body did.

“Dead.”

Zee stared at the flame.

“You count shadows?”

“I count everything.”

That sentence stayed.

By evening Zee counted everything too.

Floor grain.

Airflow.

Loose stone.

Where people looked.

Where they never looked.

Where sound gathered.

How leather creaked before metal moved.

How weight transferred through hips before a strike.

How someone touched a hidden weapon without realizing.

The second lesson was anatomy.

The League taught bodies as maps.

Nerves.

Arteries.

Joints.

Tendons.

Bone angles.

Places where one finger could collapse balance.

Places where a knife ended a fight immediately.

Places where a knife looked fatal and was not.

Places where supernatural creatures differed.

Vampires required speed and separation.

Wolves required joint destruction before recovery.

Witches required hands, mouths, focus, or whatever channel their casting used.

Dragons required humility.

Demons required identification before engagement.

Zee learned.

Too fast.

At first they thought he memorized.

Then he demonstrated.

Blindfolded, he identified six different poisons by scent.

Three more by taste before anyone could stop him.

The poison master slapped the cup from his hand.

“You could die.”

Zee looked at him.

“No.”

The man frowned.

Zee smiled.

“Long story.”

His body rejected the first toxin violently.

The second burned for almost an hour.

By the third, his blood had begun adapting enough to slow absorption while his senses learned the chemical signature.

Not immunity.

Recognition.

The League watched.

Zee stopped letting them.

He learned countertracking by laying three false trails while leaving by a fourth route nobody noticed.

He learned disguise by changing posture before clothing.

A prince walked differently from a servant.

A servant looked at doors differently from a guard.

A frightened boy held his shoulders differently from a killer.

The Master caught him on the first attempt.

Not the third.

He learned to fight blind by smell.

Then the Master filled the room with herbs.

Zee switched to hearing.

Then bells.

He switched to floor vibration.

Then suspended ropes beneath his feet.

Zee smiled in the darkness.

Wolf instinct took over.

Air.

Pressure.

Movement.

A knife came from behind.

He moved before thought.

Caught the wrist.

Twisted.

The attacker hit the floor.

The blindfold stayed on.

“Again.”

They attacked six at once.

No magic.

No Vampire speed.

No supernatural strength beyond what he could carefully restrain.

Zee lost.

Then again.

Lost slower.

Again.

Two fell before him.

Again.

Four.

Again.

All six.

The Master watched from the doorway.

“You are adapting to them.”

Zee removed the blindfold.

“Yes.”

“What happens when they change?”

Zee smiled.

“I change faster.”

The Master attacked.

Zee lost.

⸻

Sword work came next.

The League corrected everything power had allowed Zee to ignore.

His reach was excellent.

His recovery was lazy.

His speed covered bad foot placement.

His strength allowed him to bind blades inefficiently.

The Master exploited all of it.

First duel:

Zee disarmed in eleven seconds.

Second:

Twenty-three.

Third:

Zee cut the Master’s sleeve before losing.

Fourth:

The Master used a short blade instead.

Zee lost.

Fifth:

Zee switched weapons mid-fight.

Closer.

Sixth:

No weapons.

Zee reached the Master’s throat.

The Master reached his heart.

Draw.

The seventh duel happened in rain.

The Master never touched him.

Neither did Zee.

For eight minutes they moved.

Read.

Feinted.

Tested.

Then Zee threw his sword away.

The Master frowned.

That hesitation was enough.

Zee stepped inside the blade.

Elbow to wrist.

Shoulder turn.

Two fingers beneath the jaw.

One knee behind the leg.

The Master hit the ground.

Zee’s knife stopped against his throat.

Silence.

The Master smiled.

“Better.”

Zee looked down.

“I could have done that yesterday.”

“No.”

The Master’s eyes sharpened.

“Yesterday you could have overpowered me.”

“Today you defeated me.”

Difference.

Zee understood.

That night he practiced throwing knives until he could strike by reflected sound.

Improvised weapons until a spoon became dangerous.

Escape until bindings stopped being meaningful unless magical.

Presence suppression until two League assassins passed within arm’s reach of him in a dark corridor without noticing he was there.

That one pleased him.

Vampire Zee learned to suppress even the predatory cues other Vampires read instinctively.

Heart rhythm.

Breathing pattern.

Blood scent.

Body temperature.

He learned how much his blood betrayed him.

How to mask it.

How to follow someone through a city by one drop of old blood on a cuff.

How to separate bloodlines by scent.

Human.

Witch.

Wolf.

Vampire.

Dragon.

Mixed.

His compulsion sharpened too.

Not because the League taught magic.

Because they taught control of attention.

Three guards looked into Zee’s crimson eyes during an infiltration exercise.

“Forget you saw me.”

All three obeyed.

The fourth deliberately looked away.

Zee’s voice dropped.

“Look at me.”

The man resisted.

Zee felt the resistance.

Pressed.

Not with Witch magic.

Vampire.

The man’s head turned despite himself.

Zee frowned afterward.

That had been new.

The Master noticed.

“You did not touch him.”

“No.”

“Did you compel him?”

“Yes.”

“How?”

Zee looked toward his own hand.

“I do not know.”

The Master smiled faintly.

“Then do not depend on it.”

Good advice.

Wolf Zee became terrifying in pursuit.

No spell.

No Vampire speed.

Just patience.

Scent broken over water?

Track mud displacement.

Scent masked with ash?

Find crushed grass.

False trail?

Read animal avoidance.

Target hiding inside a crowd?

Hear fear.

Dragon Zee learned pain could be endured without announcing itself.

Scales could turn a knife.

Heat could reveal poison in metal.

His developing horns changed close combat geometry.

His shoulder ridges remained unfinished, sometimes swelling beneath his shirt when Dragon energy surged, but never opening.

No wings.

No full awakening.

Witch Zee changed most subtly.

Less power.

More placement.

One whispered spell to alter a hinge.

A breath of force to shift a blade half an inch.

A tiny sensory illusion to make someone hear one footstep where none existed.

Surgical.

The League approved.

Zee hated that he cared.

Then came the final test.

⸻

The Master Assassin hunted him at midnight.

No magic allowed unless Zee was discovered.

Every corridor was guarded.

Every exit trapped.

Every roof watched.

The rule was simple.

Survive until dawn.

Zee disappeared before the Master finished explaining.

At first, the hunt worked as expected.

The Master found a disturbed curtain.

A missing rooftop tile.

A footprint deliberately placed in dust.

He followed.

Then stopped.

Too easy.

He turned.

Found another trace.

Also deliberate.

The Master smiled.

Zee was countertracking.

Good.

He changed route.

For twenty minutes he found nothing.

Then one of his assassins vanished.

Not dead.

Unconscious inside a storage chamber.

No sound.

Another disappeared.

Then another.

The Master stopped hunting Zee.

He began hunting the pattern.

Wrong.

There was no pattern.

That was the lesson.

Zee used Vampire senses without Vampire speed.

Wolf instinct without pursuit.

Witch precision without visible spellwork.

Dragon endurance to remain motionless in freezing water beneath a bridge for seven minutes while two assassins searched above him.

Then ordinary assassin skill for the rest.

Hours passed.

The Master found no target.

Near dawn he entered the highest courtyard.

Empty.

Snow falling.

He stopped.

Something was wrong.

No guards.

He turned.

Nobody.

His hand moved toward his sword.

A knife touched the artery beneath his jaw.

Zee stood behind him.

Completely silent.

Not even the Master had heard him.

The Master smiled slowly.

“When?”

“Six minutes ago.”

“You were behind me six minutes?”

“No.”

Zee smiled.

“I was above you.”

The Master looked toward the roofline.

Then understood.

Zee had not survived the hunt.

He had reorganized it.

Removed observers.

Driven the Master through controlled routes.

Predicted every correction.

Then taken the final position.

The Master asked, “Why am I alive?”

Zee could feel the pulse beneath the blade.

One movement.

Done.

He enjoyed knowing that.

That was different now.

Not rage.

Not hunger.

Control.

“I came here to learn how to kill.”

The Master waited.

Zee lowered the knife.

“I already knew how to choose.”

The Master turned.

For the first time, something close to respect entered his face.

“You surpassed the lesson.”

Zee shook his head.

“No.”

He looked toward the knife.

“I made it mine.”

Then he left Nanda Parbat before sunrise.

No ceremony.

No oath.

No League membership.

No extended farewell.

He had what he came for.

Skill.

⸻

Aurelia knew something was wrong the moment Zee walked into Vesper.

Not because he looked injured.

He did not.

Not because his clothes had changed.

Barely.

Because she did not hear him enter.

Aurelia turned sharply in the private solar.

Zee stood three steps behind her.

She stared.

“How long have you been there?”

“Maybe twelve seconds.”

Her eyes narrowed.

“Zee.”

He smiled.

“Hi, Mother.”

Rez entered from the opposite hall.

Stopped.

Looked at Zee.

Then immediately looked toward the window.

The knife on the table.

Aurelia’s hand.

Distance to the door.

He saw Zee seeing all of it.

“What happened to you?”

Zee frowned.

“Nothing.”

Ner arrived next.

Zee’s attention cataloged automatically.

Golden Mana dormant.

Left sleeve loose enough to conceal a blade.

Weight balanced.

No injury.

Pulse slightly elevated because he had hurried.

Ner stopped.

“You are looking at me strangely.”

Zee realized.

Immediately looked away.

“Sorry.”

That made Ner more worried.

Zareth landed outside.

The Dragon Link opened.

Then recoiled.

Not from Zee.

From the difference.

Sharper.

Quieter.

Predatory focus disciplined into stillness.

Zareth lowered his head.

Question.

Zee touched his snout.

“I am fine.”

Aurelia folded her arms.

“Where were you?”

Zee hesitated.

That was enough.

Rez stared.

“You left without telling anyone.”

“Yes.”

Ner’s face hardened.

“For how long?”

“Long enough.”

“That is not an answer.”

Zee looked toward him.

Automatically saw three ways to disable his casting without injuring him.

His stomach turned.

There.

That was the cost.

The League had taught him too well.

He looked at Rez.

Knee.

Wrist.

Carotid.

Blind angle.

Aurelia.

Never.

His mind still cataloged.

Weapon hand.

Heart position.

Dragon-Witch durability.

Zareth.

Eyes.

Wing joints.

Neck scales.

Zee took one step backward.

Ner noticed.

“What?”

Zee looked at his hands.

“I can see it.”

Rez frowned.

“See what?”

Zee looked around the room.

Three exits.

Two hidden blades.

Four breakable sightlines.

One balcony route.

Seven objects usable as weapons.

Distances.

Timing.

Bodies.

Weak points.

Kill opportunities.

“All of it.”

Aurelia’s face changed.

Not fear.

Concern.

“Who taught you?”

Zee looked at her.

“The League of Assassins.”

Silence.

Rez blinked.

“You went to Nanda Parbat alone?”

“Yes.”

Ner stared.

“Without telling us?”

“Yes.”

Zareth’s Link erupted with anger.

Zee winced.

“I know.”

Aurelia approached.

Slowly.

Zee instinctively measured the distance.

Hated himself for it.

She saw.

Then did something the League had not taught him to counter.

She hugged him.

Zee froze.

All calculations vanished for one heartbeat.

Then his arms closed around her.

Mother.

Safe.

Still.

Aurelia whispered:

“They did not make you this.”

Zee’s eyes closed.

“No.”

“They only taught it discipline.”

He pulled back enough to look at her.

“How do you know?”

“Because I have known your darkness longer than they have.”

Rez folded his arms.

“Next time you decide to disappear into a legendary assassin stronghold, leave a note.”

Ner looked furious.

“A note?”

Rez shrugged.

“I am starting achievable.”

Zee laughed.

Still Zee.

Good.

But when a servant entered unexpectedly from behind, Zee had a knife in his hand before anyone else turned.

The blade stopped one inch from the servant’s throat.

Silence.

Zee stared.

The servant stared.

Zee lowered the knife.

“Sorry.”

The servant fled.

Ner looked toward him.

“That is going to become a problem.”

Zee slid the knife away.

“Yes.”

He knew.

Because Nanda Parbat had not made Zee Darkheart darker.

It had taught his darkness where every artery was.
