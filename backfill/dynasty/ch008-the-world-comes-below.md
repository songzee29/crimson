Chapter Eight

The World Comes Below

The old enemy crypts had not been opened since the Black Winter.

Captain Vale broke the first seal with the pommel of his sword.

The sound echoed through the buried corridor like a hammer striking a coffin.

Dust poured from the archway.

Queen Aurelia stood behind him with Zee held tightly against her chest while the crimson hatchling pressed along her shoulder, his small claws tangled in the queen’s cloak. The royal witch kept one hand raised, blue-violet mana trembling around her fingers as she listened for movement beyond the stone.

Ser Caldrin Marr watched the darkness.

“Why were these sealed?”

Vale pushed against the ancient door.

“Because House Darkheart won the war.”

Caldrin glanced at him. “That does not answer the question.”

“No.”

The stone groaned.

Vale shoved harder.

“Because what we defeated refused to stay defeated.”

The door opened.

Cold air rushed over them.

The hatchling lifted his head immediately.

Fear passed through the Dragon Link.

Not the sharp terror he had felt when Zee disappeared.

Something older.

Something waiting.

Zee stirred against Aurelia.

His crimson eyes opened.

The queen looked down.

“It is all right.”

She no longer believed those words when she said them.

But Zee recognized her voice.

That was enough.

For now.

They entered.

The crypt was enormous.

Far larger than anything that should have existed beneath Castle Darkheart.

Columns disappeared into darkness overhead. Broken statues lined a sunken avenue, each depicting warriors whose faces had been deliberately carved away. Rusted chains hung from walls covered in dead runes. In the distance, black water reflected the witch’s spell-light without revealing how deep it went.

Caldrin stopped.

“This is not a crypt.”

Vale looked ahead.

“No.”

“What is it?”

“A prison.”

Aurelia’s eyes hardened.

“You brought my son into a prison?”

“I brought him somewhere Victor would hesitate to enter.”

“That is not the same as safe.”

“There is no safe.”

The answer silenced them.

That truth had followed Zee since the sky began bleeding.

A sound came from somewhere beyond the columns.

Stone scraping stone.

The hatchling growled.

Vale raised his sword.

Caldrin drew his own.

The royal witch strengthened her light.

Aurelia held Zee closer.

“Move.”

They continued deeper.

Behind them, far above, Castle Darkheart remained at war.

And beneath it…

Something had begun waking.

⸻

King Victor reached the lower passage minutes later.

He found blood first.

Aurelia’s.

Drops marked the old stones where the wound on her arm had reopened during the escape.

Victor crouched beside one.

His expression tightened.

Captain Vale had betrayed him.

Aurelia had fled.

His son had been taken into the oldest foundations of the castle.

Every part of him wanted to pursue.

Then he remembered the dagger.

The stillness after Zee’s heartbeat stopped.

The impossible breath that followed.

Victor’s hand closed around the hilt of his sword.

He would not do that again.

That certainty had formed the moment Zee returned.

Not from mercy alone.

Fear had changed shape.

Victor no longer feared only what Zee might become if allowed to live.

He now feared what trying to kill him might create.

The prophecy had warned him.

He had tested it.

Once.

That mistake would not be repeated.

But he would not allow Aurelia to disappear with something that could bring twelve kingdoms down upon House Darkheart either.

Victor rose.

“Seal every tunnel leading beyond the castle.”

The soldiers behind him exchanged glances.

“My King?”

“You heard me.”

“What about the queen?”

“She remains within Darkheart territory.”

“And the prince?”

Victor’s face hardened.

“Especially the prince.”

The commander bowed.

Victor looked into the darkness ahead.

“Send men through the southern passages.”

“How many?”

Victor considered.

Then stopped himself.

Not an army.

Not near Zee.

“Six.”

The commander looked surprised.

“Only six?”

“I said six.”

Victor stared into the tunnel.

“Do not attack the child.”

The soldiers went still.

Victor’s next words came colder.

“Do not provoke him.”

That order spread through the castle before sunset.

The king who had been the first person to kill Zee now became the first ruler to warn his own soldiers against attempting it again.

Not because he had accepted his son.

Because he had learned to fear the result.

⸻

Outside Castle Darkheart, the foreign armies learned something else.

The siege was failing.

Not because House Darkheart was stronger.

Because the coalition was beginning to fracture.

The twelve kingdoms had agreed that the child needed to die.

They had not agreed on who should control what remained afterward.

Some wanted the body burned.

Others wanted it studied.

One vampire-backed kingdom demanded the blood.

A northern wolf king wanted proof that the newborn’s existence had caused the madness among his packs.

Three witch courts wanted the child alive long enough to examine the strange mana witnessed during the birth.

The moment survival became politics…

Unity began dying.

Inside the largest command tent, King Orsik of Dunmere slammed his gauntlet onto a wooden table.

“We breach the western wall tonight.”

A foreign general shook his head.

“And lose another two thousand men.”

“Then lose them.”

“They are not your soldiers.”

“They will be dead either way if that creature grows.”

Across the table, Lady Maevra of the Ash Coven watched them with visible contempt.

“You continue calling him a creature because it makes your fear sound noble.”

Orsik glared at her.

“You lost your magic when he was born.”

“No.”

The witch’s expression sharpened.

“I could not reach it.”

“That distinction will comfort the dead?”

“It comforts truth.”

“Truth is that something happened.”

“Yes.”

“And the child was at its center.”

“Perhaps.”

Orsik laughed.

“You witches hide cowardice behind uncertainty.”

Maevra leaned forward.

“And kings hide stupidity behind certainty.”

Hands moved toward weapons.

A vampire envoy seated near the rear of the tent smiled.

This was more familiar.

Fear dividing prey.

King Orsik noticed.

“You find this amusing?”

The vampire’s smile faded.

“No.”

“You people felt him.”

The tent grew quieter.

The vampire looked toward Castle Darkheart.

“Yes.”

“What did you feel?”

He considered lying.

Instead, he said, “A heartbeat that did not belong inside my blood.”

“Whose?”

“I do not know.”

“Was it the child?”

“I do not know.”

Orsik struck the table again.

“Does anyone know anything?”

No one answered.

That was the truth none of them wanted.

They had marched thousands of soldiers across kingdoms to destroy something they did not understand.

Then a runner entered.

“My lords.”

Orsik turned.

“What?”

“Movement beneath the eastern ridge.”

“How many?”

“We do not know.”

“Darkheart troops?”

The runner hesitated.

“No.”

The vampire envoy stood.

“What did you see?”

The soldier swallowed.

“Something came out of the ground.”

⸻

In the enemy crypts, Aurelia heard the first scream.

It came from ahead.

Human.

Short.

Then silence.

Vale stopped walking.

“That was not one of ours.”

Caldrin looked back. “You sound disappointed.”

“It means someone else found another entrance.”

The witch dimmed her light.

“How?”

“Siege damage,” Vale said. “Or the foreigners knew more about the old tunnels than we thought.”

Aurelia looked down at Zee.

The child remained awake.

Too awake.

His crimson eyes moved slowly through the darkness.

The hatchling’s body had gone rigid.

Danger.

Closer now.

Not one thing.

Many.

Vale whispered, “Stay behind me.”

Aurelia almost laughed.

“Everyone keeps saying that.”

This time, she obeyed.

They entered a wider chamber.

Bodies covered the floor.

Foreign scouts.

Six, perhaps seven.

All dead.

No visible wounds.

Their faces were frozen in expressions of terror.

Caldrin crouched beside one.

“No blood.”

The witch stepped closer.

“What killed them?”

Caldrin turned the body.

Nothing.

No cuts.

No broken bones.

No burns.

Vale looked toward the far side of the chamber.

An ancient door stood open.

Black mist crawled beneath it.

“That.”

Aurelia’s voice lowered.

“What was imprisoned here?”

Vale did not answer.

She looked at him.

“Captain.”

“My grandfather called them Hollow Men.”

Caldrin stood.

“That sounds comforting.”

“They are not men.”

The mist thickened.

The royal witch raised both hands.

Violet mana gathered around her.

“What are they?”

Vale’s grip tightened around his sword.

“Things that learned how to wear people.”

The first one stepped through the doorway.

It wore the body of a dead soldier.

Almost.

Its joints bent incorrectly.

Its smile stretched too far.

Its eyes were empty black pits.

The hatchling screamed.

Zee’s Crimson Mana surged.

The Hollow thing stopped.

For the first time, it looked uncertain.

Then more shapes emerged behind it.

Three.

Five.

Nine.

Caldrin whispered, “How many did your ancestors bury?”

Vale answered without taking his eyes off them.

“Enough.”

The first creature rushed forward.

Vale met it with steel.

His sword passed through its chest.

The body folded around the blade as though empty.

Then one black hand closed around his throat.

Caldrin struck from the side.

The creature twisted toward him.

The royal witch released a blast of violet force.

The Hollow thing flew backward and struck a column.

It stood immediately.

“Magic works,” she said.

“Not well enough,” Vale choked.

The others came.

Aurelia backed toward the wall.

The hatchling spread his tiny wings.

Teal-and-gold mana flashed violently around him.

One Hollow creature turned toward Zee.

Everything changed.

The thing ignored the adults.

It moved toward the child.

Aurelia felt the shift instantly.

“No.”

The witch stepped between them and cast.

A binding spell wrapped around the creature’s limbs.

It tore through the spell.

Caldrin intercepted it.

Its hand struck his chest and threw him across the chamber.

Vale cut through another.

Two more replaced it.

The Hollow creature kept coming.

Zee watched.

He did not understand monsters.

He understood Aurelia’s fear.

He understood the hatchling’s panic.

He understood something approaching.

Danger.

His heartbeat accelerated.

Crimson Mana thickened around him.

The creature reached.

The hatchling lunged first.

Newborn claws struck its face.

Teal-and-gold Dragon Mana burst against black flesh.

For the first time…

The Hollow creature screamed.

Not from pain.

From recognition.

The sound stopped everyone.

The creature recoiled from the hatchling.

Then looked at Zee again.

Something in its stolen face changed.

Hunger.

Aurelia saw it.

“Get away from him!”

The Hollow thing attacked.

Zee’s Crimson Mana erupted.

Not outward.

Inward.

Across his skin.

The creature’s hand touched him.

Then froze.

Blackness spread across Zee’s blanket.

Aurelia screamed.

The witch reached for the creature.

“Do not touch it!”

Too late.

The Hollow thing began draining something from Zee.

Not blood.

Not mana alone.

Warmth.

Life.

The child’s body went cold against Aurelia.

The Dragon Link exploded with terror.

Gone?

No.

No.

Stay.

The hatchling bit into the Hollow creature’s wrist.

Teal-and-gold mana flooded through the contact.

The creature shrieked again.

Zee reacted.

Instinct met threat.

His vampire nature reached first.

Tiny fangs extended.

Aurelia felt him turn.

Before she could understand what he was doing, Zee bit down into the Hollow creature’s hand.

The chamber went silent.

The creature convulsed.

Black essence flowed toward Zee.

Not willingly.

The Hollow thing tried to pull away.

It could not.

Crimson Mana wrapped around its arm.

The essence entered Zee.

His body seized.

Aurelia nearly dropped him.

“Zee!”

The creature collapsed.

Its stolen human shape withered inward until nothing remained but black ash.

Everyone stared.

Zee released the dead hand.

His crimson eyes remained open.

Something dark moved briefly beneath his skin.

Then Crimson Mana consumed it.

The blackness vanished.

Zee inhaled sharply.

Warmth returned to his body.

The witch stared in horror.

“What did he just—”

“Do not,” Aurelia snapped.

No explanations.

Not now.

The remaining Hollow creatures stopped attacking.

Every one of them looked toward Zee.

Then they retreated.

Not from the adults.

From him.

Vale watched them disappear beyond the ancient doorway.

Caldrin pushed himself upright, one hand pressed against his ribs.

“That seems bad.”

Aurelia looked down at her son.

Zee was breathing normally again.

Yet his Crimson Mana had changed.

Only slightly.

A thin black-red edge now appeared whenever it moved across his skin.

Gone within seconds.

Present long enough to be real.

The hatchling pressed his forehead against Zee.

Safe?

Zee’s tiny hand found him.

Safe.

For the moment.

The royal witch approached carefully.

“My Queen.”

“No theories.”

“I was going to say we need to leave.”

Aurelia looked toward the open doorway.

The Hollow creatures had vanished.

Vale wiped blood from his mouth.

“They will come back.”

Caldrin frowned. “Why?”

Vale looked at Zee.

“Because everything does.”

⸻

By nightfall, the story escaped the crypts.

Not the truth.

Never the truth.

A wounded foreign scout reached the coalition camp babbling about dead men walking beneath Castle Darkheart.

Another claimed a crimson-eyed infant had devoured a shadow.

A third insisted the dragon hatchling breathed gold fire.

None of them had seen the same thing.

None of them agreed.

That did not stop the rumors.

The child had survived death.

The child commanded monsters.

The child ate souls.

The child had awakened something under the castle.

Every retelling became worse.

Kingdoms that had marched to destroy Zee became more determined.

Others became afraid to approach him at all.

The first bounty appeared that night.

Not from a king.

From merchants.

Ten thousand silver crowns for proof of Zee Darkheart’s death.

Twenty thousand if the body remained intact.

By morning, assassins would hear.

By the end of the week, hunters would come.

Not armies.

Individuals.

Patient people.

Cruel people.

People who did not care about prophecies or kingdoms.

Only payment.

The world had learned something from the siege.

Armies were too loud.

Fear would become clever next.

⸻

Aurelia emerged from the lower crypts shortly before dawn.

Not into Castle Darkheart.

Into the forest beyond the western cliffs.

Captain Vale had known one forgotten exit.

The tunnel opened behind a waterfall half a mile from the fortress walls.

Cold dawn air struck Aurelia’s face.

She stopped.

For the first time since Zee was born, there was no ceiling above him.

No castle.

No nursery.

No execution chamber.

Only sky.

The hatchling looked upward.

He had never seen open sky either.

His tiny wings spread instinctively.

Teal-and-gold mana shimmered around him.

Zee followed his movement.

The Dragon Link carried something new.

Wonder.

Aurelia felt tears rise unexpectedly.

Caldrin emerged behind them.

“We cannot remain here.”

She knew.

The armies were east.

Darkheart patrols were everywhere.

Assassins would come soon.

Victor would search.

The world would keep moving.

Aurelia looked toward Castle Darkheart in the distance.

Smoke rose from its broken towers.

Her kingdom.

Her husband.

Her home.

All behind her.

She looked down at Zee.

His crimson eyes stared up at the bleeding colors of sunrise.

He had lived three days.

In those three days, the heavens had rained blood.

The world had marched against him.

His father had killed him.

He had returned.

And something buried beneath House Darkheart had already tried to consume him.

Aurelia tightened the blanket around him.

“Your life will not always be this.”

Vale looked away.

Caldrin said nothing.

Even Aurelia knew it was a lie.

The hatchling curled against Zee.

The Dragon Link carried a simple feeling between them.

Go.

Aurelia looked west.

Beyond the mountains lay kingdoms that had not yet chosen whether to fear Zee.

Among them…

One kingdom had refused to send soldiers to Castle Darkheart.

House Vesper.

Aurelia did not know whether that meant safety.

She had stopped believing in safety.

But it meant a direction.

“West,” she said.

Vale nodded.

Caldrin adjusted his sword.

The witch pulled up her hood.

Behind them, Castle Darkheart continued burning beneath siege fire.

Ahead, the ancient world waited.

And for the first time, Zee Darkheart left the kingdom of his birth.

Not as a prince beginning a royal journey.

As a hunted child.

The world had tried an army.

It had failed.

Now it would begin sending everything else.
