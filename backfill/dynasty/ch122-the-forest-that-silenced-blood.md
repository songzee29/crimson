Chapter One Hundred Twenty-Two

The Forest That Silenced Blood

The trees bent toward Zee and Rez.

Not from wind.

There was none.

Every trunk leaned.

Every branch angled inward.

Leaves turned their undersides toward the road.

The entire forest behaved like one thing had noticed them.

Rez’s Blue-White Mana flickered again.

Then stabilized.

Zee’s Crimson Phoenix fire dimmed across one wing.

Only slightly.

Enough to matter.

The old Witch beside them stumbled.

Her magic disappeared completely.

Zee caught her before she hit the ground.

“Back.”

Rez moved first.

Blue-White Witchcraft formed a barrier around the surviving villagers.

The barrier held.

Barely.

The forest pushed.

Not physically.

Supernaturally.

Every bloodline inside the village reacted.

Vampires weakened.

Wolves shook.

Witches lost casting entirely.

Zee listened.

The silence was wrong.

No natural supernatural rhythm.

No blood signature.

No heartbeat.

Nothing to hunt.

That irritated him.

Then a voice came from the forest.

Not spoken.

Heard.

Too many.

Rez’s eyes narrowed.

“You heard that?”

“Yes.”

Too many bloodlines in one body.

Zee’s expression changed.

The forest was speaking to him.

Specifically.

He stepped beyond Rez’s barrier.

Rez grabbed his shoulder.

“Do not.”

Zee looked back.

“You asked me here.”

“I did not ask you to walk into the thing that removes supernatural nature.”

“Fair.”

Rez released him.

Zee continued anyway.

Rez closed his eyes.

“Of course.”

⸻

The first step into the forest weakened Zee’s Witchcraft.

The second dulled Vampire hearing.

The third muted Wolf scent.

His Dragon heat cooled.

Phoenix flame retreated beneath his skin.

Crimson Sovereign synchronization remained.

But there was less to synchronize.

That was new.

Zee stopped.

Then smiled.

Not because this was easy.

Because it was not.

The voice returned.

Wrong.

Zee looked between the trees.

“What?”

You should collapse.

“That sounds disappointing for you.”

The forest tightened.

Pressure struck.

Zee’s knees nearly bent.

Nearly.

His body reacted differently than the villagers.

Not immunity.

Adaptation.

His blood had survived suppression before.

Witch-suppression poison.

Dragonbane.

Dark Divinity wounds.

Phoenix fire.

Divine blood.

Silver.

Compulsion.

Restraints.

His biology knew how to resist being told what it was allowed to be.

The forest suppressed.

Zee’s body pushed back.

Not enough to break it.

Enough to remain standing.

Rez watched from the road.

“That is very annoying.”

Zee glanced toward him.

“What?”

“You are proving difficult to suppress through spite.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

⸻

Something moved behind Zee.

He turned.

Too late.

A pale limb struck him across the back.

His Phoenix wings failed to open.

He hit a tree hard enough to crack it.

The creature stepped into view.

Tall.

Thin.

Gray skin stretched across a body almost Human.

No eyes.

No mouth.

No obvious organs.

Its chest was hollow.

Literally.

A cavity opened between the ribs.

Inside—

nothing.

No heart.

No blood.

No flame.

No Mana.

No scent.

Rez looked at it.

“That explains the tracking problem.”

The creature attacked again.

Zee blocked with one arm.

Without Dragon strength fully available, the impact hurt more than expected.

Bone cracked.

Zee smiled.

The creature tilted its head.

Then Zee punched it.

Human strength compared to usual.

Still enough to stagger it.

His broken arm began healing.

Slowly.

Vampire regeneration fighting suppression.

Phoenix vitality barely answering.

The creature reached for his throat.

Zee caught its wrist.

Its skin was cold.

Not dead.

Empty.

That was different.

He looked toward the hollow chest.

Then understood.

“It is not taking powers.”

Rez heard him.

“What?”

“It is creating absence.”

The creature slammed Zee into another tree.

He hit the ground.

Rez moved.

Blue-White Mana erupted.

The forest suppressed half the spell.

Half remained.

Enough.

A blade of Blue-White force cut across the creature’s back.

It turned toward Rez.

Good.

Zee stood.

His Witchcraft flickered.

One word.

“Hold.”

Nothing.

Then—

a fraction of effect.

The creature slowed.

Not enough.

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“Again.”

His magic answered harder.

“Hold.”

The creature stopped for one heartbeat.

Rez understood.

He attacked.

Blue-White chains wrapped its legs.

Zee crossed the distance.

Claws barely formed.

Enough.

He drove one through the hollow chest.

Nothing there.

No heart.

No core.

No immediate kill.

The creature grabbed him.

Then Zayrlen screamed across the bond of blood.

Not literally across miles.

Not a Link.

Something more primitive.

Pack.

Family.

Zee froze.

⸻

Darkheart.

Ner sat upright in the nursery.

Zayrlen was crying.

Not normal crying.

Crimson-and-Gold Witch pressure pushed against the cradle.

Tiny claws extended.

Heat rolled through the room.

Elias entered first.

“What happened?”

“I do not know.”

Ner picked Zayrlen up.

The baby did not calm.

His heartbeat hammered.

Wolf distress.

Dragon heat.

Witch pressure.

All three reacting to something not inside the room.

Ner immediately understood one thing.

“Zee.”

Elias’s expression changed.

“Can Zayrlen sense him?”

“Not like a Link.”

“No.”

Ner held Zayrlen closer.

Bloodsong opened.

Not controlling the child.

Listening.

The baby’s blood resonance was pulling toward something distant.

A familiar rhythm.

Father.

Ner’s face hardened.

“Something is happening to him.”

Zareth arrived through the balcony in Human form before anyone called.

His face was already wrong.

“The Link is fading.”

Silence.

Ner looked at him.

“What does that mean?”

“I can still feel Zee.”

Zareth’s Teal-and-Gold Mana flickered.

“But less.”

Rez’s location.

Vesper.

Everyone understood.

Ner handed Zayrlen to Elias.

Elias blinked.

“What are you doing?”

“Going.”

“No.”

Ner looked at him.

Elias continued quickly.

“I mean—absolutely not alone.”

Amara entered behind him.

“Already coming.”

Kael appeared from the hall.

“So am I.”

Rhyven arrived next.

Tessa last.

Zayrlen screamed again.

Zareth’s expression became lethal.

“Move.”

⸻

Back in Vesper, Zee felt the forest push deeper.

Witchcraft dimmed again.

Wolf instinct went quiet.

Vampire senses collapsed to almost Human range.

Dragon blood cooled.

Phoenix fire disappeared entirely.

For the first time in years—

Zee felt small.

Not powerless.

But reduced.

The hollow creature noticed.

Its fingers closed around Zee’s throat.

Rez attacked from behind.

The creature released Zee and struck him instead.

Rez’s Vampire speed failed halfway through the dodge.

The blow caught his ribs.

He flew.

Zee heard the crack.

Something inside him changed.

Not power.

Decision.

He could not overpower the forest as himself.

So he stopped trying.

Zee looked at the creature.

Human heartbeat absent.

No blood.

No scent.

No Mana.

No supernatural core.

But it still occupied space.

Still moved air.

Still had weight.

Still bent branches.

Assassin training answered.

Not bloodline.

Skill.

Zee watched the shoulders.

Hip rotation.

Foot placement.

Balance.

The creature lunged.

Zee stepped aside.

Human speed.

Perfect timing.

He caught the elbow.

Turned.

Used momentum.

The creature hit the ground.

Zee drove his knee into the shoulder joint.

Bone snapped.

The creature reacted.

Good.

Physical anatomy.

Different.

But present.

Zee smiled.

“There you are.”

⸻

Rez rose slowly.

“You look happy.”

“I found something it cannot suppress.”

“What?”

Zee broke the creature’s second arm.

“Practice.”

Rez smiled.

“Finally useful.”

The creature twisted violently.

Its leg struck Zee’s chest.

He went backward.

Then rolled.

Stood.

No supernatural flourish.

No flight.

No spells.

Just Nanda Parbat.

Angles.

Breath.

Joint destruction.

Momentum.

Pressure.

Zee stopped fighting like a supernatural King.

He fought like the assassin he had trained to become.

The creature adapted.

So did he.

It reached.

He broke fingers.

It kicked.

He took the leg.

Twisted the knee.

It tried using the hollow chest to release suppression directly.

Zee jammed a broken branch through the cavity.

The creature froze.

Rez stared.

“That worked?”

Zee did too.

Then smiled.

“Apparently.”

Blue-White Mana returned slightly around Rez.

The forest shuddered.

The creature had been part of the suppression network.

Not the source.

But an anchor.

Zee drove the branch deeper.

The hollow body cracked from inside.

Then collapsed into gray ash.

The forest screamed.

Every tree bent away.

Zee’s powers returned at once.

Not fully.

Enough.

Phoenix fire ignited.

Crimson wings erupted.

Vampire hearing flooded back.

Wolf scent returned.

Witchcraft sharpened.

Dragon heat roared beneath his scales.

The Crimson Sovereign snapped into complete alignment.

Rez looked at him.

“There he is.”

Zee’s eyes became Crimson-Gold.

“No.”

He looked deeper into the forest.

“Now I am angry.”

⸻

The surviving villagers behind Rez’s barrier began recovering.

Slowly.

A Wolf shifted accidentally.

A Vampire’s wound started closing.

Witches felt Mana return in weak pulses.

The suppression had not permanently stripped them.

Good.

Zee looked toward the ashes.

Then farther into the trees.

“How many anchors?”

Rez closed his eyes.

His Blue-White Mana spread carefully.

“More than one.”

Zee’s Wolf scent found nothing.

Blood Mark had nothing.

The King’s Hunt had no biological prey.

So he used the one thing the forest could not erase.

Pattern.

The trees bent toward active anchors.

Not randomly.

Directionally.

Zee studied them.

Then pointed northeast.

“There.”

Rez frowned.

“How?”

“They lean.”

Rez looked.

Then smiled.

“Very annoying.”

“Yes.”

They moved.

⸻

The second anchor died faster.

The third fought harder.

By the fourth, the forest understood Zee had learned.

So it changed.

Suppression no longer attacked all his bloodlines equally.

It targeted Witch first.

Then Phoenix.

Then Wolf.

Trying different order.

Zee noticed.

“So you learn.”

The voice answered.

Yes.

“Good.”

Rez looked at him.

“Why is that good?”

Zee smiled.

“Because now I know it is choosing.”

Choice meant responsibility.

Responsibility meant prey.

⸻

They found the center before midnight.

Not a creature.

A tree.

Ancient.

Larger than any living tree in Vesper.

Its roots spread beneath all three affected villages.

Its trunk was pale.

No leaves.

No bark.

The surface looked like bone.

Hundreds of hollow creatures were embedded inside it.

Sleeping.

Or waiting.

At the base stood one person.

Human.

Or looked Human.

An old woman in gray robes.

She watched Zee approach.

“You killed four children.”

Rez’s expression changed.

Zee did not.

“The hollow ones?”

“They are extensions.”

“Yours?”

“Yes.”

“Then you attacked Vesper.”

“I corrected imbalance.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“What imbalance?”

“You.”

There it was.

The woman looked at him.

“Creation has accelerated.”

Phoenix–Wolf.

Phoenix–Dragon–Witch.

Demon–Witch.

Natural Tribrid Prince.

New bloodlines.

Old boundaries collapsing.

The woman continued.

“You create where nature did not.”

Zee’s expression remained flat.

“Nature created me.”

“And the world broke around your birth.”

“Sounds like a world problem.”

Rez almost laughed.

The woman did not.

“You multiplied the error.”

“No.”

Zee’s wings opened.

“I multiplied survivors.”

The tree behind her pulsed.

Suppression slammed outward.

Rez dropped to one knee.

Zee’s wings extinguished.

Again.

The woman smiled.

“You cannot kill what has no bloodline to consume.”

Zee stared.

Then drew a knife.

Plain steel.

The woman’s smile disappeared.

Zee stepped forward.

“I have killed people without magic before.”

⸻
