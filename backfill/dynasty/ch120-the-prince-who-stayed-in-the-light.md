Chapter One Hundred Twenty

The Prince Who Stayed in the Light

The morning after the kidnapping attempt, Darkheart expected Zee to disappear his son.

The rumor moved before breakfast.

Some thought Zayrlen would be moved beneath the castle.

Others believed Rez would take him somewhere beyond Darkheart.

A few assumed Zee would place so many concealment spells around the newborn Prince that nobody outside the family would ever see him again.

They were wrong.

At noon—

Zee carried Zayrlen into the central courtyard.

Publicly.

No disguise.

No veil.

No hidden carriage.

No illusion changing his face.

Zayrlen slept against his father’s chest beneath a dark blanket embroidered in Crimson and Gold.

Ner walked beside them.

Still recovering.

Still armed.

Still capable of killing anyone stupid enough to mistake recovery for weakness.

Rez stood near the steps.

Zareth leaned against one column in Human form.

Elias, Kael, Tessa, Amara, and Rhyven were spread through the courtyard.

Not arranged like guards.

Family rarely needed arranging.

Darkheart gathered quietly.

Zee stopped in the center.

Zayrlen stirred.

His tiny heartbeat changed.

Then settled again when Zee adjusted his hold.

A councilor stepped forward carefully.

“Majesty.”

Zee looked at him.

“We assumed the Prince would remain within the inner keep.”

“He will not.”

Silence.

Another official asked:

“After yesterday?”

“Yes.”

Someone farther back whispered:

“They nearly took him.”

Zee heard.

Of course he did.

He looked toward the crowd.

“They nearly reached him.”

Different.

The courtyard quieted.

“My son will grow up knowing his kingdom.”

His Crimson Phoenix wings remained folded.

His voice did not need them.

“He will know its roads.”

Its people.

Its walls.

Its names.

“He will not spend his childhood inside a locked room because enemies decided his existence frightened them.”

Ner looked at him.

Something passed between them.

Agreement.

Not discussion.

Already decided.

Zee continued.

“If they come again—”

His pupils narrowed.

“Darkheart kills them again.”

No grand applause followed.

The statement was too simple.

Too believable.

Then an old Wolf woman stepped out of the crowd.

She held one of the badly carved wooden animals Darkheart children had been leaving outside the royal rooms.

Zee recognized it.

A Wolf.

Mostly.

One leg was shorter than the others.

The woman stopped several feet away.

“For the Prince.”

Zee looked at Ner.

Ner nodded.

The woman placed the toy on the stone.

Then walked away.

Another person came.

Then another.

Within minutes, the courtyard floor held gifts.

Nothing expensive.

Nothing royal.

Blankets.

Carvings.

Small charms.

A tiny practice wand that would not be useful for years.

A child’s wooden shield painted Gold.

A Wolf tooth tied to Crimson cord.

Zee looked down at Zayrlen.

The baby opened his eyes.

Crimson-Gold.

Then sneezed.

A tiny spark burned a hole in the blanket.

Ner closed his eyes.

“Wonderful.”

Zee looked at the smoke.

“He is improving.”

“He damaged clothing.”

“Accurately.”

“He is a month old.”

“Still.”

Ner looked toward the crowd.

Darkheart was laughing.

Not at Zayrlen.

With him.

That was the point.

The Prince stayed in the light.

⸻

Darkheart changed around him anyway.

Not by hiding.

By becoming harder.

Rez rewrote inner access systems until even Zee complained about them.

“You added three checkpoints between my rooms and the west balcony.”

Rez did not look up from his papers.

“Yes.”

“I live here.”

“Yes.”

“I am King.”

“Unfortunate.”

Zee stared.

Rez continued writing.

“You wanted Darkheart harder to enter.”

“I meant for enemies.”

“You look suspicious.”

“I have wings.”

“Exactly.”

Zee considered killing him.

Did not.

Barely.

⸻

Elias changed the concealment wards.

Not by making them stronger.

By making them stranger.

The kidnappers had masked heartbeats.

Suppressed scent.

Hidden Witch signatures.

So Elias stopped designing detection around what intruders possessed.

He designed it around what was missing.

A hallway with no scent where scent should exist.

A heartbeat gap moving through populated space.

Magic behaving too quietly.

Fear existing without a visible source.

His Demon-Witch nature proved unexpectedly perfect for it.

Deep-blue Witch circles spread beneath Darkheart’s inner floors.

Black demonic sigils lived inside them.

Not corrupted Witchcraft.

His Witchcraft.

His race.

His expression.

Zee watched Elias finish the nursery corridor.

“You made the ward unpleasant.”

Elias smiled.

“Thank you.”

“That was not praise.”

“I accepted it anyway.”

The ward activated.

For one second, Zee felt as though the hallway itself had noticed he had secrets.

He frowned.

“I dislike this.”

“Excellent.”

Ner walked through carrying Zayrlen.

Nothing happened.

Elias looked satisfied.

“It recognizes family patterns.”

Zee looked at him.

“Everyone?”

“Core family, approved guards, healers, household staff.”

“Rez?”

Elias smiled.

“Especially Rez.”

From somewhere beyond the corridor came:

“WHY DID THE FLOOR BITE ME?”

Elias looked delighted.

Zee almost smiled.

⸻

Zareth took the aerial defenses personally.

He remained in Darkheart for four days instead of returning immediately to Drakonis.

On the second day, Zee found him in true Dragon form above the keep.

Huge.

Teal-and-Gold scales catching the sunlight.

Original Dragon wings stretched over the towers.

Phoenix fire traced their edges.

Witch sigils rotated in the air around him.

Zareth was mapping every possible flight path toward the nursery.

Through the Link came:

There are seventeen approaches.

Zee looked upward.

There were.

Zareth sent an image.

Towers.

Crosswinds.

Roofs.

Blind angles.

Then several routes disappeared beneath Teal-and-Gold ward structures.

Now there are four.

Make it two.

A pause.

You are unreasonable.

Yes.

Good.

Zareth continued.

⸻

Ner changed too.

Not power.

Behavior.

Before Zayrlen, Ner had slept lightly because he was a Bloodsong.

After the kidnapping attempt—

he barely slept at all.

Zee noticed by the third night.

Ner sat beside the cradle.

Zayrlen slept.

Zee stood in the doorway.

“You need sleep.”

“So do you.”

“I do not.”

“You absolutely do.”

Zee entered.

Ner did not move.

“The ward is active.”

“I know.”

“Elias is outside.”

“I know.”

“Amara has Wolves in the lower hall.”

“I know.”

“Rhyven is on the eastern roof.”

“I know.”

“Rez has somehow turned the west staircase into a legal process.”

Ner almost smiled.

Still did not move.

Zee crouched beside him.

“They almost took him from me.”

There.

Not weakness.

Truth.

Zee looked at Zayrlen.

“From us.”

Ner stared at the cradle.

“I heard that restraint open.”

His voice stayed controlled.

“I saw his hand reach down.”

Zee’s jaw tightened.

Ner looked toward him.

“I know you killed him.”

“Yes.”

“I wish I had.”

Zee understood.

No moral correction.

No reminder that the attacker was already dead.

He simply reached for Ner’s hand.

Ner let him.

After a moment, Zee said:

“He stays public.”

“Yes.”

“We do not make him smaller because they frightened us.”

“No.”

“We make them more afraid to try again.”

Ner finally smiled.

“There you are.”

⸻

Zayrlen woke.

Both fathers turned immediately.

The baby blinked.

Then stared at them.

His pupils narrowed.

Slits.

Wolf.

Heat spread over the cradle.

Dragon.

Crimson-and-Gold magic moved faintly around his fists.

Witch.

Then all three disappeared when he realized both fathers were there.

He relaxed.

Ner stared.

“He was checking.”

Zee listened.

The heartbeat had changed the moment Zayrlen woke.

Searching.

Pack.

Then settled.

“He was.”

Ner picked him up.

Zayrlen buried his face against his chest.

Zee looked at the small child who had already learned enough instinct to recognize absence.

Something in him hardened.

Not toward Zayrlen.

Toward the world.

No one would teach his son fear before he learned language.

Not if Zee could help it.

⸻

The next attack did not come for Zayrlen.

It came for the people rebuilding the northern settlement destroyed by the Pale Court.

A message arrived near midnight.

Thirty-one workers trapped.

Unknown creatures in the foundations.

No enemy banners.

No political demand.

Just danger.

Zee listened.

Then looked toward the cradle.

Ner understood immediately.

“Go.”

Zee hesitated.

Only a second.

That second mattered.

Ner saw it.

“I am here.”

“Yes.”

“So are all the unreasonable people you call family.”

“Yes.”

“Then be King.”

Zee looked at Zayrlen.

The baby slept.

Then Zee opened his wings.

“Call me if his heartbeat changes.”

Ner stared.

“You are twenty miles away.”

“I can still get back.”

“You are insane.”

“Yes.”

Zee flew.

The Prince remained in Darkheart.

Not hidden.

Protected.

And the King returned to work.

⸻

The creatures beneath the northern settlement were not supernatural experiments.

That almost felt novel.

They were burrowing predators.

Ancient.

Six-legged.

Blind.

Drawn toward magical concentrations.

Rebuilding had disturbed a buried nest.

No mastermind.

No laboratory.

No kingdom.

Just the world being dangerous.

Zee landed beside Amara.

She pointed toward a collapsed foundation.

“Thirty-one below.”

“Alive?”

“Twenty-eight.”

Zee’s expression tightened.

“Three?”

“Already dead.”

No one to kill for that.

No villain.

No revenge.

Only rescue.

Sometimes those were harder.

Zee entered the ground.

⸻

He used everything differently.

Not execution.

Precision.

Witchcraft held collapsing tunnels.

Vampire hearing separated human heartbeats from the creatures’ strange internal pulses.

Wolf scent followed trapped workers.

Dragon strength moved stone.

Phoenix wings shielded survivors when sections collapsed.

Crimson fire provided heat without burning oxygen too quickly.

No new evolution.

No new race.

No dramatic transformation.

Just mastery.

The Crimson Sovereign at work inside a body carrying far more than Sovereign blood now.

Zee found the first twelve.

Then six.

Then five.

The final five were trapped beyond the nest.

The largest creature waited between.

It was nearly the size of a house.

Zee looked at it.

The creature screamed.

Zee’s wings opened in the tunnel.

Barely.

“Move.”

It did not.

Fair.

He killed it.

Quickly.

Then continued.

Twenty-eight came home alive.

Three did not.

Darkheart rebuilt anyway.

⸻

Zee returned before dawn.

Ner was asleep.

Finally.

Zayrlen was awake.

Alone in the cradle.

Not unguarded.

Elias sat outside.

Amara’s Wolves occupied the hall.

Zareth’s aerial wards covered the roof.

Rez’s ridiculous checkpoints still existed.

But inside the room—

Zayrlen was simply awake.

Zee approached.

Crimson-Gold eyes found him.

Tiny hands moved.

Reaching.

Zee picked him up.

The baby relaxed against his chest.

“Your father finally slept.”

Zayrlen made a small sound.

“Yes.”

Zee looked toward Ner.

Then down at his son.

“This is what we chose.”

Not safety.

Not peace.

Life.

Openly.

Danger included.

And Zee would not take that choice back.
