Chapter Fifty-One

The Place Beneath Places

The rescued hunter woke screaming.

Not because of pain.

Because he saw the sky.

He sat upright in Rez’s arms, clawed at the frozen ground, and shouted:

“Do not let it turn!”

Rez grabbed both shoulders.

“Look at me.”

The hunter did not.

His eyes stayed fixed on the forest floor.

“Do not let it turn!”

Ner knelt beside him.

“What turns?”

“The land.”

“What does that mean?”

The hunter looked at him as though language had become useless.

Then he saw Zareth.

He froze.

“A Dragon.”

Zareth lowered his head.

The man started laughing.

Not relief.

Despair.

“Oh, gods.”

Rez’s expression hardened.

“Stay with us.”

The hunter looked toward him.

“You crossed the stone.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Looking for you.”

The hunter stared.

Something like guilt entered his face.

“You should not have.”

Zee crouched across from him.

“What happened to the others?”

The hunter’s eyes found Zee.

Recognition came slowly.

“The Darkheart boy.”

“Zee.”

The man swallowed.

“We found animals.”

“The eyeless six-legged things?”

His face changed.

“You saw them?”

“Yes.”

“Then it is moving.”

Rez looked toward Ner.

Ner frowned.

“What is moving?”

The hunter shook his head.

“We thought they were monsters.”

Zee smiled faintly.

“Were they?”

“No.”

The answer came instantly.

“They were running.”

Just as Zee had thought.

The hunter continued.

“We followed them.”

“Found a valley that wasn’t on our maps.”

“A river running uphill.”

“Trees growing beneath the ground.”

“One of my men stepped across a shadow.”

Zee frowned.

“A shadow?”

“He never came back.”

No blood.

No body.

No sound.

The hunter’s hands shook.

“Then the world turned.”

Rez’s eyes narrowed.

“Describe that.”

“I cannot.”

“Try.”

The man stared toward the trees.

“The mountain was above us.”

“Then beside us.”

“Then inside the river.”

“One moment I was standing.”

“The next I was falling without moving.”

Ner went still.

That matched his boundary.

Distance changing without movement.

The hunter continued.

“Something found us.”

Zee leaned forward.

“The eye?”

The man stopped breathing for a moment.

“You saw it.”

“Yes.”

“Then it saw you.”

Zareth growled.

The hunter flinched.

Zee asked, “What is it?”

“I do not know.”

Good.

Terrible.

Good.

The hunter looked toward the unconscious forest.

“We never saw a body.”

“Only pieces.”

“An eye.”

“Something like antlers.”

“A mouth inside water.”

“Tracks that started on tree trunks.”

Rez glanced toward Zee.

“Not one creature?”

The hunter shook his head.

“Maybe.”

Ner’s expression changed.

“Or one thing appearing through different boundaries.”

The hunter stared.

Zee looked toward Ner.

“Can something do that?”

Ner gave him a flat look.

“We just saw a sky under dirt.”

Fair.

Rez stood.

“We take him back to the border stone.”

Zee looked up.

“And then?”

“Then we return.”

The hunter shouted, “No!”

Everyone turned.

He grabbed Rez’s coat.

“If you cross back now, you might not reach the same side.”

Silence.

Rez slowly looked toward the distant direction of the border.

“What?”

The hunter’s face had gone pale.

“It changes after it notices you.”

Ner stood.

“What changes?”

“The paths.”

Of course.

Zee smiled.

Rez glared.

“Why are you smiling?”

“This is terrible.”

“That was not an answer.”

“I know.”

The forest vibrated.

Closer now.

The hunter screamed.

“Move!”

This time no one argued.

⸻

Rez took command before they had run fifty yards.

“Zareth, air.”

The Dragon launched.

“Ner, watch boundaries.”

Gold gathered around Ner’s hands.

“Zee, track living things.”

Zee listened.

“Most are moving northeast.”

“Then we move northeast too.”

Zee frowned.

“Toward what?”

“Not what is making everything run southwest.”

Good point.

They ran.

The forest began changing around them.

Not dramatically at first.

A tree appeared twice.

Same split trunk.

Same patch of white fungus.

Zee passed it once.

Then again thirty seconds later.

“Ner.”

“I saw.”

Rez looked back.

“We turned?”

“No.”

Ner fired a Golden line behind them.

It stretched straight through the trees.

Then curved upward.

Then vanished into empty air.

His face hardened.

“The path is folding.”

Rez shouted, “Do not follow sight.”

Zee understood first.

“Scent?”

“Anything stable.”

Zee inhaled.

Bad idea.

The scents layered.

Wolf.

Snow.

Hunter blood.

Zareth.

Ner.

Rez.

Then all of them again from three different directions.

“Not scent.”

Rez adjusted immediately.

“Sound.”

Zee listened.

Same problem.

Footsteps repeated ahead before they made them.

“Not sound.”

Ner shouted, “Mana!”

He threw a Golden marker against a tree.

It stayed.

They passed it.

Thirty seconds later another tree carried the same marker.

Rez cursed.

“Not Mana.”

Zee looked upward.

Zareth.

The Link.

Unique.

Not a path in the forest.

Not magic anchored to terrain.

Bond.

“Zareth!”

The Link struck back.

Sky.

Three versions of the forest below.

One real?

No.

All real.

Zareth was seeing overlapping landscapes.

Mountains where trees should be.

A silver lake suspended vertically.

A herd of massive pale animals walking through air hundreds of feet above Zee’s current position.

Zee stumbled under the sensory flood.

Rez caught him.

“What?”

“Zareth sees more than one land.”

Ner looked upward.

“Simultaneously?”

“I think.”

“Ask him where we are.”

Zee sent the concept.

Us.

Position.

Zareth answered with frustration.

Four Zees.

Four groups.

All moving differently.

Zee went cold.

“There are copies.”

Rez’s face changed.

“Copies of us?”

“Not sure.”

Ner looked around.

“Do not interact with anything that looks like us.”

Zee smiled.

“That is very specific.”

“Because you would.”

Zee opened his mouth.

Then closed it.

Fair.

A voice called from ahead.

“Zee!”

Rez stopped dead.

It was Aurelia.

Zee’s entire body reacted.

Heart.

Blood.

Wolf instinct.

Everything.

“Mother?”

Rez grabbed him.

“No.”

The voice came again.

“Zee!”

Exactly her voice.

Warm.

Afraid.

Close.

Zee’s feet moved before his mind did.

Ner stepped in front of him.

“No.”

“That is her.”

“No.”

“I know her voice.”

Ner looked directly at him.

“So do I.”

The voice changed.

Closer.

“Baby, come here.”

Zee froze.

Aurelia had called him that when he was little.

Not often now.

Almost never where others heard.

His chest tightened.

Ner grabbed both his hands.

Zee looked down.

Golden light flickered between their fingers.

Not spellwork.

Ner’s Mana reacting to his own fear.

“Your mother is in Vesper.”

Zee looked at him.

“I know.”

“Then choose what you know.”

The words cut through instinct.

Zee breathed.

The false Aurelia called again.

He closed his eyes.

Listened past sound.

Heartbeat?

None.

Blood?

None.

Scent?

Only forest.

Dragon Link?

Zareth sent disgust.

Wrong.

Zee opened his eyes.

“Not her.”

Ner did not release his hands immediately.

Neither noticed until Rez said:

“Wonderful. Move.”

They separated.

Zee’s face warmed.

Ner looked away.

Even here?

Apparently.

The voice behind them stopped.

Then laughed.

Not Aurelia anymore.

Something wet and enormous moved between the trees.

They never saw the whole body.

Only antlers.

Too many.

White structures taller than houses passed between trunks without disturbing branches.

Then an eye opened in the bark of a nearby tree.

Same horizontal pupil.

Zee moved.

The eye followed.

Zareth dove from above.

Teal-and-Gold Dragonfire struck the tree.

The flame hit bark.

Then disappeared.

Not extinguished.

Gone.

Zareth pulled upward violently.

The Link carried shock.

Zee shouted, “Do not attack!”

Too late.

The forest answered.

Every tree opened an eye.

Hundreds.

Rez stopped.

“New plan.”

“Good,” Ner said.

“What?”

“Do not make it angry.”

Zee looked around.

“You think it is angry?”

A branch bent toward him.

The tip opened into a mouth.

Zee stepped back.

“Yes.”

Rez grabbed the wounded hunter.

“Run.”

They ran.

⸻

The first direct strike came from the ground.

A pale antler burst upward beneath Rez.

Zee saw it.

Moved.

Crimson Mana hit Rez sideways.

Rez flew clear.

The antler impaled Zee through the lower abdomen.

Pain erased everything.

Ner shouted.

Zareth roared.

Zee looked down.

White bone through his body.

Blood soaked his coat.

He did not die.

Could not.

That did not make it pleasant.

The antler lifted.

Zee screamed as his feet left the ground.

Rez regained his footing.

“Do not cut it!”

Ner stopped mid-cast.

“Why?”

“We do not know what that does!”

Good.

Zee hated him.

Good.

The antler began pulling Zee downward.

Not into soil.

Into somewhere else.

The ground beneath him turned transparent.

Violet sky.

Stars.

The enormous eye.

Closer.

Zee’s Dragon nature exploded.

Crimson scales covered his abdomen around the wound.

His horns burned.

The ridges beneath his shoulder blades pushed violently outward without becoming wings.

Crimson Dragon energy surged into the antler.

Not an attack he understood.

Reaction.

The white structure shuddered.

For the first time, the eye below changed.

Its pupil narrowed.

Recognition?

Pain?

Zee did not know.

His blood touched the antler.

Something strange passed through him.

Cold without temperature.

Distance without movement.

A sense of edges existing where none should.

His body reacted.

Not copying.

Adapting.

Crimson Mana changed texture around his hands.

Thinner.

Sharper.

It began outlining something invisible.

Zee saw lines.

Not with his eyes.

Boundaries.

Folds.

The places where the world bent.

“Ner!”

Ner looked toward him.

Zee pointed with one shaking hand.

“There!”

Nothing visible.

Ner trusted him anyway.

Golden Mana struck the point Zee indicated.

The world flashed.

The antler released him.

Zee fell.

Rez caught one arm.

Ner caught the other.

They dragged him away as the transparent ground snapped shut.

Zee collapsed.

Blood everywhere.

His body already repairing.

Slowly.

Painfully.

Ner knelt.

“Can you move?”

“Yes.”

“That sounded false.”

“Mostly.”

Rez looked at the wound.

“It went through.”

“I noticed.”

Zareth landed beside them in a fury.

The Link hit Zee like a storm.

Pain.

Rage.

Protect.

Destroy.

Zee grabbed his snout.

“No.”

Zareth’s Mana flared.

“No fire.”

The Dragon snarled.

Zee pushed a concept back.

Not enemy?

Maybe territory.

Unknown.

Zareth hated the uncertainty.

So did Zee.

Ner looked at Zee’s hands.

Crimson lines still shimmered faintly between his fingers.

“What is that?”

“I do not know.”

He smiled through pain.

Ner glared.

“This is not funny.”

“I know.”

Rez crouched.

“You saw the fold.”

Zee looked toward him.

“Yes.”

“How?”

“The antler touched my blood.”

Ner stiffened.

“You absorbed something?”

“No.”

Important.

Zee knew the difference.

Nothing foreign sat inside him waiting to become a copied power.

His body had encountered something impossible and changed how it perceived danger.

Adaptation.

Not theft.

Zee lifted one hand.

Crimson lines appeared again.

The forest suddenly looked different.

Not normal.

Layered.

He could see where paths bent against one another.

Where one tree existed in two positions.

Where a patch of snow opened toward violet sky.

Not clearly.

Enough.

“Rez.”

“What?”

“I can see where not to walk.”

Rez smiled.

“There is our route.”

Ner immediately said, “Maybe.”

Zee looked at him.

Ner continued.

“We test it first.”

Good.

Always annoying.

Good.

Ner formed a tiny Golden sphere and threw it toward the nearest Crimson-outlined fold.

The sphere vanished.

Reappeared twenty feet above them.

Then fell.

Rez stepped aside.

It struck the ground.

Ner looked at Zee.

“Accurate.”

Zee smiled.

“Thank you.”

“Do not get arrogant. You were impaled thirty seconds ago.”

“Forty.”

“That is worse.”

Zareth’s amusement slipped through rage.

The hunter stared at all four.

“You are insane.”

Rez stood.

“Yes.”

Then pointed.

“Zee leads.”

“Ner verifies.”

“Zareth stays above and warns us when the landscape overlaps.”

“I manage movement.”

Zee looked at him.

“And the hunter?”

Rez glanced down.

“Stays alive.”

The man looked touched.

Rez added:

“Because I refuse to explain losing the person we came here to rescue.”

Zee laughed.

There he was.

They moved.

⸻

The deeper forest stopped pretending to obey ordinary geography.

They passed a waterfall flowing sideways through open air.

A field of black flowers turned their petals away from Zee’s blood.

A herd of eyeless six-legged creatures watched from a cliff that had not existed a minute earlier.

None attacked.

That mattered.

Zee began understanding.

“This place is not hunting us.”

Rez looked toward him.

“What?”

“It attacked after Zareth burned the tree.”

Zareth sent offense.

Ner thought.

“The antler came after the fire.”

“And the false Aurelia?” Rez asked.

Ner frowned.

“Maybe defensive behavior.”

Zee looked toward the eyes opening in distant bark.

“Or curiosity.”

The wounded hunter shook his head.

“It killed my people.”

“Did they attack first?” Zee asked.

Silence.

The man looked away.

Rez noticed.

“What did your group do?”

The hunter swallowed.

“We found one of the six-legged animals.”

Zee’s face hardened.

“And?”

“We killed it.”

Of course.

Ner closed his eyes.

“Why?”

“We thought it was dangerous.”

“Did it attack?”

“No.”

Zee’s darkness stirred.

Not enough to act.

Enough to judge.

The hunter continued quickly.

“Then we cut it open.”

Zareth growled.

The Link filled with disgust.

Rez’s expression went cold.

“You crossed into unknown territory, killed an animal that did not threaten you, and dissected it.”

The hunter looked ashamed.

“We needed to know what it was.”

Ner stared.

“That is not how asking works.”

Zee almost smiled.

Not because it was funny.

Because the world had once used the same logic on him.

Unknown.

Dangerous.

Cut it open.

Find out afterward.

He understood this place a little better now.

Maybe.

“Where did you kill it?”

The hunter pointed uncertainly.

“No idea.”

Zee looked at the Crimson folds.

“I do.”

His new perception pulled faintly toward something.

Blood.

Not ordinary blood.

The creature’s blood had touched one of the folds and left a pattern.

Zee followed.

Rez did not question.

Ner tested every turn.

Zareth mapped from above.

For once, the strange place allowed them through.

Or they stopped fighting it enough to move correctly.

Both were possible.

They found the dead creature near sunset.

Its body had not rotted.

Six legs folded beneath it.

Smooth eyeless face turned toward the ground.

A long wound opened across its abdomen.

Hunter tools lay nearby.

The forest around it was silent.

Zee approached slowly.

No threat.

No touch.

He crouched.

“I am sorry.”

Rez looked at him.

The hunter bowed his head.

Something moved beneath the earth.

The enormous eye opened again.

Not under Zee.

Ten yards away.

Watching.

Zareth lowered himself but did not attack.

Ner kept his Mana quiet.

Rez lowered his sword.

The eye remained.

Zee looked toward the hunter.

“Your weapon.”

The man hesitated.

Then handed over his hunting knife.

Zee placed it beside the dead creature.

Then the man’s specimen pouch.

Inside were pieces of pale tissue taken from the animal.

Ner saw.

His face tightened.

Zee placed them beside the body too.

The forest shifted.

Not violently.

Trees straightened a fraction.

The sideways waterfall slowed.

The eye blinked.

Zee’s new Crimson perception saw a fold opening nearby.

Different.

Stable.

A path.

Rez saw Zee’s face.

“Exit?”

“I think.”

Ner threw a Golden sphere.

It crossed the fold.

Did not vanish.

Rolled normally through the snow beyond.

Zee smiled.

“Yes.”

The hunter started crying.

Quietly.

No one mocked him.

They crossed one by one.

Rez took the hunter first.

Ner next.

Zee waited.

Zareth remained behind him.

The Dragon Link carried a question.

The eye still watched.

Zee looked back at it.

“What are you?”

No answer.

Good.

He did not expect one.

The world did not owe him explanations simply because he survived meeting it.

Something passed through the Link from Zareth.

Old Dragon concept.

Not name.

Not certainty.

A category.

Deep wild.

Places older than borders.

Regions Dragons remembered only enough to avoid.

Not evil.

Not safe.

Zee nodded.

Then crossed.

Zareth followed.

The fold closed.

The forest behind them became ordinary trees.

Mostly.

The border stone stood less than a hundred yards away.

Impossible.

They had traveled for hours.

Ner looked back.

“No.”

Rez smiled tiredly.

“Do not start.”

Ner pointed.

“We were miles inside.”

“Yes.”

“That is impossible.”

Zee looked at his hands.

Crimson lines flickered once around his fingers.

Then disappeared.

“Apparently not.”

The hunter sat in the snow.

Alive.

The core four stood around him.

Bloodied.

Exhausted.

Different.

They had entered expecting a monster.

Found an ecosystem.

Provoked a territory.

Learned that some supernatural things did not fit neatly into Witch, Vampire, Wolf, Dragon, demon, curse, spell, or kingdom.

And Zee carried a new scar through his abdomen that had already begun fading into something his body would remember.

Not a copied power.

Not victory.

A new way to see when the world folded wrong.

Ner looked toward him.

“Do not experiment with that.”

Zee frowned.

“I was not going to.”

Rez stared.

Zareth sent immediate disbelief.

Zee looked offended.

“Fine.”

Ner smiled.

The wounded hunter laughed weakly.

For the first time since they crossed the border stone, the sound did not feel inappropriate.

Rez looked back toward the unknown forest.

“We tell the wolf clans to mark this entire region.”

“As forbidden?” Zee asked.

Rez thought.

“No.”

Ner looked toward him.

Rez continued.

“As inhabited.”

That was better.

Much better.

Zee looked at the forest one last time.

Kingdom maps had called the land empty.

It was not.

They simply had not understood what lived there.

The known world had not expanded because Zee conquered something.

It expanded because, for once, he had survived something without needing to own it, kill it, or understand all of it.

Beyond the trees, deeper places waited.

Older species.

Stranger laws.

Things kingdoms had never named.

Zee’s Crimson eyes brightened.

Fear remained.

So did wonder.

And now that he knew how little of the world belonged to crowns—

he wanted to see all of it.
