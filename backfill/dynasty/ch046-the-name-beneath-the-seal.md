Chapter Forty-Six

The Name Beneath the Seal

The first person to recognize the Northern Council storage mark was not Rez.

It was Seraphine Veyrath.

She took one look at the copied seal after the group returned to Vesper and said, “That office has changed names three times.”

Rez stopped halfway through removing his travel cloak.

“What?”

Seraphine leaned over the council table and turned the parchment toward herself.

“The symbol beneath Renn’s administrative mark.”

Ner moved closer.

Pure Golden Mana had already exposed the layered signatures twice on the journey home, but neither he nor Rez had recognized the oldest pattern buried beneath them.

Seraphine did.

Aurelia stood beside her sister.

“You have seen it?”

“Years ago.”

Theron looked up from his chair near the fire.

“Where?”

Seraphine’s expression hardened.

“In trade documents.”

The room quieted.

That answer carried history none of them liked.

Seraphine continued.

“Not Khell’s.”

Zee watched her face.

Good.

He did not want Khell resurrected into importance simply because an old seal existed.

“Before him?”

“Yes.”

Seraphine tapped the lower mark.

“House Veyrath used neutral northern routes generations ago. This symbol belonged to a regional trade registry.”

Rez frowned.

“So Renn’s office sits on older infrastructure.”

“Probably.”

“Probably?”

Seraphine looked toward Zee.

“Your mother trained all of you to be irritating about certainty.”

Zee smiled.

“Yes.”

Aurelia almost looked proud.

Rez unfolded three additional records.

“If the registry existed before Renn, then we may be dealing with corruption inside an existing trade structure rather than something he built.”

Lucien nodded.

“Better.”

Zee looked at him.

“How is that better?”

“Because institutions can be repaired.”

Theron muttered, “That is optimistic.”

Lucien looked toward him.

“I rule one.”

“Exactly.”

Seraphine laughed.

Rez ignored them.

“What did the registry control?”

“Storage authorizations.”

“Cross-border trade exemptions.”

“Emergency provisions during famine.”

“And custody declarations for restricted supernatural materials.”

Ner’s face darkened.

“Materials.”

Seraphine looked toward him.

“That was the old word.”

“It is a bad one.”

“Yes.”

Zee watched Ner’s hand close around the edge of the table.

A few days ago, jars containing preserved pieces of his Golden Mana had traveled under that same structure.

Something inside Zee turned cold.

Not rage yet.

Something cleaner.

Attention.

He had begun recognizing that too.

Anger shouted.

This feeling listened.

Rez spread the records into a line.

“So Renn did not create the office.”

“No.”

“He used it.”

“Yes.”

“And someone else inside it helped move preserved Golden Mana.”

Seraphine nodded.

“Most likely.”

Zee looked toward Ner.

“What do you want?”

Ner did not answer immediately.

Five years ago Zee might have answered for him.

Found the culprit.

Broken something.

Called that protection.

Now he waited.

Ner traced one finger over the seal.

“I want the registry opened.”

Rez frowned.

“Publicly?”

“Yes.”

“That could expose every restricted transaction attached to it.”

“That is the point.”

Lucien leaned back.

“Not all restricted transactions are criminal.”

Ner looked toward him.

“I know.”

“Some protect refugees.”

“I know.”

“Hidden bloodlines.”

“I know.”

“Political asylum.”

“I know.”

His voice sharpened.

“I am not asking you to dump every protected name into the street.”

Lucien held his gaze.

“Then what are you asking?”

Ner looked toward Rez.

“For the office to prove which records were accessed, altered, and moved under Renn’s authority.”

Rez understood.

“An audit.”

Zee frowned.

“That sounds boring.”

Rez smiled.

“It will ruin lives.”

Zee considered.

“Less boring.”

Ner looked at both of them.

“I want to know who used my Mana.”

Not who vaguely benefited.

Not which region disliked Vesper.

Who.

Specific.

Earned.

Lucien nodded.

“Then we request a joint audit.”

Rez looked toward him.

“They will refuse.”

“Probably.”

Ner almost smiled.

Zee noticed.

Everyone had apparently become infected by Aurelia.

Lucien continued.

“If they refuse, that becomes information.”

Zee leaned against the table.

“And if they accept?”

“Then Rez goes north.”

Rez looked up.

“Me?”

“You are Crown Prince.”

“Yes.”

“And this dispute now involves trade law, Vesper citizens, political sabotage, and stolen supernatural material.”

Rez sighed.

“I liked it better when people just attacked us in forests.”

Zee smiled.

“No, you didn’t.”

“No.”

Rez looked toward the seal.

“But forests are simpler.”

Theron raised his cup.

“To simple violence.”

Aurelia stared.

Theron slowly lowered it.

“Bad toast.”

Seraphine nodded.

“Terrible.”

⸻

The Northern Council accepted the audit.

That made everyone more suspicious.

Especially Zee.

“They said yes too quickly.”

Rez adjusted the clasp on his formal Vesper coat.

“You distrust people when they refuse.”

“Yes.”

“You distrust them when they cooperate.”

“Yes.”

“You understand that makes diplomacy difficult.”

“I do not do diplomacy.”

“You keep coming to diplomatic meetings.”

“To protect you.”

Rez looked at him.

“I have guards.”

“They are not me.”

“That was disgustingly affectionate.”

Zee shoved him.

Rez laughed.

They stood in Vesper’s eastern departure court while a small diplomatic party prepared to ride north.

Rez would lead.

Ner would accompany him as the injured party and magical specialist.

Two Vesper legal witches would attend.

Caldrin would go because apparently nobody could stop him.

Zee was not going.

Again.

He hated the pattern.

Again.

This time Lucien did not need to explain why.

The Northern Council had agreed to receive Crown Prince Rez Vesper.

Not Zee Darkheart.

Bringing Zee would turn an audit into a threat before anyone spoke.

Zee understood.

Understanding remained irritating.

Ner approached wearing formal black and deep gold.

Zee looked.

Then looked away.

Too late.

Rez saw.

Of course.

Zee immediately said, “No.”

Rez smiled.

“I have said nothing.”

Ner stopped in front of them.

“What is happening?”

“Nothing,” Zee said.

Rez answered, “Everything.”

Zee glared.

Ner looked between them.

Then at Zee.

A faint smile appeared.

Worse.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You are all terrible.”

Ner adjusted one glove.

He no longer wore them to hide his Mana regularly.

Today the gloves were ceremonial.

Zee noticed that too.

Annoying.

Ner looked toward the horses.

“We will probably be gone four days.”

Zee frowned.

“Probably?”

“Maybe five.”

“No.”

Rez stared.

“You do know you are not in charge of distance.”

“I should be.”

“That would be horrifying.”

Ner stepped closer.

“You will survive.”

Zee crossed his arms.

“Barely.”

Ner’s smile softened.

There.

That look again.

Not exactly friendship anymore.

Not only.

Zee hated not understanding things about himself almost as much as he hated not understanding his powers.

Ner touched his wrist.

Brief.

Simple.

“We will come back.”

Zee looked at the hand.

Then at him.

“Good.”

Rez made a very quiet choking sound.

Zee turned.

“I swear—”

Rez raised both hands.

“Cough.”

“You are bad at lying.”

“I learned from you.”

Ner laughed.

Then mounted.

Rez followed.

Caldrin paused beside Zee.

“Should I supervise them?”

Zee looked at him.

“Yes.”

Caldrin smiled.

“I was asking Rez.”

Rez shouted from horseback, “No!”

Caldrin mounted anyway.

The party left.

Zee watched until they passed beyond the outer road.

Then kept watching.

Aurelia appeared beside him.

“You are doing better.”

“No.”

“You did not follow.”

“That is the lowest possible standard.”

“Yes.”

He looked at her.

She smiled.

Zee sighed.

Then something huge landed behind them.

The ground shook.

Zareth.

Back from Drakonis.

Zee turned.

The Dragon Prince folded his wings and lowered his head.

The Link opened.

Drakonis council.

Border dispute.

His mother furious at three elder Dragons.

A broken ceremonial table.

Zee blinked.

“You broke it?”

Zareth sent immediate denial.

Then an image of another Dragon’s tail striking the table.

Zee laughed.

“Fine.”

The Link shifted.

Ner absent.

Rez absent.

Question.

“They went north.”

Zareth stared.

Then sent Zee an image of himself flying after them.

Zee smiled.

“No.”

The Dragon Prince looked offended.

“They need to do this.”

Zareth sent suspicion.

Zee nodded.

“I know.”

Then another image came.

Zee.

Bored.

Dangerous.

Zee narrowed his eyes.

“I am not that bad alone.”

Aurelia coughed.

Zee turned.

“You too?”

She walked away smiling.

⸻

By the second day, Zee stopped pretending he was not restless.

He trained.

Then stopped before training became punishment.

He spent an hour with Theron learning an old Veyrath breathing technique meant to regulate Dragon heat during partial surges.

Theron warned him it might not work properly because Zee’s Dragon nature did not exist alone.

Zee appreciated the honesty.

It helped anyway.

Crimson Dragon energy no longer jumped through his horns every time irritation sharpened.

It gathered lower.

More controlled.

More concentrated.

Seraphine watched from the courtyard wall.

“You look constipated.”

Zee opened one eye.

Theron sighed.

“Please leave.”

“I am helping.”

“You are not.”

Zee laughed and lost the breathing rhythm completely.

Theron glared at his sister.

“See?”

Seraphine smiled.

“Much better.”

Later Zee helped Aurelia review letters concerning Veyrath survivors scattered through three kingdoms.

Not lost siblings.

Not another rescue.

Just people deciding whether to use their family name publicly now that Veyrath survival was no longer rumor.

One wanted recognition.

One wanted anonymity.

Another wanted permission to visit Vesper without joining anything.

Aurelia granted all three wishes.

Zee watched.

“You are not rebuilding House Veyrath.”

Aurelia looked toward him.

“Not the way it was.”

“Theron wants to.”

“Theron wants a banner.”

From the next room:

“I heard that.”

Aurelia continued.

“Seraphine wants everyone left alone.”

“I heard that too,” Seraphine called.

Zee smiled.

“And you?”

Aurelia looked toward the letters.

“I want Veyrath to mean people have somewhere to return if they choose.”

Not command.

Not ownership.

Choice.

Zee understood why that word kept appearing in his life now.

At fifteen, strength was no longer the difficult part.

Choice was.

⸻

The audit turned violent on the third day.

Not because Rez attacked anyone.

Because someone tried burning the registry.

The message reached Vesper at dusk.

Zee read it once.

Then looked toward Zareth.

The Link had already sharpened.

Fire.

North.

Ner.

Rez.

Danger.

This time Zee did not ask permission.

Aurelia saw his face.

“Go.”

He stopped.

“What?”

“They asked for help.”

She handed him the second message.

Rez’s seal.

Three words beneath the emergency mark.

Bring Zee now.

Zee smiled.

Not because people were in danger.

Because this time he was needed.

Zareth spread his wings.

Zee looked toward the Dragon.

No riding.

Never.

“I will meet you there.”

The Link answered with challenge.

Race.

Zee grinned.

“You are going to lose.”

Zareth launched.

Zee ran for the northern gate.

The audit had finally found the person willing to burn evidence rather than let the truth survive.

And Zee Darkheart was very interested in meeting them.
