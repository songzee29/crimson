Chapter Thirty

The Blood That Would Not Stay Still

Sevran Veyrath entered House Vesper under guard.

He complained about that before they reached the first gate.

“I came willingly.”

Captain Vale did not look at him.

“You were brought willingly.”

“There is a difference?”

“Yes.”

“What?”

Vale finally glanced over.

“You are surrounded by twenty armed soldiers.”

Caldrin walked on Sevran’s other side.

“And me.”

Sevran looked at the knives strapped across him.

“You count separately?”

“I like to.”

Zee walked ahead with Aurelia.

Zareth remained close enough that one crimson wing occasionally brushed Zee’s shoulder. The Dragon Link had been restless since the fight on the cliffs.

Not because of Sevran.

Because of Aurelia’s blood.

Zee could smell it.

She had already closed the wound in her side with witchcraft and wrapped it tightly beneath her clothes, but knowing she had been cut would not leave his head.

Every few minutes he looked up at her.

Every time, Aurelia noticed.

“I am still here.”

“I know.”

“Then stop checking.”

“I am not checking.”

“You looked at me six times since the gate.”

“Maybe I like looking at you.”

Aurelia raised an eyebrow.

Zee looked away.

Zareth sent amusement through the Link.

Zee glared at him.

“You are supposed to help.”

Bad lie.

Zee frowned.

The thought had been clearer than usual.

Not a word exactly.

But close.

The Link kept changing.

So did everything else.

His horns burned again.

Zee grabbed one.

Aurelia stopped.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“Zee.”

“They are hot.”

“How hot?”

“Hot.”

“That is not useful.”

“They are attached to my head. I do not have a measuring spell.”

Sevran looked sharply toward him from behind.

“When did that start?”

Aurelia turned.

“Do not.”

Sevran ignored her.

“During surges?”

Zee looked at him.

“Sometimes.”

“After Dragon Mana?”

“Sometimes.”

“When angry?”

“Sometimes.”

Sevran frowned.

“You really do not know.”

Zee crossed his arms.

“That seems to disappoint everyone.”

“No.”

Sevran studied the crimson-black horns.

“It worries me.”

Aurelia stepped between them.

“Then worry quietly.”

They continued.

Zee’s horns cooled.

But his heartbeat did not.

Something inside him felt wrong.

Not sick.

Crowded.

As though every supernatural instinct he possessed had decided to speak at once.

He could hear Vesper soldiers behind the walls.

Smell the blood drying beneath Aurelia’s clothes.

Feel Zareth’s agitation.

Sense Sevran’s strange Veyrath Dragon presence.

Taste magic in the air from the castle wards.

And beneath all of that came something he could not identify.

A pressure.

Not around him.

Inside him.

Waiting.

⸻

Rez met them in the inner courtyard.

At seventeen, he had finally grown into the kind of prince strangers noticed before they noticed his attitude.

Tall.

Fast.

Sword belted properly.

Vesper blue and silver across his coat.

Blue-White Mana controlled cleanly beneath his skin.

Then he opened his mouth.

“You look terrible.”

Zee smiled immediately.

“I missed you too.”

Rez looked at the dried blood across Zee’s clothes.

Then at Aurelia.

Then Sevran.

Then the captured hunters.

His expression lost its humor.

“What happened?”

Ner arrived seconds later.

He was still carrying a book.

Pure Golden Mana shimmered faintly around its cover where he had apparently been practicing seals.

His eyes immediately found Zee.

Then Aurelia.

“You were gone for one conversation.”

Zee shrugged.

“It became complicated.”

Ner looked at Sevran.

“Who is that?”

Sevran answered.

“Sevran Veyrath.”

Ner looked toward Aurelia.

“Family?”

Zee nodded.

“Cousin.”

Sevran looked at him.

“Formerly.”

Ner frowned.

“That is not how blood works.”

Zee pointed at him.

“I said the same thing.”

Rez looked between Sevran and Aurelia.

“Veyrath.”

His eyes sharpened.

“As in her?”

Aurelia sighed.

“Yes.”

Rez looked at Sevran again.

“You are a Dragon?”

“Yes.”

“Dragon-Witch?”

“No.”

“Disappointing.”

Sevran stared.

Rez smiled.

“Welcome to Vesper.”

Lucien’s voice came from the palace steps.

“Nothing about this is a welcome.”

Rez looked over.

“I was being polite.”

“No one believed you.”

King Lucien descended.

His gaze moved across the prisoners first.

Then Sevran.

Then Aurelia’s wrapped side.

He stopped.

“You are injured.”

“I noticed.”

“Who?”

She indicated the prisoners.

Lucien’s face became cold.

His attention turned to Sevran.

“And you brought them.”

Sevran stiffened.

“I was followed.”

“Into territory sheltering my son, the heir of Drakonis, Nereus Azrion, and Zee Darkheart.”

“When you say it like that—”

“There is no better way to say it.”

Caldrin smiled.

“I like him angry.”

Lucien ignored him.

“Council chamber.”

He looked at the captured hunters.

“Cells first.”

Then Sevran.

“You come with us.”

Sevran exhaled.

“I expected hospitality.”

Aurelia walked past him.

“You survived meeting us.”

“That was the hospitality.”

⸻

The council chamber doors closed.

No servants remained.

Lucien sat at the head of the table.

Aurelia sat to his right.

Sevran sat opposite her under the watch of Vale.

Caldrin occupied a chair he had not been invited to use.

Rez stood beside the map table.

Ner sat on its edge.

Zee was supposed to be resting.

He had ignored that instruction.

Zareth could not comfortably fit inside, so he lay outside the open balcony doors with his head inside the room.

Lucien looked toward Zee.

“You should be in bed.”

“I am sitting.”

“That is not bed.”

“It is rest.”

Rez smiled.

Lucien looked at him.

“Do not encourage him.”

“I said nothing.”

“You have the face.”

Zee immediately looked toward Aurelia.

She almost laughed.

Lucien returned his attention to Sevran.

“Start talking.”

Sevran folded his arms.

“About?”

Vale moved closer.

Sevran unfolded them.

“House Veyrath.”

Aurelia’s expression hardened.

“What remains of it.”

Sevran looked at her.

“More than you think.”

“How many?”

“I do not know.”

“Where?”

“Scattered.”

“That tells me nothing.”

“It tells you the truth.”

Aurelia went silent.

Zee noticed.

She respected that answer even while hating it.

Sevran continued.

“When the old hunts broke House Veyrath apart, survivors chose differently.”

“Some disappeared completely.”

“Some hid inside human kingdoms.”

“Some entered witch territories under other names.”

“Some Dragons joined distant Houses.”

“Some were captured.”

His expression darkened.

“And some decided surviving mattered more than blood.”

Aurelia understood.

“They joined the hunters.”

“Yes.”

Ner frowned.

“The people on the cliff?”

“Not Veyrath.”

“Then who?”

Sevran looked toward him.

“One faction that has hunted Veyrath blood for years.”

“Name?”

Sevran hesitated.

Aurelia’s eyes narrowed.

“Do not make me ask twice.”

“They are called the Ashen Talons.”

Caldrin leaned forward.

“I know the name.”

Vale looked toward him.

“Of course you do.”

“They hunt Dragons.”

Sevran shook his head.

“Not only Dragons.”

“Then what?”

“Bloodlines they consider unstable.”

Aurelia’s jaw tightened.

“Veyrath.”

“Yes.”

“Dragon-Witch blood.”

“Especially.”

Zee touched one of his scales.

Sevran saw.

“They will want you now.”

Aurelia’s Mana flared.

Sevran raised both hands.

“I am not threatening him.”

“You are speaking too comfortably about people wanting my child.”

“I am warning you.”

Lucien intervened.

“Why did they keep hunting after House Veyrath broke?”

Sevran looked toward the table.

“Because a broken bloodline is easier to harvest than a united one.”

Silence.

Ner’s face changed.

He understood that kind of enemy too well.

“Blood.”

“Yes.”

“Experiments?”

“Sometimes.”

“Sale?”

“Yes.”

“Execution?”

“Yes.”

“Depends on the buyer?”

Sevran looked at Ner differently.

“You have dealt with people like them.”

Ner’s Golden Mana flickered.

“People like them dealt with me.”

Zee looked toward Ner.

An old memory surfaced.

Underground Vesper.

His mother chained in a circle.

The demon wanting Gold.

Zee’s horns suddenly burned.

He grabbed the table.

Rez noticed.

“You okay?”

“Yes.”

His voice came too quickly.

Rez frowned.

Zee’s scales spread across his wrist.

No anger.

No attack.

No obvious reason.

Aurelia saw.

“Zee.”

“I said I am fine.”

Crimson Mana escaped between his fingers.

The wooden table groaned.

Zee released it immediately.

A shallow handprint remained burned into the surface.

Everyone stared.

Zee stared hardest.

“I did not do that.”

Aurelia stood.

“Enough for tonight.”

“No.”

“Zee.”

“We finally have answers.”

“You are losing control.”

“I am not.”

A candle across the room went out.

Then relit.

The flame leaned toward Zee.

Not from wind.

The room became unnaturally quiet for one heartbeat.

Dust suspended in the light.

Then everything returned.

No one spoke.

Zee’s face went pale.

“What was that?”

Sevran rose.

“I have never seen—”

Aurelia rounded on him.

“Sit down.”

He sat.

Rez walked toward Zee.

“Hey.”

“I am fine.”

“You keep saying that.”

“Because I am.”

Rez crouched enough to look directly at him.

Zee hated that.

Rez had been doing it since Zee was small enough to be carried everywhere.

Older-brother eyes.

The ones that saw too much.

“When did you sleep?”

“Last night.”

“How long?”

Zee looked away.

Rez sighed.

“When?”

“I do not know.”

“You do.”

“Maybe an hour.”

Aurelia’s expression changed.

“Zee.”

“I could not.”

“Why?”

He looked toward Zareth.

The Dragon Link had been loud all night.

Not Zareth intentionally.

Just present.

Zee had heard the dragon dreaming.

Seen flashes of flight.

Felt Aurelia’s old fear.

He had heard heartbeats through stone.

Smelled blood from rooms away.

Every nature inside him had refused to settle.

“I could hear everything.”

Aurelia moved closer.

“Everything what?”

“Everything.”

Zee pressed both hands against his head.

“People.”

“Blood.”

“Magic.”

“Zareth.”

“Your wound.”

“Ner downstairs.”

“Rez walking around at three in the morning because he thinks no one knows.”

Rez blinked.

“How did you know that?”

“I heard you.”

“From the western wing?”

“Yes!”

The word came out too loud.

Crimson Mana burst.

Every window rattled.

Zareth rose outside.

The Dragon Link surged.

Zee. Wrong. Pain.

“I know!”

Everyone froze.

Aurelia spoke softly.

“Who are you talking to?”

“Zareth.”

“You shouted before he sent anything.”

Zee stared.

He had.

The boundaries felt wrong.

Thought.

Mana.

Instinct.

Emotion.

He could not tell where one ended.

Aurelia reached for him.

Zee flinched away.

Her face broke.

He saw it immediately.

“I am sorry.”

“You did nothing wrong.”

“I moved.”

“You are overwhelmed.”

“I do not want to hurt you.”

“You will not.”

“You do not know that.”

Aurelia stopped.

Truth.

She did not.

Zee backed toward the balcony.

Zareth moved closer.

The Dragon Link flooded him with concern.

Too much.

Zee recoiled.

“Stop!”

Zareth froze.

Hurt came through the Link.

Zee’s face crumpled.

“No. Not you. I mean—”

The room pulsed.

Blue-White Mana flashed around Rez defensively.

Golden Mana rose around Ner.

Aurelia’s Witch magic gathered.

Zee felt all of it.

Every source.

Every heartbeat.

Every emotion.

The pressure inside him answered.

His horns ignited crimson-black.

Scales rushed across his neck.

The faint ridges beneath his shoulders pushed painfully against skin.

The air tightened.

Lucien stood.

“Everyone out.”

Zee looked toward him.

“No.”

“That is not punishment.”

“I do not want to be alone.”

The words silenced the room.

Aurelia moved first.

“I am staying.”

Zee shook his head.

“You are hurt.”

“I am your mother.”

“That does not make you unhurt.”

Zee looked at Ner.

Golden Mana.

Bright.

Too bright.

His senses recoiled.

“Ner…”

Ner understood.

He lowered his power immediately.

“I am going.”

Zee looked guilty.

Ner stopped beside him.

“This is not me leaving you.”

“I know.”

“You better.”

Then Ner looked at Rez.

“Do something useful.”

Rez frowned.

“I am always useful.”

Ner left.

Lucien gestured for Vale, Caldrin, Sevran, and the royal witch to follow.

Aurelia remained.

Zee looked at her.

“Please.”

That hurt worse than any command.

“Zee—”

“Mother.”

His eyes filled.

“I can feel your blood.”

Aurelia went still.

The wound.

Her presence was feeding his panic.

Rez looked toward her.

“I will stay.”

Aurelia immediately said, “No.”

Rez looked at her.

“He asked you to go.”

“He did not ask you to remain.”

Zee spoke.

“Rez stays.”

Aurelia closed her eyes.

The room shuddered again.

Rez moved slowly toward Zee.

“I have him.”

Aurelia stared at the seventeen-year-old prince she had watched grow beside her son.

Not a servant.

Not a guard.

Family.

She nodded once.

“Do not challenge the surge.”

Rez gave her a look.

“I was not planning to wrestle his magic.”

Zee almost laughed.

Almost.

Aurelia approached her son one final time.

She did not touch him.

“I am right outside.”

Zee nodded.

“Promise?”

“Yes.”

“Do not leave.”

“I will not.”

She went.

Lucien paused at the doorway.

His eyes met Rez’s.

Father and son.

No words.

Rez nodded.

Lucien closed the doors.

Only Zee, Rez, and Zareth remained.

The dragon stayed on the balcony.

Close.

Quiet.

Rez looked at Zee.

Zee looked terrible.

Sweat against his forehead.

Crimson scales spreading without rhythm.

Horns glowing.

Hands shaking.

Mana moving around him like something alive and frightened.

Rez took off his sword belt.

Set it aside.

Then removed the dagger at his ankle.

Zee frowned.

“What are you doing?”

“Making sure you do not accidentally throw me through a wall while I am carrying something sharp.”

“I would not.”

Rez looked at the burned table.

Zee sighed.

“Fair.”

Rez stepped closer.

“What does it feel like?”

Zee swallowed.

“Like I am standing inside everyone.”

Rez stopped.

“That sounds awful.”

“It is.”

“Can you hear me?”

“Yes.”

“My heartbeat?”

“Yes.”

“Mana?”

“Yes.”

“Anything else?”

Zee looked at him.

For one moment his expression became frightened.

“I do not know.”

The candle flames bent backward.

Dust lifted from the floor.

Rez’s Blue-White Mana moved toward Zee without being summoned.

Rez looked down.

“That is new.”

Zee’s eyes widened.

“I am not doing that.”

“I know.”

“Rez.”

“I know.”

The air became still.

Not peaceful.

Waiting.

Rez stepped forward anyway.

He placed one hand on Zee’s shoulder.

Zee flinched.

Then held on.

Rez tightened his grip.

“I am here.”

The words did not fix anything.

But Zee believed them.

For a moment.

Then his horns burned brighter.

The entire room went silent.

Not quiet.

Silent.

As though sound itself had forgotten how to move.

Rez felt Zee’s fingers close around his wrist.

Too tight.

Desperate.

And somewhere beneath blood, Mana, Dragon heat, predatory instinct, fear, memory, and the impossible pressure gathering inside Zee Darkheart…

something new opened its eyes.
