Chapter One Hundred Five

The First Three Names

Years passed.

Darkheart grew.

Roads widened.

Settlements became towns.

Towns became districts.

Children born after the first sieges grew old enough to train beside soldiers who remembered when Darkheart had barely possessed enough people to fill its walls.

Zee grew with it.

Not older in the way ordinary men feared.

Older in experience.

Sharper.

Quieter.

More certain about what he was willing to do.

Aidan remained safely beyond the story Darkheart knew.

Vesper kept its protected royal ward.

Rez kept his secret.

And Zee kept moving.

Until one winter morning, he stopped.

⸻

The archive chamber had never held this much blood without a body inside it.

Not literal blood.

Records.

Every attack Darkheart had survived.

Every raid.

Every poisoned blade.

Every dead child.

Every burned village.

Every abducted family.

Every hybrid hunt.

Every assassination attempt.

Every mercenary contract.

Every order bearing a royal seal.

Every payment routed through merchants who thought changing the name on an account changed what the money purchased.

Kael brought military records.

Tessa brought casualty ledgers.

Amara brought field reports and captured correspondence.

Merek brought old court files.

Ner brought lists of blood-signatures he had preserved from interrogations and rescues.

Rez sent sealed Vesper copies where foreign money had passed through Vesper banks.

Zareth contributed Drakonis records when attacks against Darkheart had overlapped Dragon territory.

For three days, Zee read.

He barely spoke.

Ner remained nearby.

Not watching him for loss of control.

There was none.

That was almost worse.

On the fourth day, Zee closed one file.

Then another.

Then another.

Ner stood across the table.

“How many?”

Zee looked at the surviving names.

“Too many.”

Dead enemies were removed immediately.

Victor.

Dead.

Oren Vael.

Dead.

Cassel Rhune.

Dead.

Kharos.

Dead.

Commanders Zee had already killed.

Sponsors executed by their own kingdoms.

Organizers who had died of age, illness, war, revenge, or stupidity.

Gone.

He wanted no ghosts.

Only survivors.

People who had actually harmed his family or people.

People who had known what they were doing.

People who had survived because younger Zee had been forced to move toward the next emergency.

Six names remained.

Ner read the first.

Then looked at Zee.

“You are sure?”

“Yes.”

“You are not reopening every old war?”

“No.”

“Then what is this?”

Zee pulled an empty black book toward himself.

No magic.

No enchantment.

Paper.

Leather.

Ink.

He wrote the first name.

Then the second.

All six.

“The debts I left alive.”

Ner studied him.

“And what happens when the debt is paid?”

Zee closed the book.

“It ends.”

No speech about mercy.

No apology.

No promise.

Just certainty.

The Blood Ledger began.

⸻

The first name belonged to:

SERATH VEYNE.

Witch.

Alchemist.

Former royal poisoner.

Now an old man living behind six layered wards in a mountain estate.

Serath had spent decades denying involvement in Aurelia’s death.

The evidence said otherwise.

He had not fired the bolt.

He had not stood at the attack.

He had done something worse.

He had perfected the poison that made the bolt matter.

Dragon-killing venom combined with Witch suppression.

A compound designed specifically to prevent Dragon blood from burning contamination away while simultaneously collapsing active Witch healing.

Aurelia had taken that poison into her body protecting Zee.

She had died before he could save her.

Serath had lived long enough to convince himself distance made him innocent.

It did not.

⸻

Zee arrived alone.

The first barrier noticed him.

The second attacked.

The third released silver mist.

The fourth inverted gravity inside the entrance corridor.

The fifth tried binding Dragon blood.

The sixth whispered directly into Witch channels, attempting to collapse spell formation.

Zee kept walking.

Serath watched through ritual mirrors from the central chamber.

Old.

Thin.

Still proud.

Still afraid enough to prepare.

He triggered the house.

Poison gas flooded the halls.

Zee inhaled once.

Recognized fourteen ingredients.

Nine harmless to him now.

Three irritating.

Two dangerous.

His body began processing before Serath understood he had already failed.

Zee entered the ritual chamber.

Serath stood inside a circle of black salt and bone.

“You cannot cross.”

Zee looked at the circle.

“Yes, I can.”

“You do not understand what this is.”

“I understand exactly what it is.”

Serath raised both hands.

The ritual walls ignited.

“Your mother did too.”

Zee stopped.

There.

Not rage.

Memory.

Aurelia bleeding.

Aurelia falling.

Aurelia telling him not to make the pain ugly simply because it hurt.

Zee looked at Serath.

“You remember her.”

Serath’s expression changed.

“I remember the assignment.”

Wrong answer.

Zee moved.

The circle detonated.

Suppression chains snapped toward his arms.

Poisoned symbols activated beneath his feet.

Serath began a twelve-word binding invocation.

Zee heard the first two.

Then said:

“Silence.”

The room died.

Not sound.

Magic.

The invocation stopped existing as an active structure.

Serath’s mouth continued moving.

Nothing answered.

The ritual circle flickered.

Collapsed.

Three prepared spell channels broke simultaneously.

Even Serath looked confused.

Zee did too.

For half a heartbeat.

Then understood.

His Witch nature had changed.

Not copied from Serath.

Not stolen.

The entire confrontation had taught his magic something about control.

About spell interruption.

About shutting the door before an enemy finished building what came through it.

Absolute Silence.

Localized.

Dominant.

Heavy.

Zee felt weaker magical channels around him simply fail to form.

Serath forced power through anyway.

Experienced enough.

Strong enough.

His hand burned with effort.

A single spell formed.

Zee smiled.

“So you can fight it.”

Serath fired.

Zee stepped aside.

“Good.”

Then broke his wrist.

Serath screamed.

The silence allowed that.

It was not ordinary quiet.

It was Zee deciding magic would have difficulty speaking here.

Serath fell.

“You cannot kill me for work I did decades ago.”

Zee crouched.

“Why?”

“Because it is finished.”

“My mother is still dead.”

“I never touched her.”

“You built the poison.”

“I was paid.”

Zee looked at him.

“That is touching enough.”

Serath started bargaining.

Names.

Gold.

Vaults.

Other poisoners.

Zee listened until one thing became obvious.

The man still thought information was currency.

Zee had not come to buy anything.

He placed one hand against Serath’s chest.

“No trial?”

“No.”

“I surrender.”

“I know.”

Serath stared at him.

Zee’s eyes remained human.

Clear.

Deliberate.

“You were never forgiven.”

Then he stopped the Witch’s heart.

One name crossed out.

⸻

The second belonged to:

MAEL CORVIN.

Once one of the Red Covenant’s senior organizers.

Never its supreme leader.

Never secret master of every attack.

Just one man who had repeatedly signed orders authorizing Darkheart civilians as acceptable losses.

When the Covenant collapsed, Mael vanished.

Different hair.

Different name.

Different country.

Different wife.

Different guards.

Different face altered by Witchcraft twice.

For years, he lived as Lord Hadren Vale.

Respected.

Boring.

Safe.

Or so he thought.

Zee found the first trace in an old prison record.

A preserved blood sample from a Covenant interrogation.

Dried.

Weak.

Barely useful.

He tasted it anyway.

Old blood.

Decades changed it.

Still—

something inside his Vampire nature locked onto the signature.

Not location.

Not omniscience.

Identity.

The blood existed in his memory now differently than ordinary scent.

Unique.

Specific.

Impossible to confuse with another.

Zee smiled.

⸻

Mael changed identities again within hours of hearing Serath Veyne had died.

Too late.

He abandoned his estate.

Shaved his head.

Used Witch masking to alter skin tone.

Changed his gait.

Burned his papers.

Boarded a merchant caravan under a servant identity.

Zee followed from three kingdoms away.

Not perfectly.

The signal weakened.

Wards confused it.

Rain buried scent.

Distance stretched the connection thin.

But it remained.

Blood Mark.

The Vampire in Zee had learned to remember prey through blood itself.

When Zee reached the caravan, he walked past fourteen people.

Then stopped beside a bent servant carrying grain.

Mael did not look at him.

Zee smiled.

“You changed your nose.”

The servant froze.

Zee leaned slightly closer.

“Again.”

Mael ran.

Good.

Zee loved when they ran.

⸻

The hunt lasted through a city.

Over rooftops.

Through alleys.

Across a market.

Mael had Vampire guards.

Witch protectors.

Three body doubles altered to resemble his former face.

None mattered.

Zee ignored every false Mael.

Blood knew.

One guard tried holding him.

Zee broke the arm.

Another fired silver.

Zee kept moving.

Mael reached a cathedral and slammed both hands against a prepared ward.

It opened.

He entered.

The barrier closed.

Zee hit it at full Vampire speed.

Witch power struck with him.

The barrier cracked.

Mael screamed through the door.

“You cannot execute me because a dead organization gave orders!”

Zee struck again.

“Your signature is on them.”

“I was following command!”

Third strike.

Barrier broke.

Zee entered.

Mael backed toward the altar.

“I have not touched Darkheart in twenty years.”

Zee stopped.

“You thought time forgave you.”

Mael’s eyes widened.

Zee crossed the distance.

Fangs descended.

He fed.

Not wildly.

Enough to taste fear.

Enough to preserve the signature forever even though it no longer mattered.

Mael weakened beneath him.

Zee pulled back.

The man gasped.

“Please.”

Zee smiled.

“No.”

Then killed him.

Two names crossed out.

⸻

The third belonged to:

DUKE ALDRIC SORREN.

The Southhold sponsor.

Not the soldiers.

Not the killers Zee had already hunted.

The man who financed bloodfire.

Aldric had never visited Southhold.

Never smelled the children burning.

Never heard parents screaming.

He had transferred gold through six merchant houses and purchased bloodfire stock from three separate alchemists.

His letters described civilian terror as:

necessary pressure against Darkheart expansion.

Zee read the sentence twice.

Then went hunting in Wolf form.

⸻

Aldric lived behind fortress walls.

Two hundred guards.

Silver-lined gates.

Watchtowers.

Scent masking across the surrounding forest.

Wolf repellent burned continuously along the walls.

Zee entered the forest at dusk.

Four-legged.

Near-black fur.

Deep crimson undertones visible only when moonlight caught them.

Crimson-gold eyes.

No horns.

No scales.

No Dragon features.

Wolf.

Pure.

Aldric’s people released false scent trails.

Blood.

Zee.

Ner.

Kael.

Dead Darkheart soldiers.

They had somehow purchased old battlefield materials hoping to confuse him.

Wolf Zee stopped.

Sniffed once.

Then looked toward the fortress.

Insulting.

He continued.

The false trails scattered.

His instinct ignored them.

Not because he knew every answer.

Because the hunt had changed.

Aldric had been confirmed.

Letters.

Money.

Orders.

Bloodfire.

The Wolf inside Zee recognized something deeper now.

Prey.

Not prey because Zee disliked him.

Prey because Zee had personally confirmed that this man had harmed his people.

Predator’s Claim settled.

Fear magic tried turning Zee away.

Failed.

A false forest illusion redirected the path.

His instincts rejected it.

Scent masking flooded the inner gate.

The imprint remained.

Aldric could run.

Hide.

Change rooms.

Change clothes.

It did not matter.

The hunt stayed alive.

⸻

The guards discovered Zee when he was already inside.

They expected a monster.

They saw a wolf.

Then he moved.

One guard hit stone.

Another lost his sword hand.

A third dropped his weapon and backed away.

Zee ignored him.

Not the target.

Aldric fled through the upper palace.

Zee followed.

Door.

Stairs.

Private corridor.

Hidden passage.

Aldric reached an underground carriage room.

Twenty armored men surrounded him.

Zee shifted back to human form.

Crimson-black scales returned where they belonged across his body.

Horns.

Fangs.

Crimson-gold eyes.

Aldric stared.

“This is an act of war.”

Zee looked around.

“With whom?”

“My House.”

“No.”

“My allies.”

“No.”

“My kingdom will answer.”

“Maybe.”

Aldric’s face twisted.

“You think being King means you can kill anyone you want?”

“No.”

Zee stepped closer.

“It means I have enough people trying to stop me that I learned to become very good at reaching the ones I chose.”

Aldric backed away.

“I never entered Southhold.”

“You bought the bloodfire.”

“I funded a military operation.”

“You funded children burning.”

“I did not order children targeted.”

Zee pulled the letter from his coat.

Aldric recognized it.

Zee read:

“Civilian terror will create necessary pressure against Darkheart expansion.”

Silence.

Aldric’s face emptied.

Zee folded the paper.

“You wrote it.”

Aldric looked at his guards.

“Kill him.”

None moved fast enough.

The fight lasted thirty seconds.

When it ended, Aldric remained alive.

Everyone else who had attacked Zee did not.

Aldric crawled backward through blood.

“You cannot—”

Zee shifted Wolf again.

Aldric finally understood.

The last thing he saw was crimson-gold eyes.

Three names crossed out.

⸻

Zee returned to Darkheart before midnight.

Ner stood in the archive chamber.

Kael leaned against the far wall.

Tessa had come only because she wanted to see whether the book had actually changed Zee.

Amara sat on the table despite Merek’s repeated objections.

Zareth arrived through the Link before he arrived physically.

Three?

Zee answered:

Three.

The door opened moments later.

Zareth entered in human form.

Zee placed the Ledger down.

Three names crossed through.

Three remained.

Ner studied him.

Something felt different.

Not new power alone.

The way Zee stood.

Witch magic no longer seemed separate from Vampire presence.

Wolf instinct no longer retreated when his Witch senses widened.

Each nature seemed aware of the others.

Not unified.

Not yet.

But listening.

Zee looked toward the candle near the Ledger.

“Silence.”

The magical flame died.

Kael frowned.

Zee looked toward him.

“You changed.”

“Yes.”

Ner’s eyes narrowed.

“Three times.”

“Yes.”

Zareth folded his arms.

“And tomorrow?”

Zee looked at the remaining names.

Three debts.

Three living people.

Zee closed the Blood Ledger.

The smile that followed was small.

Calm.

Predatory.

“Tomorrow will be worse.”

⸻
