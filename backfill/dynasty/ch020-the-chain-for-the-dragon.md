Chapter Twenty

The Chain for the Dragon

The world discovered Zee Darkheart’s weakness before Zee was old enough to understand what weakness meant.

It was not steel.

It was not poison.

It was not witchcraft.

It was not the ancient cold of the mountains.

It was love.

The hunters came for Zareth at dawn.

Not Zee.

That was why they nearly succeeded.

The crownless valley had changed with spring. Snow still clung to the highest ridges, but green had returned beneath the cliffs, mountain flowers pushed through thawing earth, and black water moved freely through cracks in the frozen lake.

Aurelia had begun believing they could remain another season.

Caldrin told her that was exactly when people became easiest to kill.

She had ignored him.

She regretted that shortly after sunrise.

Zareth flew above the valley alone.

He was larger now, strong enough to remain airborne for long stretches and arrogant enough to believe this meant he no longer required supervision.

Aurelia had already shouted at him twice.

The second time, he had climbed higher.

Zee sat beneath a fur blanket near the campfire, watching the crimson dragon circle overhead.

Every turn Zareth made crossed the Dragon Link.

Wind.

Height.

Cold.

Freedom.

Zee leaned forward whenever Zareth descended and immediately protested whenever he climbed again.

The link had become clearer with age.

Still no conversations.

No words exchanged across distance.

But emotions now arrived with crude images attached.

Mountain.

Fire.

Aurelia.

Vale.

Food.

Danger.

Zee.

Zareth swept low across the valley.

Zee laughed.

Then the Link vanished.

Not completely.

It snapped tight.

Pain tore through it.

Zee screamed.

Aurelia was beside him instantly.

“What happened?”

Zee could not explain.

His hands reached upward.

Zareth.

Fear.

Falling.

The sound came from the northern ridge.

A roar.

Not powerful.

Pained.

Captain Vale drew his sword.

Caldrin emerged from the trees with an animal carcass over one shoulder and immediately dropped it.

“Dragon.”

Aurelia lifted Zee.

“Zareth.”

The royal witch was already running.

They found the first chain buried in the snow.

Thick.

Black.

Covered in hooked silver teeth.

Caldrin crouched beside it without touching.

“Dragonbane.”

Vale’s face hardened.

“Hunters.”

Another roar echoed through the valley.

Farther north.

Moving away.

Zee began struggling in Aurelia’s arms.

“Zee.”

His Crimson Mana surged.

The Dragon Link flooded him.

Dark.

Pain.

Metal.

Taken.

Zareth’s fear struck harder than anything Zee had felt from him since his first death.

Aurelia saw Zee’s expression change.

Not confusion now.

Rage.

“They took him.”

Vale looked toward the mountain pass.

“How?”

Caldrin pointed toward the trees.

A net had been hidden between them.

Metal rings covered every strand.

“Professional dragon hunters.”

The royal witch’s face tightened.

“Why take Zareth?”

Aurelia already knew.

“Because they finally listened.”

Everyone looked toward her.

She remembered the newest contracts.

Who does he love?

Who can be taken from him?

“They do not need to kill Zee.”

Her voice turned cold.

“They only need something he will follow.”

Zee screamed again.

The Dragon Link stretched farther.

Zareth was moving.

Fast.

Aurelia mounted the nearest horse.

Vale grabbed the reins.

“My Queen.”

“Move.”

“You cannot carry him into a hunter camp.”

“He knows where Zareth is.”

“He is a child.”

“He is the only tracker we have.”

Caldrin was already saddling another horse.

“She is right.”

Vale looked toward him.

“That is becoming an irritating sentence.”

The royal witch mounted.

Vale cursed and followed.

They rode north.

The dragon hunters had planned well.

They used no main road.

No obvious trail.

Their horses wore padded shoes that left shallow marks across stone, and they scattered crushed bitterroot behind them to confuse anything tracking by scent.

It would have worked against wolves.

Zee was not tracking scent.

The Dragon Link pulled him forward.

Zareth’s terror arrived in fragments.

Trees moving.

Metal around wings.

Men.

Blood.

A covered wagon.

Zee could not understand all of it.

But each image sharpened direction.

He kept turning toward the northwest.

Vale watched from beside Aurelia.

“How certain are you?”

Aurelia looked down.

Zee pointed.

His tiny finger remained aimed toward the same ridge.

“Certain enough.”

They crossed the mountains for nearly an hour before Caldrin raised one hand.

Everyone stopped.

Voices ahead.

The hunters had established camp inside an abandoned quarry.

Eight men.

Three women.

No royal banners.

No coven markings.

No uniforms.

This hunt belonged to money.

At the center of the quarry sat an iron transport cage.

Zareth was inside.

Chains wrapped around his legs, wings, neck, and tail.

The hooks had been designed to tighten whenever he struggled.

Blood stained his crimson scales.

Teal-and-gold mana flickered weakly around him and vanished every time the chains glowed.

Zee saw him.

The Dragon Link exploded.

There.

Zareth raised his head.

His golden eyes found Zee across the quarry.

Relief struck first.

Then panic.

No.

Go.

Danger.

Zee leaned from Aurelia’s arms.

Aurelia held him tighter.

Caldrin studied the camp.

“They expected us.”

Vale nodded.

“Obviously.”

Three hunters stood nowhere near Zareth.

They watched the ridges instead.

Bait.

The royal witch whispered, “We can attack from here.”

“No.”

Aurelia looked toward Vale.

“They want us to attack.”

Caldrin smiled faintly.

“Now everyone is learning.”

One hunter stepped into the open quarry.

He raised both empty hands.

“Queen Aurelia.”

Vale’s sword came free.

The man called again.

“We know you are there.”

Aurelia stepped from cover.

Vale swore.

She ignored him.

Zee remained in her arms.

The hunter smiled.

“Good.”

“Release the dragon.”

“Prince.”

Aurelia’s eyes narrowed.

So they knew.

The Dragon Crown’s proclamation had spread farther than anyone wanted.

“You know who he is, and you still chained him?”

“That is why the chains cost so much.”

Zareth growled.

The metal tightened.

His growl became a cry.

Zee’s Crimson Mana flared.

The hunter saw it.

Interest entered his face.

Not fear.

“That.”

Aurelia understood.

“You wanted to see what Zee would do.”

“No.”

The hunter smiled.

“We wanted to see what he would trade.”

“For Zareth?”

“Yes.”

“What do you want?”

“Blood.”

Aurelia’s face went still.

“One vial.”

“No.”

“Then a tooth.”

“No.”

“A piece of flesh.”

Vale stepped forward.

“You are about to lose pieces of yourself.”

The hunter ignored him.

“One sample from the Tribrid and the dragon walks free.”

Aurelia’s expression became murderous.

“Who paid you?”

“More than one person.”

“Names.”

“I do not have them.”

Caldrin whispered from the trees, “Possible.”

The hunter continued.

“Brokers collect contracts.”

“Some want blood.”

“Some want hair.”

“Some want to know whether his bones heal.”

Aurelia’s grip tightened around Zee.

“And you?”

“I want to retire.”

Zareth struggled.

The chains bit deeper.

Zee felt every pulse of pain.

Something inside him began changing.

Not physically.

Emotionally.

Until then, most danger had reached him directly.

Cold against his skin.

Blades near his body.

Monsters coming toward him.

This was different.

Zee was untouched.

Zareth was hurting.

And Zee could feel every second.

His Crimson Mana thickened.

Aurelia felt it against her arms.

“Zee.”

His eyes remained on Zareth.

The hunter raised a small glass vial.

“Blood.”

Aurelia answered.

“No.”

He nodded.

Then pulled one chain.

Zareth screamed.

Zee went silent.

That frightened Aurelia more than the scream.

The Dragon Link filled with pain.

Then something moved beneath Zee’s instincts.

His vampire senses sharpened.

Heartbeats appeared across the quarry.

Twelve.

His wolf nature found positions through scent.

His witch nature reached outward through Crimson Mana.

Separate instincts.

One child.

Zee stared at the man holding the chain.

His heartbeat.

There.

His fingers tightened.

The hunter suddenly gasped.

His hand opened.

The chain dropped.

He grabbed his chest.

Not dying.

His heart simply refused to beat correctly for several terrifying seconds.

Zee did not understand what he was doing.

Only one thing.

Stop.

The man fell to one knee.

Every hunter raised a weapon.

“Now!” Vale shouted.

The royal witch cast first.

Violet magic exploded across the quarry.

Caldrin dropped from the ridge behind two archers.

Vale charged straight through the center.

Aurelia ran toward Zareth.

The hunters had prepared for witchcraft.

Anti-magic seals ignited beneath the quarry floor.

The royal witch’s spell weakened.

They had prepared for dragons.

Every chain around Zareth tightened.

They had prepared for soldiers.

Hidden archers opened fire from the cliffs.

They had not prepared for Zee.

One arrow came toward Aurelia.

Zee heard the string before release.

Crimson Mana knocked the arrow aside.

Another hunter rushed them.

Zee smelled him.

Turned.

The man’s legs suddenly sank into earth hardened like stone around his boots.

He fell.

Aurelia stared at her son.

Zee was not casting trained spells.

He was surviving through instinct.

And now survival included Zareth.

The cage stood ahead.

Aurelia reached it.

Zareth threw himself toward Zee.

The chains stopped him.

The Dragon Link flooded with desperation.

Near.

Near.

Near.

Aurelia tried the lock.

Sealed.

“Witch!”

The royal witch fought her way closer.

“I see it!”

A hunter intercepted.

Vale cut him down.

The witch placed one hand against the lock.

Purple mana entered.

The black metal reacted.

Her spell vanished.

“Dragonbane and witch-sealed.”

Caldrin arrived beside her.

“So break the cage.”

“With what?”

He looked toward Zee.

Aurelia’s face hardened.

“No.”

Caldrin held up both hands.

“I was going to say a hammer.”

Another chain tightened.

Zareth cried out.

Zee touched the cage.

Crimson Mana spread over the black metal.

Everyone near him stopped.

The dragonbane reacted violently.

Dark runes ignited.

Zee’s hand burned.

Aurelia pulled him back.

He reached again.

“Zee!”

He touched it.

Pain.

His palm blistered.

Then began healing.

The dragonbane tried suppressing his mana.

His mana compressed.

Tightened.

Changed its flow.

The cage had been built to disrupt dragon magic.

It had been reinforced to resist witchcraft.

Zee’s Crimson Mana was neither.

The black runes brightened.

Then cracked.

One.

Then another.

The cage door exploded outward.

Zareth lunged.

Aurelia barely caught him.

The little dragon wrapped himself around Zee so violently both nearly fell from her arms.

The Dragon Link flooded with relief.

Together.

The chains remained attached.

The royal witch could work on those later.

First they needed to leave.

The surviving hunters understood the bargain was over.

So did Vale.

“Retreat!”

Aurelia looked at him.

“We are winning.”

“That is why.”

She understood.

Professionals planned for failure too.

The quarry floor began glowing.

Caldrin cursed.

“Run.”

The hunters scattered.

A magical detonation tore through the stone behind them.

Vale shoved Aurelia into a side passage.

The royal witch threw up a barrier.

Rock filled the air.

Zee felt Zareth’s fear.

Crimson Mana covered them both.

Stone hit.

Pain came.

But Zee remained conscious.

Zareth remained alive.

Aurelia crawled from beneath Vale’s shield spell and dragged both children into the passage.

The quarry collapsed behind them.

They escaped through the opposite ravine.

No one followed.

Zareth did not allow anyone to remove him from Zee for the rest of the day.

The royal witch had to break each dragonbane chain while both remained pressed together.

Every time a chain fell away, teal-and-gold mana returned more strongly.

By sunset, Zareth could spread his wings again.

He did not fly.

He stayed beside Zee.

Aurelia cleaned Zee’s burned palm.

The injury had nearly vanished.

Not resurrection.

Not death.

Healing.

Adaptation.

The dragonbane had harmed him once.

By the second touch, his Crimson Mana had already begun changing how it met the suppression.

Aurelia said nothing about it.

She had learned not to name things too quickly.

Caldrin sat near the fire examining a broken piece of chain.

“They were not part of the masked group.”

Vale looked toward him.

“How do you know?”

“Different equipment.”

“That proves nothing.”

“No.”

Caldrin turned the chain over.

“But these people wanted payment.”

“The masked hunters wanted information.”

The royal witch nodded.

“Different motive.”

Aurelia looked toward Zee and Zareth.

“Good.”

Vale frowned.

“Good?”

“I would rather have many enemies than one enemy secretly controlling everything.”

Caldrin smiled faintly.

“That may be the most royal thing you have ever said.”

Aurelia ignored him.

The world remained exactly what it had always been.

Messy.

Violent.

Divided.

Kings feared Zee.

Merchants profited from him.

Scholars wanted him studied.

Witches disagreed over him.

Vampires wanted blood.

Hunters wanted silver.

Dragon nobles argued over Zareth.

The black-robed organization wanted observation.

No single hand controlled them.

That made the world harder to understand.

It also made it real.

Zee shifted beside the fire.

Zareth immediately opened one eye.

The Dragon Link carried something new.

Not simply safe.

Not simply together.

Memory.

Chain.

Taken.

Pain.

Zee touched Zareth’s face.

The image faded.

Something had changed between them.

Zareth had always protected Zee because instinct demanded it.

Now Zee had experienced what it meant for Zareth to be taken.

He had learned another kind of fear.

Not fear of being hurt.

Fear of losing someone.

Aurelia watched her son.

The newest bounty hunters had failed to collect his blood.

But they had succeeded in teaching Zee something no blade ever had.

The world could hurt him without touching him.

That night, Aurelia made the decision.

They would leave the crownless valley.

The world knew too much about it now.

Winter had hidden them.

Dragons had revealed them.

Hunters had followed.

There would be another place.

Then another.

Perhaps Zee’s childhood would become a map made entirely of abandoned sanctuaries.

Aurelia looked toward the distant south.

House Vesper remained the closest thing Zee had to a second home.

Lucien.

Rez.

Walls prepared for war.

And people who had already paid a price for saying yes.

“We return to Vesper,” she said.

Vale nodded.

Caldrin sighed.

The royal witch began packing.

Zareth curled around Zee.

Neither child knew they were leaving the valley that had taught them winter, monsters, flight, blood, and fear.

By sunrise, the camp was gone.

The place without a crown became empty again.

And the next stage of Zee Darkheart’s life began on the road south.
