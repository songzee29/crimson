Chapter Eighty-Eight

When the King Broke

Winter came hard after Southhold doubled.

So did the next enemy.

They were not Pureborn.

They did not care about hybrids.

They wanted people back.

The Kingdom of Rell had lost nearly eight hundred workers, servants, prisoners, debtors, and mixed-blood families to Darkheart over three years.

Rell called them property.

Darkheart called them citizens.

That disagreement became war.

⸻

The first attack hit a Safe Road before dawn.

Zee received the message at breakfast.

“Seventy missing,” Merek said.

Zee stood.

“Taken?”

“Some.”

“How many dead?”

Merek hesitated.

“Twenty-three.”

Ner stopped eating.

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“Children?”

“Five.”

Silence.

Zareth felt the change through the Link from Drakonis.

Zee.

No answer.

Merek continued.

“They left a message.”

He placed a bloodstained strip of cloth on the table.

RETURN RELL PROPERTY OR WE TAKE IT BACK PIECE BY PIECE.

The table cracked beneath Zee’s hand.

Ner looked at him.

Not frightened.

Watching.

“Where?”

“Old quarry east of Southhold.”

Zee was already moving.

⸻

The quarry was a trap.

Zee knew before entering.

He entered anyway.

Forty prisoners knelt at the bottom.

Darkheart citizens.

Hands bound.

Silver collars around Vampires.

Witch suppression cuffs.

Wolves chained through iron floor rings.

Children among them.

Rell soldiers stood above.

Two hundred.

Archers.

Witches.

Blood weapons.

Their commander stepped onto the ridge.

General Oren Vael.

Human.

No supernatural blood.

“You came.”

Zee looked at the prisoners.

“Release them.”

Oren smiled.

“They belong to Rell.”

“No.”

“They fled contracts.”

“Contracts children signed?”

Oren’s smile faded.

Zee continued.

“Open the chains.”

“Return our labor population.”

Zee looked at him.

“You crossed my border.”

“Yes.”

“Killed my people.”

“Yes.”

“Took children.”

Oren shrugged.

“They were with the runaways.”

Something changed.

Ner felt it from thirty feet away.

Not magic.

Zee.

His heartbeat slowed.

His face emptied.

Zareth hit the Link again.

Do not let him decide the pace.

Zee answered this time.

I know.

But rage underneath the thought was so violent Zareth felt heat in Drakonis.

Oren raised one hand.

A soldier pulled a little Wolf girl forward.

Knife against her throat.

“Order Darkheart to return what belongs to us.”

Zee stared.

The girl was crying silently.

Maybe nine.

“You think she belongs to you?”

“Her family owed Rell fourteen years.”

Zee’s eyes turned fully predatory.

Crimson.

Gold.

Slits.

“No.”

Oren cut the girl.

Not deep.

Just enough for blood.

That was the mistake.

Zee smelled it.

Fear.

Child.

Blood.

Something inside him snapped.

⸻

The first Witch never finished casting.

Zee crossed the quarry in Vampire speed and tore her throat open.

The second died beneath one word.

“Break.”

Her neck turned.

The archers fired.

Zee did not dodge.

Bolts struck his shoulders.

Chest.

Side.

He kept moving.

Crimson-black scales spread violently across his body.

Not transformation.

Not Dragon form.

But more of the Demonic Dragon nature surfaced than anyone had seen in years.

Heat exploded outward.

Snow vanished.

Stone blackened beneath his feet.

Oren shouted:

“Kill the prisoners!”

That destroyed whatever restraint remained.

Zee screamed.

Not a spell.

Not a command.

Rage.

Witch power detonated through the quarry.

Chains shattered.

Soldiers flew from the ridges.

Three towers collapsed.

One landed on Rell troops.

Another almost struck prisoners.

Ner’s Gold caught it inches above them.

“ZEE!”

Zee did not hear him.

Or did not care.

He reached Oren.

The general drew a silver sword.

Zee caught it barehanded.

Silver burned.

Zee smiled.

Then crushed the blade.

Oren finally looked afraid.

Good.

Zee hit him once.

The general flew twenty feet.

Alive.

Zee could have ended it.

He did not.

He turned toward the soldiers.

And hunted.

⸻

Wolf form.

Near-black.

No horns.

No scales.

Pure predator.

Zee tore through fleeing troops.

One surrendered.

Zee killed him anyway.

Another dropped his sword.

Zee bit through his throat.

A third begged.

Zee fed from him.

Then another.

And another.

Blood hit snow.

Vampire hunger mixed with Wolf pursuit.

Witch rage moved without words.

Crimson Demonic Dragon heat followed him through the quarry like wildfire.

This was not strategy anymore.

Not punishment.

Not protection.

Control had left.

Kael arrived with Southhold soldiers.

He saw Zee feeding from a man already too injured to fight.

“Majesty!”

Zee looked up.

Blood covered his mouth.

Kael stopped.

For the first time since Zee saved him—

Kael looked afraid of him.

That should have mattered.

It did not.

Not yet.

A Rell soldier ran behind Kael.

Zee moved.

He crossed the distance and ripped the man apart before Kael could turn.

Kael stared.

“He was running away.”

Zee’s fangs remained down.

“I know.”

That frightened Kael more.

⸻

Ner freed the prisoners.

Tessa stabilized the wounded.

Amara carried the bleeding Wolf girl herself.

Nobody could stop Zee because stopping him would mean fighting him.

Zareth arrived last.

True Dragon form struck the quarry hard enough to shake the ridge.

His voice thundered.

“ZEE!”

Zee turned.

Blood.

Heat.

Predator.

The Link slammed open between them.

Zareth did not send words first.

He sent what Zee could not see anymore.

The prisoners behind him.

Ner shielding children.

Kael standing frozen.

Tessa covered in civilian blood.

The Wolf girl alive.

Darkheart soldiers staring at their King.

Then words.

Look.

Zee snarled.

They attacked my people.

Yes.

They took children.

Yes.

They deserve this.

Zareth’s answer came harder.

Some do.

That reached something.

Some.

Not all.

Zee looked around.

A Rell boy in military armor lay beneath a broken wagon.

Sixteen.

Maybe seventeen.

Terrified.

No blood on his sword.

He had been trapped before reaching the fight.

Zee had been walking toward him.

He had not even noticed.

The boy stared at him.

“Please.”

Zee stopped.

His hand was already raised.

One spell would kill him.

One step would reach him.

The hunger wanted blood.

Wolf wanted pursuit.

Dragon rage wanted destruction.

Witch power wanted obedience.

Zee stood there shaking.

Then lowered his hand.

Human form remained.

But his mind returned.

Slowly.

Painfully.

He looked behind him.

Bodies.

Dozens.

Some fighters.

Some surrendering soldiers.

People he would have spared an hour earlier.

Dead.

Because he wanted them dead.

No calculation.

No evidence distinction.

No judgment.

Just rage.

Ner approached carefully.

Not like Zee was fragile.

Like he was dangerous.

That hurt more.

Zee wiped blood from his mouth.

“How many?”

Nobody answered.

Zee looked at Kael.

“How many?”

Kael swallowed.

“Seventy-four Rell dead.”

“Darkheart?”

“Twenty-three before we arrived.”

“The prisoners?”

“Thirty-nine alive.”

Zee looked toward the Wolf girl.

Amara held her.

Alive.

One prisoner missing.

Zee understood.

“They killed one.”

Tessa nodded.

“Before we broke the chains.”

Zee looked at Oren.

Still alive.

Broken.

Crawling.

That was deliberate now.

Good.

Zee walked to him.

Oren coughed.

“You monster.”

Zee crouched.

This time the word did not bore him.

Because this time—

it landed differently.

Zee looked into his eyes.

“You cut a child to control me.”

Oren laughed weakly.

“And it worked.”

Zee went still.

Yes.

It had.

Not the way Oren intended.

But it had.

Zee killed him.

Quickly.

No feeding.

Then stood.

“Take the prisoners home.”

Nobody moved immediately.

Zee looked toward his people.

They obeyed.

But for the first time—

some obeyed because they were afraid of him.

Zee could smell it.

And he hated that he liked the scent.

That terrified him more than anything Oren had done.
