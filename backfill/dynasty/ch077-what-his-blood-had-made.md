Chapter Seventy-Seven

What His Blood Had Made

Kael Marrin hated mornings now.

Not because of Vampire weakness to daylight.

There was none.

Not in him.

At least not yet.

He hated mornings because everyone wanted to examine him.

Healer.

Witch.

Ner.

Zee.

Elara.

His mother.

His sister.

Then Merek.

Then two Darkheart scholars who Kael threw out after one called him “the subject.”

Zee approved.

Strongly.

By the third day, Kael stood in the training courtyard with a sword.

Elara stared.

“You were dead four days ago.”

“Almost.”

“Put the sword down.”

“No.”

“You are still healing.”

Kael looked at the wound in his side.

Closed.

Not scarless.

But impossible compared to four days earlier.

“That seems debatable.”

Elara hit him.

Wooden practice blade to ribs.

Kael moved faster than expected.

Caught it.

Both froze.

Vampire speed.

Not Zee’s.

His own.

Kael stared at his hand.

Then released the blade.

“I hate that.”

Elara lowered hers.

“You will learn.”

“What if I don’t want to?”

Zee stood above the courtyard balcony.

He heard.

Did not interrupt.

That question belonged to Kael.

Elara answered:

“Then you decide what kind of life you want with what happened.”

Good.

Zee left before either noticed him.

He did not own Kael’s future.

Blood did not make ownership.

That law formed naturally.

Because the alternative disgusted him.

⸻

Darkheart noticed anyway.

People talked.

The northern village knew Kael should have died.

Soldiers had watched his heart stop.

Now he walked.

Cast Witch magic.

Healed too quickly.

Smelled blood through walls.

And had fangs.

Rumors moved before Zee could control them.

By the end of the week:

King Zee brought a dead Witch back as a Vampire.

Wrong.

King Zee can turn any Witch into a creature with two bloodlines.

Unproven.

King Zee is building an immortal army.

Completely false.

Darkheart has a Witch that drinks blood.

Unfortunately accurate.

Kael hated that one most.

⸻

Ner found Zee in Aurelia’s old solar.

The room had changed over the years.

Not erased.

Changed.

Zee kept her chair.

Her books.

One hairpin.

But the room now held kingdom maps too.

Reports.

Blood samples sealed behind wards.

Not trophies.

Research.

Careful.

Ner placed Kael’s latest notes on the desk.

“No instability.”

Zee leaned back.

“Yet.”

“Yet.”

“Magic?”

“Still Witch.”

“Hunger?”

“Manageable.”

“Compulsion?”

Ner smiled.

“He accidentally told his sister to stop yelling.”

Zee frowned.

“And?”

“She stopped.”

Silence.

Then Zee almost laughed.

“Accidentally?”

“He panicked.”

“That sounds familiar.”

Ner gave him a look.

Fair.

Zee read further.

Kael could cast.

Heal.

Feed.

Use Vampire strength.

His Witch nature did not weaken.

No new Siren blood.

No Bloodsong.

Just Witch and Vampire.

Two stable natures.

Ner sat opposite him.

“You know what this means.”

“Yes.”

“You can do this again.”

“Maybe.”

“Do you want to?”

Zee looked at him.

“No.”

Ner waited.

Zee continued.

“I want people to stop dying.”

Different.

Ner nodded.

“That is not the same thing.”

“No.”

Silence.

Then Ner asked:

“If another Kael happens?”

Zee looked toward the window.

Snow.

Darkheart rooftops.

People.

More than Victor ever allowed inside these walls.

“If they are dying.”

“And they choose?”

“Yes.”

Ner’s face softened slightly.

“Then?”

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

“Then I give them the choice Kael had.”

Not creation program.

Not army.

Emergency.

Life.

For now.

⸻

Kael’s first real problem came that night.

Hunger.

Not ordinary.

Not manageable.

Three bags of animal blood were not enough.

His body rejected the fourth.

He became restless.

Aggressive.

Scared.

His Witch Mana reacted to the hunger and started breaking objects around him.

Kael locked himself inside the northern barracks before he hurt anyone.

Zee arrived to find six soldiers outside.

One held a silver chain.

Zee looked at him.

“Put that away.”

“He asked us to use it.”

“Put it away.”

The soldier obeyed.

Not compulsion.

King.

Zee entered alone.

Kael stood near the far wall.

Eyes dark.

Fangs visible.

Crimson Witch energy crawling around his fingers.

“Stay back.”

Zee closed the door.

“No.”

“I can hear everyone.”

“Yes.”

“I can smell them.”

“Yes.”

“I want—”

Kael stopped.

Ashamed.

Zee’s expression cooled.

“Say it.”

“I want blood.”

“Good.”

Kael stared.

“Good?”

“You know what it is.”

Zee stepped closer.

“Now control what you do with it.”

Kael’s breathing worsened.

“I don’t know how.”

“Then learn.”

Zee opened his wrist.

Kael recoiled.

“No.”

“You need supernatural blood.”

“I am not feeding from you.”

“Why?”

“You are the King.”

Zee stared.

“That is a terrible reason.”

Kael almost laughed despite himself.

Then hunger hit again.

He bent over.

Zee held out his wrist.

“One swallow.”

Kael hesitated.

Then fed.

One.

Only one.

He pulled away himself.

Good.

The effect was immediate.

His Witch Mana stabilized.

The hunger retreated.

Kael stared.

“Why did that work?”

Zee looked at the wound closing on his wrist.

“Because animal blood feeds the Vampire.”

“And yours?”

Zee’s expression sharpened.

“Apparently speaks to both.”

Witch.

Vampire.

His blood had created the bridge.

Interesting.

Dangerous.

Zee was beginning to understand.

Not enough to be comfortable.

Enough to continue.

⸻

The next attack came eleven days later.

Not on Zee.

Not Darkheart Castle.

A frontier refuge.

Forty miles east.

Zee was in council when the alarm arrived.

Sixty mercenaries.

Demon-backed.

Wards preventing communication.

Three Darkheart units trapped.

Zee stood immediately.

Then another messenger entered.

Northern forest.

Monster breach.

Children missing.

Two crises.

Again.

Merek looked toward him.

Zee looked at the maps.

Old instinct:

Go.

Personally.

Everywhere.

Impossible.

Kael entered the war room wearing guard armor.

Still pale.

Still adjusting.

But standing.

“Send me east.”

Zee looked over.

“No.”

Kael’s jaw tightened.

“Why?”

“You are eleven days old in this body.”

“I am twenty-four years old.”

Good correction.

Zee stared.

Kael continued.

“My unit is trapped there.”

“Elara commands them.”

“She trained me.”

“You are not ready.”

Kael stepped closer.

“Neither were the seven people we buried.”

Silence.

That landed.

Zee’s face hardened.

Kael did not back down.

“I am stronger now.”

“Unproven.”

“Then let me prove it.”

Zee hated the logic.

Not because Kael was wrong.

Because Zee finally saw the future inside it.

People would choose to become stronger if the alternative was watching everyone die.

Not soldiers made by Zee.

People choosing survival.

Merek interrupted.

“Majesty, we need a decision.”

Zee looked at the map.

East.

North.

Two places.

He could not take both.

Fine.

“Kael goes east.”

Kael nodded once.

“Elara commands.”

“Yes.”

“You follow her orders.”

Kael looked annoyed.

“Yes.”

“Ner goes with them.”

Ner stood.

“What about you?”

“North.”

Rez’s voice came through a communication mirror from Vesper.

“I can send ten riders east within the hour.”

Zee nodded.

“Do it.”

Zareth’s voice entered Zee’s mind through the Link from Drakonis.

There is movement near your northern mountains. I can reach the forest faster than you.

Zee stopped.

More beneath the sentence.

Wind.

High altitude.

No current Drakonis emergency.

Zareth was already flying.

You are supposed to be in council.

So are you.

Zee smiled faintly.

Take the forest. I will go east.

Zareth answered instantly.

Done.

There.

No one person everywhere.

Network.

Kingdom.

Family.

Choice.

Zee looked at Kael.

“Change of plan.”

Kael frowned.

“You are coming east?”

“Yes.”

“That defeats the point.”

Zee smiled.

“No.”

He looked toward Merek.

“Next time I may not.”

That silenced the room.

Because everyone understood there would be a next time.

⸻

The eastern refuge was already breaking when they arrived.

Demon-backed mercenaries had breached two walls.

Darkheart soldiers held the inner courtyard.

Elara fought with one arm burned nearly useless.

Kael saw her.

Something changed.

Vampire speed.

He disappeared.

Not Zee-fast.

Fast enough.

Kael hit the first mercenary before the man reached Elara.

Witch force threw another backward.

Then Kael bit the third.

Everyone froze.

Including Kael.

Blood entered his mouth.

Human.

He released immediately.

Horror.

Zee saw.

Not weakness.

First feeding in battle.

Different.

“Kael!”

Kael looked over.

Zee pointed toward Elara.

“Protect your commander.”

Simple.

No shame.

No lecture.

Kael moved.

Good.

Zee took the center.

Silent Witchcraft broke a Demon ward.

Wolf senses found the hidden caster.

Vampire speed crossed the courtyard.

Assassin knife ended him.

A Demon emerged behind Zee.

Crimson Demonic Dragon heat forced it solid when it tried slipping into shadow.

Zee caught its throat.

Fangs descended.

Demon blood.

Old enemy.

Familiar pressure.

He drank until it weakened.

Then killed it.

Around him, Darkheart fought.

Not perfectly.

Not safely.

But stronger than months earlier.

Kael moved among them like something the attackers had never seen.

Witch sigils in one hand.

Vampire strength in the other.

He took a blade through the stomach.

Elara screamed his name.

Kael looked down.

Then back up.

Healing already starting.

The mercenary who stabbed him froze.

Kael smiled.

Not like Zee.

His own smile.

Nervous.

A little disbelieving.

Then he punched the man unconscious.

Zee saw it.

History beginning.

Not as a title.

As one person refusing to fall where he should have.

The refuge survived.

Six Darkheart soldiers died.

Too many.

But without Kael—

it would have been more.

That mattered.

It also terrified Zee.

Because now he had proof of the thing he feared wanting.

His blood could make survival more likely.

⸻

Three weeks later, the first foreign letter arrived asking to purchase Kael’s blood.

Zee burned it.

The second asked whether Darkheart would “supply transformed soldiers.”

He burned that too.

The third came from a kingdom demanding Kael be surrendered for “containment.”

Zee sent that one back.

With one sentence.

Come take him.

No one did.

Not yet.

Kael read the copy later.

“You did not have to do that.”

Zee looked at him.

“Yes, I did.”

“I am not property.”

“I know.”

“Then why speak for me?”

Zee paused.

Good.

Kael had caught him.

Zee corrected immediately.

“You are right.”

Kael blinked.

“That was easier than expected.”

Zee frowned.

“Do not ruin it.”

Kael smiled.

Then looked toward the letter.

“If they come?”

Zee leaned back.

“You decide whether you stay.”

“And if I leave Darkheart?”

“You leave.”

Kael stared.

“No curse?”

“No.”

“No blood oath?”

“No.”

“No ownership because you made me?”

Zee’s face went cold.

“I saved you.”

“That is all.”

Kael nodded slowly.

Good.

Person first.

Always.

Outside the chamber, people had begun calling Kael something.

Not officially.

Not yet.

The Witch with Vampire blood.

The Blood Witch.

The Night Witch.

Most names were terrible.

History would eventually settle on something simpler.

Witch-Vampire.

One of the first original hybrid bloodlines Zee Darkheart would ever create.

But Zee did not care about history tonight.

He cared that Kael’s mother still had a son.

His sister still had a brother.

Elara still had a soldier.

And six children from the northern village still had the man who held the cellar door.

That was enough.

For now.

Then the northern report arrived.

Zareth had stopped the monster breach.

Three missing children recovered.

One dead.

Zee read the name twice.

A girl.

Eight years old.

Too late.

Again.

The victory soured instantly.

Kael watched him.

“You cannot save everyone.”

Zee’s eyes lifted.

Human pupils.

Calm.

Cold.

“No.”

He folded the report.

“But I can change the odds.”

There it was.

Not an army.

Not yet.

Not ambition.

A belief.

And somewhere beyond Darkheart, kingdoms had begun hearing that King Zee Darkheart had done something no ordinary ruler could do.

A dying Witch had survived.

Now he possessed fangs.

Vampire strength.

Vampire hunger.

And all of his Witchcraft.

The world had spent twenty-two years fearing what Zee Darkheart was.

Now it had something new to fear.

What his blood could make.
