Chapter Sixty-Nine

Greyhaven

Greyhaven was asleep when Zee entered.

It stayed asleep.

That was the point.

No army.

No Darkheart banners.

No declaration.

Just Zee and Ner beneath black cloaks while Rez remained outside town coordinating exits with four trusted Darkheart scouts.

The Red Covenant occupied a former hospice near the northern wall.

Twenty-three heartbeats inside.

Three Vampires.

Nine Humans.

Seven Witches.

Four Wolves.

No children.

No servants.

No innocent household staff.

Good.

Zee smelled blood beneath the building.

Stored.

Supernatural.

Dragon.

Wolf.

Witch.

Vampire.

Samples.

Ner smelled something else.

“My blood.”

Zee stopped.

“How much?”

“Not much.”

“Old?”

“Months.”

His face hardened.

“Taken from one of the monastery samples.”

So House Veylan had not been the end.

Different group.

Different purpose.

Same theft.

Zee’s pupils became slits.

Ner touched his wrist.

Not stopping him.

“Do not kill anyone until we know what they are doing.”

Zee looked over.

Reasonable.

Annoying.

“Fine.”

Ner smiled faintly.

“You hate when I am right.”

“Yes.”

They entered through the roof.

⸻

The first room held maps.

Not kingdoms.

People.

Zee.

Ner.

Rez.

Zareth.

Theron.

Seraphine.

Lucien.

Darkheart nobles.

Drakonis officers.

Vesper routes.

Not one planned strike.

Dozens of possible ones.

Ner stared.

“They are profiling us.”

Zee moved to the wall.

Notes.

Not prophecy.

No destiny.

Patterns.

Zee responds fastest to family threat.

Rez prioritizes civilians and infrastructure.

Ner protects boundaries and blood-related victims.

Zareth returns to Drakonis under pressure.

The Covenant had studied behavior.

Zee smiled.

Ner looked toward him.

“That smile is bad.”

“They think they understand us.”

“That is why you are smiling?”

“Yes.”

Zee touched one page.

A projected strike against Vesper grain storage.

Another against Drakonis hatcheries.

Another against House Veyrath survivors.

Not executed yet.

Plans.

Preparations.

Potential.

There.

The next moral line.

No attack yet.

Not technically.

Only preparation.

Ner saw it too.

His expression tightened.

“We have evidence.”

“Yes.”

“Enough to arrest them.”

“Probably.”

Zee looked toward the next room.

“Maybe.”

Ner watched him.

“You are thinking about killing them before they act.”

“Yes.”

No lie.

No shame.

Ner breathed slowly.

“Then prove they mean to.”

Good.

Not conscience.

Standard.

Evidence.

Zee nodded.

“Agreed.”

They kept moving.

⸻

The basement proved it.

A Dragon scale from Zareth.

Ner’s dried blood.

Hair from Rez.

Blood from three Darkheart guards.

One of Zee’s discarded bandages from a battlefield years earlier.

Stored.

Cataloged.

Beside weapons built around each sample.

Blood-targeting needles.

Resonance disruptors.

Dragon-link interference dust.

Compulsion-resistant charms.

Crimson suppression mixtures.

Nothing complete.

Everything experimental.

One Witch stood over the workbench.

She looked up.

Saw Zee.

Stopped breathing.

He smiled faintly.

“Continue.”

She stared.

“What?”

“You were working.”

Ner stepped beside him.

“Keep going.”

The Witch looked between them.

Then reached slowly toward an alarm.

Zee barely moved one finger.

The alarm shattered.

“Not that.”

Her hands trembled.

“Who leads the Covenant?”

Silence.

Ner looked toward the blood samples.

His Bloodsong sharpened.

He hummed once.

Every vial containing his blood rang.

The Witch flinched.

“Who?”

“Council of Five.”

Rez’s voice came through the open stairwell.

“Names?”

He had entered from below after scouts secured the street.

The Witch stared.

Zee smelled the lie before it came.

“I don’t know.”

“Wrong.”

Vampire compulsion pressed outward.

No eye contact needed.

“Names.”

She resisted.

Strong charm.

Zee heard her heartbeat jump when the command hit the ward.

Interesting.

He stepped closer.

His predatory presence filled the room.

“Names.”

The charm cracked.

Not physically.

Mentally.

The Witch gasped.

“Orin Bale.”

“Mara Vess.”

“Dren Solvar.”

“High Witch Keira Morn.”

“And?”

She shook violently.

“Father Caldus.”

Rez frowned.

“Priest?”

“No.”

“Title?”

“Yes.”

No divine smell.

Good.

No God.

Not yet.

Zee looked toward the plans.

“Which attack happens next?”

The Witch began crying.

“Vesper.”

Ner’s face changed.

“When?”

“Tomorrow night.”

Rez went still.

Target.

His kingdom.

Not theoretical anymore.

“Where?”

“South aqueduct.”

“Explosives?”

“Bloodfire charges.”

Rez’s expression became cold.

Future king.

Not brother.

Different.

He turned toward the scouts.

“Send word now.”

One left immediately.

Zee looked at him.

“You want this one?”

Rez understood.

Not the Witch.

The operation.

Vesper.

“Yes.”

Good.

Zee turned back.

“Where are the Five?”

The Witch swallowed.

“Not here.”

“Where?”

“Separate.”

Smart.

No easy decapitation.

Rez looked toward Zee.

“We take the cell alive.”

Zee stared at the plans.

Burn Darkheart villages.

Break Vesper aqueduct.

Attack Drakonis hatcheries.

Target Ner’s blood.

Target Veyrath.

All prepared.

Not yet all executed.

His definition of threat shifted again.

He did not need to wait for fire anymore.

Still, Rez was right.

Living prisoners would expose the Five faster.

Zee nodded.

“Alive.”

The Witch exhaled.

Relief.

Then a door opened above.

Someone shouted.

The building woke.

⸻

The fight lasted six minutes.

Twenty-three members.

Three surrendered immediately.

Zee spared them.

Four tried escaping.

Rez’s scouts caught two.

Zee caught the others.

One Human fighter threw down his sword.

“Mercy.”

Zee stopped.

The man had not attacked civilians personally.

No blood scent from the burned villages.

No direct participation.

“Stay down.”

He lived.

A Vampire lunged from the balcony.

Zee caught him by the throat in midair.

The Vampire snarled.

“Monster.”

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“Original.”

He slammed him into the floor.

The Vampire reached for a bloodfire capsule.

Zee saw.

Decision made.

Fangs.

He drained him.

Fast.

No speech.

No guilt.

Supernatural blood entered.

Crimson adaptation stirred.

The Vampire had consumed resistance compounds against compulsion.

Zee’s blood separated them.

Studied them.

Rewrote around them.

Another small step toward making ordinary anti-Vampire defenses less reliable.

Useful.

He dropped the body.

A Witch cast from behind.

Ner intercepted.

Pure Golden Mana folded around the spell.

Bloodsong followed.

“Still.”

The Witch froze.

Not from Zee.

From Ner.

Her blood obeyed just long enough for Rez to disarm her.

Ner’s control had become frighteningly clean.

Zee noticed.

Loved him for it.

Did not say so during combat.

Probably wise.

Two Wolves attacked together.

Zee smiled.

Then transformed.

Four-legged Wolf.

No horns.

No scales.

Just near-black fur and crimson-gold eyes.

The Wolves hesitated.

One recognized him.

“The King.”

Wrong moment to hesitate.

Wolf Zee hit them hard.

No Vampire speed.

No Witchcraft.

Just strength, balance, teeth, instinct.

He drove one down.

Circled the second.

The enemy tried fleeing.

Zee let him run ten seconds.

Then hunted.

He enjoyed the chase.

Caught him at the stairwell.

Dragged him back by the shoulder.

Alive.

Because they had agreed.

That mattered.

He shifted human again.

Blood on his mouth.

Ner looked at him.

“Show-off.”

Zee smiled.

“Worked.”

“Unfortunately.”

⸻

By dawn, Greyhaven knew nothing had happened.

That was how cleanly they moved.

The hospice remained standing.

The Covenant cell was gone.

Seventeen captured.

Six dead.

No civilian casualties.

No city panic.

The records belonged to Darkheart now.

Rez had the Vesper strike plan.

Ner had every sample of his blood destroyed under his own control.

Zee stood in the basement over the map of future attacks.

He could see the next step clearly.

Once, he would have waited.

Then responded.

Then hunted whoever attacked.

Now?

He had names.

Five.

Somewhere in the world.

Planning more strikes.

Rez looked at him.

“You are going after them.”

“Yes.”

“All five?”

“Yes.”

“Before they attack again.”

Zee met his eyes.

“Yes.”

Ner leaned against the table.

No surprise.

No immediate objection.

Only:

“Verify each one.”

Zee nodded.

“I will.”

That was the line now.

Not innocence until attack.

Evidence before elimination.

Different.

Darker.

Still a line.

For now.

A scout entered.

“Prince Rez.”

Rez turned.

“Vesper message.”

He read.

His face tightened.

“What?”

“The aqueduct team moved early.”

Zee’s pupils narrowed.

Rez folded the note.

“My father is handling it.”

Zee looked at him.

“You are going home.”

“Yes.”

No hesitation.

Good.

Ner looked toward Zee.

“And us?”

Zee studied the five names on the wall.

Then one blood sample marked for House Veyrath.

He thought of Theron.

Seraphine.

Aurelia.

His people.

The next attack had not happened yet.

That used to matter more.

Now the preparations were enough.

Zee pulled the first name from the wall.

Orin Bale.

Then the second.

Mara Vess.

He folded both into his coat.

“We start verifying.”

Ner smiled faintly.

“Verifying.”

“Yes.”

“And if they are guilty?”

Zee’s expression stayed calm.

His crimson-and-gold eyes shifted into slits.

“Then they die before they get another chance.”

No one called him good.

No one called him evil.

No one needed to.

The descent had become simpler than labels.

Zee Darkheart had spent most of his life surviving people who decided what he might become was reason enough to act first.

Now, for the first time, he was preparing to do something dangerously similar.

The difference, in his mind, was evidence.

The question was how long that difference would remain enough.
