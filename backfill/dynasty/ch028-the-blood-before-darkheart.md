Chapter Twenty-Eight

The Blood Before Darkheart

Aurelia took Zee beyond Vesper’s walls after nightfall.

Not far.

Only north, past the final patrol road and into the high cliffs where pine forest gave way to open stone.

Zareth came with them.

Of course he did.

Rez tried.

Aurelia refused.

Ner asked once, heard the answer, then told Zee he expected every detail that did not belong to Aurelia alone.

Zee promised nothing.

Ner told him he was a terrible friend.

Rez told Ner that Zee had always been terrible.

Zareth stole Rez’s glove while they were arguing.

That ended the conversation.

Now only Aurelia, Zee, and Zareth stood beneath a clear night sky.

Vesper glowed faintly in the valley behind them.

Zee sat near the cliff edge with his knees pulled toward his chest.

His ribs had finished repairing.

The torn shoulder had closed.

Only dried blood remained on the shirt Aurelia had ordered him to change twice.

He had ignored her twice.

Zareth lay behind him, enormous crimson body curved around both Zee and the cold wind.

The Dragon Link remained quiet.

Listening.

Aurelia stood several feet away.

For a long moment, she watched the moon.

Then she spoke.

“House Darkheart is not where my story begins.”

Zee looked up.

“I thought you were born there.”

“I was.”

“Then how can it not begin there?”

Aurelia smiled faintly.

“Birthplace and beginning are not always the same.”

Zee thought about that.

Then raised one finger.

“You said I could ask questions.”

“I have not said that yet.”

“You were going to.”

Aurelia stared.

Zareth sent amusement.

Zee smiled.

Aurelia sighed.

“You may ask questions.”

“Good.”

His hand shot up again.

“What is your Dragon family called?”

“That quickly?”

“You said questions.”

“I did.”

Aurelia sat across from him.

Her expression became quieter.

“House Veyrath.”

The name settled between them.

Zareth lifted his head.

The Dragon Link offered nothing.

No recognition.

No remembered Drakonis history close enough for him to understand.

Zee noticed.

“Zareth does not know it.”

“I would not expect him to.”

“Why?”

“Because House Veyrath was never part of House Drakonis.”

Zee frowned.

“Enemies?”

“Sometimes.”

“Friends?”

“Sometimes.”

“Which one?”

Aurelia almost smiled.

“Both, depending on the century.”

“That is stupid.”

“That is politics.”

“That is what I said.”

Aurelia laughed.

Zareth’s head lowered again.

Zee continued.

“So we are not related to Zareth?”

“Not through my immediate bloodline.”

“Good.”

Zareth opened one eye.

Zee looked toward him.

“You are annoying enough already.”

The Dragon Link returned an image of Zee falling into Lucien’s ornamental pond.

“That happened once.”

Zareth added a second memory.

Another pond.

Zee frowned.

“Twice.”

Aurelia smiled despite herself.

Then Zee became serious.

“What made Veyrath different?”

Aurelia looked toward her hands.

“Compatibility.”

“With what?”

“Dragon nature and witchcraft.”

Zee leaned forward.

“Like you.”

“Yes.”

“House Veyrath had Dragon-Witches?”

“Eventually.”

“How?”

Aurelia paused.

“Not one single way.”

Zee waited.

She had promised truth, not everything.

“Veyrath Dragons possessed an unusual ability to coexist with certain forms of witch blood without one nature destroying or suppressing the other.”

“And you were born with both?”

“Yes.”

“A real Dragon.”

“Yes.”

“A real witch.”

“Yes.”

“Not half of each.”

Aurelia looked directly at him.

“No.”

“Both.”

“Both.”

Zee smiled faintly.

He understood that word.

Better than most.

“What happened to them?”

The smile vanished from Aurelia’s face.

“House Veyrath was hunted.”

Zee became still.

Hunted.

That language belonged to him.

“By dragons?”

“Some.”

“Witches?”

“Some.”

“Why?”

“Fear.”

“That is not enough.”

“No.”

Aurelia looked toward the distant mountains.

“Some wanted Veyrath destroyed because Dragon-Witch blood challenged rules they believed should never be crossed.”

“Some wanted our knowledge.”

“Some wanted our blood.”

“Some had older political reasons that had nothing to do with what we were.”

Zee thought of his own enemies.

Kings.

Hunters.

Researchers.

Vampires.

Demons.

People who wanted money.

People who wanted answers.

Not one reason.

Never one reason.

“How many Veyrath are left?”

Aurelia’s face tightened.

“I do not know.”

Zee looked at her.

“Are you the last?”

“I do not know.”

“You really do not?”

“No.”

That answer mattered.

She was not making certainty where none existed.

“I know some died.”

“I know some disappeared.”

“I know some learned to hide.”

Aurelia looked toward him.

“I was one of them.”

Zee touched one of his horns.

“So you hid the Dragon.”

“Yes.”

“For how long?”

“Most of my life.”

“Even from Father?”

“At first.”

Zee’s expression changed.

“Victor did not know?”

“No.”

“When you met?”

“No.”

“When you married him?”

Aurelia hesitated.

“Not immediately.”

Zee looked almost offended on Victor’s behalf before remembering who they were discussing.

“When did you tell him?”

“When I trusted him enough.”

“Why?”

“Because I loved him.”

Zee frowned.

“That seems dangerous.”

“It was.”

“Was it stupid?”

Aurelia laughed quietly.

“Sometimes love is.”

Zee watched her.

“What did he do?”

“When I told him?”

“Yes.”

“Nothing terrible.”

Zee blinked.

That was clearly not the answer he expected.

“He kept my secret.”

“For how long?”

“Years.”

“He did not tell House Darkheart?”

“No.”

“Did he hate that you were Dragon?”

“No.”

“Fear you?”

“Not then.”

Aurelia looked toward Vesper’s distant lights.

“Victor was not always the man you know through other people’s pain.”

Zee became quiet.

He had grown up hearing fragments of his father.

King.

Husband.

Enemy.

The first person who ever killed him.

The only one who ever would.

It was easier when Victor existed as one thing.

Monster.

But Aurelia would not let him become simple.

“When did it change?”

“Gradually.”

“Because of me?”

Aurelia looked sharply at him.

“Not because you did anything.”

“But after I was born.”

“Yes.”

Zee nodded.

He had stopped confusing sequence with blame years ago.

Mostly.

“The Blood Rain.”

“Yes.”

“The prophecy.”

“Yes.”

“The kingdoms.”

“Yes.”

“My three natures.”

“Yes.”

“And then he remembered you were Dragon too.”

Aurelia’s expression tightened.

“Fear changes the meaning of things.”

Zee looked toward her.

“You said people become what they keep choosing.”

“I did.”

“Father kept choosing fear.”

“Yes.”

“Even when he loved us.”

Aurelia was quiet.

“Yes.”

Zee looked away.

“That is stupid.”

Aurelia touched his hair.

“Sometimes.”

They sat quietly.

Then Zee asked, “Did he think your Dragon blood made me worse?”

Aurelia did not answer quickly.

Eventually:

“He wondered whether there were things inside you none of us could see.”

Zee touched the crimson-black scales at his neck.

“He was right.”

“That does not make what he chose right.”

Zee looked at her.

Aurelia continued.

“Fear can accidentally notice a truth and still build a lie around it.”

Zee thought about that longer than she expected.

Then his expression became uncertain.

“Mother?”

“Yes?”

“Am I four things?”

Aurelia looked at him.

The Vault’s words lingered.

Four.

Three awake.

“Yes.”

Zee stared.

“But I am the First Natural Tribrid.”

“Yes.”

“That makes no sense.”

“It does.”

“How?”

Aurelia moved closer.

“You were born carrying four natural blood natures.”

She touched the center of his chest.

“Witch.”

“Vampire.”

“Wolf.”

“Dragon.”

Zee looked down.

“At birth?”

“All four were yours.”

“But only three—”

“Woke with you.”

He looked back up.

“Witch, Vampire, Wolf.”

“Yes.”

“Dragon slept.”

“Yes.”

“Then the Vault…”

“Four.”

Aurelia held his gaze.

“Three awake.”

Zee looked toward Zareth.

The Dragon Crown Prince lifted his head.

Zee’s Crimson Mana stirred.

Teal-and-Gold Mana answered faintly beneath Zareth’s scales.

Aurelia continued.

“The world saw what was active.”

“Witch.”

“Vampire.”

“Wolf.”

“The impossible child born complete in all three.”

“That is why you became known as the First Natural Tribrid.”

Zee touched one horn.

“And Dragon was already there.”

“Yes.”

“Mine.”

“Yes.”

“Not Zareth’s.”

“No.”

Zareth sent offense through the Link.

Zee grinned.

“I did not say yours was bad.”

Zareth answered with the clear concept:

Mine better.

Zee’s eyes narrowed.

“Your horns are ugly.”

Zareth growled.

Aurelia ignored them.

“Zareth did not give you Dragon blood.”

Zee looked back at her.

“He wakes it.”

“We think his presence helps stimulate what was already inside you.”

“The Link.”

“Possibly.”

“His Mana.”

“Possibly.”

“Being born the same day.”

“Possibly.”

Zee frowned.

“That is a lot of possibly.”

“It is the truth.”

He sighed.

“Fine.”

Aurelia touched one crimson scale.

“Your Dragon nature belongs to you.”

“It does not replace anything.”

“You are still Witch.”

“Still Vampire.”

“Still Wolf.”

“And Dragon.”

“Yes.”

Zee sat with that.

Then something quieter entered his face.

Aurelia noticed immediately.

“What?”

“Sometimes…”

He stopped.

“What?”

Zee stared at his hands.

“I feel like too many things.”

No grand declaration.

No tears.

Just a ten-year-old boy admitting something he did not know how to explain.

“Everyone has one thing they are supposed to be.”

“Ner is a witch.”

“Rez is a witch.”

“Zareth is a Dragon.”

“You are two things.”

Zee looked at her.

“I keep getting more.”

Aurelia moved beside him.

“You are not a pile of pieces.”

“That sounds like a mother answer.”

“It is.”

“Is it true?”

“Yes.”

“How do you know?”

“Because I knew you before anyone taught you names for yourself.”

Zee looked at her.

“You were Zee before anyone called you Tribrid.”

“Before anyone called you monster.”

“Before anyone called you prince.”

“Before you had horns.”

“Before the world decided what your existence meant.”

She touched his cheek.

“You are still Zee.”

Zareth pressed his snout into Zee’s shoulder.

The Link carried the same simple certainty.

Zee.

Nothing else.

Zee smiled faintly.

“That was also a Dragon answer.”

Aurelia smiled.

“Apparently he agrees with me.”

“Rare.”

Zareth shoved him.

Zee shoved back.

For a moment, the weight disappeared.

Then Zee looked at Aurelia.

“Show me.”

Her smile faded.

“My Dragon.”

“No.”

Zee’s face fell.

“Why?”

Aurelia looked around the empty cliffs.

Then back toward distant Vesper.

Zee saw the hesitation.

His eyes widened.

“You are going to.”

“I did not say that.”

“You looked around.”

“That proves nothing.”

“You sound like Caldrin.”

Aurelia closed her eyes.

“Stay here.”

Zee scrambled upright.

“You are!”

“Sit.”

He sat immediately.

Zareth rose.

Excitement flooded the Link.

Aurelia walked farther onto the open stone.

She stopped beneath the moon.

Then, for the first time in Zee’s life, Queen Aurelia Darkheart allowed the part of herself hidden beneath decades of discipline to emerge completely.

Heat rolled across the cliff.

Scales appeared first.

Dark red.

Deep crimson-red under moonlight.

Near-black shading spread beneath her arms and across her back.

Her body expanded.

Bone changed.

Wings unfolded.

Claws struck stone.

Her neck lengthened.

Her face reshaped.

The transformation continued until the woman Zee knew was gone from human sight.

A true Dragon stood beneath the stars.

Large.

Powerful.

Distinct from every Drakonis Dragon Zee had seen.

Dark red scales covered her body while near-black shading marked the undersides of her wings and parts of her spine.

And woven naturally between the scales were patterns that looked almost like witch sigils.

Not painted.

Not carved.

Born into her.

Magic moved through them.

Dragon and Witch.

Both.

Zee forgot every serious question he had ever asked.

His mouth fell open.

“Mother?”

The Dragon lowered her head.

Same eyes.

Same presence.

Aurelia.

Zee laughed.

Not nervously.

Not politely.

Pure childhood joy exploded out of him.

He ran.

Aurelia barely had time to lower her head before Zee wrapped both arms around her snout.

“You are huge!”

Aurelia released a deep rumble.

“Can you understand me?”

She nodded.

“Can you talk?”

Aurelia gave him a look.

Zee grinned.

“Right.”

Zareth had not moved.

For perhaps the first time in his ten years of life, Crown Prince Zareth Drakonis appeared completely stunned.

He stared at Aurelia.

Then at Zee.

Then back at Aurelia.

The Dragon Link erupted.

Mother. Dragon.

“Yes.”

Hidden.

“Yes.”

Years.

“Yes.”

Zareth looked personally betrayed.

Zee started laughing again.

“You lived with her for ten years and did not know.”

Zareth growled.

“You are a terrible Dragon.”

Zareth lunged.

Zee ran around Aurelia’s foreleg.

Aurelia shifted her head and blocked Zareth.

The young Dragon stopped.

Looked at her.

Then stepped closer.

He sniffed Aurelia.

Once.

Again.

Teal-and-Gold Mana shimmered along his spine.

Not hostile.

Instinct.

Recognition of another Dragon whose blood was completely separate from his own.

Aurelia’s Dragon energy answered.

Dark crimson-red power moved beneath her scales alongside faint witch patterns.

Zee felt both.

The Dragon Link tightened.

Zareth’s Mana.

Aurelia’s full Dragon presence.

Something inside Zee responded.

His smile vanished.

Heat struck his horns.

Zee grabbed them.

“Ow.”

Aurelia turned.

Crimson-black light moved through the developing horns.

His permanent scales brightened.

Then spread.

Across his temples.

Down his neck.

Over both shoulders.

Along his ribs.

Across his forearms.

More than combat usually triggered.

“Mother.”

Aurelia immediately shifted her attention fully toward him.

Zareth moved closer.

The Link flooded with concern.

Hot. Zee.

“I know.”

Crimson Dragon energy surged inside Zee’s Crimson Mana.

Not Aurelia’s energy.

Not Zareth’s.

His.

It rolled through him with enough force to make his knees buckle.

Zee fell.

Aurelia changed.

Her massive Dragon body folded rapidly back toward human form.

She reached him before he hit the stone.

“I have you.”

Pain developed beneath Zee’s shoulder blades.

Not a wound.

Pressure.

Internal.

Something pushing against bone and muscle.

He gasped.

“My back.”

Aurelia moved behind him.

Two ridges formed beneath the skin.

One on each side.

Zareth whined.

The Link became frantic.

Pain. Wrong. Stop.

“I cannot stop it!”

Aurelia placed both hands against Zee’s shoulders.

“Breathe.”

“Is it wings?”

“I do not know.”

“Mother!”

“I will not lie to you because you are frightened.”

The pressure intensified.

Zee gritted his teeth.

His body wanted to move toward a transformation it could not yet complete.

The fourth nature stirred.

Reached.

Then stopped.

No wings broke free.

No completed Dragon form emerged.

The ridges remained beneath the skin for several painful heartbeats.

Then slowly receded.

Crimson Dragon energy collapsed back into his Mana.

The scales along Zee’s face withdrew.

The permanent ones remained.

His horns cooled.

Zee sagged against Aurelia.

Zareth pushed his snout beneath Zee’s hand.

The Link carried one desperate feeling.

Stay.

Zee touched him.

“I am here.”

Aurelia examined his back.

No wound.

Two faint raised lines remained near the shoulder blades.

Not wings.

Possibility.

She stared at them.

Zee noticed her expression.

“What?”

Aurelia looked at him.

“I think both of us affected it.”

“Both?”

“My full Dragon form.”

She looked toward Zareth.

“His Dragon Mana.”

“The Link?”

“Perhaps.”

Zee groaned.

“More possibly.”

“Yes.”

He lay back against her.

“Am I getting wings?”

“Not tonight.”

He looked disappointed.

Aurelia stared at him.

“You were just in pain.”

“Yes.”

“And you are disappointed.”

“I want wings.”

Zareth sent smug approval.

Aurelia shook her head.

“You are both impossible.”

Zee smiled.

Then Zareth went still.

The Dragon Link changed instantly.

No humor.

No pain.

Warning.

Dragon.

Aurelia sensed it a heartbeat later.

Her head turned toward the forest below.

Zee inhaled.

He smelled it too.

Dragon.

But not Zareth.

Not Drakonis.

Not Aurelia.

Different.

Aurelia rose slowly.

Something changed in her face.

Not fear exactly.

Recognition approaching before sight.

A branch moved beneath the trees.

Footsteps followed.

A man emerged into moonlight.

Tall.

Silver hair fell past his shoulders.

His eyes carried a dark red Dragon glow.

He wore no Drakonis colors.

No Vesper crest.

No Darkheart sigil.

Aurelia went completely still.

Zee looked toward his mother.

“Mother?”

The stranger did not look at Zee.

Not first.

His attention belonged entirely to Aurelia.

For a long moment, neither moved.

Then the man spoke.

“Aurelia Veyrath.”

The name struck harder than any spell.

Aurelia stepped in front of Zee and Zareth.

Her hands began glowing with Witch Mana.

Dragon energy moved beneath it.

Her voice dropped.

“You should be dead.”

The stranger smiled.

“So should our bloodline.”

Silence consumed the cliff.

Only then did his attention shift.

Past Aurelia.

Toward Zee.

His eyes moved over the ten-year-old boy.

Crimson eyes.

Developing crimson-black horns.

Permanent Crimson scales.

The faint residual Dragon energy still moving beneath them.

Recognition entered the stranger’s face.

Not recognition of Zee’s full nature.

Not understanding of the First Natural Tribrid.

Recognition of something older.

Blood.

Veyrath.

His gaze returned briefly to Aurelia.

Then back to Zee.

“Well.”

Aurelia’s body tightened.

The stranger’s smile changed.

“You really did have a son.”

Zareth stepped beside Zee.

Teal-and-Gold Mana rolled over his scales.

The stranger noticed the Drakonis prince too, but offered no explanation.

No prophecy.

No answer for the Dragon Link.

No secret knowledge of Zee’s birth.

Whatever had brought this man to the cliffs had begun long before Zee Darkheart opened his crimson eyes beneath Blood Rain.

This was not one of Zee’s hunters.

Not yet.

He had not come because of Victor.

He had not come because of the Twelve Crowns.

He had not come because of a bounty, an experiment, or the prophecy.

He had come for Aurelia Veyrath.

For House Veyrath.

For a past Zee had only begun to discover.

And for the first time in Zee Darkheart’s life, the danger standing before him belonged to his mother before it belonged to him.
