Chapter Ninety-Eight

The Kingdom Beneath the Ocean

Three months passed before Zee visited Nhalaris.

Nymeris reminded him twice.

The third message contained no words.

Only a sphere of seawater floating above the Darkheart council table.

Zee stared at it.

Rez stared at Zee.

Ner smiled.

Zareth leaned back in his chair.

“She is getting impatient.”

Zee looked at him.

“You sound pleased.”

“I am.”

The sphere burst.

Water hit Zee directly in the face.

Silence.

Rez started laughing first.

Ner followed.

Zareth did not even try hiding it.

Zee wiped water from his eyes.

“I hate her.”

Ner stood.

“No, you don’t.”

“I am reconsidering.”

A folded shell rested where the sphere had been.

Zee opened it.

One sentence.

COME BELOW, SURFACE KING.

Zee’s eye twitched.

⸻

Nhalaris did not sit on the ocean floor.

It existed inside it.

That distinction became clear when Nymeris opened the passage.

Zee, Ner, and Zareth stood on a black coastal cliff while seawater climbed upward into an arch.

Not a portal.

A road.

Water held apart by pressure and ancient magic.

Nymeris waited beyond it.

Dark armor.

Silver-black crown.

Completely dry despite standing inside the sea.

“You came.”

Zee stepped through.

“I was assaulted by correspondence.”

“You ignored two requests.”

“They were very long.”

“They were six sentences.”

“Exactly.”

Ner walked behind him.

Zareth entered in human form.

Nymeris looked toward the Dragon prince.

“No true form below the inner gates.”

Zareth raised an eyebrow.

“Why?”

“Because you are enormous.”

Fair.

Zee smiled.

Zareth privately sent:

I dislike this kingdom already.

Zee answered:

You dislike doors.

Correct.

⸻

The descent took nearly an hour.

Not because they walked downward.

Because Nhalaris moved around them.

Currents shifted.

Stone opened.

Water became corridors.

Then suddenly—

the ocean disappeared.

A city stretched beneath a living dome of deep-blue pressure.

Towers of black coral.

Silver bridges.

Bioluminescent gardens.

Water channels flowing upward along walls.

Siren guards in dark armor.

Others without visible Siren traits.

Families.

Merchants.

Soldiers.

Children.

An entire civilization hidden beneath the surface kingdoms.

Zee stopped.

Nymeris noticed.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

“You are staring again.”

“This one is deserved.”

That almost made her smile.

Almost.

Ner’s Bloodsong hummed faintly.

Dozens of distant voices answered the city around him.

Not directly.

Resonance.

Ancient Siren blood everywhere.

His expression changed.

Nymeris looked toward him.

“Too much?”

“A little.”

She nodded.

No mockery.

“Do not listen to everything.”

“I am not trying.”

“That is usually when it is worst.”

Ner looked at her.

Recognition.

Not identical power.

But experience.

That mattered.

⸻

The Nhalaris council hated Zee immediately.

That helped.

Seven ministers.

Three military commanders.

One Abyssal priestess.

None bowed.

Good.

Zee did not ask them to.

One minister spoke first.

“You killed Kharos.”

“Yes.”

“You ruptured our eastern current.”

“Yes.”

“You entered Nhalaris armed.”

Zee looked down at himself.

“I am always armed.”

Nymeris closed her eyes.

The minister continued.

“Your presence threatens the stability of this court.”

Zee looked toward Nymeris.

“Do they always speak like this?”

“Yes.”

“That sounds exhausting.”

“It is.”

The minister looked offended.

Good.

Then another woman placed a map of the eastern current across the table.

The damage remained.

The fracture they closed on the surface had prevented collapse.

It had not repaired the deeper channels.

Three villages still operated under emergency pressure wards.

Nymeris looked toward Zee.

“This is why I summoned you.”

“Ordered.”

“Yes.”

Zee smiled.

“What do you need?”

No argument now.

Work.

Nymeris pointed.

“Divine residue embedded itself into an old Abyssal boundary.”

Zee studied the map.

“And if we remove it?”

“The boundary may fail.”

“If we leave it?”

“It spreads.”

“How many people?”

“Eight thousand seven hundred within the affected reach.”

Zee’s humor disappeared.

There.

Same burden.

Different kingdom.

Nymeris noticed.

“You understand why I did not wait politely.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Zee looked toward the map.

“Then we fix it.”

One minister objected.

“He is the reason it exists.”

Zee looked at him.

“Yes.”

The man paused.

Zee continued.

“That is why I am helping.”

No defensiveness.

No excuse.

Just responsibility.

Nymeris watched him carefully.

Another small piece of trust moved into place.

⸻

The repair took four days.

Zee did not command Nhalaris.

That was important.

Nymeris did.

Her engineers.

Her Siren wardens.

Her military.

Her laws.

Zee listened.

Mostly.

On the second day he ignored one safety order and nearly got crushed under deep-current pressure.

Nymeris found him afterward bleeding from both ears.

“You are impossible.”

“I survived.”

“That is not the same as listening.”

“I listened.”

“You did the opposite.”

“I heard you first.”

Nymeris stared.

Ner turned away to hide a smile.

Zee looked toward him.

“Do not.”

“I said nothing.”

“You are thinking loudly.”

“Zareth says the same thing.”

Through the Link from another chamber:

Because you are both annoying.

Zee smiled.

Good.

⸻

The final breach sat beyond Nhalaris’s eastern shelf.

Open ocean.

Dark enough that even Zee’s Vampire sight struggled.

Nymeris entered the water first.

Not swimming.

Belonging.

The ocean carried her.

Ner followed inside a Golden breathing sphere.

Zee had one too.

He hated it.

“This is humiliating.”

Nymeris’s voice reached them through water resonance.

“You cannot breathe underwater.”

“I do not need much air.”

“You need some.”

“Technicality.”

She ignored him.

Zareth remained in human form until the passage widened.

Then shifted.

True Dragon form filled the dark water.

No fire.

No flight.

But enormous strength.

The Dragon Link carried his displeasure.

I hate swimming.

Zee answered:

You look majestic.

I will drown you.

I cannot permanently die.

Then I will drown you repeatedly.

Nymeris looked back.

“You two are arguing.”

Zee frowned.

“How do you know?”

“You both look irritated.”

Fair.

⸻

The divine residue had become a knot of storm-light beneath the shelf.

Nymeris anchored the current.

Ner used resonance to locate living pressure around it.

Zareth held the collapsing rock.

Zee entered the center.

The old Black Divinity scars across his body burned.

Not because Dark Divinity was present.

Because divine pressure recognized wounds that had once denied divinity.

Useful.

Painful.

Zee smiled.

Nymeris saw.

“You enjoy pain too much.”

“Only interesting pain.”

“That sentence is deeply concerning.”

Zee raised one hand.

Witch precision.

No giant blast.

He separated divine residue thread by thread.

Nymeris held the current around it.

Their magic did not merge.

Did not need to.

They worked beside one another.

Queen.

King.

Each controlling what belonged to them.

The final divine strand broke.

The ocean surged.

Zareth roared through the water.

Ner’s Gold snapped outward.

Nymeris lifted both hands.

Her voice changed.

Deep Siren resonance moved through miles of water.

The current obeyed.

Not like compulsion.

Like inheritance.

The Abyss recognized her.

The rupture closed.

Completely.

Nhalaris survived.

⸻

That night, Nymeris invited them to dinner.

Not diplomacy.

Dinner.

A distinction Zee noticed immediately.

No ministers.

No guards at the table.

Only Zee.

Ner.

Zareth.

Nymeris.

Zee looked around.

“Where is everyone?”

“I dismissed them.”

“Why?”

“Because I wanted one meal without hearing someone explain why your existence threatens oceanic stability.”

Zee smiled.

“Reasonable.”

Nymeris poured wine.

Ner looked toward the food.

“Is that still moving?”

“Yes.”

“No.”

Zareth picked it up and ate it.

Ner stared.

Zareth shrugged.

“Dragon.”

Zee laughed.

Nymeris finally did too.

Not almost.

Actually.

The sound changed something.

Small.

Not romance.

Ease.

By the end of the night, Nymeris no longer called Zee Surface King every time she addressed him.

Only half the time.

Progress.

⸻
