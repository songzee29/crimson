Chapter Ninety

The Night the King Chose Blood

Darkheart had learned to survive attacks.

It had not learned how to survive what Zee chose to do after one.

The attack began during rain.

Southhold had just finished rebuilding the school.

Again.

Children filled it.

Again.

And someone decided that made it a target.

Again.

Ner detected the crisis through Bloodsong.

His expression changed.

“Southhold.”

Zee stood.

“How bad?”

“I don’t know.”

“Children?”

Ner’s silence answered him.

Zee vanished.

⸻

The attackers had used bloodfire.

Not ordinary flame.

It crawled through living blood before spreading to wood.

Darkheart healers fought through smoke and screaming civilians as the fire moved from wound to wound.

Kael was already there, carrying children from the burning school with Witchcraft blazing in both hands.

Tessa had three wounded across her shoulders and another clinging to her back.

Amara hunted attackers beyond the southern wall, moving through rain and smoke like a Wolf-shaped shadow.

Ner stood in the courtyard, directing healers and stabilizing panicked families.

Zee reached the school.

Then saw the bodies.

Small ones.

Four covered.

One not.

A little boy lay beside the steps.

Maybe seven.

His wooden sword still in his hand.

Zee stopped.

The world narrowed.

Sound became heartbeats.

Rain became blood.

Smoke became heat.

The Crimson Demonic Dragon surged beneath his skin.

Scales spread across his throat, jaw, chest, and back.

His horns darkened toward the tips.

His pupils narrowed.

But his mind remained clear.

That was the first terrible thing.

He knew exactly where he was.

He knew exactly what had happened.

And he knew exactly what he wanted.

Ner reached him.

“Zee.”

Zee looked toward the southern road.

The attackers were retreating.

He could smell bloodfire residue, Darkheart blood, school smoke, weapons, and the sharp adrenaline of people who had already decided they had done enough damage.

“How many?”

“Thirty-two crossed the wall,” Ner said. “Amara has taken down several.”

“Alive?”

“Some.”

Zee’s gaze remained fixed on the road.

“Keep the wounded alive.”

Ner understood before Zee moved.

“Zee.”

“I know who they are.”

“That is not what I was going to say.”

Zee looked at him.

For one moment, Ner saw the rage.

Not madness.

Not absence.

Choice.

“Do not make me ask twice,” Zee said.

Then he disappeared into the rain.

⸻

The retreating attackers reached the forest.

They thought distance meant safety.

It did not.

Zee struck the last man first.

The attacker turned with a bloodfire blade in hand.

Zee broke his arm, took the weapon, and drove it through his chest.

The next tried to run.

Wolf pursuit carried Zee through the trees.

He caught the man by the back of his armor and slammed him into the ground.

The third raised both hands.

“I surrender!”

Zee stopped.

The man’s heartbeat hammered beneath his skin.

His clothes smelled of smoke.

His hands carried bloodfire residue.

The same residue coated the school’s outer wall.

Zee knew he had been there.

The man dropped to his knees.

“I surrender.”

Zee looked at him with human pupils.

Calm.

Focused.

“You surrendered after the school burned.”

The man opened his mouth.

Zee killed him before he could answer.

No frenzy.

No confusion.

No loss of recognition.

Only decision.

The forest seemed to understand.

The remaining attackers scattered.

Zee hunted them one by one.

A Witch threw suppression chains.

Zee tore through them.

A mercenary fired a crossbow bolt.

Zee caught it and returned it through his throat.

Another attacker begged while clutching a bloodfire wound.

Zee fed from him until the man stopped moving.

Then Zee followed the next heartbeat.

And the next.

He did not kill everyone he found.

He killed everyone he verified had entered Southhold, carried weapons, and participated in the assault.

That distinction should have mattered.

It did not.

Not tonight.

One attacker threw down his sword.

“He surrendered,” Kael said from somewhere behind Zee.

Zee turned.

Kael stood at the edge of the clearing, blood on his coat and smoke in his hair.

The surrendered man knelt between them.

Zee looked at him.

“I know.”

Kael’s expression tightened.

The man had participated in the attack.

Kael knew it.

Zee knew it.

That was not the question.

“He surrendered,” Kael repeated.

“Yes.”

“And you are killing him anyway.”

“Yes.”

Zee stepped forward.

Kael did not move toward him.

He moved between Zee and the prisoner.

Not with a weapon.

Not with fear that Zee would strike him.

With the certainty that Zee would recognize him.

Zee stopped.

His fangs remained down.

Heat rolled from his body and steamed the wet ground.

“Move.”

Kael held his gaze.

“No.”

Zee’s eyes shifted past him.

The prisoner trembled.

Zee could hear every beat of his heart.

He could smell the bloodfire on his hands.

He could remember the school burning.

He could remember the child beside the steps.

His anger sharpened.

But Kael was not a target.

Kael was family.

Mine.

Not target.

Zee’s hands closed at his sides.

Ner entered the clearing.

Then Tessa.

Then Amara.

Tessa’s sleeves were soaked with blood from the wounded.

Amara’s claws were still extended.

Ner’s Gold glimmered faintly around his hands, not raised against Zee, only ready to protect the prisoners if necessary.

None of them drew a weapon.

None of them expected Zee to attack them.

They were standing on the belief that there was still a line he would not cross.

Zee looked at each of them.

Angry.

Predatory.

Blood-covered.

Still choosing.

“Move,” he said.

Ner stepped beside Kael.

“No.”

Zee’s gaze settled on him.

The command in his voice remained.

“Do not stand between me and them.”

“They surrendered.”

“They burned children.”

“Yes.”

“They killed civilians.”

“Yes.”

“They came here to destroy what we rebuilt.”

“Yes.”

“Then move.”

“No.”

The word did not challenge his strength.

It challenged his decision.

Zee could have forced them aside.

He could have blasted the ground beneath them.

He could have reached around them.

He could have killed the prisoners before any of them could react.

He did none of those things.

Because Ner was not an obstacle.

Kael was not an obstacle.

Tessa was not an obstacle.

Amara was not an obstacle.

They were his.

Family.

Not targets.

Zareth reached him through the Dragon Link.

Not with force.

With images.

The school’s surviving children huddled beneath blankets.

Tessa pressing both hands over a bloodfire wound.

Amara standing guard over the captured attackers.

Ner in the rain, refusing to move.

Kael watching Zee without fear for himself.

Then one word.

Enough.

Zee’s breathing slowed.

The prisoner remained alive.

Zee looked at him.

Then at the people standing between them.

He could still kill the man.

He wanted to.

But wanting was not the same as doing.

Zee stepped back.

The heat did not vanish.

The blood did not disappear.

The choice remained his.

“Take them,” he said.

Tessa exhaled slowly.

“Alive?”

Zee’s eyes stayed on the prisoners.

“For trial.”

Kael studied him.

“And if the trial confirms they ordered the school attack?”

“Then they die.”

No one argued with that.

The difference was not mercy.

It was judgment.

Zee turned away from the prisoners.

Behind him, the surviving attackers were bound.

None of them understood why they were still alive.

Zee did.

He had chosen blood.

He had also chosen where it stopped.

⸻

Southhold remained awake until dawn.

The school was ruined again.

The healers worked until their hands shook.

Tessa directed emergency treatment while Kael carried the last children from the smoke.

Amara returned with six captured attackers.

Ner stayed with the families.

Zee stood outside the broken school, blood drying across his hands.

He had killed twenty-one attackers.

All verified participants.

Some while fighting.

Some after surrender.

The distinction would follow him into every council chamber in Darkheart.

Zareth’s voice remained quiet through the Link.

You knew what you were doing.

Zee looked at the dead.

Yes.

You recognized them.

Yes.

You recognized the surrender.

Zee’s jaw tightened.

Yes.

A pause.

And you still chose to kill them.

Zee looked toward the school.

Toward the covered child.

Yes.

Zareth did not condemn him.

That made it worse.

⸻

Zee returned to Darkheart before sunrise.

No gates closed.

No guards raised weapons.

The city saw him enter with blood on his clothes and rainwater running from his hair.

Some citizens bowed.

Some did not.

Some looked at the blood.

Some looked at the school survivors being carried through the gates behind him.

Darkheart did not speak with one voice.

Some believed the attackers deserved worse.

Some were disturbed that surrender had not saved them.

Some soldiers became quieter around Zee.

Others stood straighter.

No one believed he had turned against his own people.

They had seen where his darkness pointed.

Outward.

That did not make it harmless.

It made it deliberate.
