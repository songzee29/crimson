Chapter Twenty-Two

The Boy With Golden Hands

Nereus Azrion discovered three things about Zee Darkheart during his first week inside House Vesper.

The first was that Zee hated being watched while he slept.

The second was that Zee cheated at every game involving hiding.

The third was that Prince Rez Vesper became unbearable whenever he lost.

“You listened for my heartbeat.”

Rez stood in the center of the western gardens with mud on one knee and betrayal across his face.

Zee sat behind a stone fountain, smiling.

“You were hiding.”

“That is the point.”

“So I found you.”

“With vampire hearing.”

Zee shrugged.

“You said find you.”

Ner sat beneath an old ash tree trying not to laugh.

Rez pointed at him.

“Do not encourage him.”

“I did not say anything.”

“You are smiling.”

“That is not talking.”

Zee laughed.

Rez looked between them.

“I liked him better before you arrived.”

“No, you did not,” Zee said.

Rez considered.

“No. But it sounded better.”

Zareth lay stretched across the garden wall with his wings open beneath the afternoon sun. He had grown large enough that servants no longer attempted moving him when he slept somewhere inconvenient.

The dragon had decided inconvenience was everyone else’s problem.

Ner looked toward him.

“Does he understand us?”

Zee followed his gaze.

“Some.”

“What does he understand?”

Zee thought about it.

The Dragon Link had changed enormously since infancy. Words still did not pass cleanly between them, but emotions now carried sharper images and simple ideas. Zee could feel when Zareth wanted food, flight, distance, danger, Aurelia, Rez, Ner, or solitude.

And Zareth knew when Zee lied.

That had become annoying.

“He understands when I call him fat.”

One golden eye opened.

Rez immediately stepped away.

Ner frowned.

“He is not fat.”

Zee grinned.

Zareth stood.

Rez backed farther.

“Zee.”

“What?”

“You did that on purpose.”

The dragon jumped from the wall.

Zee ran.

Zareth chased him across the garden.

Ner laughed as Zee ducked beneath a hedge and emerged on the opposite side only for Zareth to leap over it.

Rez looked toward Ner.

“This happens every day.”

“Good.”

Rez stared.

“You really are staying.”

Ner looked at him.

“Yes.”

The answer was simple.

But something changed inside the older prince’s expression.

Ner had arrived less than a week earlier.

Already, assassins had shot at him.

Already, court officials had begun asking questions about his Golden Mana.

Already, two Vesper noble houses had sent quiet requests asking that he be examined by their private witches.

Lucien denied them all.

Ner knew some of this.

Not everything.

He was three.

The adults were trying to keep it that way.

They were failing.

A shadow crossed the garden.

Zee stopped running.

Zareth stopped too.

His head lifted.

A dragon descended beyond the western tower.

Not the Dragon Queen.

Smaller.

Crimson-brown scales.

Zareth immediately recognized the scent.

Drakonis.

Zee felt it through the Link.

Known.

Not close.

A royal messenger.

Aurelia entered the garden moments later.

“Inside.”

Zee frowned.

“Why?”

“Because I said so.”

“That is not a reason.”

Aurelia stopped.

Rez immediately looked fascinated.

Ner looked terrified for Zee.

Aurelia slowly turned toward her son.

“You have been spending too much time with Rez.”

Rez looked offended.

“I have never said that.”

Lucien’s voice came from the archway.

“You have said exactly that.”

Rez turned.

“Why is everyone here?”

Lucien looked toward Aurelia.

“The dragon brought news.”

Aurelia’s expression darkened.

“Drakonis?”

“Yes.”

Zee walked toward them.

“What happened?”

The adults hesitated.

Zee noticed.

He had learned that pause.

It meant they were deciding whether he was too young for the truth.

He hated that pause.

Aurelia crouched in front of him.

“Nothing happened to Zareth’s mother.”

Zee looked immediately toward Zareth.

The dragon relaxed through the Link.

Not mother.

Good.

“What then?”

Aurelia looked toward Lucien.

He nodded.

She returned her attention to Zee.

“Your father sent a message.”

The garden went silent.

Zee knew King Victor Darkheart existed.

He knew Victor ruled the kingdom where he had been born.

He knew Aurelia became quiet whenever someone mentioned him.

He knew Vale’s face hardened.

He knew Zareth disliked Victor’s scent even though years had passed.

He knew one other thing.

Victor was not allowed near him.

No one had explained exactly why.

Not yet.

“What does he want?”

Aurelia stood.

“You.”

Zee stared at her.

Rez’s Blue-White Mana flickered.

Ner moved slightly closer to Zee.

Zareth’s wings spread.

Aurelia noticed all three.

“Victor is not coming here.”

Zee remained silent.

Lucien spoke carefully.

“He has petitioned Drakonis and House Vesper for your return to House Darkheart.”

Zee frowned.

“Why?”

Aurelia’s expression changed.

“That is the question.”

The message waited in Lucien’s council chamber.

It had been delivered under Darkheart royal seal through a neutral dragon courier because no Darkheart messenger could cross Vesper territory without being detained.

Lucien had already read it twice.

Aurelia read it once.

That was enough.

Zee Darkheart is blood of House Darkheart.

He remains prince and heir by birth.

His prolonged removal from his kingdom has destabilized our sovereignty, invited foreign interference, and allowed hostile courts to claim authority over Darkheart blood.

Queen Aurelia is commanded to return with the prince.

Prince Zareth Drakonis may accompany him under diplomatic protection.

The next line destroyed any pretense of reconciliation.

The Tribrid will be contained according to Darkheart law until his nature can be controlled.

Aurelia placed the parchment on the table.

“No.”

Lucien leaned back.

“I expected that.”

Caldrin sat near the window.

“I enjoy how he placed ‘contained’ after ‘prince.’”

Vale’s expression remained hard.

“He is trying to reclaim legal authority.”

Aurelia looked toward him.

“Not fatherhood.”

“No.”

The royal witch tapped the seal.

“Darkheart nobles may support him.”

“They will,” Vale said. “Not all because they fear Zee.”

Lucien looked toward him.

“Explain.”

“Some believe Aurelia fleeing with the heir weakened the crown.”

“Others think Vesper stole him.”

“Others hate the Twelve Crowns and believe Zee should be raised as Darkheart’s answer to them.”

Caldrin smiled faintly.

“A child turned into a political weapon before he can read.”

Aurelia’s face hardened.

“Victor will not have him.”

“No,” Lucien said.

“But ignoring the letter does not end the issue.”

Aurelia looked at the map.

House Darkheart remained scarred from the siege.

Its walls had been rebuilt.

Its armies had recovered.

Victor still ruled.

And now the world had fractured enough that he could begin claiming something he had once tried to destroy.

Control.

That was more like him.

“He knows another direct attempt would be dangerous,” Aurelia said.

Vale glanced toward her.

“Yes.”

“So he will use law.”

“Law.”

“Blood.”

“House loyalty.”

“Politics.”

Caldrin nodded.

“Much cleaner than daggers.”

Aurelia looked toward Zee through the open doorway.

He sat on the corridor floor with Ner while Zareth blocked half the passage.

Rez was attempting to teach them some complicated card game and clearly inventing rules as he went.

Three years old.

Still a child.

Still somehow at the center of kingdoms.

Lucien followed her gaze.

“You cannot hide his existence forever.”

“I know.”

“You cannot keep making every choice for him either.”

Aurelia looked sharply at him.

“He is three.”

“Yes.”

Lucien’s voice remained calm.

“But he hears more than you think.”

That hurt because she knew it was true.

Zee knew when guards were doubled.

He knew which doors were locked.

He knew when Aurelia lied about danger.

He knew Victor’s name changed rooms.

Aurelia closed her eyes.

“Not yet.”

Lucien nodded.

“Not all of it.”

That mattered.

Not secrecy.

Timing.

A child deserved truth in pieces he could survive.

That evening, Zee found Aurelia alone in the library.

He climbed onto the chair beside her.

She closed the Darkheart letter before he could see it.

Zee looked at the parchment.

Then at her.

“Father wants me.”

Aurelia’s chest tightened.

“Yes.”

“Does he love me?”

There were questions armies could not prepare a person for.

Aurelia looked at her son.

His crimson eyes carried no accusation.

Only curiosity.

She refused to lie.

“I think part of him does.”

Zee frowned.

“Part?”

“People can feel more than one thing at once.”

“Like Zareth when he wants my food but knows I will bite him?”

Aurelia laughed unexpectedly.

“Yes.”

“Like that.”

Zee thought.

“What else does Father feel?”

Fear.

Regret.

Control.

Shame.

Hatred growing where love had become too painful.

Aurelia chose carefully.

“He is afraid of you.”

Zee stared.

“Why?”

“He does not understand you.”

“Ner does not understand me.”

“No.”

“He is not afraid.”

Aurelia’s eyes softened.

“No.”

Zee looked down at his hands.

“Is Father bad?”

Another impossible question.

“He did something terrible.”

“Does that make him bad?”

Aurelia hesitated.

“I think people become what they keep choosing.”

Zee looked back up.

“What does he choose?”

Her throat tightened.

“Fear.”

The answer stayed between them.

Zee leaned against her.

Aurelia held him.

He did not ask what Victor had done.

Not tonight.

Then Ner appeared at the library entrance.

He had no idea he was interrupting anything.

“I found him.”

Zee looked over Aurelia’s shoulder.

“You were looking for me?”

“No.”

Zee frowned.

Ner smiled.

“Rez was.”

“Why?”

“He says Zareth ate his boot.”

Zee immediately climbed down.

Aurelia caught his arm.

“Do not encourage him.”

“I am going to investigate.”

“You are three.”

“So is Ner.”

“That does not help.”

Ner looked toward Aurelia.

“I can watch him.”

Aurelia stared.

Zee grabbed Ner’s hand.

“We are going.”

They ran.

Aurelia sat alone for several seconds.

Then smiled despite herself.

She looked at Victor’s letter.

The smile disappeared.

She placed it into the fire.

The Darkheart seal blackened first.

Then the parchment curled.

Victor wanted containment.

He would receive an answer.

Not through letter.

Through absence.

Zee would not return.

Not until Zee himself became capable of deciding what House Darkheart meant to him.

And by then…

Victor might discover that the child he wanted to control had grown beyond every cage he could build.
