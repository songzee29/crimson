Chapter Sixteen

The Winter Hunt

The first snow came early.

It fell over House Vesper in thick white sheets that softened rooftops, buried roads, and turned the surrounding forests into something deceptively peaceful.

Zee had been in Vesper for three weeks.

Three weeks without an army at the gates.

Three weeks without a dagger entering the nursery.

Three weeks without a monster climbing through a wall.

It was the longest uninterrupted stretch of his life.

Aurelia did not trust it.

Every morning she woke before dawn and checked Zee’s breathing.

Every night she inspected the windows herself, tested the wards with the royal witch, and refused to sleep until Captain Vale confirmed the corridors had been cleared.

Lucien never mocked her.

He understood too well what fear could turn into when ignored.

Rez, meanwhile, had decided that Zee was his.

Not in the political sense.

In the way children claimed friendships long before adults had time to define them.

He visited constantly.

He brought toys.

He brought stories Zee could not understand.

He brought food for the crimson hatchling and then complained when the hatchling preferred stealing from his plate instead.

The dragon had also grown.

Not dramatically.

But enough that Aurelia could no longer tuck him comfortably beneath one arm.

His crimson scales had deepened in color. Teal-and-gold mana appeared more often whenever he became excited or protective, threading over his body like fire beneath glass.

Rez had begun calling him Red.

The hatchling hated it.

Everyone knew because he bit Rez every time.

“Then stop calling him that,” Aurelia said.

Rez rubbed his wrist.

“He needs a name.”

“He has one somewhere.”

“Then he should tell us.”

“He cannot speak.”

“That sounds like his problem.”

The hatchling hissed.

Zee laughed.

The sound was still rare.

But it had become easier.

Aurelia watched from beside the fire as Rez sat on the floor near Zee’s cradle, offering the hatchling a strip of roasted meat with one hand while protecting his sleeve with the other.

For a few minutes, the world looked almost kind.

Then every ward in the western palace wing went dark.

Lucien entered the room before the final light disappeared.

“Vale.”

The captain was already moving.

Caldrin drew both blades.

The royal witch raised her hands.

Nothing happened.

Her expression changed.

“My magic.”

Aurelia stood immediately.

“Gone?”

“No.”

The witch tried again.

“It is there.”

“But?”

“I cannot reach it.”

The room froze.

Aurelia looked at Zee.

His Crimson Mana remained calm.

No pressure.

Nothing resembling his birth.

The witch understood the thought.

“It is not him.”

Caldrin walked toward the window.

“Someone outside.”

The snow-covered courtyard appeared empty.

Then one footprint formed.

No person.

Just a footprint.

Another appeared beside it.

Then another.

Moving toward the palace.

Vale’s face hardened.

“Invisible.”

Lucien raised one hand.

His mana answered.

Dark blue light gathered around his fingers.

“So only selected wards.”

The royal witch nodded grimly.

“Something is suppressing witchcraft.”

Rez stood.

Blue-white mana appeared around his hands.

“That is not suppressing mine.”

Lucien looked sharply at him.

“Do not test it.”

“I already did.”

“Rez.”

“I am helping.”

“You are seven.”

“That has never stopped anyone from attacking me.”

Lucien had no immediate answer.

Caldrin looked toward the courtyard.

“More footprints.”

Not one intruder.

Several.

They crossed the snow without bodies.

Aurelia lifted Zee.

The hatchling climbed beside him.

The Dragon Link carried immediate tension.

Hidden.

Near.

Zee’s vampire senses sharpened.

Heartbeats.

He heard them.

Six.

No.

Nine.

Spread across the courtyard.

His head turned slowly.

Aurelia followed his gaze.

“He hears them.”

Vale nodded.

“Good.”

Caldrin smiled.

“Finally, invisibility has a disadvantage.”

The first window shattered.

Nothing entered.

At least nothing visible.

Vale swung.

His sword struck metal.

A body flickered into sight for half a heartbeat.

White cloak.

Bone mask.

Then vanished again.

Caldrin attacked the empty space beside him.

A cry answered.

Blood hit the floor.

Visible.

The intruders did not stay unseen once wounded.

“Hit the blood,” Caldrin shouted.

More glass broke.

The hunters entered.

They carried no banners.

No royal seals.

Their weapons were silver-white and etched with seals Aurelia did not recognize.

The royal witch tried magic again.

Nothing.

One hunter threw a chain.

Vale cut through it.

The pieces landed on the floor and began moving on their own.

They wrapped around his boots.

He fell.

Lucien cast.

Dark blue mana struck one hunter directly and threw them through the wall.

Rez followed.

Blue-white force froze the moving chains around Vale.

The captain shattered them with the pommel of his sword.

Aurelia backed toward the interior chamber.

The hatchling growled.

Zee listened.

Heartbeats.

Nine.

Then eight.

Then seven.

He began tracking them instinctively.

One behind Lucien.

Zee cried out.

Lucien turned.

The invisible blade missed his throat.

He struck the attacker.

A second heartbeat moved toward Rez.

The hatchling saw the disturbance in the snow first.

He lunged.

Teal-and-gold fire exposed the hunter’s cloak.

Rez ducked beneath a blade and slammed blue-white mana into the attacker’s chest.

The figure struck the ceiling and collapsed.

Aurelia stared.

The children were learning too quickly.

That frightened her.

Because danger was the teacher.

One hunter remained outside.

Zee heard that heartbeat differently.

Steady.

Not attacking.

Waiting.

The same pattern from the palace assassin.

Observer.

His Crimson Mana stirred.

The hunter outside raised something.

Not a crossbow.

A small glass sphere.

Caldrin saw it.

“Down!”

The sphere shattered against the floor.

White smoke filled the room.

Not poison.

Cold.

Impossible cold.

The fire died.

Ice spread across stone.

The royal witch collapsed to one knee.

“I cannot feel my hands.”

Aurelia wrapped Zee inside her cloak.

The hatchling shivered.

The Dragon Link flooded with cold.

Zee reacted.

His wolf blood resisted first.

Heat rose beneath his skin.

His vampire blood slowed what should have been panic.

Crimson Mana pushed outward.

Not attacking.

Insulating.

Warmth formed around him.

Then around the hatchling.

The Dragon Link carried relief.

Near.

Warm.

Aurelia felt the temperature shift against her arms.

She looked down.

Zee’s body had adapted faster than hers.

Not because he understood the cold.

Because something inside him refused to let it win.

The hidden observer moved closer.

Zee heard the heartbeat.

He turned toward it.

His eyes fixed on empty air.

The hunter stopped.

Zee growled.

The sound came deeper than before.

The snow outside the broken window compressed beneath invisible feet.

The hunter backed away.

Then ran.

Caldrin reached the window.

“Let them go.”

Vale looked toward him.

“What?”

“They came prepared.”

“For witches.”

“For invisibility.”

“For cold.”

Caldrin looked toward the forest.

“And one stayed outside to watch.”

Lucien’s expression hardened.

“They were testing us.”

“No.”

Aurelia looked at Zee.

“They were testing him.”

Silence.

The royal witch finally managed to reach her mana again.

The suppression faded with distance.

She rubbed warmth into her fingers.

“Whoever they are, they now know cold does not affect him normally.”

Lucien looked toward the shattered windows.

“They also know he can hear what he cannot see.”

Vale stood.

“And track movement without sight.”

Caldrin sheathed one blade.

“Which means the next group will change methods.”

Aurelia’s face hardened.

The pattern had become undeniable.

The world hunted Zee.

Each failure became information.

Each survival became preparation for the next attempt.

Outside, the surviving hunter disappeared into the snowy forest.

Zee watched the empty tree line.

He could not know he had been observed.

He only knew the danger had gone.

For now.

The hatchling pressed against him.

Safe?

Zee relaxed.

Safe.

Temporarily.

⸻

The captured hunters carried nothing identifying them.

No coin.

No names.

No letters.

No crests.

But their masks were made from the same pale bone.

Lucien laid one on the council table.

Theron stared at it.

His face changed.

Aurelia noticed.

“You know it.”

Theron shook his head too quickly.

Caldrin smiled without humor.

“That was convincing.”

Theron looked away.

Lucien leaned forward.

“Speak.”

The former prisoner swallowed.

“I saw masks like that once.”

“Where?”

“At the sanctuary.”

The room went still.

“When?”

“Months ago.”

“Who wore them?”

“I never saw faces.”

“What did they do?”

Theron looked toward the mask.

“They collected survivors.”

Aurelia’s voice lowered.

“Survivors of what?”

“The experiments.”

Silence.

Vale frowned.

“Why?”

“I do not know.”

Theron rubbed his hands together.

“They came after each trial.”

“If someone lived longer than expected, they took them somewhere else.”

Caldrin’s expression sharpened.

“Where?”

“I never found out.”

Lucien looked toward Aurelia.

The sanctuary.

The assassins.

The masked hunters.

Still not enough to prove a single organization.

But closer.

Aurelia tapped the mask.

“They did not try to kill Zee.”

Vale nodded.

“They incapacitated the room.”

“Tested his senses.”

“His response to cold.”

“His defenses.”

Caldrin looked toward the door.

“And withdrew once they had enough.”

Theron whispered, “They are measuring him.”

The room fell silent.

Aurelia stared toward the upper floor where Zee slept.

“They will not do it again.”

Lucien looked at her.

“You cannot guarantee that.”

“No.”

Her eyes hardened.

“But I can stop giving them easy opportunities.”

Vale understood first.

“You want to move him.”

“Yes.”

“To where?”

Aurelia looked toward Lucien.

“Somewhere they cannot prepare for.”

Caldrin smiled faintly.

“That rules out every known sanctuary, palace, fortress, and road.”

“Exactly.”

Lucien leaned back.

“What are you proposing?”

Aurelia looked toward the winter map.

Mountains.

Forests.

Old borders.

Unclaimed regions.

Dangerous places civilized armies avoided.

“If safety attracts hunters…”

She paused.

“Then we stop looking for safety.”
