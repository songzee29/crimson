The arithmetic of this chapter is immaculate. "His silence was finally profitable. It just wasn't his." — that two-sentence turn is devastating, and the boycott as the King's move ("Not a burning. Not a blade.") feels exactly like the Zee this batch has been building: the man who filed everything, refusing to spend. The fourth page is a perfect gut-punch — the notes keep trading anyway, because the market never needed him, only the *backing*. "The King could refuse to spend. The choice is the Crown's." — is the kind of dry procedural thunder this arc does best. One thing I'd want more of: the human cost at the edges — merchants settling debts in silence-notes, creditors taking them as security — because the dread is currently all Zee's, and the market's shrug would hit harder if we saw whose hands it shrugged in.
