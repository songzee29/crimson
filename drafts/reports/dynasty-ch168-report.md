## Pass 2 — Beta reader notes
"The ocean had raised a theologian" — I laughed out loud, and then the chapter got quiet and broke my heart. The treaty scene under the waterline is the emotional center: two boys inventing a language the ocean can't read, writing under its handwriting, and Ner understanding "the one page the ocean couldn't read now carried the ocean's own handwriting — and the boys had written beneath it anyway." That is the whole saga in miniature: small people writing under the powers that claim them. The shell on the windowsill — warm as skin, holding a palmful of sea that wrote nothing — is a perfect object; it holds menace and tenderness in the same breath, which is exactly the Nymeris question the draft keeps open. One thing I'd want more of: Zayrlen counting the high tides at the nursery window is doing heavy quiet work — the oldest son learning the tide's books untaught. I want one more beat where the reader sees the cost of that learning, because right now it's dread-adjacent rather than dread-earned.
