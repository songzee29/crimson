## Pass 2 — Beta reader notes

What landed: the daughters' ages did the heavy lifting — "They don't know what I drew. They think their father runs a bait shop and is bad at it" is the line this chapter will be remembered for. Zee's refusal of the lie is the moral center of the piece: "Men like us don't get natural deaths, Drovak. We get honest ones" is the best line in the draft, and the follow-up — "they will never once in their lives mistake a map for a neutral thing" — makes the mercy *hurt* the way dark fantasy mercy should. Senn's laugh ("I never once drew that… that you'd let me talk") humanized him in six words. And the clean kill after all that fire-talk was the right subversion: the hunger screams *throat*, the wolf lunges, the fire rises — and Zee just breaks the man's neck like snapping a branch. Restraint as terror. That's Zee.

What confused: the counting. The opening lists six roles ("Selk Varren built it… Jorren Vale sold the tickets"), then Senn as the seventh named, then Pell appears late and offstage as the eighth — a reader has to assemble the eight across two scenes. It's followable on a second read but it wobbles on the first. And "Vale bought you seven" / "Seven down" tripped me for a beat before I realized one *seven* is days and the other is kills.

Pacing: strong. The opening exposition reads a little like a card-catalogue rundown ("he laid out the eight the way you lay out a hand of cards") — but Senn's voice earns it back quickly. The ending ("He wanted the truth on his own doorstep, too") is a perfect out.

One thing I'd want more of: the hunger's new behavior — "it didn't sing, it *sharpened*" — is the most intriguing thread in the draft and it gets one paragraph. That single sensory detail (the absence of the song) is the Zerlin escalation becoming *felt* rather than plotted. I'd linger on it one beat longer next time it appears.
