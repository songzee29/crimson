## Pass 2 — Beta reader notes

What landed: the dismantled-house opening is the best image in the chapter — "the way you dismantle a stage after the play" reframes Senn's passivity as agency, and the doors-left-open detail ("I'd rather keep mine") is quietly devastating. The hunger being *disappointed* by a quiet death gives the revenge arc's spectacle a cost: six weeks of fire, and the last one refuses to feed it. The twist that "we were the demonstration, not you" is the chapter's coldest beat — it turns the whole arc from victory lap into surveillance report. The final collapse of the list-shape ("not even as a door off its hinges, but as a room he was already standing inside, with the walls gone") is the strongest writing in the batch so far and earns the blackout setup.

What confused: Senn's line "I wrote it for yours, too" promises a page "near the back you'll want" — the three-line payoff lands, but Zee's reaction to it is mostly withheld (he reads, folds the pages, moves on). A beat of Zee *sitting with* those three lines before the levee scene would pay off the mystery the draft itself sets up. "Vane's ghost" reads as metaphor (guilt/paper trail), but one word could clarify it's the ledgers, not a haunting.

Pacing: quiet by design, and the quiet is the point. The kill is merciful and fast — a genuine contrast to the six weeks of spectacle, which makes the denial of the hunger feel like restraint rather than mercy. This is the tonal inverse of 153/155, which is exactly why the collision is a Zee call, not a quality call.

One thing I'd want more of: the levee read-through. The draft lists the eight ("Varren's engine, Brannic's fist, Thoss's knife, Vane's money, Sera's mind, Vale's ledger, Senn's eyes") as autopsy — but never names Pell among them, and the envelope man is the only live thread left. A sentence or two of Zee weighing *whether* to hunt a man who was only ever described to him would sharpen the cliffhanger into a decision.
