## Pass 2 — Beta reader notes
This chapter terrifies me in the best way, and it's not because of violence — it's the nursery-door argument between Zee and Ner, "whose turn" to be the one standing ready with a loaded no. That's the most married-couple fight in the whole saga, and it's over ocean law. Ner arguing the arrangement calmly, "gold banked low and steady," while Zee can't get his no out fast enough — that's a relationship with history. Nymeris kneeling "like any grandmother" wrecked me, because the dread that follows is exactly what Zee felt: "she hadn't taken the boy. She'd taken the word. And she was keeping it." A creditor who doesn't collect — only keeps the receipt — is colder than any theft. One thing I'd want more of: Rez's warning at the gate ("It always names something") is delivered and then vanishes — I want to see him after the shore-party returns, because he was the one who read the second word "small, low, where only I was standing," and that knowledge is still sitting unspent.
