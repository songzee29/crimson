What confused me: "eight names, seven dead, one bought" took a beat to parse — Pell "bought" his life with the ledgers, but "one bought" first reads as if someone purchased him. A half-line of grounding ("the one who'd bought his life with paper") would clear it instantly. The shell-ledger mechanics ("a shell inside a shell... a title, a direction, a hand that never touched paper") are evocative but abstract — I trust the ledgers matter without quite seeing them.

Pacing: clean three-beat structure (war table → counting room → home → war table again). Nothing drags; nothing rushes. The Ner scene earns its quiet.

One thing I'd want more of: Victor. He's the whole chapter's gravity — the ninth name, the feather, the test — and I felt him precisely as much as the chapter wanted me to. But now I want the cost made concrete: what does "the ninth name costs" actually mean in blood or leverage terms? The next chapter that answers that is the one I'll be waiting for.
