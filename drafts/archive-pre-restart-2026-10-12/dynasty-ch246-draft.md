# Chapter 246 — "The Verse, Filed Back"

**DRAFT — PROPOSAL ONLY. Not canon until Zee approves.**

---

The morning's harbor paper came to the gate in the clerk's basket, the way it always did: docket pages, tide items, the file's small change of the day. And under it, face down, one page that was not the file's.

Ner found it before the clerk could catalog it. That was the whole of the trick now — being faster than the filing, the way you'd be faster than weather. He turned it over in the gate-yard, in the open air, where nothing the house had said yet could be quoted as said indoors.

The handwriting was the nursery hand. The cadence of every line taught in the corridor — the hand that had taught the boys to hold a pen, to read the sea, to ruin a song on purpose. But the grammar was the registry's. Every line in the sea's grammar, the kind the file could quote and file and cite in triplicate, written in the hand that belonged to the house.

A continuation. A verse of the lullaby the house had bought — the one Ner had paid for in gold, by hand, in the dark, with the receipt in the registry's hand reading *paid, for the sleeping* — continued, finished, in the tide's own grammar, and never taught to anyone. Never sung at the shore. Never wrong-noted. Never heard.

Ner read it twice. The first time for the words. The second time for what it cost.

That was the arithmetic he did standing in the gate-yard with the clerk's basket on the stone beside him and the page in his hands, and it came up the way tide-arithmetic always came up: with a second column he hadn't been told about. He had paid the tide retail for Azren's humming. Gold. By hand. In the dark. The receipt had said *paid, for the sleeping*. And he had understood it, standing there at the waterline with the gold going out of his hands, as the end of something.

It had not been the end of something. The sea kept every receipt, and a receipt is not a closing. It is a standing. The tide had accepted the payment — *paid* — and now the tide had filed one back, in the nursery hand, in registry grammar: *received*. Not *settled*. Not *closed*. *Received* — the way you write it on a package you're going to open later.

The sea didn't do gifts. It did transactions. And the house had just been paid change for a purchase, in a currency nobody had agreed to, and the change was a verse, and the verse was in his son's mouth already — because Ner could feel it, the way you feel weather, that the boy would hum this before nightfall, untaught, the way the water hums through walls.

He folded the page and put it in his sleeve. Not in the drawer. Not in the basket. The drawer was for things the house kept, and the basket was for things the file took. The sleeve was for things his hands could find in the dark.

Inside, the boys were at the table. Zayrlen, crimson-gold eyes on the harbor book he'd been set to read, the eldest-of-the-two with the watcher's patience. Azren, gold shimmer soft at the elbows, humming already — the old cadence, the old bought song, on-key and unthinking, the sea in the walls at low tide.

Ner stood in the doorway and did the thing he had been doing for a year now: the keeping, in his own voice, by hand, in the open, so there was no record to argue with.

"This is the rule of the verse," he said. "I am saying it once, out loud, in this room, because rules said in the dark get misquoted."

Zayrlen put the harbor book down. Azren's humming stopped mid-breath, the shimmer going still the way water does when it's told something.

"The verse may be held," Ner said. "Hands may touch it. Eyes may read it. That is the whole of what hands and eyes may do."

He looked at Azren — his gold one, his youngest, the boy the tide answered for and the registry quoted. "It may be hummed. In this room. In the dark. By hand, with no record. A song is not a filing, and what you hum in your sleep belongs to you the way your blood does."

He looked at Zayrlen — crimson-gold eyes, steady, the boy who had held the inside of the door and asked for nothing for it. "You may read it. You are not asked to sing it. The sea never answered for you, and I will not spend your silence buying its grammar."

And then, because he was Ner, and the keeping was his, and nobody's keeping but his: "It may never be spoken where the file can quote it. Not in the registry's grammar — not to the clerk, not at the well, not to the shore, not to any mouth that files. If the water wants its verse answered, it can hum it through the walls. The house answers nothing in the sea's grammar, because answering the sea in its own tongue is a filing, and this house files nothing by hand that the ocean can quote."

Azren's hands were small in his lap. "It's a pretty verse," he said.

"It is," Ner said, and his voice did the hot thing, the sea-disrespected thing, but gently, the way you speak to a son you are not angry with about a sea you are. "That is how you know it isn't ours."

Zayrlen's crimson-gold eyes stayed on him. The boy understood more than was safe, the way he always did. "What do we do with the receipt?" he asked. "The one that said *received*."

Ner looked at his eldest-of-the-two, and loved him the way the tide grips the shore — hardest and gentlest — and said the truest thing in the house: "We keep it. We keep everything the sea gives us, by hand, in the dark, with no record. And we never, ever cash it."

That night, after the lamp was lit and the boys' breathing evened out — Zayrlen's first, measured; Azren's later, slower, the shimmer dimming to ember-under-ash — Ner stood at the nursery door and hummed the new verse once.

Wrong. On purpose. In the house's own tongue, ruined the way he'd taught, the wrong-notes laid over the sea's grammar like stones over a grave.

The first mouth that spoke the new verse in the house spoke it ruined. That was the whole of the law in the corridor, and Ner was the whole of the law.
