# Chapter 236 — The Tide Gets Docketed

**DRAFT — PROPOSAL ONLY. Not canon until Zee approves.**

---

The Order's guardian had standing in two courts.

That was the file's own arithmetic, and I had done it the way you do any arithmetic: twice. The dated court, where his oath was sworn in nursery-hand time. The hour-court of the missing hour, where his second oath was sworn in a gap the file owned but did not run. Two standings, two venues, one man — and the Order's counsel read that fact aloud in the corridor the way men read a map when they intend to redraw it.

He filed for standing in a third.

I read the petition standing in the harbor wind with the tide at my ankles, because the petition named its venue in the first line, and the first line was the whole play. *Petitioner: the guardian, of the Order, standing in both courts, by and through both oaths. Venue requested: the water between the courts — the drowned carrier-name record, the second tide's phenomenon — recognized as a venue of this registry, and the petitioner as the only man standing in it.*

He wasn't asking for access. He was asking for a courtroom.

The guardian's cold read — I grant him this, he was not a stupid man — was that the file had exhausted itself arguing about the sea and was now ready to argue about the paper that said where the sea stood. Two courts had spent a season fighting over the drowned record, the carrier-name transaction the second tide raised, the phenomenon the fixed direction built. The corridor between them was water. Water, un-docketed, was the only place in the kingdom where no motion could be filed — because no court owned it, no clerk stamped it, no docket had a page for it.

Make it a venue, and it has a docket. Own the docket, and you own the tide-twin — phenomenon or not. The tide wouldn't be studied anymore. It would be *litigated*, in a courtroom where the Order's man was the only person allowed to stand.

The magistrate called the hearing for low tide.

We stood in the corridor — the King, the counsel, the guardian, the registry's clerk with the triplicate forms — and the water came up around our ankles the way it always does at the hour, indifferent, patient, filing nothing and forgetting nothing. The guardian made his argument standing in it. He said the sea had been summoned as a party, had testified in cadences the file could cite but never quote, and had been ruled a non-person in a docket it still hadn't left — and a party that the file insists on hearing but refuses to seat needs a venue that belongs to no court and answers to the registry. He said his two oaths gave him standing wherever time was in dispute. He said the phenomenon was already doing the work of a courtroom, and the registry was simply late to the acknowledgment.

He said the Order had been arguing about the sea. He wanted to start arguing about the water.

I watched the King. Zee stood with his boots in the tide and said nothing, and I have learned to read the King's silence the way brokers read a held price: it is not absence. It is arithmetic being done where nobody can audit it.

The Order's advocate — the disowned man, the one who swore to the waterline and now files for the sea against his own clients — stood apart from all of us, at the water's edge, with his hands empty. He had not been invited to the hearing. Nobody had un-invited him. The tide doesn't take briefs, and it doesn't take attendance either.

The magistrate looked at the water. Then at the guardian. Then at the file the clerk held, the petition in triplicate, the two oaths cited, the older liturgy cited against it — the one that said the sea carried the body and the sea keeps the drowned, and by the older grammar the carrier of the body is the guardian of the hand.

"Granted," the magistrate said.

One word. The clerk's pen stopped, because a clerk's pen always stops when a court invents geography.

The magistrate spoke it into the record the way men speak sentences into weather. The water between the courts — the drowned carrier-name record, the phenomenon the second tide raised — is a venue of this registry, duly constituted. Its docket is opened. Its officer is the petitioner, the only man standing in it. Motions in the tide's docket will be heard at the waterline, at low tide, in the tide's cadence, with the registry's clerk present to certify what the water files.

The guardian bowed. It was the bow of a man who had just been given the keys to a room that was going to fill with water.

Because here is the cold arithmetic, and it is the whole of this chapter, and I would stake my stipulation on it: the enemy opened the venue to own the tide, and a venue is not a throne. It is a *docket*. A docket has rules. A docket has pages. A docket has a first motion and a second motion and the cold, procedural, total fact that once you give the water a place to file, the water files.

The Order wanted a courtroom in the corridor so it could docket the phenomenon against the King.

The corridor is now a courtroom. The phenomenon has a docket.

And the tide — which the file has spent a season summoning, serving, taxing, and taxing again, without ever once getting it to say a word the registry could quote — just got handed the one thing it never asked for and always wanted: a drawer of its own, in the enemy's filing cabinet, with the enemy's officer standing in it, waiting to be told what to file.

The magistrate closed the hearing. The tide came in. The clerk stamped the docket open in triplicate, in the harbor wind, with wet boots, and the first thing the new venue owned was the water around our ankles — and the second thing it owned, I understood walking back, was the Order's own guardian, standing in it, alone, the only man in the kingdom who is now legally required to be present wherever the tide decides to file.

He wanted standing in the water. He has it now. He can never leave it.

Forward: the treasury assesses a venue fee on the docket, payable by the venue's officer. The guardian who petitioned for the water now pays rent to stand in it, quarter by quarter, in triplicate. The dread: the Order opened the courtroom and inherited the overhead, and the only revenue the venue generates is the tide's.
2. **"The Docket's Seniority."** The dated court, the hour-court, and the tide's docket now run in parallel — and filings arrive in all three. Forward: a litigant files the same motion in two dockets and wins in one, loses in the other. The registry must rule which docket's judgment binds. The dread: the Order built the third venue to control the tide and accidentally built a conflict-of-laws machine that eats every ruling the file has made since the second tide arrived.
