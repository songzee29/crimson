# Chapter 185 — "The Debt, Assigned"

**DRAFT — PROPOSAL ONLY. Not canon until Zee approves. Reviewed by Vigil next shift.**

---

The debt got an owner the way the toll always got its things: quietly, correctly, in the registry's own hand.

Tessa found it on the consolidated docket at the ninth bell, filed under the debt's own line — twelve children, at toll price, carried forward, interest compounding — and the new entry underneath it was shorter than the line it bought, which was the registry's way of telling the truth without saying anything.

*Assignment of Crown debt instrument. Holder, current: the Order of the Hollow Crown.*

Nine read it standing in the war-room doorway, the nursery dust still on his boots, and the shake was in his hands before he'd finished the second line.

"They bought it," he said. "They bought — us."

"Not you," Tessa said. She didn't soften it, because the house didn't soften true things anymore. "The arithmetic of you. The registry's claim for twelve children the nursery refused to furnish. The Order didn't buy the children. It bought the Crown's right to collect them."

"That's the same thing."

"That's what the assignment says it isn't." Tessa laid the second page down — the Order's filing, in the nursery hand, sealed with the Hollow Crown's stamp. "They paid the Crown for the instrument in full. In consideration, they released the Crown's lien on the toll. The Crown's arithmetic is satisfied. The debt is satisfied — as against the Crown." She paused. "As against the house, the debt is now the Order's. And the Order doesn't collect debts the way the Crown does. The Order furnishes. The Order places. The Order's nursery is the Crown's licensed supplier of placed children."

Nine sat down. The war table was covered in the morning's paper — the rota's warrants, the contempt line, the ash — and the new page sat on top of it all like a stone dropped into shallow water.

"Twelve children," he said. "At toll price. Carried forward. And now the Order holds the paper."

"The Order holds the paper," Tessa agreed. "And the Order's paper has a price list attached. They don't want the Crown's kitchens paid. They want the toll's arithmetic settled the way the nursery settles things —" She turned the page. The Order's nursery hand was exact, the way all the nursery's exactness was exact. "Final placement. By the licensed supplier. The Order will furnish the twelve to the Crown's account from its own stock — children it already holds — and collect from the gate school in satisfaction of the assignment."

"In satisfaction," Nine repeated. "Their children, first. Then ours."

"Theirs first. Ours after. The registry calls it *consideration*."

---

The war-marshal brought the council's reading at the tenth bell, the way the council brought all its readings now — late, and already argued.

"It's clean," the marshal said. "That's the worst of it. The Order bought a Crown debt instrument through the registry's own transfer rules — the ones the Crowns wrote when they still thought debt was something that happened to other people's arithmetic. The assignment is filed. The holder is current. The Crown can't call the debt back because the Crown already sold it, and the registry won't unwind a filed assignment because unwinding filed assignments is what the magistrate calls *undermining the registry*, and the magistrate will not undermine himself six days before a hearing."

"The hearing is five days out," Tessa said. "Four, if you count the way the registry counts."

"However you count it," the marshal said, "the advocate will argue, in open session, that the King's household owes the Order of the Hollow Crown twelve children, filed, assigned, and transferable. And the magistrate will have the assignment in his own file."

Zee stood at the window. He hadn't turned around since the assignment came in.

"There's a countersignature," he said.

Tessa blinked. "My King?"

"The roster." Zee's voice was flat, exact, the tribrid stillness banked underneath it. "The debt is twelve children from the gate school's nursery. The nursery's roster is the satchel-master's roster. For the Order to collect — even the Order, even with the assignment stamped and current — the toll's arithmetic moves through the master of the count. That's the toll's own grammar. The registry can transfer the debt. It can't transfer the children without the hand that keeps the count."

The marshal looked at Tessa. Tessa looked at the assignment.

"He's right," she said slowly. "The assignment buys the *claim*. The roster is Nine's. The Order can hold the debt, but to move twelve names from the gate school's books to its own placements, it needs the master's hand. The toll was built so no clerk could ever move children without the man who counted them." She set her pen down. "That's why the Order sent the second page."

Nine looked at the second page. He hadn't read it yet. Nobody had read it to him.

It was addressed to the satchel-master of the nursery. In the nursery hand. It named a price.

*Countersignature of the master of the count, transferring twelve names from the gate school's toll roster to the Order's placement roll, in satisfaction of the assigned debt instrument. In consideration of the countersignature, the Order will strike the household's contempt line from the consolidated docket.*

Nine read it twice. The shake ran through his hands, and he didn't hide it.

"They'll strike the contempt," he said. "If I sign the children over."

"One line for twelve names," Tessa said. "That's the market's arithmetic. That's what the nursery's always been — a place that trades children for paper, and then trades the paper for less paper."

---

He went down to the courtyard at dusk, the assignment folded in his pocket the way the roster used to be, and Rhyven was at the gate with his hand flat on the warm stone, the way he was at every dusk now — listening to the road.

"They want my signature," Nine said.

Rhyven didn't turn. "To move the children."

"To move the children. The debt's been sold to the Order. The Order will pay the Crown's account with its own, and take ours after. All it needs is my hand. And it offered —" Nine stopped. The offering was the contempt line, and the contempt line was the house's oldest wound, and saying it aloud in the courtyard felt like reading the roster in the registry's cadence.

"It offered to strike a paper," Rhyven said.

"Yes."

"The paper the magistrate has been holding over the house since the winter."

"Yes."

Rhyven turned then. The dusk was doing its work on his scars — the Specimen Seven line at his throat, the wrap at his wrists — and his eyes were the color the gate's dust turned at evening.

"Nine," he said. "The registry has been holding that paper since before you had a name. The Order is offering to strike it for the price of twelve names you'd have to write yourself." He paused. "The contempt line is the house's paper. The names are the children's. Which one of those is yours to spend?"

Nine looked at the gate. Thirty children breathing in the dust beyond it. The eight-year-old running the count for the new arrivals, the way she'd learned it — count what comes, count what leaves, never count what's staying, because the staying isn't the toll's to know.

"The names aren't mine either," Nine said. "That's the whole of the refusal. That's what I wrote on the three lines." He took the assignment out of his pocket and folded it once, the way the nursery folded things it was sending back. "My signature's not for sale. Not for the contempt. Not for anything with the nursery hand on it."

He went back up to the war room and wrote four lines in the gate book, in his own hand, underneath the three lines of the refusal:

*The debt is assigned. The holder is the Order. The roster is still mine. Furnish nothing. File this.*

He signed it with the number the nursery had given him, and underneath, the name the eight-year-old had promised to hold for him:

*Nine. The leaving kind.*

Tessa sealed it for the registry with the house's wax. The refusal of the countersignature went out the way the refusal of the sale had gone out — quietly, correctly, filed where the registry could read it.

The registry read it. The registry filed it.

*Countersignature refused. Assignment stands. Collection rights: holder, current.*

The debt had an owner. The owner was the Order. And the roster — the master's hand, the only hand the toll would take — had refused to move.

The collection would come anyway. That's what collection rights meant. The paper just wouldn't carry Nine's name on it when it did.
