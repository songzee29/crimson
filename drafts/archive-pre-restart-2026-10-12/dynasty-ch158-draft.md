# Chapter 158 — "The Blackout"

**DRAFT — PROPOSAL ONLY. Not canon until Zee approves. Reviewed by Vigil next shift.**

---

The list was done, and the hunger had nowhere to go.

Zee understood it the way you understand a tide chart — not as a feeling but as an arithmetic. Seven men dead in as many weeks, and every death had fed the thing, taught it the shape of being fed, carved the shape so deep it had become the shape of him. Thoss's blood. Vane's heart. Sera's mind. Vale's fire. And then Senn — the quiet one, the small one — and the hunger denied, cheated, *starving* in the middle of a finished feast, turning in the dark of him like a dog that has eaten the whole house and is starting on the walls.

So he did the thing he had never done before.

He asked Ner to stay home.

Not told. *Asked.* At the door of the dust-sheeted house on the edge of the Quarter — the same house, the same locked doors, the same dark — Zee put his hands on Ner's shoulders and said it plainly, the way you say a thing you've rehearsed and hate: "Not tonight. Please. One night. Let me have this one night."

Ner looked at him for a long moment. The gold was banked low under his skin, steady, unafraid. Thirty-four years of never flinching, and this was the first time Zee had ever asked him not to come.

"You're afraid I'll see something," Ner said.

"Yes."

"Something you think would change what I do when I see it."

"Yes."

Ner was quiet. Then he nodded — once, the way you nod when you're agreeing to something you hate because the man asking is the man you love. "One night," he said. "Rez stays."

It wasn't a negotiation. It was a condition. Zee took it.

Rez was already there, of course. Rez was always already there — in the corridor's shadow, blue-white mana turned down to a pilot light, saying nothing. He had been sleeping with his back against Zee's door for weeks now, and nobody had asked him to, and nobody thanked him, because some things in a household you don't name or they break.

"Lock it from the outside," Zee told him, at the door. "And don't open it. Whatever you hear."

Rez looked at him. "And in the morning?"

"In the morning," Zee said, "you tell Ner I'm fine."

It was a lie and they both knew it was a lie, and Rez didn't say so, which was the closest thing to kindness either of them had left.

---

The hunger woke at the wrong hour.

It didn't creep in this time. It didn't tune itself in the next room. It came up through the floorboards of him all at once — vampire hunger and wolf fury and dragon heat braided into a single red scream — and the embers at the edges of everything *breathed*, in and out, in and out, like something asleep that was almost awake, like something that had been waiting for the list to end so it could finally have the whole of him.

Zee locked the doors from the inside. He locked the windows. He sat on the floor in the dark of the dust-sheeted house and tried to outwait his own blood.

It didn't work. It had never worked. The hunger wasn't a thing you outwaited; it was a tide, and the tide was in, and there was no list left to aim it at, no throat that belonged to it, no heart it was allowed — and the house on the edge of the Quarter was full of his *people*, Ner with his gold turned low, the boys asleep in the soft dark, Rez in the corridor —

*No.*

The wolf lunged at the leash hard enough to shake his bones. The fangs were down. His hands were shaking. The dragon heat rolled off his scales in waves, and the air in the dark room shimmered, and the hunger *screamed* for a throat, any throat, *the nearest throat* —

Zee put his own arm in his mouth and *bit down*.

The fangs went through the forearm to the bone. His own blood hit his tongue — hot, iron, *his* — and the hunger seized it the way a drowning thing seizes a rope, and *fed*. Zee sat on the dust-sheeted floor in the dark with his teeth in his own flesh and let the tide have him, let it drink from the only throat it was ever going to get tonight, and the wolf snarled and the dragon heat rolled and the embers breathed, brighter, *brighter* —

— and it worked.

That was the terrible part. Not the pain — the pain was nothing, the pain was *honest*. The terrible part was that it worked. The hunger coiled down, sated, sullen, *fed*, and the wolf settled, and the heat banked, and Zee sat in the dark with his arm in his teeth and his blood on his chin and understood, with perfect cold clarity, what he had just taught the thing inside him.

He had taught it where the hinges were.

*Me,* he thought. *When there's no one left on the list. Me.*

He took his arm out of his mouth. The wound was already closing — crimson light knitting the torn flesh, the tribrid blood doing what tribrid blood did, healing the damage even as he made it. By morning there would be nothing but a scar, and then not even that. The body would erase the evidence. The body was complicit.

He laughed, once, in the dark. It sounded wrong.

---

He woke at the wrong hour with ash in his mouth.

Not his blood this time. Ash. Gray, fine, bitter — the taste of the unnamed fire, banked somewhere behind his ribs, breathing in its sleep. He sat up on the dust-sheeted floor and looked at his hands in the dark, and his hands were clean. His arm was healed. The doors were still locked from the inside. The windows were still locked.

But there was a gap.

An hour. Maybe more. Gone the way hours went in the blackout nights — not asleep, not awake, just *missing*, a room in his own head with the door closed. He'd been somewhere in that hour. Done something. The ash in his mouth said the fire had been awake. The clean hands said it hadn't touched anything.

Or that it had, and the night had washed it clean.

Zee sat in the dark with his healed arm and his clean hands and his missing hour, and for the first time since the harbor, since the eight, since the whole long counting began — he was afraid.

Not of the hunger. Of the gap.

---

Rez opened the door at dawn.

Zee was sitting on the floor where he'd left him, back against the wall, arm bare where he'd pushed the sleeve up. The scar was already fading — a pale line, then a paler one, then nothing. Rez looked at the arm. Looked at Zee's face. Said nothing for a long moment.

"You didn't go far," Rez said finally.

"I stayed."

"That's not what I'm asking about." Rez crossed the room and crouched in front of him, blue-white mana turned low and steady, the way you'd bank a lamp in a sickroom. "Your arm, Zee."

"It's fine."

"That's not what I asked either." Rez's voice was flat, the way it got when he was holding something heavy with both hands. "How many nights?"

Zee didn't answer. The ash was still in his mouth. The gap was still in his head, an hour wide, door closed.

"One," he said. "This was the first."

Rez nodded slowly, like a man filing something he hoped he'd never have to read back. "And the hour?"

"Gone."

"And the fire?"

"Awake." Zee looked at his clean hands. "It didn't touch anything. I don't think it touched anything."

"You don't think." Rez stood. He went to the window and unlocked it, let the dawn in, gray and honest. "Here's what I know, brother. You asked Ner to stay home, and he did, and that cost him more than any night he ever spent holding your face while you shook. You asked me to lock the door, and I did. And whatever you did in here" — his eyes flicked to the fading scar — "you did it so the hunger wouldn't choose. That's the arithmetic." A pause. "The question is what it costs you the second time. And the time after that. Because it will ask again."

Zee stood. His legs held. That surprised him, obscurely. "Victor's watching."

"Victor's always watching." Rez's mouth did something that wasn't a smile. "Let him watch this. Let him see what his window bought him: a king who'd rather eat his own arm than let the hunger near his house." He held Zee's eyes. "That's not the answer he wanted, Zee. That's the answer that *ends* the test. A man who'll do that can't be starved on command. He can only be starved by his own hand — and his hand is the one thing Victor never learned to move."

Outside, the city was waking. The wards in the streets hummed faint and blue. Somewhere, thirty-four years of attention rested on the dust-sheeted house like a held breath —

— and let go.

Zee felt it go the way you feel a hand lift off your shoulder. Not an ending. A *recalculation*. The test hadn't ended. It had just stopped being about hunger.

Whatever came next, it wouldn't be a window. It would be a door. And it would have Victor's hand on the handle.

He walked home through the dawn with Rez beside him, and Ner was waiting at the door — hadn't slept, obviously, gold banked low and eyes bright with the particular fury of a man who'd kept a promise he hated — and Ner looked at Zee's arm, at the place where the scar had already finished fading, and said nothing.

He just held out his hand.

Zee took it. They went inside. The boys were waking up in the soft dark of the house, and the kitchen smelled like bread, and the king darkness chose sat at his own table with his lover's hand in his and his best friend at the door, and let himself be, for a little while, just a man come home from the night.

The hunger coiled under his ribs, sated, patient, *waiting*.

The embers breathed.

And in the back of his head, behind a closed door, an hour sat in the dark like a held breath — and did not open.
