# Chapter 210 — "The Inch That Follows"

**DRAFT — PROPOSAL ONLY. Not canon until Zee approves. Reviewed by Vigil next shift.**

---

The unconfirmed hour had been nine inches.

Nine inches of ledger, unfilled, unfiled — the gap in the registry's record where Azren Neyron Darkheart's name was supposed to sit complete, and didn't. Ner had held the filing brush over it before. He had written, stopped, written. The file had taken his writing wrong — taken the defense as a correction, filed the father's hand over the son's name like an erasure wearing a father's face.

Tonight it was ten.

Ner stood in the registry anteroom after the boys were asleep — the nursery corridor quiet in the way it had been quiet since the wolf — and looked at the ledger under the lamplight, and the unconfirmed hour had *grown*. An inch. One more inch of unfiled time, where the registry could not say whether the child had been there, or had been noise, or had been anything at all.

The dread came up through him like tidewater. Not the file's dread — his. The dread of a parent who understands, finally, that the file isn't uncertain about the boy. It is *deciding*. One filing at a time, one inch at a time, it is deciding whether Azren was ever there.

He picked up the brush.

He had not been summoned. That was the thing the magistrate would say later, in the tone of a man reading a charge: *you were not summoned to the ledger, Nér Azrion. The filing was not yours to make.* And Ner would say, in the tone of a father, which is to say the tone of a person who has stopped being afraid: *it is my son's name. It has always been mine to write.*

The brush was loaded. Gold-black ink, the registry's own. He set the tip to the page — to the half-inscribed name, *AZREN NEYR*, the last four letters drowned in the unconfirmed inch — and he wrote.

He wrote the way the sea writes on a shore: once, and meaning it. *AZREN NEYRON DARKHEART.* He pressed harder than the registry's hand ever pressed, hard enough that the ink bit the page, hard enough that the letters stood up out of the paper like scars. He finished the name. He finished it the way he had finished carrying the boy — all the way, no abbreviation, no correction, no mercy for the file's uncertainty.

The page took the ink wrong.

He watched it happen — watched the gold-black bleed at the edges of the letters, watched the *R* of NEYRON feather into the paper's grain, watched the registry's enchanted stock do what enchanted stock does when a father's hand writes too hard — and the name, his son's name, finished and true and *his*, came up through the ink wrong. Blotted. The blot read, to the file's eye, as a strikethrough wearing a signature.

A correction. Filed over.

Ner stood with the brush in his hand and looked at what the page had made of his defense, and he did not cry. That was the first thing. The second thing was the sound from the doorway.

"You're bleeding."

Azren. In his nightshirt. Gold shimmer low and flat in the lamplight, the gray still at its edges from the corridor. He had followed — of course he had followed, he was a boy who had learned this week that the people who loved him wrote things down about him — and he was looking at Ner's hand. The brush had cut him. Pressing that hard, the nib had scored the heel of his palm, and the gold of his mana was welling out of the cut, bright, wrong, *his*.

Ner set the brush down. He crossed the anteroom in three strides and went to his knees — a king's lover, a Pure Gold Mana wielder, on his knees on a registry floor — and took his son's face in his hands, and the gold of him went warm, the way it had gone warm in the corridor when the boys split the honey, the way it went warm every time the file tried to tell him what his children were.

"It's nothing," he said. "It's a scratch. Come here."

But Azren was looking past him, at the ledger. At the name. At the blot that the file had made of his father's defense.

"Is that me?" Azren asked. Quiet. Precise. The words of a boy who had learned, from the file, how to say things that cannot be unsaid.

Ner closed his eyes. The sea in him — the monarch's blood, the old gold water — went very still, the way it went still before it decided to take the shore back.

"Yes," he said. "That's you. And it's wrong."

"Why is it wrong?"

"Because the file is deciding," Ner said, and he did not soften it, because the boys had grown past softening, the wolf had seen to that, "whether you were ever there. And I am telling it that you were. And it is filing what I tell it as a correction. Do you understand?"

Azren was quiet for a long moment. The gold shimmer of him came up — slow, uncertain, the gray burning off at the edges like fog. "Then tell it louder," he said.

Ner laughed. It came out of him like a wound opening — the hot, wet, *feeling* laugh of a parent who has been given, by a small boy in a nightshirt, the only instruction that has ever mattered. He pulled Azren into his arms, onto the registry floor, the brush forgotten, the ledger open, the blot drying into the page like a scar — and he held his son, and the gold of him went loud, the way the boy had asked, loud enough that the registry's lamps guttered.

Behind them, in the doorway, Zee.

Ner felt him before he saw him — the pressure change, the heat at the edges, the King's terror folded down into a man in a corridor. Zee looked at the ledger. At the blot. At the finished name under it, scarred into the page, legible despite the file's best effort — because a father's hand, pressed hard enough, writes deeper than enchantment.

Zee said nothing. He came into the anteroom. He sat down on the registry floor — the King, on the floor, knees up, too big for the room — and he put his hand over Ner's bleeding hand, and his other hand on Azren's back, and the three of them sat in the ten-inch hour like it was the only room in the house.

"The file filed it as a correction," Ner said. His voice was steady. It cost him everything.

"I know," Zee said.

"Then it's wrong on the record."

Zee looked at the name. *AZREN NEYRON DARKHEART.* Scarred in. Legible. His.

"Let it be wrong," said the King who had filed everything. "The record is the file's. The boy is ours."

And Azren — gold shimmer bright now, the gray gone, his father's lover's arms around him and the King's hand on his back — reached out, with one small finger, and touched the blot on the page. Touched his own name, through the file's erasure.

"I'm here," he said. To the page. To the file. To the ten inches. Quiet. Precise. Unfileable.
