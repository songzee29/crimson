# Chapter 152 — "The Window"

**DRAFT — PROPOSAL ONLY. Not canon until Zee approves. Reviewed by Vigil next shift.**

---

The fire took Jorren Vale standing up.

Zee's hand was on the paymaster's chest, and the broker was still talking — still *brokering*, even then, "the network, the names, it's all yours, just—" — and the hunger rose up through Zee like a tide coming in, and this time he didn't reach for the throat at all.

This time he let the fire have him whole.

It came off Zee's shoulders in wings of shadow and burning light, vast in the wood-and-leather study, and the real fire in the grate *bowed* to it like a lesser thing acknowledging a greater. The air shimmered. The bought wards on the walls crisped and died like moths. Vale's eyes went wide — not with fear, with *awe*, the broker's mind finally, finally out of calculations — and the black-crimson fire folded around him like a closing eye.

He didn't scream. There wasn't time. There was a sound like a bell struck once, deep, and then the smell of ash and rain, and then Jorren Vale was gone the way men go in stories told to frighten children — all at once, and completely, and without anything left to bury.

Zee stood in the ruined study with the wings folding away and his breath steaming ember-red, and the hunger *sang* — sated past sating, taught past teaching, the shape of being fed carved so deep now it had become the shape of him — and at the edges of the song the embers breathed, brighter than they'd ever been, and Zee felt the window not as an opening anymore but as a *door*, and the door was off its hinges.

*Six,* he thought. *Selk Varren. Brannic Dor. Vaeren Thoss. Kaldrek Vane. Sera Quill. Jorren Vale.*

*Six.*

He walked out of the Garden District with ash on his coat and the city opening for him the way it always did, and he could feel it now — the watching. Not eyes; *attention*. Thirty-four years of it, patient as geology. His father, somewhere, watching through whatever instruments watched kings.

*Testing, not killing.*

*Let's see what you do with the window, my son. Let's see what you choose.*

*Hunger or family.*

---

He didn't go home. Not at first.

He went to the empty house on the edge of the Quarter instead — one of the family's places, dust-sheeted, the wards sleeping — and he locked the doors from the inside, and the windows, and he sat on the floor in the dark and tried to outwait his own blood.

It didn't work. It had never worked. The hunger wasn't a thing you outwaited; it was a tide, and the tide was in, and the wolf was snarling at the leash hard enough to shake his bones, and the dragon heat was rolling off his scales in waves, and the embers at the edges were *breathing*, in and out, in and out, like something asleep that was almost awake.

*Not yet,* he told it. *Not yet. Not tonight.*

His fangs were down. His hands were shaking. He'd killed six men in as many weeks and every one of them had fed the thing he was trying to starve, and Victor — *his father* — was watching to see if the king could be starved on command.

He heard the door unlock at midnight.

He didn't move. He knew the step. He knew the gold.

Ner came into the dark room with his mana turned up — not a pilot light now, a *lamp*, warm gold light spilling across the dust sheets — and he looked at Zee on the floor, fangs down, shaking, wings of shadow breathing off his shoulders, and he didn't flinch. He never flinched.

"Don't," Zee said. It came out rough. "Ner. Don't come closer."

"No." Ner kept walking. "You don't get to decide that."

"I could hurt you—"

"You won't." Ner sat down on the floor in front of him, close enough to touch, gold light pooling between them. "Thirty-four years I've known you, Zee Darkheart. You've never hurt your own. Not once. Not when the hunger was new, not when the wolf was wild, not tonight." His hand came up — slow, giving Zee every chance to pull away — and cupped Zee's jaw, thumb along the cheekbone, gold mana warm against dragon heat. "I'm not afraid of you. I'm *here*."

The hunger *screamed* — so close, the gold so close, the throat so close — and the wolf lunged at the leash, and the fire surged —

— and Zee held. Shaking, fangs down, embered breath steaming in the dark, he *held*, because the man in front of him had chosen to be there, because love wasn't a leash you put on hunger, it was the reason hunger didn't get to decide.

Ner's forehead touched his. Gold against crimson. The hunger coiled down, sullen, *waiting* — not beaten, never beaten, but denied, tonight, by choice.

"Stay," Ner said quietly.

"I'm trying."

"I know." Ner kissed him — salt and ash and rain — and held his face while the shaking eased, and the king darkness chose sat on a dust-sheeted floor at midnight and let himself be held, because that was the other half of the code, the part no one warned their children about: that the man who kills without hesitation is held together by the hands that never flinch.

Outside, the rain fell on the wards carved into the streets of New Orleans, humming faint, blue, patient.

And somewhere, his father watched.
